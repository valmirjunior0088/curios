# curios-pipeline

The Curios compile driver: `compile_entrypoint`, `compile_units`, `Stage`, and the fold that strings the pipeline together from a parsed `curios_surface::Entrypoint` to a `curios_wasm::Module` — plus, in `standard.rs`, the same fold with the fixed prelude supplied. It is the compiler boundary: everything a compilation needs and nothing a product decides. How the stages work belongs to each stage's crate; where judgment sits in the sequence and what `Stage` observes belong to the crate rustdoc.

## Design

### The driver is the compiler boundary, and its predecessors are not its decision

**Decision.** This crate depends on no runtime, no Binaryen, no CLI and no `curios-package`, and folds its stages over whatever predecessors it is handed. `curios-package` sits beside the boundary, and `curios-js` does not touch it.

**Rationale.** Manifests, dependency resolution and the cabinet answer what is in a compilation and where each part came from, which is a product's question: `curios` answers it over `curios-package`, and the browser, with no filesystem, answers it differently. A driver that knew manifests would need a plausible answer from every caller that has none. The boundary is the manifest's — no `curios-package` row here — and [`curios-package`'s README](../curios-package/README.md#one-subsystem-one-owner) states it from the other side.

**Rejected.** The driver resolving its own inputs, which puts a filesystem assumption below the browser product.

### The standard predecessors are a function here, not a policy

**Decision.** `compile_entrypoint` takes a `Predecessors` and cannot tell which unit is `/std`. `standard.rs` sits above it and supplies the fixed prelude, `/sys` and `/std` first in scope, and nothing in the half that knows no particular predecessors calls it. A package named `std` that is a fold's first unit takes the archived `/std`'s place, compiled over the archived unit as its baseline and granted what that root could see, and every later unit is compiled against it; only the first unit and the last root can qualify, and both are asserted ([A stored unit is a baseline for an item-level recompile](../documentation/design/compilation/a-stored-unit-is-a-baseline-for-an-item-level-recompile.md)).

**Rationale.** The native product, the browser product and this crate's fixtures would each spell the prelude by hand, and three callers agreeing on one spelling is a missing function. The standard library is the largest Curios program and the one edited most, and a question about one of its modules would otherwise be a collision of two claims on `/std`. Reserving the name makes the answer independent of which binary answers, and a baseline is correct for any tree, since a declaration is kept only where its lowering is the baseline's and everything its elaboration asked is answered as it was.

**Rejected.** Superseding by prefix for any unit of the fold, when a later unit's predecessors are not the ones the archived unit was compiled against; identifying the tree by the record's directory, which depends on the binary's checkout; a manifest key or root kind for the standard library; recognizing the prelude in `curios-wonder` or the package layer, neither of which can reach the archive.

### A baseline crosses the cache seam as a unit, and the cache decides

**Decision.** `Cache::baseline` hands the fold a unit compiled from an earlier text of the same sources, or nothing; the fold compiles over it with `compile_unit_over` and hands the result to `put` as any unit. Whether a cabinet offers a baseline and files what was compiled over one is the cache implementation's decision: the cabinet's own cache offers the nearest earlier unit and files everything, and the `wonder` engine's offers the same and files only what it compiled from the text on disk ([`curios-wonder`'s README](../curios-wonder/README.md#a-question-files-the-units-it-compiled-from-disk)).

**Rationale.** A baseline crosses the seam as the one thing the fold understands, a `Unit`, never a path or a record, and which commands take one is written in one place: a build takes one because the cabinet's cache answers, and nothing of the fold says so.

**Rejected.** A `baselines` parameter on `compile_units`, a second seam for the same fact; deciding by entry point, when `wonder --stage` and `curios test` reach the fold through the same ones.

### The puts are this crate's, behind `test-support`

**Decision.** What hands a program or a module to the checkers as an instrument, rather than building it, sits in `test_support` behind `test-support`. `examine_with_prelude` puts a program whole to both checkers at the tail it is handed — the program contract, as a suite guarding a rule puts one, or `False`, as the product's put does over `examine_entrypoint` ([below](#a-term-put-at-false-is-answered-here)) — and hands back the elaborator's error and the obligations and verdicts each checker reached, each with the report a compilation makes of it; `typecheck_with_prelude` and `recheck_with_prelude` are its two halves. `prelude_form_scope` is the scope Curios/Core is read over in front of the prelude. The instruments beneath, `examine_entrypoint` and `typecheck_entrypoint`, take the predecessors they are handed. What the product asks of the checkers is no instrument and ships: Curios/Core read and compiled ([below](#curioscore-is-read-here-outside-the-trusted-base)), and a closing term put at `False` ([below](#a-term-put-at-false-is-answered-here)).

**Rationale.** This is the lowest crate that puts a program over the prelude, so the board's puts and the rules' tests in `curios` are one function, and a ticket and a rule test cannot disagree on what refused a program and where. Behind a feature, no build that ships holds an instrument.

**Rejected.** A put per consumer, which states twice how a refusal is read. A put in the board's crate, which a test of `curios` cannot reach.

### Curios/Core is read here, outside the trusted base

**Decision.** A text of Curios/Core is read for the product by this crate (`read_core_text`): parsed with each item's span, read by `curios-surface`'s reader over the units in scope, its closing term put at the type its question puts it at, its foreign rows gathered and its declarations anchored where the text writes them. `compile_read` compiles what it reads as a unit, where a program's is lowered and elaborated, judged and erased as any unit is, over the baseline a cabinet offers; `enter_core_text` enters a loose text on top of a fold as a program is entered, its closing term judged at the program contract over its unit and erased, which `Fold::check_core_text` is the check of. `wonder`, the soundness board and the board's histories read through this one door, and a refusal of what it reads is placed where the text writes the declaration it is about, as a lowered unit's is placed where its lowering wrote it.

**Rationale.** The trusted base is `curios-cert`'s dependency closure ([its README](../curios-cert/README.md#the-trusted-base-is-a-crate-boundary)), which holds no parser; this crate depends on the reader and on the certifier's production walks both, so it composes them without moving either. Nothing the certifier is trusted for rests on the reader: a term the certifier admits at `False` is a flaw whatever text it was read from, and what a refusal of a text says rests on the round trip that holds the reader to the writer. One door for the product, the board and its histories means what the board says of a ticket is what `wonder` says of the same text. A text form nothing reads back rots, as GHC's External Core did until it was removed ([ghc-devs](https://mail.haskell.org/pipermail/ghc-devs/2014-April/004798.html)); read by the product, Curios/Core is held by what users write in it as well as by the round trip. A refusal stands where Lean puts a kernel's, at the declaration that was being added ([`Lean.Exception`](https://lean-lang.org/doc/api/Lean/Exception.html)).

**Rejected.** The door in `curios-cert`, out of `test-support`, which makes the parser, printer and formatter part of the trusted base, as `coqchk` reads its input inside the checker and an external Lean kernel parses its export file inside its own; and behind a feature of `curios-cert` other than `test-support`, which makes that base a function of the features a build turns on, the call-closure reasoning its decision rejects. A stable exchange format, as Lean's external kernels read one that has moved under them ([lean4export](https://github.com/Kha/lean4export)): Curios/Core is read by the build that writes it and promises nothing across releases, and a ticket whose text rots is archived.

### A term put at `False` is answered here

**Decision.** `put_program` and `put_core_text` put a subject's closing term at `False` — a program whole to both checkers through `examine_entrypoint`, Curios/Core through the door to the certifier alone — and hand back what each checker said (`Put`): every refusal by its checker's own error, named as that checker's crate names it, and where it stood, with the reports a compilation makes of it, which `wonder --false` renders for its reader and the board for a reader standing nowhere. A subject that does not lower or read was put to no checker and is no answer. `wonder --false` and the soundness board ask through these two functions.

**Rationale.** A proof of `False` is a claim about the checkers a release ships, so the question is the release's: asked through one put, what the board says of a ticket's witness is what any build says of the same text, and whoever suspects a forgery asks it with no checkout, as Rocq ships [`coqchk`](https://man.archlinux.org/man/coqchk.1.en.txt) and Lean [`leanchecker`](https://reservoir.lean-lang.org/@leanprover/lean4checker) and `lake check` ([comparator](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover/comparator/master/README.md)); a check runs no code of the program's, so the question needs no binary of its own to be untainted. An answer states what each checker said, as each of Metamath's verifiers is heard ([mmverify.py](https://github.com/io12/mmverify.py)), each refusal in its checker's own words and placed. The names of the checkers' errors ship because the answer names them.

**Rejected.** The put in the board's crate, which no release reaches; in `curios-wonder`, which the board would reach only through an engine of editor and command-line questions. An answer naming a refusal by its message, which changes with its wording and does not say which checker made it; an opaque code beside each error's name, as rustc's and GHC's diagnostics carry, a second name for each error that nothing here reads. A summary of what an answer rests on, as `coqchk -o` and Lean's `#print axioms` give: Curios has no postulate — a written goal stops a build, and a `foreign` row states a host action — so an answer rests on the checkers and on the units in scope, believed as every build believes them.
