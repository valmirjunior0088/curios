# curios-anf

The Curios erased IR, in A-normal form: the flat, explicit, first-order stage between `curios-erase`'s type-directed erasure and the continuation IR of `curios-cps`, with the one-way door `lower_to_cps` between them. Types, proofs and erasable binders are gone by construction. How the module prints and how its identities are spelled is [A printer states each fact once, where it is bound](../documentation/design/tools/a-printer-states-each-fact-once-where-it-is-bound.md); the representation and its derived analyses belong to the crate rustdoc.

## Design

### The ANF optimizer is thin

**Decision.** The ANF optimizer runs exactly the transformations whose leverage is semantic — pruning, compile-time partial evaluation of closed terms and literal spines, reconstruction recovery, the monoid worker/wrapper rebase, and the inversion of a structural recursion into a loop — and nothing else. Folding, dead code, inlining, contification and specialization belong to `curios-cps`, after the lowering.

**Rationale.** ANF's leverage is what it still knows: hand CPS no work it can delete, run what compile time has decided, and make a loop of a recursion that would take a frame an element, re-based around a monoid or inverted to take its levels in turn, while the peels it splits on are still there to read it by. A second local-rewrite engine would restate CPS's reductions over a second representation, and the two would drift.

### Shapes stay distinct

**Decision.** The erased alphabet keeps erased Core's semantic identities — distinct scalar shapes, schema-carrying products and variants, dedicated `Bool` and `Nat` switches, first-class folds. One shape's operations are never reused for another, and conversions between shapes are explicit operations.

**Rationale.** Every encoding decision — carriers, tag layouts, dispatch, loop synthesis — belongs to the lowering into CPS. Collapsing shapes early discards information the backend cannot recover, and an operation reused across shapes acquires a per-context meaning no node-local classification can read.

### An item imports what it reads of another's row

**Decision.** A row another declaration declares is one of a fragment's imports, as a value or a function it uses is: a structure's schema at its width, and a family at the count of constructors it declares, with each constructor the item names at its position, which is its discriminant. Erasure reads a mask where the declaring item holds it, and only the declaring item lists a row whole, as does every program, which has no import; linking maps an imported constructor by its position and asserts its row resolved. A module that reads no row, as an item's evaluation does, holds each row as its fragments do, an import at what they state of it ([`Resolution::imports_rows`](src/link.rs)), so evaluating a row's readers lists none of it.

**Rationale.** A unit of erasure is a declaration, not a module, so a row copied into each fragment that names it is a copy per declaration: a family's constructors, each an item reading its family, cost the square of the family. [Lean's](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover/lean4/master/src/Lean/Declaration.lean) constructor carries its index (`cidx`) and [OCaml's](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ocaml/ocaml/trunk/typing/data_types.mli) its tag and its type's constructor counts (`cstr_tag`, `cstr_consts`, `cstr_nonconsts`): an importer reads the declaration and keeps the tag.

**Rejected.** A position on every constructor's record, which every reader of a record would carry for an importer's sake; a row held by reference, where a fragment would no longer verify alone.

### Numeric carriers are exact

**Decision.** The erased carriers are Core's, unbounded — `Nat` and `Int` as `curios-num`'s `Natural` and `Integer`, `Flt` as binary64 — with the carrier methods every stage's constant folder shares. The runtime's split between an i31 and a boxed magnitude appears nowhere in the IR; it is `curios-cps`'s and `curios-emit`'s ([Nat and Int are an i31 until they outgrow it](../documentation/design/arithmetic/nat-and-int-are-an-i31-until-they-outgrow-it.md)).

**Rationale.** One shared semantics keeps the folders from drifting from each other or from emitted code, and keeping the runtime's representation out of the IR keeps a representation choice from becoming a semantic one.

### Reconstruction recovers the existing value

**Decision.** The erased optimizer's `reconstruct` pass (`optimize/reconstruct.rs`) substitutes a value rebuilt from the fields of an existing one with that value, before layout selection, while schema and constructor identities are still explicit: a complete product rebuilt in order from one base's projections, and an exhaustive match that rebuilds its scrutinee's constructor in every arm; a unit field is forwarded only where immutable value flow proves the source field unit. Its facts are a graph of construction sites, shared and never expanded (`reconstruct/facts.rs`): a known call connects to its function's return, a recognized constructor wrapper to a site whose fields are its arguments, and a parameter, a state read, an unknown call and a computed recursive member are opaque; a key reached by more than 32 origins widens to opaque, and an empty flow proves nothing. A wrapper is recognized by its body as an argument-to-field template composed through acyclic forwarding (`reconstruct/templates.rs`). Plans settle through a deterministic worklist over one snapshot, substituting and removing the bindings a substitution strands are one transformation, so a call immediately returned reaches the tail-call lowering, and a match is removed only where every arm holds administrative work alone.

**Rationale.** Erasure can leave a runtime reconstruction around an answer whose source only restated evidence, and before layouts obscure them, schema and constructor identities make the equality explicit. A computed recursive member is a forcing boundary, and effects, traps and unknown calls keep their order.

**Rejected.** A second general simplifier in ANF; recognizing a constructor by its prelude name; an arm's return value alone as proof that its evaluation may be deleted.
### A recursion closed by a monoid is a loop

**Decision.** The erased optimizer makes a loop over accumulators of a recursion whose leaves close their own call with combines of one monoid: tail recursion modulo a monoid context, as Leijen and Lorenzen derive it ([*Tail Recursion Modulo Context*](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2023/07/trmc-popl23.pdf), §4.4–4.5). A leaf ends in a self-call and a chain of combines, each reading the answer before it once, in a place the monoid's product takes, every other operand a value from before the call, so the chain is one word `l ⊕ □ ⊕ r`. A monoid that commutes folds both sides into one accumulator; a sequence's concatenation, which does not, threads the left side's accumulator, the right side's, or both, as the leaves use them. Which scalar operation is a monoid and its identity, and which sequence operation is its grain's free monoid's product or append, are the oracle's to state (`semantics.rs`), by matches over the closed alphabets, and a law test holds every one against the oracle's own folds. A recursion each of whose leaves closes its call as the second operand of an operation its first can decide (`Semantics::decided_by_first`: `a && f(t)`, `a || f(t)`) is left as written where the function may be dropped (`droppable_functions`, `summary.rs`): placement runs the call only where the first operand does not decide, as a tail call ([`curios-cps`'s README](../curios-cps/README.md#a-droppable-computation-runs-where-it-is-read)).

**Rationale.** A recursion left as written takes a frame and a return an element, on the stack up to a budget and on the heap past it ([A recursion is as deep as the heap allows](../documentation/design/compilation/a-recursion-is-as-deep-as-the-heap-allows.md)), which a loop does not pay. Only the combines move across the call — Koka's liberal context, whose operands after the hole are values — so nothing observable is reordered, and the licence is associativity and an identity, since no carrier here is partial. The laws are the language's own: `Nat`, `Bytes` and `List` are Core's free monoids, and conversion decides their concatenation's associativity and identities ([The carriers' algebra stays in conversion](../documentation/design/arithmetic/the-carriers-algebra-stays-in-conversion.md)). A rope's concatenation is one node, so the accumulator builds what the written recursion's cons built at the same allocations and needs no reverse, the case Erlang finds faster than a body recursion ([The Seven Myths of Erlang Performance](https://www.erlang.org/docs/22/efficiency_guide/myths)); it leans the rope left, as `List/fold`'s builders already do. A conjunction's accumulator carries an answer its first operand already decided to the recursion's end, where the call placed in the arm that has not decided stops at the first operand that does; a recursion that may not be dropped keeps its call where it is, and the accumulator's loop is the one it gets.

**Rejected.** A destination with a hole, OCaml's [tail modulo constructor](https://ocaml.org/manual/5.3/tail_mod_cons.html) and Koka's constructor contexts: a rope node states its length where it is built, and that length is the part not yet built. A twin proved equal, Lean's `@[csimp]`: a second definition and a proof for every builder, a door that swaps a definition where it is erased, and `/std` alone reached. A move across the call licensed by the effect summary: the summary decides whether an unused binding may be deleted, a division takes its proof by position, and that proof can be the recursive call's answer, erased. Commutativity as the licence, as LLVM's accumulator rewrite takes it: it leaves every sequence out. A continuation as the accumulator: it moves the frames to the heap, where the stack already holds them. Mutually recursive groups and a recursion of two calls: `/std`'s descend a command tree and a key's bits, never a list's length. An exit at a short-circuiting monoid's absorbing element, the accumulator tested every turn: a second statement of the case the operation already is, which placement reads.

### A structural recursion is a loop

**Decision.** The erased optimizer's `invert` pass (`optimize/invert.rs`) makes a loop of a recursion that peels one parameter — a `Nat` at `0` and its predecessor, a sequence at its head and suffix — and calls itself once, on what the peel left, with every other parameter as it came. Its body computes the base and enters a worker that takes the levels in turn, each turn the step arm handed that level's peel and the answer so far, and its own answer handed on to the next turn in its tail: a `Nat`'s worker counts the predecessor up from zero to the parameter, and a sequence's counts the indices down from its length, reading each level's element there. The worker is a tail recursion, which the CPS optimizer makes a loop like any other, and it is what the recursion was, its role and its termination mark among it. Every run of the step arm reads the call's answer, the peel is the only read of the peeled parameter, the call the only read of a sequence's suffix, and the statements ahead of the call perform nothing observable by the effect summary (`summary.rs`). It runs after the rebase.

**Rationale.** The recursion and its loop compute one function by induction on the peeled parameter: a level's step is the arm, and its hypothesis the call's answer. That is the iteration Liu and Stoller derive from a recursion by incrementalization ([*From recursion to iteration: what are the optimizations?*](https://doi.org/10.1145/328690.328700), PEPM 2000) at its simplest increment, one peel, where the loop needs no state but the answer so far and a count of the levels — the frames the recursion would have kept hold nothing an index does not give back. A sequence's levels are taken from its end because a right fold's innermost level is its last element, which is what Lean's tail-recursive `List.foldr` does by hand, `Array.foldr` over the list (`List.foldrTR`). A recursion left as written keeps a frame a level, so the loop is what lets a structural recursion cost what a loop an author would write costs. The loop runs the base first and each level whole from the innermost out, where the recursion runs the statements ahead of each call from the outermost in: what follows each call keeps its order, and what runs later than it did performs nothing a program could tell the order of. Nothing runs earlier than it did: the base reads only what the function was handed, and a level's statements only its own peel, so a proof erased from them still holds where they run, which is the hazard a move licensed by the summary meets in the rebase. A call only some runs read is placed in them ([`curios-cps`'s README](../curios-cps/README.md#a-droppable-computation-runs-where-it-is-read)), so a recursion closed by `&&` stops at the first level that decides it, which a loop running every level would not. The rebase goes first: a recursion it takes is a loop already, and one closed by a deciding monoid that may not be dropped is a loop only the rebase makes.

**Rejected.** Recovering the loop after the lowering, in CPS: what makes it one is that each level's peel is a count, which is plain in ANF and gone once its arms are continuations. A form of its own for the loop, a fold node the lowering builds a loop from: a loop below Core is a tail call, which the CPS optimizer already makes one, and a second form is a second shape for every pass to read. Reading a sequence's suffix where a step reads it: the loop reads each element by its index and builds no suffix, and a step that reads one is left as written. Moving the statements ahead of the call whatever they perform: the recursion runs them from the outermost level in and the loop from the innermost out, so a trap or an effect among them would be met in another order. A recursion of two calls: the second stays a recursion inside the loop, and one of two calls on the same peel recomputes at every level what the loop had.
