# curios-surface

The Curios surface language as a tree: the scannerless parser that reads a `.crs` file into it, the printer that writes it back as written, the formatter `curios format` runs, and the way back for a report — `from_core`, which translates a Core term to the tree whole or not at all, and `spell`, which names a declaration as resolution takes it back from where a reader stands ([A diagnostic spells what its reader can write](../documentation/design/tools/a-diagnostic-spells-what-its-reader-can-write.md)) — and Core's own printer with the naming policy every term is spelled by. What the surface language *is* belongs to [syntax.md](../documentation/syntax.md), which is normative and which `src/parse.rs` implements; that a surface feature is a tree node desugared during lowering, never in the parser, is [Syntax forms are closed, semantics extend by witness](../documentation/design/surface/syntax-forms-are-closed-semantics-extend-by-witness.md). Resolution and lowering are [`curios-module`](../curios-module/README.md)'s. Local architecture — the combinator grammar, the printer's layouts — belongs to the crate rustdoc.

## Design

### Core is written here, and the kernel writes through a hook

**Decision.** The naming policy a term is spelled by — `Spelling` and its axes, `Shorten`, `Rename`, `Minting` — and the printer of Core text live in this crate, beside `from_core`, which translates a report's term to the surface tree. The printer writes Core text proper — every field a node holds stated, so what it writes reads back as the term — under the spelling a dump and a kernel's refusal are written in (`Spelling::core_text`), and the same forms in their reader's spelling under every other: no decorator, an intrinsic by its surface name, a level metavariable as `?u1`, since a reader can write none of what a decorator states. `curios-core` keeps what a unit's names are spelled by, the `Lexicon` kept with the unit, and `Notation`, the hook a crate below this one writes a term or a level through: a kernel refusal renders through the one its reporter hands in, `Spelling::for_refusal` where a reader reads it. Nothing below this crate implements `Display` by writing Core's syntax; a term, a level, a module and a program are written by `Spellable`, under a spelling or faithfully.

**Rationale.** The translation and the printer read one policy, so they live together, and the kernel stays out of the printer's graph: what `curios-cert` reaches is its trusted base, and a printer decides nothing.

**Rejected.** The policy in `curios-core` with a hook back to this crate for the translation (`ReaderPrinter`), a seam that existed only because the two halves sat in two crates. The kernel depending on this crate, which would put the parser and the formatter in the trusted base's graph.

### A top-level item is dispatched on its head, and a reserved head commits

**Decision.** `parse_top_item` reads the optional `pub` and the item's leading word once and switches on it. A head that is reserved and cannot begin a term — `mod`, `use`, `induct`, `struct`, `foreign` — commits, so its arm owns the diagnosis. The other four fall through, and the language decides which: `concept`, `satisfy` and `test` are contextual words [syntax.md](../documentation/syntax.md) keeps as identifiers outside a declaration position, so one may begin a program's tail, and `let` is shared with the term grammar, since `let x = 1; tail` must fall through to a local binding. A `pub` or a `---` documentation comment in front makes every arm commit, since after either there is no tail for a failed item to become. A head that names no item is recoverable under the same exception — it is how the item loop ends before a tail — and reports the heads that would have named one. A module, having no tail, re-runs the item parser when input remains, since the repetition keeps only committed failures. The head is read raw, without its trailing whitespace, so an unrecognized one is reported against the word.

**Rationale.** Every top-level form is led by one word from a disjoint set, so ordered choice encodes a dispatch as backtracking: all nine alternatives fail at one offset, the furthest-failure tie-break keeps the earliest and blames `mod` for every unrecognized head, and the recoverable failures leave a library reporting `Expected 'end-of-file'` at its first column. Reading the head once makes the arm that owns the error the arm that produced it. Commitment is stated at the dispatch, as `curios-parse`'s choice asks ([its decision](../curios-parse/README.md#choice-backtracks-until-an-alternative-commits)), because depth is the wrong selector.

**Rejected.** Reserving `concept`, `satisfy` and `test`, which syntax.md keeps contextual and which `is_keyword`, owned by `curios-common`, would then refuse as a module or package name. An offset threshold, trusting the item error only past the first token, which leaves `satisfy` blaming `mod`. Committing on all nine in modules only, a flag threaded into nested bodies for a message the module's leftover-input path already gives.

### A broken item stays in the tree, and `str::parse` is whole or nothing

**Decision.** A committed parse failure inside a top-level item does not end the module. The item loop keeps it as `TopItem::Broken` — the report, the text skipped, and the name the head declared where it parsed that far — and resumes at the next line beginning at column 0 with `pub`, a `---` comment, or an item's head word followed by whitespace (`curios_parse::recover`, with this crate's anchor rule). A candidate is parsed, never trusted, and only committed failures recover; a `let` commits once its signature and `=` are read. A failure inside an inline module body is the `mod` item's, and its name is nobody's. Lowering registers a broken item's name as a public binding and lowers nothing for it; the compile boundary refuses a unit holding one, listing every broken item ahead of elaboration's reports, and the elaborator withholds every dependent of a broken name. `Module::parse` and `Entrypoint::parse` recover — the compilation's, the editor's and standard input's reading — while `str::parse` is whole or nothing, refusing at the first broken item, for a fixture, the header `curios-package` reads, and the formatter.

**Rationale.** Without recovery one parse failure costs the whole file's diagnostics, the one stage stopping at its first mistake where elaboration recovers per item. Recovery is commitment read one level up, so the item-or-tail ambiguity gains no heuristic. Column 0 is the formatter's convention, so code ignoring it only loses an anchor, and a wrong anchor produces neither a wrong tree nor a spurious diagnostic. A fixture asserting a diagnosis must not pass on a module parsed with a hole in it, and a formatter must not rewrite a file it could not read whole.

**Rejected.** Resynchronizing at an item's terminator, which needs a lexer for strings, comments and `end` nesting the scannerless grammar lacks, and meets `;` inside a body; reusing the editor grammar's recovery, a second grammar the editors own; a side list of parse errors beside a module that pretends to be whole, which a consumer could forget ([A unit holds its own definitions](../documentation/design/compilation/a-unit-holds-its-own-definitions-and-the-prelude-is-an-environment.md)); registering a broken `mod`'s scope, when a broken module declares nothing.

### A comment is placed by following the source

**Decision.** `curios format` places a comment from the source text alone. The renderer is handed the text the file was read from and steps through it with every character it writes, so a comment is due exactly when everything written before it has gone out. One written after code rides the line that code lands on, where only closing punctuation may still follow it; one written on a line of its own takes a line between the two tokens it stood between. Where the layout offers no line start between them the renderer breaks the line itself, and what continues it hangs one indent step in until the layout's next own break. The printer says nothing of where a comment goes, and one thing of a block: where its members end, so a comment written before a closer takes its line at the members' column, with the closer on the line below. What it may spell differently from the source is stated once: a list's last comma and the parentheses the tree does not record, as character classes in `src/format.rs`, and a literal printed from its value, by a mark where the term or the arm's pattern holding it ends, at which the renderer picks the text up again.

**Rationale.** Where a comment goes is a fact about the output and about two tokens of the source, and neither token need be a node of the tree: the one a comment follows or precedes is as often punctuation a printer writes — a closer, an arrow, a separator — as anything the tree records. A rule keyed on the text holds for every form the grammar has and every one it gains, with nothing for a printer to remember, and it is checkable as stated: `src/format/place_tests.rs` writes a comment at every position of its fixtures, and at a stride through the tree's sources, and asserts that it stays there and that a second run changes nothing.

**Rejected.** Position reports from the printer — where each construct begins and how far it reaches — which need one at every syntactic position and a position in the tree behind each: an arm's head, a case's end, a closer. Where one is missing or stands on a later term the comment drifts to the next report, and a comment riding an opener sinks one construct a run. Attaching each comment to the node it leads, which cannot reach a comment no node owns and depends on build order. Comments as trivia on the tokens of a lossless tree, which is what makes placement exact in a formatter that has one, and needs a lexer and a second tree the scannerless grammar lacks.

### Lists wrap between items and preserve each item's layout

**Decision.** A list that fits stays on one line. When it spans lines, runs of simple values pack within the formatter's width, wrapping at the next separator and continuing one indentation level inside the enclosing block, independently of the opening bracket's column. Calls, nested structures and spreads of structured expressions each stand on their own line and retain their internal break opportunities; a following sibling starts on a fresh line. A list that needs to wrap also lets its enclosing call, tuple or arm ladder break. Comments keep their source position, and closing punctuation shares the final element's line where it fits.

**Rationale.** A run of values is easier to read when short elements share a line, while a long call or nested expression needs its own break opportunities. Atomic fills remain appropriate for import names and packed atoms; list elements use structural wrapping.

**Rejected.** Stacking every element when a list outgrows one line, forcing complex elements flat merely to pack the gaps between them, and attaching a sibling to the closing line of a multiline element.

### A curried call is one call

**Decision.** A head followed by two or more argument lists, `h(a)(b)(c)` with `h` a name or a projection off one, is laid out as the call its arguments would make written in one list, `)(` standing where a comma would, with one exception: the first list hugs the head. Flat when the whole chain fits; otherwise the head and its first list stand on one line, the first list breaking inside only where it cannot fit there, and each later list's arguments go one level in, one per line, every closer riding its list's last argument and the next opener riding that closer, so `)(` ends a line as a comma does, and whatever follows the chain rides the last closer. The chain hugs its last argument where that call would — a lambda, a list or a tuple after nothing but atoms, in every list — with all its lists on the head's line, and a chain whose head is a ladder keeps the ladder's layout, below.

**Rationale.** A curried call is how `Fmt/render` and every function answering a function is applied, and its argument lists are one call's arguments written apart. Decided apart, only the last list breaks, wherever the line runs out: `)(` lands mid-line with the lists before it, an argument hangs below it, and two chains of one shape come out in two layouts by where their widths fall. Broken at every seam, a chain whose first list breaks is laid out exactly as the call written in one list, and one whose first list fits differs from it only where the first list hugs the head, which keeps a template's text beside the name that renders it.

**Rejected.** Each later list on a line of its own, `(` opening the line: a list that must break inside itself then leaves `(` alone on its line, which most of `/std`'s chains — `Eq()(a, b)` among them — would come to. Starting the first list on a line of its own too, which leaves the head alone on a line.

### An applied ladder measures its calls with its arms

**Decision.** A `match` or `choose` stays flat only when its whole postfix chain fits. Otherwise its arms and `end` take separate lines, and each attached call lays out its arguments within the room left after `end`. Short arguments stay attached as `end(p)`; longer argument lists may wrap in their ordinary shape.

**Rationale.** An application belongs to the expression whose result it calls. Measuring the ladder apart from its calls can keep every arm on a crowded line and send a single short argument to the next. Giving the ladder the whole chain to measure lets its structural breaks make room for the call. The call's own nested group still decides whether its arguments fit after that break.

**Rejected.** Measuring only through `end`, which leaves the application out of the decision; adding an outer group while keeping an independent inner ladder group, which still lets the ladder stay flat after the outer group breaks; and forcing argument lists flat, which cannot accommodate long arguments or comments.

### The tree keeps sugar as written

**Decision.** The surface tree is literal: function-definition signatures, infix operators, destructuring patterns, postfix `!`, and string and spread literals are kept as written, and undone only while lowering.

**Rationale.** A `Term` prints back as written, which is the printer's round trip and what the formatter rests on.

**Rejected.** Desugaring in the parser, which loses the spelling the printer and the reports print.
