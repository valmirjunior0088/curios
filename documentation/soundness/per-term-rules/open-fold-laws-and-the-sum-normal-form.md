# Open fold laws and the sum normal form

**Assumes.** Every open fold — a reduction rule firing on a symbolic operand — is a valid equation on the carrier's values

**Status.** **probed** over values: `curios-core`'s `reduce::intrinsic::laws_tests::every_open_fold_law_preserves_the_value_at_every_closed_instantiation` pins each open fold to the law's own stated reduct, so a law that stops firing fails rather than passing vacuously, and then holds original and reduct to one value at every closed instantiation; `curios`'s `tests::laws` states every law the normalizer takes, over every intrinsic carrier, as an `Eq/refl()` proof both checkers see, and every law it declines, read back through the compiler's own refl-fit report so that taking one moves a row rather than adding a test. The bit-grain copies of the byte-grain arms that used to stay **argued** are gone: one arm serves both grains, so a fold read at one grain is read at the other by the same code, and `tests::laws`'s `Bits` carrier states the free monoid's rows its `Bytes` carrier states rather than a subset of them. Folds live in `curios-core`, so a wrong one admits by [the perimeter's shared route](../../design/language/the-soundness-perimeter.md), in the sharpest way: conversion reduces both sides before comparing. Verdicts on stuck pairs are [Intrinsic fold laws and the free-monoid peel](intrinsic-fold-laws-and-the-free-monoid-peel.md)'s; the division family's laws rest on [The bounds oracle and the division family](the-bounds-oracle-and-the-division-family.md).

**The free monoid's laws, held over values.** The grid covers the full-window collapse, the empty window over a symbolic base and bound, both cons peels of `get` and `slice`, the borrow, the literal-factor distribution on either side, and the `len`/`map` homomorphisms over an append and a concatenation. One mutation was run and caught: misstating the append measure failed the length law on both obligations at once. A window on a *symbolic* concatenation whose start is a prefix's length and whose count is the following run's length is that run — `slice([..xs, ..ys], len(xs), len(ys)) = ys` — found by `seam_window`, which reads the value as a flat list of operands and then measures each the way `len` does, cancelling that measure off the distance still to cover, held in the sum normal form. **The reading flattens, and an append is one of the things it flattens.** `append(b, k) = b ++ append(x[], k)` is the peel's own law and conversion's spine decomposes both spellings to one atom list, so `x[..p, k]` and `x[..p, ..x[k]]` are one value; a locator gated on the concatenation *node* declined a window it had already decided for the other spelling, which was incompleteness with nothing on the refusing side to justify it. Nested concatenations flatten for the same reason. What this does not do is canonicalize the value: an append stays an append, because it is the accumulator's own node and the cheaper one, and the flat list is a view the locator computes rather than a form reduction settles into. A measure carrying a summand the distance lacks is an overshoot and declines, exactly rather than conservatively: a prefix only grows, so no later operand can bring a passed seam back. What cancellation sees through that whole-term equality did not is a universe instance, `Nat::cancel_common` keying its summands through `project_erased_universes` — the admitting direction, on the licence [The refinement key](../what-the-kernel-consults/the-refinement-key.md) records for reduction's other readers of that projection. Two spellings of one length that cancellation does not pair still decline, the refusing direction. **The same walk, asked for a window of one, locates an *index*.** `get([..p, k], len(p)) = k` and `get([..bs, k, ..cs], len(bs)) = k` hold because the operand beginning at that seam carries exactly one generator, which is read out of it directly — not through an inner `get`, which would need a bound on that operand the caller's own does not state and which a reducer may not invent. That closes the asymmetry the grid recorded: a window at a symbolic seam was found and an index at the same seam was not, though the locator walking to each was the same one. It holds for every value of the operands, since the bounds are exactly the lengths whatever they are; it sits beside the literal-run locators rather than inside them, which still read only literal runs. Every carrier the walk serves takes it — `Bin` at either grain and `List` — each reading its value as the same flat list of operands, nested concatenations and appends alike, so a carrier cannot locate a window at a seam without locating an index at the same seam. **A window past every operand but the last lies inside the last one.** `get([..xs, ..ys], len(xs) + i) = get(ys, i)` and `slice([..xs, ..ys], len(xs), n) = slice(ys, 0, n)` are the same walk stopping *inside* an operand rather than at its seam, and the bound is what licenses exactly that operand and no other: the caller holds `start + count <= len(whole)`, `len(whole)` is the sum of the operands' measures, and cancelling the consumed prefix off both sides leaves `rest + count <= len(ys)`, the proposition the narrowed operation states — so the caller's own proof term is handed on, as window fusion hands its proof on, and nothing is derived. Stopping inside an earlier operand would leave the later measures standing on the right, which no term in hand proves, so that case declines. The grid holds both laws at `Bin` and `List` under the narrowed bound, and `tests::laws` states them at every carrier. `len(map(xs, f)) = len(xs)` passes the measure through a map, a map being elementwise whatever `f` is. `map(xs, f) = xs` is taken when `f` is the identity as conversion reads it: one binder, whose body weak-head reduces to that binder, so `(v) => v + 0` and `(v) => ((w) => w)(v)` collapse as `(v) => v` does. That is beta and the folds under the binder, not function extensionality, and it is what both checkers already said of the function alone — `(v) => v + 0` converts with `(v) => v` — so a `map` by one staying stuck was a test of the spelling where the value was meant. `is_identity` asks the lambda as written first, which costs nothing; past it the binder is opened on a fresh identity from `Reducer::fresh_binder`, charged as the closed machine's eta probe charges the same opening, and the body reduced to weak-head form and not forced, so a recursive head is not unfolded at every `map` to learn it is no binder. It is the one fold that looks under a binder, and the identity it opens on is assumed at no type, since what is asked is a reduct and never a judgment. A function that is the identity only pointwise has a stuck match for a body and stays refused, which is where extensionality begins. What holds it: `free_monoid_tests`'s `a_map_by_a_function_whose_body_reduces_to_its_binder_is_the_list`, with a successor of the binder and a variable that is *not* the binder as controls; the value grid pins `map(xs, (v) => v + 0)` to `xs`; and `tests::laws` states the rows at `List` with the pointwise function and map fusion refused beside them. Mutation-checked: accepting any body that reduces to a variable collapses a `map` by the constant function `(v) => w`, which the unit test fails on its last control. **`fold` is an intrinsic beside `map`, for the same reason `map` is one.** The library's left fold was an index loop whose recursion no lemma could name, so nothing written over it — `reverse`, `flatten`, `filter` — had an equation. `List/fold` now reduces over the shape: `fold([h, ..t], z, f) = fold(t, f(h, z), f)`, `fold([..a, ..b], z, f) = fold(b, fold(a, z, f), f)`, and `fold([..xs, a], z, f) = f(a, fold(xs, z, f))`, each an `Eql` in the admitting direction and each sound because a left fold is defined by exactly those equations over the free monoid's generators; a window or an opaque value stays neutral. Its erasure is the loop the library spelled by hand, a bounded `Nat` fold over the length with one read per step, so nothing below erasure changed. The grid holds the cons and the concatenation laws structurally, as it holds `map`'s, and `tests::laws` states all three with the empty and singleton runs at `List`.

**The measure is computed off the spine, and it arrived with probes.** `Bin/len` folds the operands' own lengths off the spine rather than rebuilding a `Bin/len` per operand, and `Bin/get` and `Bin/slice` locate their position the same way instead of peeling one generator at a time. No equation is *new* — a length was already a homomorphism into `(ℕ, +, 0)` and a window was already reachable by peeling — but a second route to the same answer is a second chance to get it wrong, and a length that disagreed with its run would be a false definitional equation that congruence carries to `False`. What holds it: `curios-core`'s `reduce::intrinsic` tests state agreement over four groupings of one run per carrier, including the left-nested shape an accumulation builds, against the fused literal's own generator count as ground truth, for length, window and index alike; `an_unmeasurable_operand_is_not_skipped` pins the declining side, which is the sharp failure — silently ignoring an operand it cannot read would under-report a length; and `curios`'s `tests::aggregates::a_length_and_a_window_do_not_depend_on_grouping` states the same laws as `refl` proofs where both checkers see them. The *measure* is still deliberately narrower than what conversion decides, and it is the measure rather than the locator that is: `bin_measure` reads literal runs and their concatenations and declines everything else. A window's length is taken one level up instead, where the homomorphism reads it: `len(slice(b, s, n)) = n` for every carrier, since `/sys/slice` states `s + n <= len(b)` as its precondition and so the count *is* the measure at every well-typed instance, and `map(f, slice(xs, s, n)) = slice(map(f, xs), s, n)`, the window moving inside a map with its own proof unchanged because `len(map(f, xs))` is already `len(xs)`. Both were stuck even at literal bounds while the shape classifiers filed a slice as opaque; `Shape::Window` is what the `len` and `map` folds now read, the value grid holds the three laws under the precondition, and `curios`'s `tests::laws` states them at every carrier. What a window does not give a reader is a bound on its base for a position inside it, so `get` and `slice` *through* a window are declined as reductions: the outer bound is a proposition no term in hand proves, and a reducer may not invent one — the same line window fusion holds, handing a proof on rather than deriving it. They are decided where two are *compared*, which builds nothing and so owes no bound; that verdict is [Intrinsic fold laws and the free-monoid peel](intrinsic-fold-laws-and-the-free-monoid-peel.md)'s, and `tests::laws` holds the rows at every carrier. The spine's `against_identity` still holds a window's emptiness undecidable, which is the peel's question rather than the fold's.

**Window fusion rests on a typing fact and a representation fact.** `tests::aggregates::bin_slice_is_a_monoid_citizen` states window fusion, which the spine applies on a matching seam with no ordering check at all. It once rested on a *reduction* fact — an ill-bounded slice was an error over a literal base and a trap at runtime, so neither side of the equation ever held a value and the disagreement could not be observed. `/sys`'s `slice` takes `s + l <= len(b)` as its precondition, so a call that would break the law cannot be written; and the ordering condition it also used to take is gone because a count cannot spell a reversed window. The law's statement in that test carries the one surviving side condition explicitly, hands the fused window the second window's own proof, and still checks by `refl`. The distinction is worth keeping because the weaker version was nearly fatal: clamping `slice` — giving out-of-range spellings values instead of errors — was specified and would have made `split`, which that test proves *universally quantified over its bounds*, a proof of a false equation between two concretely different values. Reduction errors were the only thing standing in the way, and they are a poor thing to stand behind; the precondition is what makes the fusion rule unconditionally safe rather than accidentally so.

**Multiplication's unit, annihilation and nested literal factors.** `x · 1 = x`, `1 · x = x`, `x · 0 = 0`, `0 · x = 0` and `2 · (3 · x) = 6 · x` are each an `Eql` in the admitting direction, so each is a definitional equation conversion carries. What holds them: every literal meeting a symbolic factor goes through one function, `Nat::scaled`, whose three cases are the laws themselves, and the value grid holds each stated reduct to ground truth at closed instantiations, mutation-checked the way the rest of that grid is. A consequence to name: the scaled tail puts its coefficient on the left, so `x · 2` and `2 · x` reach one normal form and are definitionally equal — commutation of a *literal* coefficient, not of two symbols, which stays neutral.

**`Bool` and the bitwise lattice on ℕ have their units, absorbers, idempotence and self-cancellation.** Each carrier's laws are one function beside the fold, run by `then_laws` on what the two-literal fold left neutral: a literal unit on one side yields the other operand, a literal absorber yields itself, and two structurally identical reduced operands yield what idempotence or self-cancellation says. The identity test is plain structural equality without `project_erased_universes`, which is the strict reading — two identical reduced terms denote one value — and so does not rest on the premise [The refinement key](../what-the-kernel-consults/the-refinement-key.md) records as false. `xor` additionally cancels a shared operand through one nesting, which is what takes `not(not(b))` to `b`. Two complementary operands — a term beside its `xor(_, true)`, or a comparison beside its dual, read off the same table `align_comparisons` reads, so `Flt` is left out — decide a conjunction `false`, a disjunction `true`, an equality `false` and an inequality `true`. This is the complement law of the Boolean algebra on the value type `Bool`, which holds by cases on the two constructors each side computes to, and not excluded middle on propositions, which the calculus does not have and this law does not touch; it is held at every closed instantiation by the open-fold grid's `p && xor(p, true)`, `x < y && y <= x` and `x == y || x != y` shapes. A connective's fold leaves its right operand as written under a stuck left, so there the law meets `Bool/not(b)` unfolded only where a leaf was forced: both converters flatten a tree against a `Bool` literal exactly as they flatten two trees. The equality folds, and the `xor` that `!=` on `Bool` lowers through, read both operands, as the `Nat` folds do, since their laws read the right; `curios-cert`'s `an_equality_reads_its_right_operand` states that beside the connective's laziness. `tests::laws` states the rows at `Bool`, `Nat` and `Int`. **A left shift by a literal count is a coefficient.** `shl(x, k) = 2ᵏ · x` holds on the unbounded ℕ and ℤ the type level folds, and the run time cannot falsify it, since [numeric carriers narrow by refusing, never by changing a value](../../design/toolchain/numeric-carriers-narrow-by-refusing-never-by-changing-a-value.md) and the emitter refuses a shift that leaves the carrier. The two spellings do not *refuse* alike, which that decision allows: at a run-time `x = 0` and a count past the envelope the shift answers `0` where the product refuses on materializing `2ᵏ`, so what the law can move — where a term solved by unification reaches the program in its reduced spelling — is a refusal and never a value. The laws beside the fold stop at a zero shift and a zero value, which build nothing and so need no reducer; the coefficient is built by `then_coefficient`, which has one, charges `2ᵏ` under the closed shift's own bound before it exists — a left shift being the one fold whose result size its operands do not bound — and hands the product back to the reducer, so distribution and the floor law are `NatMul`'s and `IntMul`'s and are stated once. A symbolic count declines: `2ˣ` is no literal. The value grid pins `shl(x, 3)`, `shl(x + 1, 2)` and the `Int` twin to their products, the last at an instantiation below zero, mutation-checked — a coefficient of `2 · k` fails `shl(x, 3) = 8 · x` on its stated reduct before any instantiation is reached; `cost_tests`'s `a_shift_coefficient_is_refused_before_it_is_built` holds the charge; and `tests::laws` states the rows at `Nat` and `Int`, with the symbolic count and the right shift as the refused rows beside them.

**`Int` has the sum normal form over ℤ, which is where its ring laws come from.** `curios-core`'s `int` module is `Nat`'s form with integer coefficients: a constant over a linear combination of monomials, spelled in the `IntAdd`, `IntMul` and `Int` nodes the roster already has, so nothing below reduction meets a new shape. What the group buys over the monoid is that a subtraction is a negative coefficient and a negation the coefficient `-1`, so no `IntSub` survives reduction and every difference cancels in full: `i - i` is `0`, `(i + 1) - 1` is `i`, `(i + j) - j` is `i`, each an `Eql` in the admitting direction. A product distributes past a constant or a single monomial in the fold and stays stuck between two symbolic sums, the same line `NatMul` draws under [A sum is merged when it is forced, not when it is built](../../design/toolchain/a-sum-is-merged-when-it-is-forced-not-when-it-is-built.md), and `int_normalize` crosses it where the converters ask, exactly as `Nat::normalize` does. Summands keep first-appearance order for the reason `Nat::cancel_common` records, so `i + j` and `j + i` are two spellings decided by the peel: `int_cancel_common` moves the difference of two sides to the two sides by sign — sound because every relation on a group reads through `a - b ⋈ 0` — and a pair whose residuals are two constants decides, which is how `compare_int` answers `i < i + 1` and `i + a < i + b` reduces to `a < b`. What holds it: the open-fold grid carries the cancellation, the trailing constant and the single-monomial distribution at instantiations including a negative; `every_int_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` holds the peel's verdicts the way the `Nat` grid holds its own; `int`'s own tests pin the form and the stability of an untouched pair; and `curios`'s `tests::laws` states commutation, association, cancellation, distribution and the decided comparisons as `refl` proofs both checkers see. `Bool`'s commutativity and associativity are not folds at all: they are decided by the peel as a set of leaves, recorded in [Intrinsic fold laws and the free-monoid peel](intrinsic-fold-laws-and-the-free-monoid-peel.md), and a stuck `==` or `!=` on every carrier is decided against its swapped spelling by the peel, recorded there beside it. Every law is in the value grid, and `curios`'s `tests::laws` records the taken and the declined halves side by side.

**A comparison reads divisibility, and only for equality.** After normalizing and cancelling, each side of a `Nat` or `Int` comparison is a constant over literal coefficients of monomials. Every monomial is an integer, so each side is its constant modulo the gcd `g` of every coefficient on both sides, whatever the symbols take — and two constants apart modulo `g` therefore meet at no value: `2 · x + 1 == 2 · y` is `false` and its `!=` is `true`. That is the first step of the omega test and nothing past it. `Comparison` is the set of orderings the operands may still take, and the verdict is the one non-empty subset it could not spell, `Ne`: `==` and `!=` read it, `<` and `<=` do not, and it meets a verdict already reached by intersection, a forced `<=` that is also unequal being `<`. A summand that is no literal multiple has coefficient `1`, so the test goes quiet exactly where it has nothing to say, and it is asked last, only where equality is still open. It is a fold verdict and not a peel one: `classify_nat` and `peel_int_pair` are untouched, so conversion learns nothing it needed and inversion is handed no new impossibility. What holds it: `compare_tests`'s `nat_decides_floors_apart_modulo_the_coefficients_gcd_unequal` and its `int_` twin, the latter under a negative coefficient and a negative constant, each with the near-misses beside it — constants that agree modulo `g`, and coefficients whose gcd is `1`; the value grid pins `2 · x + 1 == 2 · y`, `4 · x + 6 != 2 · y + 1` and the `Int` twin to their literals and holds them at closed instantiations on both sides of each other; and `tests::laws` states the rows at `Nat` and `Int` with the two near-misses as controls on the refused side. Mutation-checked: comparing the constants without the modulus decides `2 · x` against `2 · y + 2` unequal, which both unit tests fail on their congruent control — the pair meets at `x = y + 1`.

**Subtraction's zero minuend and reassociation.** `0 - x = 0` is what truncation means; `(a - b) - c = a - (b + c)` holds for truncated subtraction as for the integers, and reduction takes the right-nested form so a later literal subtrahend meets `a`'s floor — the reassociated sum is reduced first so `cancel_common` sees its summands, and the result re-enters the arm. Both are in the value grid and in `curios`'s `tests::laws`. The division family's three unconditional laws are [The bounds oracle and the division family](the-bounds-oracle-and-the-division-family.md)'s.

**A sum is a linear combination, which is a change to the normal form rather than a law beside it — and a product of two symbolic sums reaches it on demand.** `NatMul` distributes when either operand is a literal or a single symbolic summand and leaves `(a + b) · (c + d)` stuck; `Nat::normalize` distributes such a product where a value is asked for by name — `compare_nat`, the converters' rule — and re-merges. Decided in [A sum is merged when it is forced, not when it is built](../../design/toolchain/a-sum-is-merged-when-it-is-forced-not-when-it-is-built.md). Everything below about the form holds of what the fold and `normalize` together produce. `Nat::linear` reads a symbolic inner's summands as `coefficient · factor` pairs and merges like factors by adding coefficients, keyed up to universe instances exactly as `cancel_common` keys; `Nat::from_linear` writes them back, each through `Nat::scaled`, folded left-to-right. `NatAdd` reduces through it, so `x + x` is `2 · x` and `2 · x + 3 · x` is `5 · x`; `Nat::scaled` distributes a literal over a sum, so `(x + y) · 2` is `2 · x + 2 · y`; and `cancel_common` cancels by coefficient, `2 · x + a` against `x + b` leaving `x + a` against `b`, which is the multiset rule the peel entry records with the multiplicity read off the coefficient rather than counted. Two things hold it up. Soundness: merging is the equation `c · f + d · f = (c + d) · f`, true for every value of `f`, and distribution is the one the literal-factor law already took, applied summand by summand — every shape is in the value grid. Termination: `Nat::summands` reads a sum in the order `from_linear` writes it, so read-then-rebuild is the identity on a sum already in normal form — the oscillation the peel entry records, where a rebuilt sum came back re-associated and the comparison never settled, cannot recur, and `cancel_common`'s untouched early return stays as the second guard. The prelude elaborates and certifies under the form at the same cost as before, measured on `elaborate_and_zonk_module`, so the projection each summand passes through to be keyed does not register on real programs. Two symbols under `·` have a normal form as well: a monomial, read by `Nat::monomial` as a literal coefficient and a flattened factor list, and written by `Nat::spine` with the factors in the order of their structural hash — deterministic, since the hasher is fixed-keyed; stable, so two distinct factors hashing alike keep their written order and merely fail to canonicalize against each other, which is incompleteness and never an equation. `Nat::multiply` then distributes in full, every summand of one operand against every summand of the other with each floor a constant summand, and sums the monomials through the linear combination, so `x · y = y · x`, `(x · y) · z = x · (y · z)` and `x · (y + z) = x · y + x · z` are one rule with the literal-factor law — each still stated on its own in the value grid.

**Two laws carried gates before any of this and remain the model.** The natural/integer conversion round trip, and natural comparison agreeing with the host ordering. Neither reaches conversion, so they attest to the discipline rather than to the equations that decide typing.
