# Eta and untyped child positions

**Assumes.** Eta at Π and Σ preserves equality, and comparing an untyped child at `Type` forfeits rules rather than adding them

**Status.** **argued** and **probed**, and the argument is about the direction rather than about the rule. Conversion is type-directed, so eta is what converts a function with its expansion and a pair with its projections without either side being in that shape; `kernel::whnf::tests` covers both spellings, with `eta_contracts_a_function_that_only_forwards` and `eta_declines_when_the_head_mentions_the_binder` on the contracting side. The second clause is the one worth having a row for, because it reads as a weakness and is a guard: an application spine's arguments, a stuck elimination's motive and arms under their opaque binders, and a struct type-former's parameters are compared at `Type` rather than at the types their head assigns, which *forfeits* eta and irrelevance in those positions. A forfeited rule cannot admit — it is why the kernel's own irrelevance never fires — so the clause moves only toward refusal, and a refusal is a disagreement, which is the signal the second checker exists to produce.

**Probed from the surface at both rules in both directions, and the composition this entry once called unattacked does not exist where it looked for it.** Eta at Π and at Σ each accept their expansion and each refuse a near miss — an expansion that drops its binder, and one that swaps its components — so a comparison answering *true* at every Π and every Σ would satisfy the accepting rung and fail those two. The composition is unreachable **at Π**, and structurally rather than by accident: conversion tries irrelevance *before* it dispatches on the goal type's shape, and Π into a proposition is a proposition whatever it quantifies over, so the goal is discharged whole at the top and eta never opens a binder — `a_function_into_a_proposition_is_discharged_before_eta` is that accepting rung, with a relevant codomain refused beside it so the discharge cannot read as conversion giving up on function types. Where it *is* reachable is **Σ**, because a record stays relevant as soon as one component is: irrelevance does not preempt eta, and eta is what hands the proof component to a `Prop`-sorted goal. `eta_hands_a_records_proof_component_to_irrelevance` runs both rules in one equation, with `eta_still_compares_a_records_relevant_component` holding the other component to a comparison, so the acceptance is not eta declining to look.

**The second clause holds in its direction and was wrong in its statement.** The direction is executable now rather than argued: two proofs of one proposition standing as arguments of an opaque head are compared at `Type`, irrelevance is never asked, and the kernel refuses a program the elaborator accepts. `a_grounded_argument_forfeits_irrelevance` is the two-checker matrix's first instance in that quadrant — *the kernel refusing what the elaborator accepts*, recorded conversion incompleteness, which the matrix's own documentation describes and had no instance of. The statement is another matter: what grounding forfeits is *type-directed* eta and *goal-level* irrelevance, the two rules conversion reaches by asking what the goal type is, and a nominal struct's eta is neither. It reads the literal's own declaration, so it fires from the structural comparison, which is exactly where a grounded pair lands, and it skips a `Prop`-sorted field by classifying the declaration's field type — so both rules the clause says are gone are live in an untyped position. `a_nominal_structs_eta_is_not_forfeited_there` is the sharpest spelling: every field of the struct is a proposition, the field walk therefore compares *nothing at all*, and the literal is equated with the neutral on the strength of the neutral restriction alone.

**Not unsound, and not for the reason the code gives.** That eta's own comment credits the neutral restriction with stopping the literal being equated with an arbitrary term that merely appeared in the same untyped position — and a variable is such a term: the restriction excludes only non-neutrals, not neutrals of the wrong type. What actually establishes that the neutral inhabits the struct is that every grounded pair is the corresponding children of two parents already shown convertible, so their types agree by typing — argument comparison runs only after the two heads were accepted, and every other grounded position pairs children of one shape. That is a property of the *callers* rather than of the rule, and it is written in neither place. Null result on soundness; the routes are recorded so they are not re-attacked

**What makes grounding cheap is an inventory, and this file has already learned once what an inventory is worth.** Opening two scopes at shared opaque binders assumes each of them at `Type` regardless of what it really is, and `ground_scope` says why that is sound: a binder's recorded type feeds only the conversion history's context key, identically on both sides. That is a claim about what *no other rule does*, and it is the same shape as the registry plicity vector's — where "the kernel ignores plicities" was an inventory until `recheck::tests::a_registry_plicity_vector_is_read_by_no_kernel_rule` held it shut, and `curios-analysis`'s `tests/driven.rs::a_carried_polarity_vector_is_recomputed_rather_than_believed` did the same for the other vector on that entry. Both were converted because a moving codebase invalidates an inventory in silence, and a rule taught to read a binder's assumed type would invalidate this one the same way. There is no third such fixture; the claim stands as prose. **Missing test:** a grounded comparison whose opaque binders are assumed at `Type` while their real types differ, asserting that no verdict moves
