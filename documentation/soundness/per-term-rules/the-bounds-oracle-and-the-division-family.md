# The bounds oracle and the division family

**Assumes.** Every bound `nat_bound` reports holds of every value its term can take

**Status.** **probed** by four gates, three of them in `curios-core`'s `reduce::intrinsic::tests` and labelled as such, and one in `curios`. This is the one fold family that both reaches conversion *and* carries executable backing, and `nat_bound` is trusted surface of its own rather than a restatement of the folds; the other open folds are [Open fold laws and the sum normal form](open-fold-laws-and-the-sum-normal-form.md)'s.

**The division family sees through an open dividend by two laws, each unconditional so that no value of a symbolic part can falsify it.** `Nat/div` and `Nat/rem` take the floor law `(i + f) / n = f/n + (i + f%n) / n` and its remainder twin, which hold because the literal floor contributes exactly `f/n` whole divisors whatever `i` is; and a split that divides a scaled symbol out whole when every remaining summand is bounded and those bounds together stay below `n`, so none of them can carry. Nothing conditional is admissible here and the guard is written where the rule is: `(a + b)/n = a/n + b/n` is false at `a = b = 1`, `n = 2`, and one such equation is all congruence needs. Three further laws are unconditional in the same sense: a zero dividend is `0` by any divisor, with remainder `0`; a divisor of `1` returns the dividend, with remainder `0`; and a dividend identical to its divisor is `1`, with remainder `0`, on the operation's own precondition that the divisor is nonzero, which its proof operand states for every value — the one law here that rests on a proof, and it rests on the same proof the operation already demanded to be written. All three are in the value grid and in `curios`'s `tests::laws`.

**The split rests on `nat_bound`, and what it computes a bound *for* is now a criterion rather than a history.** An arm exists where the result is bounded in every operand the value is not *antitone* in — strictly narrower than monotonicity, since a product is monotone in each factor separately and still needs both bounded, and wide enough to license the one-sided arms, since a value that only shrinks as an operand grows needs no bound on that operand at all. That is what admits a subtrahend, a divisor and a shift amount being read past. `NatShl` is refused under it for a reason outside arithmetic: a left shift is the one fold whose result size is not bounded by its operands', and `nat_bound` takes no reducer and so cannot charge the budget that exists to price exactly that. The `NatShl` that reaches the oracle is a shift by a *symbolic* count: a literal count reduces to the product `2ᵏ · x` where there is a reducer to charge, recorded in [Open fold laws and the sum normal form](open-fold-laws-and-the-sum-normal-form.md), so it arrives as a `NatMul` and is bounded by that arm. The remaining arms are unconditional for reasons outside arithmetic too: a `Byte` is `0..=255` **by its carrier**, a fact about the type rather than about how the value was produced — every producer establishes it and the oracle analyses none of them, because the operand under a stuck `ByteToNat` is normally a bare binder or a projection — and `Byte` is not a wire type, so [the foreign wire contract](foreign-wire-contract.md) keeps an embedder from supplying one outside the range; `x % n < n` holds by definition, a zero divisor having already been reported. A single *under*-report is a false definitional equation and an over-report is merely conservative, because the split fires when the bounds it sums stay below the divisor: a bound smaller than the truth admits a summand that can still carry, while one larger than the truth only withholds the rule. So the gate tests the direction that matters: `nat_bound`'s own doc comment says *upper* bound, and the gate asserts `value <= bound`.

**The comparison reads a bound against a literal on either side, strictly and exactly.** A bound below the literal is the strict verdict, `Lt` or `Gt`; a bound meeting it is the non-strict one, `Le` or `Ge`, which `<=` and `>=` read and `<` and `>` do not — so `Byte/to_nat(b) <= 255` decides as `Byte/to_nat(b) < 256` does, and `x % 7 > 6` is `false`. Both verdicts are the bound's own claim restated, so neither adds to what the oracle assumes. `compare_tests`'s `nat_decides_a_bound_met_exactly_as_non_strict` states the three readings, and `tests::laws` the rows at `Nat` and `Byte`.

**A bound is read as an operand as well as a literal.** `nat_dominators` lists, per shape, the operand a result never exceeds — the minuend, the dividend, either operand of `and`, the shifted value — and the divisor a remainder stays strictly below, on the antitone criterion the literal oracle states; the comparison compares that operand in the value's place and takes an at-most there as an at-most here, strict where either step is. It is transitivity through one operand, one strict subterm down, which is why it terminates, and what it assumes is what the literal arms assume: that every listed pair holds of every value. `dominators_upper_bound_every_closed_instantiation` holds each pair over values, block per shape as the literal gate does, `compare_tests`'s `nat_decides_a_value_against_an_operand_it_never_exceeds` states the verdicts with the may-be-equal control beside them, and `tests::laws` states the rows at `Nat` — `x - y <= x`, `x % (y + 1) < y + 1` among them.

**Four gates carry it.** `bound_upper_bounds_every_closed_instantiation` checks the oracle never under-reports across closed instantiations of each bounded shape — by hand, one block per shape, because it enumerates no variants: an arm added without a block leaves the gate passing while checking nothing, which is the one way this entry's evidence can go quietly empty. `euclid_split_is_a_euclidean_division` checks the specification itself rather than the implementation: for each split, the remainder is below the divisor and `n·quotient + remainder` agrees with the dividend at every sampled instantiation, substituted through `Scope` so the comparison is on values and not on shapes. `an_uncertain_summand_leaves_the_division_neutral` checks the refusals — an indivisible coefficient and an unbounded summand must both stay neutral, which is where an unsound rule would announce itself. And `curios`'s `a_literal_divisor_sees_through_a_symbolic_dividend` states all four resulting equations as `Eq/refl()` proofs, so the compiler checks them for every value rather than for samples.
