# The bounds oracle and the division family

**Assumes.** Every bound `nat_bound` reports holds of every value its term can take

**Status.** **probed** by four gates, three of them in `curios-core`'s `reduce::intrinsic::tests` and labelled as such, and one in `curios`. This is the one fold family that both reaches conversion *and* carries executable backing, and `nat_bound` is trusted surface of its own rather than a restatement of the folds; the other open folds are [Open fold laws and the sum normal form](open-fold-laws-and-the-sum-normal-form.md)'s.

**The division family sees through an open dividend by two laws, each unconditional so that no value of a symbolic part can falsify it.** `Nat/div` and `Nat/rem` take the floor law `(i + f) / n = f/n + (i + f%n) / n` and its remainder twin, which hold because the literal floor contributes exactly `f/n` whole divisors whatever `i` is; and a split that divides a scaled symbol out whole when every remaining summand is bounded and those bounds together stay below `n`, so none of them can carry. Nothing conditional is admissible here and the guard is written where the rule is: `(a + b)/n = a/n + b/n` is false at `a = b = 1`, `n = 2`, and one such equation is all congruence needs. Three further laws are unconditional in the same sense: a zero dividend is `0` by any divisor, with remainder `0`; a divisor of `1` returns the dividend, with remainder `0`; and a dividend identical to its divisor is `1`, with remainder `0`, on the operation's own precondition that the divisor is nonzero, which its proof operand states for every value — the one law here that rests on a proof, and it rests on the same proof the operation already demanded to be written. All three are in the value grid and in `curios`'s `tests::laws`.

**The split rests on `nat_bound`.** Its arms are unconditional for reasons outside arithmetic: a `Byte` is `0..=255` because `Nat/to_byte` wraps and `Byte` is not a wire type, so [the foreign wire contract](foreign-wire-contract.md) keeps an embedder from supplying one outside the range; `x % n < n` holds by definition, a zero divisor having already been reported; and the rest are monotone in operands the oracle has itself bounded. A single *under*-report is a false definitional equation and an over-report is merely conservative, because the split fires when the bounds it sums stay below the divisor: a bound smaller than the truth admits a summand that can still carry, while one larger than the truth only withholds the rule. So the gate tests the direction that matters: `nat_bound`'s own doc comment says *upper* bound, and the gate asserts `value <= bound`.

**Four gates carry it.** `bound_upper_bounds_every_closed_instantiation` checks the oracle never under-reports across closed instantiations of each bounded shape. `euclid_split_is_a_euclidean_division` checks the specification itself rather than the implementation: for each split, the remainder is below the divisor and `n·quotient + remainder` agrees with the dividend at every sampled instantiation, substituted through `Scope` so the comparison is on values and not on shapes. `an_uncertain_summand_leaves_the_division_neutral` checks the refusals — an indivisible coefficient and an unbounded summand must both stay neutral, which is where an unsound rule would announce itself. And `curios`'s `a_literal_divisor_sees_through_a_symbolic_dividend` states all four resulting equations as `Eq/refl()` proofs, so the compiler checks them for every value rather than for samples.
