# Nominal occurrence well-formedness

**Assumes.** An occurrence's parameters and indices inhabit the arity its declaration states

**Status.** **probed**, and it admitted a closed inhabitant of `False`. Three things an occurrence must have checked before its declaration is read had moved behind one handle — the universe instance, the parameter count, the index count. A fourth was never written: that each argument *has* the type the arity gives it. Counts are the boundary's job because no typing rule reads a length; a shape is typing's, and this shape had no rule anywhere. So an equation family applied to arguments of the wrong type was admitted as a *type*, the classifier consulting the declaration for its result sort with two counts checked and nothing else. It was then *inhabited*, and by the rule working correctly: the indices are compared at the declared domain, that domain is `Prop`-sorted, and proof irrelevance discharges both without looking. From there every step is ordinary — eliminating the forged equation under a motive that carries the same mis-typed arguments into a second family yields an arrow between an inhabited and an uninhabited instance, and the vacuous elimination coverage licenses proves `False`. Not reachable from a surface program, since the elaborator elaborates a nominal occurrence as an application against the arity's telescope and checks every argument, which is why this was constructed by hand and why the second opinion was worth nothing here. `recheck::tests::a_nominal_occurrence_types_its_arguments` is the regression, with `an_indexed_occurrence_at_a_well_typed_index_is_accepted` as the control against closing it by refusing every indexed occurrence
