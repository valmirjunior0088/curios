# Euclid's identity in the sum normal form

**Assumes.** `k · (m / k) + m % k` is `m` on ℕ for every literal `k` above one, and the collapse that reads it back out of a linear combination matches only genuine pairs

**Status.** **probed** by the value grid in `curios-core`'s `reduce::intrinsic::tests`, which holds three shapes of the law at every closed instantiation, and by `curios`'s `tests::laws` over the surface. The law is one function in `curios-core`, so a wrong collapse admits by [the perimeter's shared route](../../design/language/the-soundness-perimeter.md) — a false definitional equation conversion believes, carried to `False` by congruence with substitution.

**This is the destructive direction, and the constructive one was already here.** [The bounds oracle and the division family](the-bounds-oracle-and-the-division-family.md) recovers the digits of a sum the program *built*: `(k · x + b) / k` is `x` when `b` is bounded below `k`. It says nothing about a dividend nothing built, so `256 · (n / 256) + n % 256` stood as two independent atoms of the sum normal form and `n` was unreachable from them. That gap is what a base-`k` encoding's injectivity ran into: the proof reaches "the remainders agree and the quotients agree" and had no way to conclude that the numbers do.

**Why it lives in the normal form rather than in a fold.** `Nat`'s normal form is a linear combination — like factors merged by adding their coefficients — and `m / k` and `m % k` are two of its factors. The identity is therefore not a rule about an operation but a relation *between two entries of a combination*, and the only place that can see both is [`Nat::linear`], which builds it. A fold on `NatAdd` would see one summand at a time and never the pair.

**What the collapse matches, and what it deliberately does not.** A pair collapses when the combination carries `m / k` with a coefficient of at least `k` and `m % k` with a coefficient of at least one, at the same literal `k` above one and the same dividend `m`. It then collapses whole multiples only — `3k · (m/k) + 3 · (m%k)` is `3 · m`, and a leftover of either half stays as it was — so a partial match is left standing rather than approximated. Dividend identity is up to erased universes, exactly as `Nat::linear` keys a factor, and *not* up to the proof operand: two divisions of one dividend by one divisor are the same operation whatever inhabits their bound, so keying on the proof would make the law miss a pair that differs only in how its precondition was discharged. A divisor of one is excluded because `m / 1` and `m % 1` fold before they reach here; a divisor of zero because the operation traps.

**Termination is by a measure, which is the risk this rule actually carries.** The neighbouring open-fold rules record a rebuilt sum re-associating until the prelude overflowed the stack, so a rule that rewrites two atoms into one owes an argument that it cannot re-enter. Each collapse removes `k + 1` units of coefficient mass and adds one, with `k` above one, so the total strictly decreases; a combination is finite, so the pass ends. It is also idempotent, which is what keeps the reducer from oscillating between passes: a collapse drives one of the two halves to zero, where it is dropped, so the pair it matched cannot match again. The whole standard library elaborates and certifies under it, which is the evidence that neither the measure nor the idempotence argument has a hole a real corpus finds.

**What it unblocks.** `Key(Nat)` — a `/std/Map` witness proving `Bytes/of_nat` injective — which the retired standard-library specification named this law as the one compiler item it waited on. The proof itself is `/std`'s and is not written yet; what has changed is that it is now expressible.
