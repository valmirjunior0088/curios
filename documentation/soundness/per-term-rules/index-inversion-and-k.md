# Index inversion and K

**Assumes.** `Eq : Prop` licenses deleting a redundant constraint

**Status.** auditable only from the surface; the deletion rule's three answers and the clash rule's license are reached by construction in `curios-analysis`'s `tests/driven.rs`, and the clash rule's blind case was reached there answering wrong.

**The deletion rule's semantic half is inert, which is the condition under which a rule's mistakes stay invisible.** Inversion reconciles a binder forced in two index positions by asking whether the two forcings *convert* — a rule restored as a semantic test in place of an older syntactic non-linearity refusal, and an acceptance rather than a refusal, which is why it is the part of this entry worth attacking. Instrumented across the whole corpus, every refusal is on genuinely inconvertible spines and every acceptance has syntactically identical sides, so a plain equality test would have decided every acceptance that exists. Nothing in the corpus distinguishes the semantic rule from the syntactic one it replaced. What closes the gap between the rule as written and the rule as exercised is `curios-analysis`'s `tests/driven.rs::a_binder_forced_twice_survives_only_when_its_forcings_convert`, which forces one binder to two *different* inhabitants and varies only the family's sort — accepted and reconciled to one solution at a proposition, where irrelevance identifies them; dropped at a relevant family, leaving the binder unsolved and the arm still checked. It also pins the verdict neither may take: `Impossible` excuses an arm from being checked, and an arm excused wrongly at a `Prop`-sorted family is the vacuous-elimination route to `False` recorded in [Coverage](coverage.md).

**A third rung the pair did not reach, and nothing else did**: a binder whose *type is out of scope*, where there is nothing to compare at and the forcings are dropped. It is unreachable from the certifier by construction, both callers reaching inversion through a payload open that assumes every binder it opens. `curios-analysis`'s `tests/driven.rs::a_binder_whose_type_is_out_of_scope_drops_its_forcings` pins it anyway, because the conservative answer is not the obvious one to write: answering *nothing to disagree about* would discharge a constraint no convertibility test ever decided. Mutation-checked to fail that fixture while leaving the assumed-binder pair green

**The clash rule decided by blindness where every neighbour refuses, and the registry lookup was the way in.** Two constructors of one family definitely clash only when the family is *relevant* — at a `Prop`-sorted family irrelevance identifies every inhabitant, which is this entry's vacuous-elimination route — and the sort is read out of `induct_decl`. The guard was spelled `is_some_and`, so a lookup that answered nothing read as *not a proposition* and fell through to the tag test: the analysis answered `Impossible`, the verdict that excuses an arm from being checked, precisely when it could not see the one fact that licenses it. Demonstrated by construction while the branch was wrong — `tests/driven.rs::a_family_the_registry_cannot_answer_for_does_not_clash` handed inversion two constructors of a family absent from the registry and was answered `Impossible` — and unreachable from either checker as far as the walks are known, a well-typed variant's family being registered wherever the term came from; it is the rung above's shape at a second lookup, and it now refuses, with the fixture's control pair holding the relevant clash and the `Prop` excuse where they were
