# Index inversion and K

**Assumes.** `Eq : Prop` licenses deleting a redundant constraint, and two forcings of one binder that definitely clash make the case unreachable

**Status.** auditable only from the surface for the deletion rule's semantic half; its answers and the clash rule's license are reached by construction in `curios-analysis`'s `tests/driven.rs`, the clash between two forcings is **probed** from the surface in `curios/src/tests/perimeter/index_tests.rs`, and the clash rule's blind case was reached by construction answering wrong.

**The deletion rule's semantic half is inert, which is the condition under which a rule's mistakes stay invisible.** Inversion reconciles a binder forced in two index positions by asking whether the two forcings *convert* — a rule restored as a semantic test in place of an older syntactic non-linearity refusal, and an acceptance rather than a refusal, which is why it is the part of this entry worth attacking. Instrumented across the whole corpus, every refusal is on genuinely inconvertible spines and every acceptance has syntactically identical sides, so a plain equality test would have decided every acceptance that exists. Nothing in the corpus distinguishes the semantic rule from the syntactic one it replaced. What closes the gap between the rule as written and the rule as exercised is `curios-analysis`'s `tests/driven.rs::a_binder_forced_twice_survives_only_when_its_forcings_convert`, which forces one binder to two *different* inhabitants and varies only the family's sort — accepted and reconciled to one solution at a proposition, where irrelevance identifies them; impossible at a relevant family, where they are two constructors a program tells apart.

**Two forcings that clash make the case impossible, and the verdict that excuses an arm is the one that needs holding.** `refl(@z) : (z, z)` against `Eq(false, true)` clashes at no position: each solves `z`, to `false` and to `true`. Each forcing was reached by injective steps, so the case is reachable only if the two are one value, and the rule asks the first-order walk whether they definitely clash — the walk a linear position is put to, with nothing flexible, so the tag test is licensed by the family's sort and a literal clash is the peel's — after conversion has refused at a type that was in scope. `Impossible` excuses an arm from being checked, and an arm excused wrongly at a `Prop`-sorted family is the vacuous-elimination route to `False` recorded in [Coverage](coverage.md): `Eq/refl()` inhabits `Eq(Two/a(), Two/b())` by irrelevance, so a clash read there makes `match h end` total on an inhabited type. Two guards stand in front of that and each suffices alone, which was run at the unifier rather than argued. With the sort test removed from the variant arm the proposition's verdict does not move, conversion having reconciled the pair first, and the missing test is caught on the linear route by `a_family_the_registry_cannot_answer_for_does_not_clash`. With the clash asked before conversion it does not move either, the sort test answering `Ok` before a tag is read. With both gone `a_binder_forced_twice_survives_only_when_its_forcings_convert` fails at its proposition case, reporting a re-forced binder unreachable. The same fixture holds the slip the rule most invites — answering `Impossible` for every pair that does not convert — by its third case, one opaque function at two arguments, which may agree: that mutation fails it there. From the surface, `index_tests.rs::a_clash_between_two_forcings_of_one_binder_excuses_the_arm` states the refutations both checkers accept — a literal pair, two constructors, a closed and an open `Nat` pair, a parameterized family, and an equality the program declares itself — and beside it the claims that stay refused: `two_proofs_forced_on_one_binder_do_not_clash`, which is the exploit above; `an_open_forcing_does_not_clash`; `two_applications_of_one_opaque_function_do_not_clash`; and `a_parity_disagreement_is_not_a_clash`, since that `x * 2 + 1` is never `y * 2` is a fold's verdict and deliberately not the peel's `Clash` — [A law is decided where it neither respells nor invents](../../design/toolchain/a-law-is-decided-where-it-neither-respells-nor-invents.md) defers it as a row of its own, and this rule is not how it arrives.

**A further rung the constructor cases do not reach, and nothing else did**: a binder whose *type is out of scope*, where there is nothing to compare at and the forcings are dropped — and no clash is asked for either, the clash being asked only after conversion has refused, which here it was never asked to do. It is unreachable from the certifier by construction, both callers reaching inversion through a payload open that assumes every binder it opens. The elaborator's omitted-arm site did reach it, and always: it minted a case's payload binders as names and never assumed them, so every binder it re-forced was out of scope and the deletion rule decided nothing where an arm was absent — which is where the clash above is wanted. It now opens the case as a written arm does, through the one `assume_payload` both sites share. `curios-analysis`'s `tests/driven.rs::a_binder_whose_type_is_out_of_scope_drops_its_forcings` pins it anyway, at a proposition and at a relevant family, because the conservative answer is not the obvious one to write at either: answering *nothing to disagree about* would discharge a constraint no convertibility test ever decided, and answering the clash would decide the strong verdict out of blindness. Mutation-checked both ways — keeping the forcing fails the proposition case, and asking the clash whatever the scope fails the relevant one, each leaving the assumed-binder cases green

**The clash rule decided by blindness where every neighbour refuses, and the registry lookup was the way in.** Two constructors of one family definitely clash only when the family is *relevant* — at a `Prop`-sorted family irrelevance identifies every inhabitant, which is this entry's vacuous-elimination route — and the sort is read out of `induct_decl`. The guard was spelled `is_some_and`, so a lookup that answered nothing read as *not a proposition* and fell through to the tag test: the analysis answered `Impossible`, the verdict that excuses an arm from being checked, precisely when it could not see the one fact that licenses it. Demonstrated by construction while the branch was wrong — `tests/driven.rs::a_family_the_registry_cannot_answer_for_does_not_clash` handed inversion two constructors of a family absent from the registry and was answered `Impossible` — and unreachable from either checker as far as the walks are known, a well-typed variant's family being registered wherever the term came from; it is the rung above's shape at a second lookup, and it now refuses, with the fixture's control pair holding the relevant clash and the `Prop` excuse where they were
