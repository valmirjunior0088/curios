# Definitional proof irrelevance

**Assumes.** Any two inhabitants of a proposition are interchangeable

**Status.** **argued**, by the fixpoint on (V) below; not directly observable, but its side condition — the [large-elimination guard](large-elimination-guard.md) — is probed.

**It is load-bearing in the elaborator and was inert in the kernel, and that asymmetry was the entry's real content.** No conversion goal in the kernel arrived at a `Prop`-sorted type at all, so the rule was unreachable there rather than merely redundant, and no cheaper rule was shadowing it. The cause was the incompleteness conversion's own module documentation states: a proof reaching conversion did so in an untyped child position, compared at `Type`, where the type driving the comparison is not the proposition. That is no longer true of every position. A struct literal's fields and a constructor's payload are compared at the declaration's telescope in the kernel as they always were in the elaborator, so a proof standing there reaches the rule at its proposition, and the kernel's copy fires on every proof-carrying value built from a different proof of the same fact — `Str/concat` associating, or two `Vec`s counted by different proofs. What stays untyped, and so still forfeits the rule, is the argument of an opaque head, which [Eta and untyped child positions](eta-and-untyped-child-positions.md) records. The elaborator's copy fires, and not one firing has syntactically identical sides, so unlike inversion's reconciliation rule a plain equality test would have decided none of them. The work it does is narrow: every firing is at a *computed* proposition rather than a nominal `Prop` family — the validity-predicate shapes irrelevance exists for — and overwhelmingly at one site shape.

**The measurement that established this also exposed a coverage gap and closed it.** The tests that *name* irrelevance were the kernel's, covering the copy that never runs, and at a bare `Prop`-sorted declaration — a shape no real firing takes; the elaborator's copy had no direct test, its only exercise being the prelude happening to contain those predicates, which is coverage by accident. Both copies now carry `fires_at_a_computed_proposition`, which pins the mechanism the firings rest on: the classifier reads a stuck `match` by its **motive**, not its arms, so its control is the identical term with the motive at `Type` and relevant arms in both. The kernel's pair matters more than the elaborator's precisely because the rule is inert there: no program can bring the rule and the shape together, so a fixture is the only thing that ever will. Both mutation-checked — restricting the rule to nominal propositions fails the new witness and leaves the nominal test and both controls green, which is what makes the new one a separate rung rather than a restatement

**The rule is load-bearing and argued from the obligations, and that dependency is a fixpoint rather than a circle.** Irrelevance accepts without inspecting either term, which is correct precisely because every `Prop` inhabitant is total — that is (V)'s job — and elaboration uses the rule *while* the obligations are still unchecked. [Conversion recurrence](conversion-recurrence.md) stands the same way on aggressive (T): it accepts on the absence of finite disagreement, and (T) is what removes its exposure to bottom-typed terms. The dependency does not close. Conversion consults sort classification, which reaches only reduction and neutral synthesis, and neither reaches conversion. (V)'s own pipeline — record at settle, zonk, decide sort-hood, classify by size-change, close over reached definitions — never invokes conversion at any point, and neither does zonking. So elaboration may use both rules optimistically, and *acceptance validates them after the fact*: a program is accepted only if (V) passes, and if (V) passes then no `Prop`-typed term in it is partial, so every irrelevance step taken while checking it was applied to total terms. Rejected programs are rejected; accepted ones satisfy the precondition their acceptance relied on.

**What (V) establishes is the absence of *partiality*, and that is narrower than normalization — which the argument above does not need.** (V) refuses a proof that carries a non-descending recursion or an exit. A proof can have neither and still reduce forever: Abel and Coquand's `Omega(h)`, under a hypothesis `h` equating every two propositions, is a cast along `h(Top, Top)` feeding a self-application, and inside an arm of `match h(Top, Top)` the case equation makes that proof `refl`, the cast reduces, and `Omega(h)` unfolds without end. Nothing about it is partial in (V)'s sense, and nothing about it threatens consistency — it lives only under an absurd hypothesis, which no model inhabits. What it threatens is the *checker*: any rule that reduces such a proof spends the step budget, which refuses rather than admits. The elaborator used to be one, reducing both sides of every goal before asking whether the goal's type was a proposition, so two proofs irrelevance equates outright could exhaust it; it now asks first wherever neither side is flexible, the order the kernel always had, and `tests::perimeter::a_proof_is_not_reduced_to_compare_it_with_another` is the program. The premise the fixpoint rests on is therefore that no accepted `Prop` term is partial, which (V) gives, and not that every one normalizes, which is false and which the justification never used: a model of irrelevance interprets a proposition as a subsingleton and an absurd context as empty, and that is the model this entry still names as missing.

**The premise that argument needs is that (V) inspects every `Prop`-typed term in the accepted module.** Seeding at settle gives that for everything elaboration builds, since every node of its output settled with its type. The one route around it is a metavariable *solution*, which reaches the module through unification or witness resolution rather than through settling. That route is exercised and closed: a partial proof supplied as the witness of a `Prop`-sorted concept — arriving entirely by resolution, written nowhere as an argument — is rejected by (V), and `tests::soundness::a_partial_proof_cannot_arrive_through_witness_resolution` is the regression.

**What is still missing is the model, not the reasoning.** The fixpoint above is an argument about the implementation's dependency structure and about coverage; it is not a proof that definitional proof irrelevance is sound, which needs a model in which `Prop` is a genuine subsingleton and only total terms are interpreted. That is the first properly metatheoretic work this project would undertake, but it is not required to state, or to check, the fixpoint.
