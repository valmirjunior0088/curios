# Intrinsic fold laws and the free-monoid peel

**Assumes.** A `Peel::Equal` is a genuine equation and a `Peel::Clash` a genuine disequality on the carrier's values, at every reader

**Status.** **probed** at the `Nat` peel's verdicts — over the floorless shapes its gate admits as well as the floored ones — and at the `Bin`/`List` peel verdicts, each grid asserting its own verdict tally. The bit-grain copies of the byte-grain arms that used to stay **argued** are gone, by taking the shape the decomposition beneath them already had. They branched on the grain only to reach `PackedBin`'s two readers, and that is now one seam — `element_of_run` reading a generator out of a literal run, `Generator` reading one into it — so a single arm serves both grains and there is no second copy left to be wrong. `tests::laws`'s `Bits` carrier states the rows its `Bytes` carrier states, which is what makes the bit grain's evidence its own rather than the byte grain's read across. The peel is one function in `curios-core`, so a wrong `Eql` admits by [the perimeter's shared route](../../design/language/the-soundness-perimeter.md), and a wrong `Clash` by a second route of its own: it reaches inversion as *impossible*, which excuses an omitted arm — the vacuous-elimination route both [Coverage](coverage.md) and the [Large-elimination guard](large-elimination-guard.md) record. The equations the folds themselves compute are [Open fold laws and the sum normal form](open-fold-laws-and-the-sum-normal-form.md)'s, and the bound the division family rests on is [The bounds oracle and the division family](the-bounds-oracle-and-the-division-family.md)'s.

**The `Bin`/`List` half is probed by two grids beside the `Nat` one, in `curios-core`'s `reduce::intrinsic::tests`.** `every_bin_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` and `every_list_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` take each peel verdict's own obligation — an `Eql` must hold at every closed instantiation, a `Clash` must fail at every one, a `Continue`'s residuals must be equi-satisfiable with the pair they replaced, `Stuck` promises nothing — over shapes that reach the laws the comments assert: symbolic chunks cancelling with a byte clash surviving past them, window fusion across a shared seam, the empty-window drop, append-as-concatenation, and a near-miss control beside each (windows meeting at no seam must not fuse; a one-byte symbolic cons against the identity stays undecided). The `List` grid's first shape pins the rule a copied `Bin` arm would get wrong: `[a + b]` and `[b + a]` denote one list at every instantiation, so clashing differing literal heads there would be a false impossibility. Two mutations were run and caught: fusing windows without the seam check failed the no-seam control as a false `Eql`, and applying `peel_bin`'s literal-head clash to `List` failed `[a + b] ++ xs ~ [b + a] ++ xs` as a false impossibility.

**Two things the grids established beside the verdicts.** The spine's own empty-window drop is unreachable from a reduced input — reduction's empty-slice rule collapses a zero-length window before conversion ever flattens the value, whatever its start is, so the `push` branch is a redundant twin of a rule probed above rather than a second admission surface, and the grid exercises it by construction anyway. And the window laws' instantiation domain is the precondition's own — `s + l <= len(b)` — because that is the domain the typing facts confine every written slice to. Fusion needs no ordering check because there is no ordering left to check: a window is a start and a count, so the out-of-order case the old `(s, e)` form could spell is unrepresentable.

**The `Nat` peel is probed over values by the same method.** `every_nat_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` takes each verdict's own obligation and checks it against ground truth at every closed instantiation of the shape's symbols: a `Peel::Equal` must hold everywhere, a `Peel::Clash` must fail everywhere, and — the obligation nothing had stated — a `Peel::Continue`'s residuals must be *equi-satisfiable* with the pair they replaced, since the caller compares the residuals and reports their verdict as the original pair's, so a residual pair disagreeing where the originals agreed would turn a later clash into a clash on the originals. Every shape held. The grid is mutation-checked against the defect the code's own comment warns of — cancelling a repeated summand as a set rather than a multiset makes it fail naming `x + x + 1 ~ x + 1` at `x = 1` — and it asserts its own verdict tally, because `Continue` is where a shape falls when nothing fires, so a grid that decided nothing would have passed while checking nothing.

**The gate that cancellation runs behind is a shape, not a carrier.** `peel_nat` is reached by a pair either of whose sides is headed by a successor floor or a sum spine — the two shapes `Nat::decompose` and `Nat::summands` read — spelled once, in `curios-core`'s `peel_nat_terms`, which both congruences and the inverter call, so the two checkers remain incapable of disagreeing about it. A gate keyed on `(Intrinsic::Nat, Intrinsic::Nat)` would miss a *floorless* sum: `(s + l₁) + l₂` reduces to a bare `NatAdd` node rather than to a successor floor and would fall through to shape congruence, refusing against `s + (l₁ + l₂)`. **What a verdict claims is the same at every shape.** The cancellation, its multiset rule and its soundness argument are `Nat::cancel_common`'s, recorded below; the gate decides only *where* they fire, so what it admits is the same two conclusions at more shapes — reassociation joins commutation on the `Eql` side, the admitting direction. Soundness at the wider gate rests on the fact `peel_bin` and `peel_list` already rest on: conversion and inversion ask about pairs inhabiting one type, so a side carrying a floor or a sum spine makes both sides `Nat`s. The row it unblocks is `Bin`/`List` window fusion, whose fused bound is a reassociation of the one the second window already carried — so `spine`'s `push` hands that proof on instead of composing one, which is what let the four sequence accessors take a bound field at all.

**`Peel::Stuck` is reachable for `Nat`, and the grid holds the case.** A floorless pair sharing no summand comes back from `cancel_common` *identically* — the stability contract recorded below — and handing it to the caller as `Continue` would re-enter the same congruence on the same terms and never settle, where every `Continue` off a floored pair strips a shared floor and that decrease is the termination argument. So the residuals are compared against what went in and an unchanged pair declines, falling through to shape congruence exactly as `Bin`'s and `List`'s peels do. Those two decline only from a pair already spelled as its segment list: a pair whose leading chunks the prefix step cannot match, but either of whose sides was written as something else — a nesting, an append, a run split across operands — comes back as `Continue` over both flat spellings, since regrouping is the identity on values, and the round after it is the flat pair, which declines. Without that round the caller's shape congruence met a two-operand concatenation against a three-operand one and refused on operand count before comparing a single chunk, so `[..[..a, ..b], ..c]` converted with `[..a, ..b, ..c]` only while `a` was syntactically its own twin — which two instances of one universe-polymorphic definition, or `g(a + b)` against `g(b + a)`, are not. `spine`'s `peel_bin_regroups_a_nested_concat_whose_leading_chunks_differ` and `peel_list_regroups_an_append_whose_leading_chunk_differs` state the regroup, their two `declines_a_flat_pair` twins the round after it, the `Bin` and `List` grids carry the regrouping shape beside its flat twin under the `Continue` obligation, and `curios`'s `a_symbolic_run_is_the_same_value_however_it_is_grouped` puts it to both checkers as a program. Declining is the refusing direction at all three readers, so it cannot admit anything; what it costs is only reductions. The `Nat` grid carries a case for it (`x + y ~ z`) beside the floorless shapes — reassociation and commutation on the `Eql` side, a floored side against a floorless one clashing on the surviving floor, and a floorless `Continue` — and its verdict tally counts `Stuck` with the rest, so a gate that stopped admitting the floorless shapes fails rather than passing vacuously.

**`Int` is peeled as a group, beside `Nat`'s cancellative monoid.** `peel_int_pair` reads a pair through `int_cancel_common`, which moves the difference of the two sides to the two sides by sign, and translates it into the same four verdicts `classify_nat` produces: two constant residuals decide `Equal` or `Clash`, a pair the cancellation changed carries on as `Continue` over its residuals, and a pair it left untouched declines as `Stuck`, on the same stability contract. The soundness claim is the group's, `a ⋈ b` iff `a - b ⋈ 0`, and it is held over values at instantiations that include a negative by `every_int_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation`, the `Nat` grid's obligations restated for the carrier where a rule sound on ℕ can be wrong below zero.

**`&&` and `||` are peeled as semilattices, beside the free monoids.** Each is idempotent, commutative and associative, so two conjunctions — or two disjunctions — denote one value exactly when they hold the same set of leaves, and `peel_bool` decides `Equal` on identical leaf sets and `Stuck` on anything else, never `Clash`, since unlike leaf sets may still agree as values. It sits in every reader's chain — both converters and the inverter through `peel_intrinsic` — so the checkers cannot disagree about it. Decided in the peel rather than by a canonical spelling in the fold because the roadmap records the `&&`/`||` cliff: a fold that normalized a tree whole paid for the whole tree at every leaf, where a comparison flattens each side once. The fold's laziness is kept whole: a stuck connective's right operand stays as written, so the converters ask `normalize_bool` for each side first, which forces every leaf and re-nests the tree, the same demand by name that `Nat::normalize` answers for a stuck product — paid once per comparison of two trees, never at a reduction of one. What holds it: `spine`'s `peel_bool_decides_a_commuted_and_reassociated_conjunction_equal` states the admitting direction and `peel_bool_declines_unlike_leaf_sets_without_clashing` the declining one, and `curios`'s `tests::laws` states commutation, reassociation and idempotence at `Bool` as `refl` proofs both checkers see. The symmetric comparisons are peeled the same way: `peel_symmetric` decides `x == y` against `y == x` — and `!=`, and the `xor` that `!=` on `Bool` lowers through, on every carrier — `Equal` when the operand pairs are one pair swapped and `Stuck` otherwise, never `Clash`. It is a peel and not a spelling on purpose: respelling the operands at the fold was tried and failed the prelude at the kernel, because a guard's refinement is keyed on its written spelling, which `documentation/design/toolchain/a-comparison-is-spelled-one-way-when-it-is-stuck.md` now records as rejected. `spine`'s `peel_symmetric_decides_a_swapped_comparison_equal` states the verdicts, with an order relation declined beside them, and `tests::laws` states `x == y = y == x` at `Nat`, `Int`, `Bool`, `Bytes` and `Bits`.

**A negated comparison and the `<=`/`<` step are aligned probe-side, beside the semilattice peel.** `Bool/not` is `xor(_, true)` once unfolded, so `not(a < b)` and `b <= a` were two spellings of one value that no peel related, and so were `a <= b` and `a < b + 1`. `align_comparisons` reads a negated `Nat`, `Int` or `Bool` comparison as its dual and a `<=` meeting a `<` as `<` of the successor, in both converters and before the peels — probe-side, on the same ground `normalize_bool` stands on, since a guard's refinement is keyed on its written spelling and a fold that respelled it would take every later occurrence past it. The soundness claim is the total order's, `¬(a < b) ⟺ b ≤ a` and `a ≤ b ⟺ a < b + 1`, which is why `Flt` is left out: against the NaN both directions of an ordered comparison are false. `curios/src/tests/laws.rs` holds the rows at every carrier, with De Morgan and absorption as the refused rows that mark where the leaf-set normal form ends.

**Summand cancellation, and what carries it.** `Nat::cancel_common` strips what two operands carry in common — the literal floor and the symbolic summands — on the law that `Nat` under `+` is a cancellative commutative monoid. Three readers share it: the comparison family, `Nat/sub`, and `peel_nat`, which is only its translation into `Peel`. Two conclusions are the ones to attack. Peeling and comparison decide a *commuted* sum **equal**, `x + y ~ y + x`, which is the admitting direction and reaches conversion as a definitional equality. And they decide a surviving positive floor against the identity a **clash**, `x + 2 ~ x + 1`, which reaches inversion as *impossible* and so excuses an omitted arm — the vacuous-elimination route recorded in [Coverage](coverage.md). Three properties carry both: that cancellation removes one occurrence per match rather than collapsing a repeated summand, that summands pair by syntactic equality so the incompleteness falls on the declining side, and that a pass cancelling nothing returns its operands *identically* — the last learned by losing it, since rebuilding re-associates a sum and the oscillation that produced overflowed the stack on the fixed prelude. `curios-core`'s `nat` and `spine` tests pin all three plus the two conclusions; the value grid above is the probe of the law itself. Summands are keyed up to universe instance, through `project_erased_universes`; why that is sound for a `Nat` and not in general is [The refinement key](../what-the-kernel-consults/the-refinement-key.md)'s.

**A fold's threshold is the theory's, never the host's.** The right shifts narrowed their count to a host index before consulting the value's width, so `Nat/shr(1, 2³²)` folded natively and stayed a neutral term on wasm32, and `2⁶⁴` stayed neutral everywhere — an equation the theory decides, `⌊v / 2^k⌋` being zero past the width and the sign for `Int`, left to the host's word. `curios-num`'s `>>` on both magnitudes answers a count at or past the width without narrowing it, and `curios-core`'s `reduce::intrinsic::shift_tests::a_right_shift_by_a_count_past_the_host_word_folds_to_the_arithmetic` holds both carriers at a count past `u32` and one past `u64`, so a narrowing through either primitive fails it. The left shift keeps its decline, since past the budget there is nothing to build, and `cost_tests` holds that refusal.
