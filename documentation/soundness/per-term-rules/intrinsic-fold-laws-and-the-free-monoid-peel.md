# Intrinsic fold laws and the free-monoid peel

**Assumes.** A `Peel::Equal` is a genuine equation and a `Peel::Clash` a genuine disequality on the carrier's values, at every reader

**Status.** **probed** at the `Nat` peel's verdicts — over the floorless shapes its gate admits as well as the floored ones — and at the `Bin`/`List` peel verdicts, each grid asserting its own verdict tally. The bit-grain copies of the byte-grain arms that used to stay **argued** are gone, by taking the shape the decomposition beneath them already had. They branched on the grain only to reach `PackedBin`'s two readers, and that is now one seam — `element_of_run` reading a generator out of a literal run, `Generator` reading one into it — so a single arm serves both grains and there is no second copy left to be wrong. `tests::laws`'s `Bits` carrier states the rows its `Bytes` carrier states, which is what makes the bit grain's evidence its own rather than the byte grain's read across. The peel is one function in `curios-core`, so a wrong `Eql` admits by [the perimeter's shared route](../../design/language/the-soundness-perimeter.md), and a wrong `Clash` by a second route of its own: it reaches inversion as *impossible*, which excuses an omitted arm — the vacuous-elimination route both [Coverage](coverage.md) and the [Large-elimination guard](large-elimination-guard.md) record. The equations the folds themselves compute are [Open fold laws and the sum normal form](open-fold-laws-and-the-sum-normal-form.md)'s, and the bound the division family rests on is [The bounds oracle and the division family](the-bounds-oracle-and-the-division-family.md)'s.

**The `Bin`/`List` half is probed by two grids beside the `Nat` one, in `curios-core`'s `reduce::intrinsic::laws_tests`.** `every_bin_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` and `every_list_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` take each peel verdict's own obligation — an `Eql` must hold at every closed instantiation, a `Clash` must fail at every one, a `Continue`'s residuals must be equi-satisfiable with the pair they replaced, `Stuck` promises nothing — over shapes that reach the laws the comments assert: symbolic chunks cancelling with a byte clash surviving past them, window fusion across a shared seam, the empty-window drop, append-as-concatenation, and a near-miss control beside each (windows meeting at no seam must not fuse; a one-byte symbolic cons against the identity clashes, and so does a positive run behind a symbolic chunk, since the identity check reads the whole residual rather than its head — a symbolic appended byte is a `Single` segment whose kind says its length is one, so the clash rests on the segment list and never on recognizing a spelling). At the equality fold a bare side — a variable against a spine — is read as the one-chunk spine it is, under the grain the fold carries, which is the identity on values; it is what folds `append(bs, k) == bs` to `false`, and it is the fold's alone, since conversion can only refuse such a pair and inversion solves a bare side as a binder. `free_monoid_tests`'s `a_spine_against_a_bare_variable_clashes_on_a_positive_residual` states the verdict with the may-be-empty control beside it. The `List` grid's first shape pins the rule a copied `Bin` arm would get wrong: `[a + b]` and `[b + a]` denote one list at every instantiation, so clashing differing literal heads there would be a false impossibility. Two mutations were run and caught: fusing windows without the seam check failed the no-seam control as a false `Eql`, and applying `peel_bin`'s literal-head clash to `List` failed `[a + b] ++ xs ~ [b + a] ++ xs` as a false impossibility.

**A fill is decided against the cons it equals, and its count is what says whether it may be.** `replicate(n + 1, a)` certainly begins with `a` however large `n` is, so `peel_bin` emits that leading generator and leaves the shorter fill as one symbolic chunk — the other side's own residual fill is that same term, so the two segment lists meet without either being unrolled. This is the one packed shape whose *leading generator* is known while its *length* is not: a literal run carries its bytes and a `Window` carries a count, where a fill built in one operation over a symbolic count carries neither and is still not opaque. **A count carrying no floor is where the admission stops.** Such a fill may be empty, so it exposes no generator and must decline rather than clash, which is why it is pushed as a length-less `Symbolic` segment and not as the `Single` an appended byte becomes — a `Single` says its length is one, and the identity check reads the whole residual, so claiming a length there would clash `replicate(n, a)` against the empty bytestring that `n = 0` makes it equal to. The same successor read serves the eliminator's `uncons` in `curios-core`'s `free_monoid`, through `Nat::peel_succ`, so the destructor and the conversion cannot disagree about what a fill of `n + 1` is. What holds it: `spine`'s `peel_bin_decides_a_fill_of_a_successor_against_the_cons_it_equals` states the admitting direction and `peel_bin_declines_a_fill_whose_count_is_not_a_successor` the declining one, and the `Bin` grid carries both under their own obligations over a count dimension whose instantiations include zero. Mutation-checked: letting an unknown count contribute nothing — reading it as the empty fill it might be — decides `replicate(n, c)` equal to `x[]`, which the grid fails at `n = 1` as a false `Equal`.

**Two things the grids established beside the verdicts.** The spine's own empty-window drop is unreachable from a reduced input — reduction's empty-slice rule collapses a zero-length window before conversion ever flattens the value, whatever its start is, so the `push` branch is a redundant twin of a rule probed above rather than a second admission surface, and the grid exercises it by construction anyway. And the window laws' instantiation domain is the precondition's own — `s + l <= len(b)` — because that is the domain the typing facts confine every written slice to. Fusion needs no ordering check because there is no ordering left to check: a window is a start and a count, so the out-of-order case the old `(s, e)` form could spell is unrepresentable.

**The `Nat` peel is probed over values by the same method.** `every_nat_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` takes each verdict's own obligation and checks it against ground truth at every closed instantiation of the shape's symbols: a `Peel::Equal` must hold everywhere, a `Peel::Clash` must fail everywhere, and — the obligation nothing had stated — a `Peel::Continue`'s residuals must be *equi-satisfiable* with the pair they replaced, since the caller compares the residuals and reports their verdict as the original pair's, so a residual pair disagreeing where the originals agreed would turn a later clash into a clash on the originals. Every shape held. The grid is mutation-checked against the defect the code's own comment warns of — cancelling a repeated summand as a set rather than a multiset makes it fail naming `x + x + 1 ~ x + 1` at `x = 1` — and it asserts its own verdict tally, because `Continue` is where a shape falls when nothing fires, so a grid that decided nothing would have passed while checking nothing.

**A second demand feeds the peel, and it changes what reaches the verdicts rather than the verdicts themselves.** The peel pairs summands by identity and the fold leaves a stuck application's arguments exactly as written, so `f(x + y)` and `f(y + x)` were two summands that never met though conversion decides that very pair the moment it compares them directly — each step of `Eq(f(x + y) + g(y + z), g(z + y) + f(y + x))` held while the composition did not. `Nat::normalize_atoms` forces each summand's *arguments*, keeping the applied head verbatim as `canonical_scrutinee` keeps a refinement key's, and puts the sums inside them in structural-hash order, because reduction alone is not enough: `x + y` and `y + x` reduce to two distinct `NatAdd` nodes, [`Nat::summands`] keeping first-appearance order so that read-then-rebuild stays the identity. Both halves are denotation-preserving — reduction is definitional and reordering is the commutative monoid's — so a pair the peel now decides is one it would have decided had the terms been spelled that way, and every verdict's own obligation above is untouched. `Nat::cancel_common` still takes no reducer, which is what keeps the three reduction folds and the free monoid's distance walk out of it: the demand is asked by name at each checker's conversion, where `Nat::normalize`'s stuck-product demand and `normalize_bool`'s forced leaves already are, and only from the `Peel::Stuck` arm — so it costs nothing on any pair that already decided, and a retry that still decides nothing falls through to the congruence on the *original* spelling.

**It is asked twice on purpose, and the grid caught the once it was not.** A normalization demand is per-checker, not shared like the peel it feeds, so adding it to `curios-elab` alone made the elaborator close a law the kernel refused: `tests::laws`'s `every_held_law_closes_by_refl` reported *the kernel refused `/law6`* while `every_row_is_on_the_side_the_compiler_puts_it` passed, within one run and in the failing-closed direction. The twin in `curios-cert`'s `convert::intrinsic` is what makes the row a law both checkers decide, which is the grid's own condition for holding one.

**The gate that cancellation runs behind is a shape, not a carrier.** `peel_nat` is reached by a pair either of whose sides is headed by a successor floor or a sum spine — the two shapes `Nat::decompose` and `Nat::summands` read — spelled once, in `curios-core`'s `peel_nat_terms`, which both congruences and the inverter call, so the two checkers remain incapable of disagreeing about it. A gate keyed on `(Intrinsic::Nat, Intrinsic::Nat)` would miss a *floorless* sum: `(s + l₁) + l₂` reduces to a bare `NatAdd` node rather than to a successor floor and would fall through to shape congruence, refusing against `s + (l₁ + l₂)`. **What a verdict claims is the same at every shape.** The cancellation, its multiset rule and its soundness argument are `Nat::cancel_common`'s, recorded below; the gate decides only *where* they fire, so what it admits is the same two conclusions at more shapes — reassociation joins commutation on the `Eql` side, the admitting direction. Soundness at the wider gate rests on the fact `peel_bin` and `peel_list` already rest on: conversion and inversion ask about pairs inhabiting one type, so a side carrying a floor or a sum spine makes both sides `Nat`s. The row it unblocks is `Bin`/`List` window fusion, whose fused bound is a reassociation of the one the second window already carried — so `spine`'s `push` hands that proof on instead of composing one, which is what let the four sequence accessors take a bound field at all.

**`Peel::Stuck` is reachable for `Nat`, and the grid holds the case.** A floorless pair sharing no summand comes back from `cancel_common` *identically* — the stability contract recorded below — and handing it to the caller as `Continue` would re-enter the same congruence on the same terms and never settle, where every `Continue` off a floored pair strips a shared floor and that decrease is the termination argument. So the residuals are compared against what went in and an unchanged pair declines, falling through to shape congruence exactly as `Bin`'s and `List`'s peels do. Those two decline only from a pair already spelled as its segment list: a pair whose leading chunks the prefix step cannot match, but either of whose sides was written as something else — a nesting, an append, a run split across operands — comes back as `Continue` over both flat spellings, since regrouping is the identity on values, and the round after it is the flat pair, which declines. Without that round the caller's shape congruence met a two-operand concatenation against a three-operand one and refused on operand count before comparing a single chunk, so `[..[..a, ..b], ..c]` converted with `[..a, ..b, ..c]` only while `a` was syntactically its own twin — which two instances of one universe-polymorphic definition, or `g(a + b)` against `g(b + a)`, are not. `spine`'s `peel_bin_regroups_a_nested_concat_whose_leading_chunks_differ` and `peel_list_regroups_an_append_whose_leading_chunk_differs` state the regroup, their twins `peel_bin_declines_a_flat_pair_whose_leading_chunks_differ` and `peel_list_declines_a_flat_pair_whose_leading_chunks_differ` the round after it, the `Bin` and `List` grids carry the regrouping shape beside its flat twin under the `Continue` obligation, and `curios`'s `a_symbolic_run_is_the_same_value_however_it_is_grouped` puts it to both checkers as a program. Declining is the refusing direction at all three readers, so it cannot admit anything; what it costs is only reductions. The `Nat` grid carries a case for it (`x + y ~ z`) beside the floorless shapes — reassociation and commutation on the `Eql` side, a floored side against a floorless one clashing on the surviving floor, and a floorless `Continue` — and its verdict tally counts `Stuck` with the rest, so a gate that stopped admitting the floorless shapes fails rather than passing vacuously.

**`Int` is peeled as a group, beside `Nat`'s cancellative monoid.** `peel_int_pair` reads a pair through `int_cancel_common`, which moves the difference of the two sides to the two sides by sign, and translates it into the same four verdicts `classify_nat` produces: two constant residuals decide `Equal` or `Clash`, a pair the cancellation changed carries on as `Continue` over its residuals, and a pair it left untouched declines as `Stuck`, on the same stability contract. The soundness claim is the group's, `a ⋈ b` iff `a - b ⋈ 0`, and it is held over values at instantiations that include a negative by `every_int_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation`, the `Nat` grid's obligations restated for the carrier where a rule sound on ℕ can be wrong below zero.

**`&&` and `||` are peeled as semilattices, beside the free monoids.** Each is idempotent, commutative and associative, so two conjunctions — or two disjunctions — denote one value exactly when they hold the same set of leaves, and `peel_bool` decides `Equal` on identical leaf sets and `Stuck` on anything else, never `Clash`, since unlike leaf sets may still agree as values. It sits in every reader's chain — both converters and the inverter through `peel_intrinsic` — so the checkers cannot disagree about it. Decided in the peel rather than by a canonical spelling in the fold because the roadmap records the `&&`/`||` cliff: a fold that normalized a tree whole paid for the whole tree at every leaf, where a comparison flattens each side once. The fold's laziness is kept whole: a stuck connective's right operand stays as written, so the converters ask `normalize_bool` for each side first, which forces every leaf and re-nests the tree, the same demand by name that `Nat::normalize` answers for a stuck product — paid once per comparison of two trees, never at a reduction of one. What holds it: `spine`'s `peel_bool_decides_a_commuted_and_reassociated_conjunction_equal` states the admitting direction and `peel_bool_declines_unlike_leaf_sets_without_clashing` the declining one, and `curios`'s `tests::laws` states commutation, reassociation and idempotence at `Bool` as `refl` proofs both checkers see. The symmetric operations are peeled the same way: `peel_symmetric` decides `x == y` against `y == x` — and `!=`, the `xor` that `!=` on `Bool` lowers through, on every carrier, and the bitwise `and`, `or` and `xor` on ℕ, which commute as `xor` on `Bool` does — `Equal` when the operand pairs are one pair swapped and `Stuck` otherwise, never `Clash`. It is a peel and not a spelling on purpose: respelling the operands at the fold was tried and failed the prelude at the kernel, because a guard's refinement is keyed on its written spelling, which `documentation/design/toolchain/a-comparison-is-spelled-one-way-when-it-is-stuck.md` now records as rejected. `spine`'s `peel_symmetric_decides_a_swapped_comparison_equal` states the verdicts, with an order relation declined beside them, `peel_symmetric_decides_a_swapped_bitwise_operation_equal` the bitwise three with a shift declined, and `tests::laws` states `x == y = y == x` at `Nat`, `Int`, `Bool`, `Bytes` and `Bits` and the three bitwise commutations at `Nat`.

**A law across connectives is decided by a truth table over the atoms, where two terms are compared.** The fold takes what one node can see and `peel_bool` what one connective's leaf set holds; De Morgan, absorption and distribution relate *different* connectives and have no such statement. A normal form that made them structural would be a rewriting one, which the record rejects twice: a guard's refinement is keyed on its written spelling, and a fold that normalized a tree whole paid for the whole tree at every leaf. So `reduce::intrinsic`'s `decide_bool` is asked by name in both converters, beside `normalize_bool` and `align_comparisons`, and changes no spelling. It stands behind one gate — a side headed by a `Bool` connective — forces every node as it reads it, since the fold leaves a stuck connective's right operand as written, and reads both sides as formulas over one list of atoms: every leaf that is neither a connective nor a literal, a comparison registered against `dual_comparison` so that `x < y` and `y <= x` are one atom at two polarities, which is the total order's table and so leaves `Flt` out. It then evaluates both at every assignment. **Agreement everywhere is `Equal`, and anything else is nothing.** Treating the atoms as independent *over*-approximates the values they take together — `x < y` and `x < y + 1` are not independent at all — so the table visits every combination the real values can reach and two formulas that agree at all of them agree at those; for the same reason a disagreement may sit at an assignment no value reaches, so it proves nothing and the answer is never `Clash`. A metavariable among the leaves is an atom like any other. Past `BOOL_ATOM_CAP`, eight atoms between the two sides, the question is declined; the cap is a constant of the language and not of the host, and what is evaluated — one visit per node per assignment — is charged before the first assignment. A connective against a term that is no intrinsic at all, absorption's `b || (b && c)` against the bare `b`, never reaches the intrinsic congruence, so both converters ask at their head dispatch as well, in an arm each states; the inverter is not told, keeps `peel_bool`, and so refuses where it refused before. What holds it: `truth_tests` states the laws, the polarity pair with its soundness control, the undecided pairs and the cap on both sides of it; `every_truth_table_decision_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` holds each decided pair to one value at every instantiation of booleans *and* naturals and asserts its own tally, with a law true of the values and invisible to the table among the undecided; `a_bool_tree_converts_with_the_bare_term_it_equals_at_every_assignment` states the head-dispatch arm under one name in `curios-cert` and `curios-elab`; and `tests::laws` holds nine rows at `Bool` with three controls, and the law at the cap held with the same law one atom past it refused. Mutation-checked: reading a dual at its atom's own polarity decides `x < y && c` equal to `y <= x && c`, which the grid fails at `b = false, c = true, x = 0, y = 0`.

**A position inside a window is decided as a comparison, because reduction cannot take it.** `get(slice(xs, s, l), i)` is `xs`'s element at `s + i`, and a window of a window is the window it names in the root, `slice(slice(xs, s, l), t, m) = slice(xs, s + t, m)`. Rewriting either node would owe a bound on the root — `s + i < len(xs)` — that follows from the window's bound and the inner one by transitivity and is convertible with neither, and a reducer that derives a proof is the defect window fusion was reparameterised to avoid. A comparison builds no term and so owes none: `spine`'s `rooted` reads a base through every window it was cut from to a root and an absolute start, summed by `Nat::sum` so it is the term the fold would have built; `peel_position` decides two stuck `get`s of one carrier and grain `Equal` when the roots are identical and the cancellation decides the absolute positions one number, `Stuck` otherwise; and `peel_prefix`'s window arm compares two windows by root, absolute start and count the same way. Neither ever answers `Clash` — unlike positions may hold one element — so inversion is handed no new impossibility, and the bounds are never read, which is proof irrelevance, the line `Atom::Window`'s `within` already draws. An atom keeps its written fields, so a residual is rebuilt as the node it came from and nothing is invented on the way back. `peel_position` sits in `peel_intrinsic` and in both converters' chains, so all three readers agree. What holds it: `spine`'s `peel_position_decides_a_position_through_a_window_equal`, over both carriers, a commuted sum and a nested window, with `peel_position_declines_an_unlike_position_or_base_without_clashing` and `peel_list_decides_a_window_of_a_window_against_the_window_it_names` beside it; the `Bin` and `List` grids carry the nested window and its near miss one start short under the `Equal` obligation; `every_position_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` holds the positions over values at both carriers and asserts its own tally; and `tests::laws` states the three rows at `List`, `Bytes` and `Bits`, each with the position one past and the other base as controls. Mutation-checked: dropping the inner start from the absolute position decides `get(slice(w, 1, 3), 2)` equal to `get(w, 2)`, which the position grid fails at the anchor `[9, 8, 7, 6]` and both peel grids fail on their near miss.

**A negated comparison and the `<=`/`<` step are aligned probe-side, beside the semilattice peel.** `Bool/not` is `xor(_, true)` once unfolded, so `not(a < b)` and `b <= a` were two spellings of one value that no peel related, and so were `a <= b` and `a < b + 1`. `align_comparisons` reads a negated `Nat`, `Int` or `Bool` comparison as its dual and a `<=` meeting a `<` as `<` of the successor, in both converters and before the peels — probe-side, on the same ground `normalize_bool` stands on, since a guard's refinement is keyed on its written spelling and a fold that respelled it would take every later occurrence past it. The soundness claim is the total order's, `¬(a < b) ⟺ b ≤ a` and `a ≤ b ⟺ a < b + 1`, which is why `Flt` is left out: against the NaN both directions of an ordered comparison are false. `curios/src/tests/laws.rs` holds the rows at every carrier.

**Summand cancellation, and what carries it.** `Nat::cancel_common` strips what two operands carry in common — the literal floor and the symbolic summands — on the law that `Nat` under `+` is a cancellative commutative monoid. Three readers share it: the comparison family, `Nat/sub`, and `peel_nat`, which is only its translation into `Peel`. Two conclusions are the ones to attack. Peeling and comparison decide a *commuted* sum **equal**, `x + y ~ y + x`, which is the admitting direction and reaches conversion as a definitional equality. And they decide a surviving positive floor against the identity a **clash**, `x + 2 ~ x + 1`, which reaches inversion as *impossible* and so excuses an omitted arm — the vacuous-elimination route recorded in [Coverage](coverage.md). Three properties carry both: that cancellation removes one occurrence per match rather than collapsing a repeated summand, that summands pair by syntactic equality so the incompleteness falls on the declining side, and that a pass cancelling nothing returns its operands *identically* — the last learned by losing it, since rebuilding re-associates a sum and the oscillation that produced overflowed the stack on the fixed prelude. `curios-core`'s `nat` and `spine` tests pin all three plus the two conclusions; the value grid above is the probe of the law itself. Summands are keyed up to universe instance, through `project_erased_universes`; why that is sound for a `Nat` and not in general is [The refinement key](../what-the-kernel-consults/the-refinement-key.md)'s.

**A fold's threshold is the theory's, never the host's.** The right shifts narrowed their count to a host index before consulting the value's width, so `Nat/shr(1, 2³²)` folded natively and stayed a neutral term on wasm32, and `2⁶⁴` stayed neutral everywhere — an equation the theory decides, `⌊v / 2^k⌋` being zero past the width and the sign for `Int`, left to the host's word. `curios-num`'s `>>` on both magnitudes answers a count at or past the width without narrowing it, and `curios-core`'s `reduce::intrinsic::shift_tests::a_right_shift_by_a_count_past_the_host_word_folds_to_the_arithmetic` holds both carriers at a count past `u32` and one past `u64`, so a narrowing through either primitive fails it. The left shift keeps its decline, since past the budget there is nothing to build, and `cost_tests` holds that refusal.
