# Intrinsic fold laws and the free-monoid peel

**Assumes.** A `Deduction::Equal` is a genuine equation and a `Deduction::Impossible` a genuine disequality on the carrier's values, at every reader

**Status.** **probed** at the `Nat` peel's verdicts — over the floorless shapes its gate admits as well as the floored ones — and at the `Bin`/`List` peel verdicts, each grid asserting its own verdict tally. The bit-grain copies of the byte-grain arms that used to stay **argued** are gone, by taking the shape the decomposition beneath them already had. They branched on the grain only to reach `Binary`'s two readers, and that is now one seam — `element_of_run` reading a generator out of a literal run, `Generator` reading one into it — so a single arm serves both grains and there is no second copy left to be wrong. `tests::laws`'s `Bits` carrier states the rows its `Bytes` carrier states, which is what makes the bit grain's evidence its own rather than the byte grain's read across. The peel is one function in `curios-core`, so a wrong `Eql` admits by [the perimeter's shared route](../../design/language/the-soundness-perimeter.md), and a wrong `Impossible` by a second route of its own: it reaches inversion as *impossible*, which excuses an omitted arm — the vacuous-elimination route both [Coverage](coverage.md) and the [Large-elimination guard](large-elimination-guard.md) record. The equations the folds themselves compute are [Open fold laws and the sum normal form](open-fold-laws-and-the-sum-normal-form.md)'s, and the bound the division family rests on is [The bounds oracle and the division family](the-bounds-oracle-and-the-division-family.md)'s.

**The `Bin`/`List` half is probed by two grids beside the `Nat` one, in `curios-core`'s `reduce::intrinsic::laws_tests`.** `every_bin_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` and `every_list_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` take each peel verdict's own obligation — an `Eql` must hold at every closed instantiation, an `Impossible` must fail at every one, an `Equivalent`'s residuals must be equi-satisfiable with the pair they replaced, `Undecided` promises nothing — over shapes that reach the laws the comments assert: symbolic chunks cancelling with a byte clash surviving past them, window fusion across a shared seam, the empty-window drop, append-as-concatenation, and a near-miss control beside each (windows meeting at no seam must not fuse; a one-byte symbolic cons against the identity clashes, and so does a positive run behind a symbolic chunk, since the identity check reads the whole residual rather than its head — a symbolic appended byte is a `Single` segment whose kind says its length is one, so the clash rests on the segment list and never on recognizing a spelling). At the equality fold a bare side — a variable against a spine — is read as the one-chunk spine it is, under the grain the fold carries, which is the identity on values; it is what folds `append(bs, k) == bs` to `false`, and it is the fold's alone, since conversion can only refuse such a pair and inversion solves a bare side as a binder. `free_monoid_tests`'s `a_spine_against_a_bare_variable_clashes_on_a_positive_residual` states the verdict with the may-be-empty control beside it. The `List` grid's first shape pins the rule a copied `Bin` arm would get wrong: `[a + b]` and `[b + a]` denote one list at every instantiation, so clashing differing literal heads there would be a false impossibility. Two mutations were run and caught: fusing windows without the seam check failed the no-seam control as a false `Eql`, and applying `peel_bin`'s literal-head clash to `List` failed `[a + b] ++ xs ~ [b + a] ++ xs` as a false impossibility.

**A fill is decided against the cons it equals, and its count is what says whether it may be.** `replicate(n + 1, a)` certainly begins with `a` however large `n` is, so `peel_bin` emits that leading generator and leaves the shorter fill as one symbolic chunk — the other side's own residual fill is that same term, so the two segment lists meet without either being unrolled. This is the one packed shape whose *leading generator* is known while its *length* is not: a literal run carries its bytes and a `Window` carries a count, where a fill built in one operation over a symbolic count carries neither and is still not opaque. **A count carrying no floor is where the admission stops.** Such a fill may be empty, so it exposes no generator and must decline rather than clash, which is why it is pushed as a length-less `Symbolic` segment and not as the `Single` an appended byte becomes — a `Single` says its length is one, and the identity check reads the whole residual, so claiming a length there would clash `replicate(n, a)` against the empty bytestring that `n = 0` makes it equal to. The same successor read serves the eliminator's `uncons` in `curios-core`'s `free_monoid`, through `Nat::peel_succ`, so the destructor and the conversion cannot disagree about what a fill of `n + 1` is. What holds it: `spine`'s `peel_bin_decides_a_fill_of_a_successor_against_the_cons_it_equals` states the admitting direction and `peel_bin_declines_a_fill_whose_count_is_not_a_successor` the declining one, and the `Bin` grid carries both under their own obligations over a count dimension whose instantiations include zero. `tests::laws` puts the fill to both checkers at both grains: the cons, the length and the positive fill against the empty one held, the floorless count as a control at the peel and at the equality fold, and the trailing generator — as certain as the leading one and not emitted — as a refused candidate. Mutation-checked: letting an unknown count contribute nothing — reading it as the empty fill it might be — decides `replicate(n, c)` equal to `x[]`, which the grid fails at `n = 1` as a false `Equal`.

**Two things the grids established beside the verdicts.** The spine's own empty-window drop is unreachable from a reduced input — reduction's empty-slice rule collapses a zero-length window before conversion ever flattens the value, whatever its start is, so the `push` branch is a redundant twin of a rule probed above rather than a second admission surface, and the grid exercises it by construction anyway. And the window laws' instantiation domain is the precondition's own — `s + l <= len(b)` — because that is the domain the typing facts confine every written slice to. Fusion needs no ordering check because there is no ordering left to check: a window is a start and a count, so the out-of-order case the old `(s, e)` form could spell is unrepresentable.

**The `Nat` peel is probed over values by the same method.** `every_nat_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` takes each verdict's own obligation and checks it against ground truth at every closed instantiation of the shape's symbols: a `Deduction::Equal` must hold everywhere, a `Deduction::Impossible` must fail everywhere, and — the obligation nothing had stated — a `Deduction::Equivalent`'s residuals must be *equi-satisfiable* with the pair they replaced, since the caller compares the residuals and reports their verdict as the original pair's, so a residual pair disagreeing where the originals agreed would turn a later clash into a clash on the originals. Every shape held. The grid is mutation-checked against the defect the code's own comment warns of — cancelling a repeated summand as a set rather than a multiset makes it fail naming `x + x + 1 ~ x + 1` at `x = 1` — and it asserts its own verdict tally, because `Equivalent` is where a shape falls when nothing fires, so a grid that decided nothing would have passed while checking nothing.

**A second demand feeds the peels, and it changes what reaches the verdicts rather than the verdicts themselves.** Every reader of the carriers' algebra pairs atoms by identity — the peels' cancellation, a monomial's factors, a connective's leaves, the truth table and the linear views — and the fold leaves a stuck application's arguments exactly as written, so `f(x + y)` and `f(y + x)` were two atoms that never met though conversion decides that very pair the moment it compares them directly. Each step of `Eq()(f(x + y) + g(y + z), g(z + y) + f(y + x))` held while the composition did not, and a product, a connective or a comparison of such atoms held or failed with the order its binders were declared in, which is the order the positional congruence's hash put its operands in. `force_atoms` forces each atom's *arguments*, recursively under the rigid heads they are stuck on and never under a binder, keeping each head verbatim as `canonical_scrutinee` keeps a refinement key's, and puts the sums and products inside them in structural-hash order, because reduction alone is not enough: `x + y` and `y + x` reduce to two distinct sums, [`Nat::summands`] keeping first-appearance order so that read-then-rebuild stays the identity. Both halves are denotation-preserving — reduction is definitional and reordering is each carrier's commutativity — so a pair now decided is one that would have been had the terms been spelled that way, and every verdict's own obligation above is untouched; each reduction is a probe, so an argument with no value at the type level is kept as written. The peels still take no reducer, which is what keeps the three reduction folds and the free monoid's distance walk out of them: the demand is asked by name where the readers run, `curios-analysis`'s `convert_intrinsics` and `connectives_agree`, which both checkers share, and only once the pair as it stood decided nothing — so it costs nothing on any pair that already decided, and a pair that still decides nothing goes on to the congruence on its *original* spelling. `tests::laws`'s `written` grid states the rows at both binder orders, and what forcing does not reach — an atom that is no application, whose insides keep their spelling — as a refused row.

**It is asked once, for both checkers, and the grid caught the time it was not.** While each checker ran its own copy of the chain, a normalization demand was per-checker, so adding it to `curios-elab` alone made the elaborator close a law the kernel refused: `tests::laws`'s `every_held_law_closes_by_refl` reported *the kernel refused `/law6`* while `every_row_is_on_the_side_the_compiler_puts_it` passed, within one run and in the failing-closed direction. Both checkers now run the one chain, so a demand added to it is asked by both, which is the grid's own condition for holding a row.

**The gate that cancellation runs behind is a shape, not a carrier.** The `Nat` peel is reached by a pair either of whose sides is headed by a successor floor or a sum spine — the two shapes `Nat::decompose` and `Nat::summands` read — spelled once, in `curios-core`'s `peel_nat_terms`, which both congruences and the inverter call, so the two checkers remain incapable of disagreeing about it. A gate keyed on `(Intrinsic::Nat, Intrinsic::Nat)` would miss a *floorless* sum: `(s + l₁) + l₂` reduces to a bare `NatAdd` node rather than to a successor floor and would fall through to shape congruence, refusing against `s + (l₁ + l₂)`. **What a verdict claims is the same at every shape.** The cancellation, its multiset rule and its soundness argument are `Nat::cancel_common`'s, recorded below; the gate decides only *where* they fire, so what it admits is the same two conclusions at more shapes — reassociation joins commutation on the `Eql` side, the admitting direction. Soundness at the wider gate rests on the fact `peel_bin` and `peel_list` already rest on: conversion and inversion ask about pairs inhabiting one type, so a side carrying a floor or a sum spine makes both sides `Nat`s. The row it unblocks is `Bin`/`List` window fusion, whose fused bound is a reassociation of the one the second window already carried — so `spine`'s `push` hands that proof on instead of composing one, which is what let the four sequence accessors take a bound field at all.

**`Deduction::Undecided` is reachable for `Nat`, and the grid holds the case.** A floorless pair sharing no summand comes back from `cancel_common` *identically* — the stability contract recorded below — and handing it to the caller as `Equivalent` would re-enter the same congruence on the same terms and never settle, where every `Equivalent` off a floored pair strips a shared floor and that decrease is the termination argument. So the residuals are compared against what went in and an unchanged pair declines, falling through to shape congruence exactly as `Bin`'s and `List`'s peels do. Those two decline only from a pair already spelled as its segment list: a pair whose leading chunks the prefix step cannot match, but either of whose sides was written as something else — a nesting, an append, a run split across operands — comes back as `Equivalent` over both flat spellings, since regrouping is the identity on values, and the round after it is the flat pair, which declines. Without that round the caller's shape congruence met a two-operand concatenation against a three-operand one and refused on operand count before comparing a single chunk, so `[..[..a, ..b], ..c]` converted with `[..a, ..b, ..c]` only while `a` was syntactically its own twin — which two instances of one universe-polymorphic definition, or `g(a + b)` against `g(b + a)`, are not. `spine`'s `peel_bin_regroups_a_nested_concat_whose_leading_chunks_differ` and `peel_list_regroups_an_append_whose_leading_chunk_differs` state the regroup, their twins `peel_bin_declines_a_flat_pair_whose_leading_chunks_differ` and `peel_list_declines_a_flat_pair_whose_leading_chunks_differ` the round after it, the `Bin` and `List` grids carry the regrouping shape beside its flat twin under the `Equivalent` obligation, and `curios`'s `a_symbolic_run_is_the_same_value_however_it_is_grouped` puts it to both checkers as a program. Declining is the refusing direction at all three readers, so it cannot admit anything; what it costs is only reductions. The `Nat` grid carries a case for it (`x + y ~ z`) beside the floorless shapes — reassociation and commutation on the `Eql` side, a floored side against a floorless one clashing on the surviving floor, and a floorless `Equivalent` — and its verdict tally counts `Undecided` with the rest, so a gate that stopped admitting the floorless shapes fails rather than passing vacuously.

**`Int` is peeled as a group, beside `Nat`'s cancellative monoid.** `peel_int_pair` reads a pair through the group cancellation `int_cancel_common` wraps, `curios-algebra`'s like `Nat`'s, which moves the difference of the two sides to the two sides by sign, and translates it into the same four verdicts `peel_nat_terms` produces: two constant residuals decide `Equal` or `Impossible`, a pair the cancellation changed carries on as `Equivalent` over its residuals, and a pair it left untouched declines as `Undecided`, on the same stability contract. The soundness claim is the group's, `a ⋈ b` iff `a - b ⋈ 0`, and it is held over values at instantiations that include a negative by `every_int_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation`, the `Nat` grid's obligations restated for the carrier where a rule sound on ℕ can be wrong below zero.

**Two monomials pair their factors by identity, in conversion only.** A product's factors are sorted by structural hash, which is canonical only while every factor is what it will stay: an unsolved metavariable hashes by its counter-assigned identity, not by the term it is solved to. The shape congruence compared two products factor by factor in that order, so a constructor field whose type held `(k · c) · ?d`, checked while the constructor's parameter `?d` was unsolved, met `(k · d) · c` by pairing `c` with `d` and refused — or not, by where `?d` sorted, which moved with the metavariable's number: eight byte-identical copies of one definition were refused four times. `peel_monomial` in `curios-core`'s `spine` pairs the factors of two `Nat` products, or two `Int` products, by identity up to universe instances under one coefficient: one multiset is `Equal`, and one factor left on each side is `Conclusion::Sufficient` over that pair, which leaves `?d` against `d` and solves it. It decides nothing else — two leftovers on a side, or two coefficients, decline to the shape congruence, and it never answers `Impossible`, since `2 · x` and `3 · x` meet at `x = 0`. It is asked for by name in both converters and is not in `peel_intrinsic`, whose `Deduction` has no sufficient variant to carry it: inversion would read the residual as an equation to deduce, and `x · f = x · g` does not give `f = g`; for conversion a pair of equal residuals is sufficient, which is all it claims. What holds it: `spine`'s `peel_monomial_pairs_factors_by_identity_not_by_position`, `peel_monomial_decides_a_reordered_product_equal` and the declining controls, and the `numeric` corpus unit's `products` module, eight copies of the refused definition that all compile — mutation-checked, since withdrawing the peel from the elaborator's chain fails the unit.

**Two comparisons pair their sides by identity, in conversion only.** The linear views respell two `Nat` or `Int` comparisons alike before any peel reads them, each side of the difference where its atoms' ranks put it, and a rank is a structural hash: an unsolved metavariable's is its own number. The shape congruence then compared two equalities, or two disequalities, side by side in that order, so `?w != y` against `x != y` paired `?w` with `y` or with `x` according to where `?w` sorted, and the law grid's audit saw four rows whose law solves the metavariable stop solving it once metavariables minted earlier in the item moved its number. `peel_comparison` in `curios-core`'s `spine` reads each comparison's two sides as a multiset under its carrier and relation and pairs them through the same `pair_factors` as the monomial: one multiset is `Equal`, and one side left on each is `Conclusion::Sufficient` over that pair, which leaves `?w` against `x`. It decides nothing else, never answers `Impossible`, and is asked for by name in the shared chain and not in `peel_intrinsic`, since `a == b` against `c == b` does not give `a = c`. What holds it: `spine`'s `peel_comparison_pairs_sides_by_identity_not_by_position`, `peel_comparison_decides_a_swapped_comparison_equal` and the declining control, and the law grid's `a_metavariable_is_solved_through_every_law_that_can_solve_it`, whose `Nat` and `Int` equality rows, commutativity included, now solve wherever the metavariable's number sorts it — mutation-checked, since withdrawing the peel from the chain fails the audit.

**`&&` and `||` are peeled as semilattices, beside the free monoids.** Each is idempotent, commutative and associative, so two conjunctions — or two disjunctions — denote one value exactly when they hold the same set of leaves, and `peel_bool` decides `Equal` on identical leaf sets — `curios-algebra`'s `same_leaves`, over leaves identified as written — and `Undecided` on anything else, never `Impossible`, since unlike leaf sets may still agree as values. It sits in every reader's chain — both converters and the inverter through `peel_intrinsic` — so the checkers cannot disagree about it. Decided in the peel rather than by a canonical spelling in the fold because the roadmap records the `&&`/`||` cliff: a fold that normalized a tree whole paid for the whole tree at every leaf, where a comparison flattens each side once. The fold's laziness is kept whole: a stuck connective's right operand stays as written, so the converters ask `normalize_bool` for each side first, which forces every leaf and re-nests the tree, the same demand by name that `Nat::normalize` answers for a stuck product — paid once per comparison of two trees, never at a reduction of one. What holds it: `spine`'s `peel_bool_decides_a_commuted_and_reassociated_conjunction_equal` states the admitting direction and `peel_bool_declines_unlike_leaf_sets_without_clashing` the declining one, and `curios`'s `tests::laws` states commutation, reassociation and idempotence at `Bool` as `refl` proofs both checkers see. The symmetric operations are peeled the same way: `peel_symmetric` decides `x == y` against `y == x` — and `!=`, the `xor` that `!=` on `Bool` lowers through, on every carrier, and the bitwise `and`, `or` and `xor` on ℕ, which commute as `xor` on `Bool` does — `Equal` when the operand pairs are one pair swapped and `Undecided` otherwise, never `Impossible`. It is a peel and not a spelling on purpose: respelling the operands at the fold was tried and failed the prelude at the kernel, because a guard's refinement is keyed on its written spelling, which `documentation/design/toolchain/a-comparison-is-spelled-one-way-when-it-is-stuck.md` now records as rejected. `spine`'s `peel_symmetric_decides_a_swapped_comparison_equal` states the verdicts, with an order relation declined beside them, `peel_symmetric_decides_a_swapped_bitwise_operation_equal` the bitwise three with a shift declined, and `tests::laws` states `x == y = y == x` at `Nat`, `Int`, `Bool`, `Bytes` and `Bits` and the three bitwise commutations at `Nat`.

**A law across connectives is decided by a truth table over the atoms, where two terms are compared.** The fold takes what one node can see and `peel_bool` what one connective's leaf set holds; De Morgan, absorption and distribution relate *different* connectives and have no such statement. A normal form that made them structural would be a rewriting one, which the record rejects twice: a guard's refinement is keyed on its written spelling, and a fold that normalized a tree whole paid for the whole tree at every leaf. So `reduce::intrinsic`'s `decide_bool`, which reads both sides into `curios-algebra`'s `Formula` and has it evaluated there, is asked by name in both converters, beside `normalize_bool` and `align_comparisons`, and changes no spelling. It stands behind one gate — a side headed by a `Bool` connective — forces every node as it reads it, since the fold leaves a stuck connective's right operand as written, and reads both sides as formulas over one list of atoms: every leaf that is neither a connective nor a literal, a comparison registered against `dual_comparison` — `curios-algebra`'s `Operation::negation`, read back through `Intrinsic::comparison` — so that `x < y` and `y <= x` are one atom at two polarities, which is the total order's table and so leaves `Flt` out. It then evaluates both at every assignment. **Agreement everywhere is `Equal`, and anything else is nothing.** Treating the atoms as independent *over*-approximates the values they take together — `x < y` and `x < y + 1` are not independent at all — so the table visits every combination the real values can reach and two formulas that agree at all of them agree at those; for the same reason a disagreement may sit at an assignment no value reaches, so it proves nothing and the answer is never `Impossible`. A metavariable among the leaves is an atom like any other. Past `BOOL_ATOM_CAP`, eight atoms between the two sides, the question is declined; the cap is a constant of the language and not of the host, and what is evaluated — one visit per node per assignment — is charged before the first assignment. A connective against a term that is no intrinsic at all, absorption's `b || (b && c)` against the bare `b`, never reaches the intrinsic congruence, so both converters ask at their head dispatch as well, in an arm each states; the inverter is not told, keeps `peel_bool`, and so refuses where it refused before. What holds it: `truth_tests` states the laws, the polarity pair with its soundness control, the undecided pairs and the cap on both sides of it; `every_truth_table_decision_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` holds each decided pair to one value at every instantiation of booleans *and* naturals and asserts its own tally, with a law true of the values and invisible to the table among the undecided; `a_bool_tree_converts_with_the_bare_term_it_equals_at_every_assignment` states the head-dispatch arm under one name in `curios-cert` and `curios-elab`; and `tests::laws` holds nine rows at `Bool` with three controls, and the law at the cap held with the same law one atom past it refused. Mutation-checked: reading a dual at its atom's own polarity decides `x < y && c` equal to `y <= x && c`, which the grid fails at `b = false, c = true, x = 0, y = 0`.

**A position inside a window is decided as a comparison, because reduction cannot take it.** `get(slice(xs, s, l), i)` is `xs`'s element at `s + i`, and a window of a window is the window it names in the root, `slice(slice(xs, s, l), t, m) = slice(xs, s + t, m)`. Rewriting either node would owe a bound on the root — `s + i < len(xs)` — that follows from the window's bound and the inner one by transitivity and is convertible with neither, and a reducer that derives a proof is the defect window fusion was reparameterised to avoid. A comparison builds no term and so owes none: `spine`'s `rooted` reads a base through every window it was cut from to a root and an absolute start, summed by `Nat::sum` so it is the term the fold would have built; `peel_position` decides two stuck `get`s of one carrier and grain `Equal` when the roots are identical and the cancellation decides the absolute positions one number, `Undecided` otherwise; and `peel_prefix`'s window arm compares two windows by root, absolute start and count the same way. Neither ever answers `Impossible` — unlike positions may hold one element — so inversion is handed no new impossibility, and the bounds are never read, which is proof irrelevance, the line `Atom::Window`'s `within` already draws. An atom keeps its written fields, so a residual is rebuilt as the node it came from and nothing is invented on the way back. `peel_position` sits in `peel_intrinsic` and in both converters' chains, so all three readers agree. What holds it: `spine`'s `peel_position_decides_a_position_through_a_window_equal`, over both carriers, a commuted sum and a nested window, with `peel_position_declines_an_unlike_position_or_base_without_clashing` and `peel_list_decides_a_window_of_a_window_against_the_window_it_names` beside it; the `Bin` and `List` grids carry the nested window and its near miss one start short under the `Equal` obligation; `every_position_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` holds the positions over values at both carriers and asserts its own tally; and `tests::laws` states the three rows at `List`, `Bytes` and `Bits`, each with the position one past and the other base as controls. Mutation-checked: dropping the inner start from the absolute position decides `get(slice(w, 1, 3), 2)` equal to `get(w, 2)`, which the position grid fails at the anchor `[9, 8, 7, 6]` and both peel grids fail on their near miss.

**A position inside a concatenation's operand is decided the same way, and for the same reason.** `get(xs ++ ys, i)` is `get(xs, i)` and `get(xs ++ ys ++ zs, len(xs) + i)` is `get(ys, i)`: `same_position` reads a root that is an operand of the other side's flattened concatenation at the sum of the measures before it — a literal run's length, a single element's one, a window's count, `len` of any other chunk — and compares the absolute positions by the cancellation, and `peel_prefix`'s window arm reads a window of an operand the same way. A rewrite would owe `i < len(xs)`, which the concatenation's bound does not give, so it is a comparison: the read of the operand is typed by its own bound, which places the position inside the operand at every well-typed instantiation, where the concatenation holds the operand's elements unchanged. An operand is matched whole and syntactically, as the peel matches any symbolic chunk, so a root spelled as a sub-run of several operands declines, the refusing direction; the verdicts are `Equal` and `Undecided`, never `Impossible`, so inversion is handed no new impossibility. What holds it: `spine`'s `position_tests` state the left and a middle operand at both carriers and a window of an operand, with an operand read at another offset, a position one past, and a base the concatenation does not hold as the declining side; the position grid holds `get(v ++ w, 2)` against `get(v, 2)` and `get(v ++ w, len(v) + 1)` against `get(w, 1)` at closed instantiations, with the other operand at the same offset and a position one short as near misses; and `tests::laws` states the rows at `List`, `Bytes` and `Bits`, the other operand as each one's control. Mutation-checked: dropping the operand's offset decides `get(v ++ w, 2)` equal to `get(w, 2)`, which the grid fails at its first anchor and both unit tests fail.

**A negated comparison is read as its dual, and two comparisons are aligned through their linear views, probe-side.** `Bool/not` is `xor(_, true)` once unfolded, so `not(a < b)` and `b <= a` were two spellings of one value that no peel related, and so were `a <= b` and `a < b + 1`. `align_comparisons` reads a negated `Nat`, `Int` or `Bool` comparison as its dual, then reads two `Nat` or `Int` comparisons through `curios-core`'s `LinearViews::align`: each as the difference of its sides over ℤ, `<` as the `<=` of its successor, an equality oriented by its atoms. Two whose views agree are one proposition — `x + 1 <= y` and `x < y`, `0 < j - i` and `i < j`, `Nat/to_int(m) < Nat/to_int(n)` and `m < n` — and otherwise both are respelled from their views, the positive part on the left of a `<=`, at their own carrier or at `Nat` where every atom is a widened natural, so the congruence after meets aligned operands. That one reading replaced three — the successor step, `Int`'s difference split by sign, and the pull-back of an `Int` comparison of widened naturals to `Nat` — and it is probe-side, on the ground `normalize_bool` stands on: a guard's refinement is keyed on its written spelling, and a fold that respelled it would take every later occurrence past it. The soundness claim is the total order's, `¬(a < b) ⟺ b ≤ a` and `a < b ⟺ a + 1 ≤ b` over ℤ, the group's, `a ⋈ b ⟺ a - b ⋈ 0`, and the embedding's, ℕ → ℤ preserving and reflecting order — which is why `Flt` is left out: against the NaN both directions of an ordered comparison are false. `curios-core`'s `a_floored_le_meets_the_lt_it_spells` and `two_comparisons_whose_views_differ_are_respelled_alike` pin the reading, and `tests::laws` states the rows both checkers see, the ones only the views decide among them.

**Summand cancellation, and what carries it.** `Nat::cancel_common` — whose mathematics is `curios-algebra`'s `Combination::cancel_common`, over the atoms `curios-core`'s `atoms` identifies — strips what two operands carry in common — the literal floor and the symbolic summands — on the law that `Nat` under `+` is a cancellative commutative monoid. Three readers share it: the comparison family, `Nat/sub`, and `peel_nat_terms`, which is only its translation into a `Deduction`. Two conclusions are the ones to attack. Peeling and comparison decide a *commuted* sum **equal**, `x + y ~ y + x`, which is the admitting direction and reaches conversion as a definitional equality. And they decide a surviving positive floor against the identity a **clash**, `x + 2 ~ x + 1`, which reaches inversion as *impossible* and so excuses an omitted arm — the vacuous-elimination route recorded in [Coverage](coverage.md). Three properties carry both: that cancellation removes one occurrence per match rather than collapsing a repeated summand, that summands pair by syntactic equality so the incompleteness falls on the declining side, and that a pass cancelling nothing returns its operands *identically* — the last learned by losing it, since rebuilding re-associates a sum and the oscillation that produced overflowed the stack on the fixed prelude. `curios-core`'s `nat` and `spine` tests pin all three plus the two conclusions; the value grid above is the probe of the law itself. Summands are keyed up to universe instance, through `project_erased_universes`; why that is sound for a `Nat` and not in general is [The refinement key](../what-the-kernel-consults/the-refinement-key.md)'s.

**A fold's threshold is the theory's, never the host's.** The right shifts narrowed their count to a host index before consulting the value's width, so `Nat/shr(1, 2³²)` folded natively and stayed a neutral term on wasm32, and `2⁶⁴` stayed neutral everywhere — an equation the theory decides, `⌊v / 2^k⌋` being zero past the width and the sign for `Int`, left to the host's word. `curios-num`'s `>>` on both magnitudes answers a count at or past the width without narrowing it, and `curios-core`'s `reduce::intrinsic::shift_tests::a_right_shift_by_a_count_past_the_host_word_folds_to_the_arithmetic` holds both carriers at a count past `u32` and one past `u64`, so a narrowing through either primitive fails it. The left shift keeps its decline, since past the budget there is nothing to build, and `cost_tests` holds that refusal.

**A conversion reduces back through the one that inverts it, on the ground each pair states.** Four `/sys` conversion pairs cancel a round trip through one rule, `curios-core`'s `Intrinsic::undone`, over the pairs `curios-algebra`'s `round_trip` declares, so a round trip over a symbolic operand is the operand, and a bound established on one side survives the trip. `Nat/to_byte` and `Int/to_nat` state their domains, `nat < 256` and `0 <= int`, so the value a narrowing builds *is* the number it was handed and widening it back is that number again; the other order holds because a widened value always lies in the narrowing's domain, so it narrows back whatever proof the narrowing carries, which is proof irrelevance. `Flt/of_le_bytes(Flt/to_le_bytes(f))` is `f` because every bit pattern is a distinct float, as [The binary64 model and its NaN rule](the-binary64-model-and-its-nan-rule.md) states, and that pair cancels one way only: the other order holds of the model too, but the table does not declare it, so it stays a candidate. The regrouping between `Bytes` and `Bits` inverts both ways, since it moves no bit. Each cancellation is an equation reduction takes and both checkers share, so a wrong one admits by [the perimeter's shared route](../../design/language/the-soundness-perimeter.md). The cancellations are **argued** on those grounds, and what holds them is `tests::laws`: the generated inverse-pair rows, each held at closed values as well, and the written `Byte against Nat`, `Int against Nat`, `Bits against Bytes` and `Flt against Bytes` carriers: every round trip as a `refl` proof both checkers see, beside the lengths that discharge the byte-first regrouping's alignment and the decoder's eight-byte bound with no proof written, and the float's reverse direction as a refused candidate that an arm deciding it would move. `compare_tests`'s `conversion_folds_preserve_the_number` holds the natural/integer pair over literals.

**The natural/integer pair is the whole of an ordered-semiring embedding, not only its inversion.** `Nat/to_int` goes through `Nat`'s normal form — `int_of_nat` widens a floor to the constant, a sum and a product summand by summand, and leaves a truncated difference, a quotient or a remainder the widened atom it is — because ℕ → ℤ is a semiring homomorphism. `int_preimage` reads that image back: a non-negative constant over positively weighted monomials of widened naturals is the image of one natural, which `Int/to_nat` narrows to whatever proof it carries, the one-atom inversion above being its smallest case. `compare_int` decides a pair whose sign-split sides both have preimages by `compare_nat` on the preimages, since the embedding preserves and reflects order — so `0 <= Nat/to_int(n)` is `true` on `Nat`'s own floor rather than on a floor restated for `Int` — and it only decides: a pair it cannot settle rebuilds from its own residuals, so no stuck comparison is respelled. A stuck `Int` comparison of widened naturals meets the `Nat` comparison of the same relation through `align_comparisons`, probe-side for the same reason. What holds it: `every_open_fold_law_preserves_the_value_at_every_closed_instantiation` pins each reduct and value — widening through a sum, a product over a floor, narrowing a combination back, and three comparisons decided through preimages — and `tests::laws`'s `Int against Nat` carrier states them as `refl` proofs both checkers see, with three controls the embedding must stop short of: a widened natural against an integer that may be negative, truncated subtraction read as the group's, and a difference of widened naturals read as one.
