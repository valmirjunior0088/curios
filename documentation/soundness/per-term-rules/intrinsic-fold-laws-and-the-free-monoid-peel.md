# Intrinsic fold laws and the free-monoid peel

**Assumes.** A `Peel::Equal` is a genuine equation and a `Peel::Clash` a genuine disequality on the carrier's values, at every reader

**Status.** **probed** at the `Nat` peel's verdicts — over the floorless shapes its gate admits as well as the floored ones — and at the `Bin`/`List` peel verdicts, each grid asserting its own verdict tally. What stays **argued** is the bit-grain copies of the byte-grain arms, verbatim twins whose only fixture is `bit_get_of_a_symbolic_cons_head_is_the_bit`. The peel is one function in `curios-core`, so a wrong `Equal` admits by [the perimeter's shared route](../../design/language/the-soundness-perimeter.md), and a wrong `Clash` by a second route of its own: it reaches inversion as *impossible*, which excuses an omitted arm — the vacuous-elimination route both [Coverage](coverage.md) and the [Large-elimination guard](large-elimination-guard.md) record. The equations the folds themselves compute are [Open fold laws and the sum normal form](open-fold-laws-and-the-sum-normal-form.md)'s, and the bound the division family rests on is [The bounds oracle and the division family](the-bounds-oracle-and-the-division-family.md)'s.

**The `Bin`/`List` half is probed by two grids beside the `Nat` one, in `curios-core`'s `reduce::intrinsic::tests`.** `every_bin_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` and `every_list_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` take each peel verdict's own obligation — an `Equal` must hold at every closed instantiation, a `Clash` must fail at every one, a `Continue`'s residuals must be equi-satisfiable with the pair they replaced, `Stuck` promises nothing — over shapes that reach the laws the comments assert: symbolic chunks cancelling with a byte clash surviving past them, window fusion across a shared seam, the empty-window drop, append-as-concatenation, and a near-miss control beside each (windows meeting at no seam must not fuse; a one-byte symbolic cons against the identity stays undecided). The `List` grid's first shape pins the rule a copied `Bin` arm would get wrong: `[a + b]` and `[b + a]` denote one list at every instantiation, so clashing differing literal heads there would be a false impossibility. Two mutations were run and caught: fusing windows without the seam check failed the no-seam control as a false `Equal`, and applying `peel_bin`'s literal-head clash to `List` failed `[a + b] ++ xs ~ [b + a] ++ xs` as a false impossibility.

**Two things the grids established beside the verdicts.** The spine's own empty-window drop is unreachable from a reduced input — reduction's empty-slice rule collapses a zero-length window before conversion ever flattens the value, whatever its start is, so the `push` branch is a redundant twin of a rule probed above rather than a second admission surface, and the grid exercises it by construction anyway. And the window laws' instantiation domain is the precondition's own — `s + l <= len(b)` — because that is the domain the typing facts confine every written slice to. Fusion needs no ordering check because there is no ordering left to check: a window is a start and a count, so the out-of-order case the old `(s, e)` form could spell is unrepresentable.

**The `Nat` peel is probed over values by the same method.** `every_nat_peel_verdict_holds_at_every_closed_instantiation` takes each verdict's own obligation and checks it against ground truth at every closed instantiation of the shape's symbols: a `Peel::Equal` must hold everywhere, a `Peel::Clash` must fail everywhere, and — the obligation nothing had stated — a `Peel::Continue`'s residuals must be *equi-satisfiable* with the pair they replaced, since the caller compares the residuals and reports their verdict as the original pair's, so a residual pair disagreeing where the originals agreed would turn a later clash into a clash on the originals. Every shape held. The grid is mutation-checked against the defect the code's own comment warns of — cancelling a repeated summand as a set rather than a multiset makes it fail naming `x + x + 1 ~ x + 1` at `x = 1` — and it asserts its own verdict tally, because `Continue` is where a shape falls when nothing fires, so a grid that decided nothing would have passed while checking nothing.

**The gate that cancellation runs behind is a shape, not a carrier.** `peel_nat` is reached by a pair either of whose sides is headed by a successor floor or a sum spine — the two shapes `Nat::decompose` and `Nat::summands` read — spelled once, in `curios-core`'s `peel_nat_terms`, which both congruences and the inverter call, so the two checkers remain incapable of disagreeing about it. A gate keyed on `(Intrinsic::Nat, Intrinsic::Nat)` would miss a *floorless* sum: `(s + l₁) + l₂` reduces to a bare `NatAdd` node rather than to a successor floor and would fall through to shape congruence, refusing against `s + (l₁ + l₂)`. **What a verdict claims is the same at every shape.** The cancellation, its multiset rule and its soundness argument are `Nat::cancel_common`'s, recorded below; the gate decides only *where* they fire, so what it admits is the same two conclusions at more shapes — reassociation joins commutation on the `Equal` side, the admitting direction. Soundness at the wider gate rests on the fact `peel_bin` and `peel_list` already rest on: conversion and inversion ask about pairs inhabiting one type, so a side carrying a floor or a sum spine makes both sides `Nat`s. The row it unblocks is `Bin`/`List` window fusion, whose fused bound is a reassociation of the one the second window already carried — so `spine`'s `push` hands that proof on instead of composing one, which is what let the four sequence accessors take a bound field at all.

**`Peel::Stuck` is reachable for `Nat`, and the grid holds the case.** A floorless pair sharing no summand comes back from `cancel_common` *identically* — the stability contract recorded below — and handing it to the caller as `Continue` would re-enter the same congruence on the same terms and never settle, where every `Continue` off a floored pair strips a shared floor and that decrease is the termination argument. So the residuals are compared against what went in and an unchanged pair declines, falling through to shape congruence exactly as `Bin`'s and `List`'s peels do. Declining is the refusing direction at all three readers, so it cannot admit anything; what it costs is only reductions. The `Nat` grid carries a case for it (`x + y ~ z`) beside the floorless shapes — reassociation and commutation on the `Equal` side, a floored side against a floorless one clashing on the surviving floor, and a floorless `Continue` — and its verdict tally counts `Stuck` with the rest, so a gate that stopped admitting the floorless shapes fails rather than passing vacuously.

**Summand cancellation, and what carries it.** `Nat::cancel_common` strips what two operands carry in common — the literal floor and the symbolic summands — on the law that `Nat` under `+` is a cancellative commutative monoid. Three readers share it: the comparison family, `Nat/sub`, and `peel_nat`, which is only its translation into `Peel`. Two conclusions are the ones to attack. Peeling and comparison decide a *commuted* sum **equal**, `x + y ~ y + x`, which is the admitting direction and reaches conversion as a definitional equality. And they decide a surviving positive floor against the identity a **clash**, `x + 2 ~ x + 1`, which reaches inversion as *impossible* and so excuses an omitted arm — the vacuous-elimination route recorded in [Coverage](coverage.md). Three properties carry both: that cancellation removes one occurrence per match rather than collapsing a repeated summand, that summands pair by syntactic equality so the incompleteness falls on the declining side, and that a pass cancelling nothing returns its operands *identically* — the last learned by losing it, since rebuilding re-associates a sum and the oscillation that produced overflowed the stack on the fixed prelude. `curios-core`'s `nat` and `spine` tests pin all three plus the two conclusions; the value grid above is the probe of the law itself. Summands are keyed up to universe instance, through `project_erased_universes`; why that is sound for a `Nat` and not in general is [The refinement key](../what-the-kernel-consults/the-refinement-key.md)'s.
