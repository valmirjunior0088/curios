# The binary32 model and its two canonicalizing sites

**Assumes.** `curios_num::Floating` computes IEEE 754-2019 binary32 with exactly one NaN, and the engine executing the emitted `f32.*` agrees with it — including at the two operations whose non-NaN result would otherwise read a NaN's bits, which the emitter canonicalizes

**Status.** **probed** by two differentials: the model against the host `f32` over every unary operation at all 2³² inputs and over the binary operations at the IEEE edge grid, a cancellation sweep and a seeded sample; and the fold against the emitted program over the operation family, both canonicalizing sites included. What stays **argued** is the closure claim — that those two operations are the *only* ones whose result can read a NaN's bits.

Every `Flt` operation folds at the type level by calling the model, so this row admits the way [Intrinsic fold laws](intrinsic-fold-laws-and-the-free-monoid-peel.md) admits: conversion reduces both sides before comparing, so a wrong fold is a false definitional equation, and congruence with substitution carries one to `False`. It carries that row's worst asymmetry too — the model lives in `curios-num` and `reduce_intrinsic` in `curios-core`, so both checkers run one function on one input and no disagreement between them is structurally possible. The second opinion here is not the other checker; it is the host, and then the engine.

**The model against the host.** The host's `f32` is the oracle and never the definition, which is the whole reason a disagreement is readable as a defect rather than as a difference of opinion. `curios-num`'s `floating::tests` runs `neg`, `abs`, `sqrt`, `floor`, `ceil`, `trunc` and `nearest` over **all 2³² inputs** — 2026-08-24, release, aarch64-apple-darwin, 12 threads: zero mismatches, 566.92 s wall — and the binary operations over the IEEE corner grid with every pair, an exponent-difference sweep from zero to past the significand's width where cancellation decides the answer, and a seeded sample. One divergence is stated rather than found: `copysign(x, nan)` differs, because the host reads the NaN's sign bit and the model has no sign to read. That case is excluded from the oracle and closed at the emitter, below.

**The fold against the engine.** `curios`' `tests::numeric::folded_and_executed_scalar_ops_agree_inside_the_envelope` compiles each expression twice — closed, so it folds, and with a runtime-zero perturbation, so it executes — and demands identical output. That is what holds `curios-core`'s fold, `curios-ersd`'s and `curios-cont`'s partial evaluators, and the emitted Wasm to one semantics; all three of the compile-time folders now call the model, so what the differential compares is the model against the engine.

**The two canonicalizing sites, and why a differential over them is easy to write vacuously.** Wasm leaves a computed NaN's sign and payload to the implementation, and exactly two emitted operations produce a non-NaN result from a NaN operand: `FltToLeBytes`, which reinterprets, and `FltCopysign`, which reads a sign bit. `into_wasm` closes both — the first selects `0x7fc00000` when the operand is a NaN, the second substitutes `+0.0` for a NaN sign operand, and `x != x` decides NaN at both without reading a bit.

A row built on a *computed* NaN does not test any of that. aarch64's default NaN is already `0x7fc00000`, so folded and executed agree whether or not the canonicalization is emitted — **measured 2026-08-24 by neutering both selects and watching the differential still pass.** The rows that stand assemble the NaN from bytes with a payload bit set and a runtime-tainted byte, because reinterpreting a chosen pattern is bit-preserving on every engine. Neutered, those fail: folded `:0:0:192:127` against executed `:1:0:192:127`, the payload surviving into a result the model says has none. The reproduction is recorded beside the rows.

**What is argued rather than probed** is that the list of two is closed. The argument is the one that closes it for the model: an operation either returns a NaN — in which case which NaN it returns is unobservable, since the only readers are these two — or it does not read NaN bits at all. Arithmetic, `min`/`max`, `neg` and `abs` propagate *some* NaN and are covered by the first case; `of_le_bytes` needs nothing at runtime because the pattern it builds is read only through the same two. A future intrinsic that reads NaN bits into a non-NaN result joins the list or is not admitted, and nothing checks that obligation mechanically.

**The narrowings out of `Flt` are this row's, the bounds are not.** `Flt/to_nat` and `Flt/to_int` answer the exact unbounded natural or integer on the domain `/syn/Flt/NonNeg` and `/syn/Flt/Finite` state, and outside it the fold declines and the operation stays stuck — a well-typed call cannot reach that, and reduction does not rely on being handed only well-typed terms. How the bound itself is discharged is [A bound is stated in a decided proposition](../../design/language/a-bound-is-stated-in-a-decided-proposition-and-discharged-by-reduction.md)'s, and that the proof reaches Core where the kernel re-checks it is [Intrinsic signatures](intrinsic-signatures.md)'.
