# The evaluation memo

**Assumes.** A remembered reduct is the kernel's own pure function of the definition store, and replaying one changes what a judgment spends but never what it decides

**Status.** **argued**, in the four invariants the component states for itself, and **probed** at the interlock between a live case equation and a remembered reduct and at the clears of the local-bearing tables. The reason it is not merely a performance note is that a wrong reduct admits by [the perimeter's shared route](../../design/language/the-soundness-perimeter.md). *Keys are valid by construction*: an unfold entry is keyed by a name whose body is closed, and `invalidate` is called whenever a name is overwritten, so validity never rests on the store being append-only; a whnf entry for a local-free term has a scope-independent key, and one for a local-bearing term — a function of the case equations in force as well — lives in tables cleared wherever that set changes, an equation assumed, retracted, withheld or restored, so that within one span it is as fixed as a closed term's is within a declaration; which table a term belongs to is decided inside the component rather than at the call sites that would have to remember it. *They are consulted at every reduction level, not only at the crate's boundary*: `whnf_within` probes on entry and stores on exit, so a scrutinee, an application's head and each turn of `force`'s loop are served by the same table, and what makes reaching further safe is that `Memos::storable` gates the lookup as well as the store. *The term-keyed tables live exactly as long as a budget does*, cleared wherever it is restored — which is also why they are stored against that budget alone and charge the compilation's retention allowance nothing; the name-keyed unfold table outlives a declaration and is the one that pays. *A store cannot charge*: `Memos` hands back a `Replay` and applying one to the budget and the entropy counter is `Spend`'s job, so nothing can both remember and charge twice.

**A term-keyed hit costs nothing, and that is part of the assumption.** A term-keyed hit spends no steps; only the name-keyed unfold hit is charged what its computation consumed. Minted identities are replayed either way, so the entropy trajectory is unchanged. What that gives up is exhaustion points: a free hit can only *reduce* what a judgment spends, so it can only turn an exhaustion refusal into an acceptance, and a semantic refusal is budget-independent and cannot move. Charging a hit what recomputation would cost prices work the kernel has not performed, and compounds: recorded costs that include other recorded costs make a structure the memos evaluate linearly exponential to charge. The second invariant is what keeps the surviving property deterministic, since a free hit on a table that outlived a declaration would make one declaration's spend depend on which declarations were walked before it. **Not covered:** an `unfold` record is measured over a computation that may itself have taken free term-keyed hits, so it can record less than the same body costs cold, and which declaration first unfolds a name decides what every later one is charged for it. The direction is undercharging, so it can only accept

**The load-bearing half of the first invariant is about intermediates, and it is argued where the interlock is probed.** `storable` asks whether the *term being stored* is local-free; what the argument needs is that reduction of a local-free term never *encounters* a local-bearing stuck form along the way, which is what makes it impossible for a memoized reduct to depend on an equation later retracted. That claim is stated in `Scope::refine`, one component over. What stands behind the pair is `kernel_memo_parity`, which runs the whole prelude with the memos on and off and requires identical semantic verdicts, and `Kernel::uncached`, which exists so that test can be written; both are whole-prelude, so what they establish is coverage by corpus, the same standard [An independent kernel re-checks what the elaborator accepts](../../design/language/an-independent-kernel-re-checks-what-the-elaborator-accepts.md) declines to accept for conversion. The claim carries weight at every reduction level, since `whnf_within` probes and stores at each of them — a scrutinee, an application's head, each turn of `force`'s loop — with the same `storable` test gating every one, on the lookup as well as the store; an argument held in another component and tested by whole-prelude parity is relied on at every one of those sites.

**The local-bearing tables are probed from both sides.** `a_remembered_reduct_does_not_outlive_the_equations_it_was_taken_under` reduces an open term before an arm, inside it and after it — stuck, `1`, stuck — and agrees with `Kernel::uncached` on all three; dropping the clear at `Kernel::refine` serves the inside reduction from the entry the outside one stored, and dropping the clear at the retract serves the outside one from the arm's, which the fixture below sees. What neither fixture reaches is the settlement bracket, where equations are withheld and restored around one reduction; the clears there rest on the same argument and on `kernel_memo_parity` over the prelude.

**The interlock is probed, and the result was null.** `kernel::whnf::tests::a_case_equation_reaches_the_reduct_and_not_the_memos` reduces two terms reduced inside an arm refining a binder, then again after it retracts. The open one is what the equation answers — `1` inside the arm, the stuck successor outside — and the retraction assertion is what a leaked entry would fail; the closed one crosses the *other* gate, since `machine_admissible` declines the closed machine while any equation is live, so its inside reduct comes from the recursive strategy and the outside call is served by the entry that strategy stored, where the machine would otherwise have run. Both routes reach what a kernel that never entered the arm reaches, and the whole sequence agrees with `Kernel::uncached`. Mutation-checked, and the mutation is the exact one the argument turns on: dropping the clear of the local-bearing tables at the arm's retract makes the arm's answer for the open term outlive the arm, and this fixture is the only one that sees it — the two module fixtures that also move under it are resource assertions.

**What the fixture does not establish** is the general claim, which is about intermediates rather than about the two sets being disjoint at one live equation. It shows the boundary holding where an equation is assumed and a memoizable term is reduced beside it; it does not show that no reduction of a local-free term can reach a local-bearing stuck form, which is a property of every reduction rule rather than of a fixture. The rules were read once against it — substitution, delta into a closed body, iota binding a closed payload, and `step_func`'s eta probe, whose fresh binders are minted unique and so match no recorded key — and a global with no body (`Definition::value` is `None` for a `foreign` declaration or a deliberately opaque name) is the one local-free term that can stick, which `Scope::refine` then declines to record. Declining to record is the safe direction. That reading is an audit, not a probe, and it is what a rule taught to reduce under a binder would invalidate in silence
