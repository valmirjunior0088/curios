# Case equations inside an arm

**Assumes.** An arm's scrutinee equation is definitionally true within that arm and reaches nothing outside it

**Status.** **argued** at the equation's justification, **probed** at both consultation points. The equation is assumed by the elimination rule that justified it — within an arm the scrutinee, already in weak-head normal form, *is* the case's value — and `whnf` consults it at every stuck head it reaches, and now also on each term *before* taking that term apart. That makes it the second thing besides the definition store from which the reducer's answer can come, and an equation escaping its arm is a false definitional equation with the same consequences as a wrong fold. The probes are `kernel::whnf::tests`'s `a_case_equation_answers_a_term_the_budget_cannot_reduce` and `the_two_consultation_points_answer_one_equation_alike`, one per point: the first answers an equation keyed as written under a budget its own control proves cannot afford the fold, and the second reaches an equation keyed on a spelling that exists only as a reduct, holding the idempotence argument — a stored key re-reduces to itself — in executable form on the way. Each was mutation-checked against the removal of exactly one consultation point and failed under its own while passing under the other's, and no pre-existing fixture moved under either mutation, which is the coverage gap the pair was written to close.

**The earlier of those two consultation points is new, and it is an ordering change rather than a new rule.** A stored key is what `reduce` returned for the scrutinee, so it is already a weak-head normal form; a term matching it is therefore that same normal form, and reducing before asking reaches the later probe with the identical term. What the early ask removes is only the requirement that the reducer be able to *afford* that reduction before the equation applies — which it could not for a guard whose operand is an accumulation, where the intrinsic folded first and evaluated the very thing the guard exists to avoid evaluating; the budget fixture above is that scenario made executable. One case sits outside the idempotence argument and is admitted deliberately: a key stored on the error path is the unreduced scrutinee rather than a normal form, and there the term *equals* the key, so the arm's own hypothesis is what fires — the same fixture stores its key that way. This entry used to admit a second case beside it, a match up to universe instances pairing spellings whose reducts differ. That case no longer exists: `Scope::refinement_of` probes by `Term`'s structural equality, levels included, ever since [the refinement key](the-refinement-key.md) was rekeyed to the scrutinee itself, so the clause was this entry still describing the projection that entry records as closed — and the comment above the early ask in `whnf_within` carried the same stale claim, in the direction a stale account admits, until it was corrected with this entry

**Scoping is structural rather than disciplined, which is the strongest thing this entry has.** The local telescope and the equation stack are one component because they retract as one: a `Mark` is a checkpoint into both, `Scope::retract` truncates both, and `Kernel::scoped` runs a walk with everything it opened closed again afterwards on the failing path as well as the succeeding one. Splitting them would be two checkpoints that must agree, which is a thing to get wrong rather than a thing to state.

**Its interlock with the memo is the part written in only one place.** A local-free scrutinee is *skipped* rather than recorded, on the ground that local-free terms reduce to their case values instead of sticking — and that skip is simultaneously what keeps the evaluation memo sound, since the memo stores local-free terms only and reduction of one never meets a local-bearing stuck form. One sentence in `Scope::refine` therefore carries the soundness of two components, and neither entry's evidence reaches it
