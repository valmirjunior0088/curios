# Case equations inside an arm

**Assumes.** An arm's scrutinee equation is definitionally true within that arm and reaches nothing outside it

**Status.** **argued** at the equation's justification, **probed** at both consultation points and at each half of the two-tier key. The equation is assumed by the elimination rule that justified it — within an arm the scrutinee *is* the case's value — and `whnf` consults it at every stuck head it reaches, and also on each term *before* taking that term apart. That makes it the second thing besides the definition store from which the reducer's answer can come, and an equation escaping its arm is a false definitional equation with the same consequences as a wrong fold. The probes are `kernel::whnf::tests`'s `a_case_equation_answers_a_term_the_budget_cannot_reduce` and `the_two_consultation_points_answer_one_equation_alike`, one per point: the first answers an equation keyed as written under a budget its own control proves cannot afford the fold, and the second reaches an equation keyed on a spelling that exists only as a reduct. Each was mutation-checked against the removal of exactly one consultation point and failed under its own while passing under the other's, and no pre-existing fixture moved under either mutation, which is the coverage gap the pair was written to close. Four more cover the key itself and are named below.

**The consultation before a term is taken apart is an ordering change rather than a new rule.** What the early ask removes is only the requirement that the reducer be able to *afford* a reduction before the equation applies — which it cannot for a guard whose operand is an accumulation, where the intrinsic would fold first and evaluate the very thing the guard exists to avoid evaluating; the budget fixture above is that scenario made executable. `Scope::refinement_of` probes by `Term`'s structural equality, levels included, since [the refinement key](the-refinement-key.md) is the scrutinee itself.

**An equation is recorded under two spellings, and both satisfy the rule for the same reason.** The key is the scrutinee **as written**, with the reduct computed at most once and only when a probe has already missed. What licenses a spelling to be a key is that it identifies terms already definitionally equal, and the written form has the strongest claim there is — it *is* the scrutinee — while the reduced form is what the kernel's own reduction says that scrutinee computes to. The two probe points ask about two spellings rather than one spelling twice, so no idempotence of a stored key is needed

**Computing a reduct later has to reconstruct the view it would have had earlier, and that is the one obligation the deferral creates.** At registration the equations inside the arm did not exist and the stack below was already frozen, so the reduct rested only on equations that outlive the entry holding it. A settlement therefore withholds the equation being settled *and every equation inside it* for the duration of its reduction: without the first half the reduction meets its own equation and settles the reduct to the case value it was assuming; without the second half the reduct rests on an inner equation that retracts first. `Scope::hide_refinements_from` is where that is enforced and `whnf::tests`'s `a_case_equation_answers_a_spelling_only_reduction_reaches` and `settling_a_reduced_spelling_withholds_the_equations_inside_it` are the two fixtures, the second mutation-checked against a relaxation that moves it and nothing else. The same limit is read by the selection of what to settle next, which is what keeps the loop finite

**Scoping is structural rather than disciplined, which is the strongest thing this entry has.** The local telescope and the equation stack are one component because they retract as one: a `Mark` is a checkpoint into both, `Scope::retract` truncates both, and `Kernel::scoped` runs a walk with everything it opened closed again afterwards on the failing path as well as the succeeding one. Splitting them would be two checkpoints that must agree, which is a thing to get wrong rather than a thing to state.

**Its interlock with the memo is written in two places, and the second is the probe rather than the store.** A local-free scrutinee is *skipped* rather than recorded, on the ground that local-free terms reduce to their case values instead of sticking — and that skip is simultaneously what keeps the evaluation memo sound, since the memo stores local-free terms only and reduction of one never meets a local-bearing stuck form. That gate covers the written spelling, which is what `Scope::refine` is handed. It cannot cover the *reduced* spelling, which is whatever reduction returned and may perfectly well be local-free; what holds the line there is `refined_reduct`'s refusal to probe a term with no local free at all. `whnf::tests`'s `a_local_free_term_is_never_refined` is the fixture, mutation-checked, and it asserts both sides of the arm for the reason the neighbouring interlock fixture does: the inside reduct is what a leak corrupts, and the outside one is what a leaked memo entry hands back afterwards

**The filter that decides whether to settle at all is a cost mechanism and not a rule, and it fails in the direction that refuses.** `Scope::could_reduce_to` settles an equation's reduced spelling only for a candidate whose free locals are a subset of the key's — necessary because reduction substitutes closed definition bodies and subterms of the term itself, so it can drop a local and never introduce one. Relaxing it admits more settlements and changes no verdict; tightening it drops refinements silently, which is why `a_reduct_that_drops_a_local_is_still_reached` guards that direction and why the subset is not an equality. Without it the deferral buys nothing: a probe under freshly opened binders presents a stuck form on almost every reduction, so some term triggers a settlement in any arm whatever
