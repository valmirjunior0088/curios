# Design

This document owns Curios design decisions: what was decided, why, and what was rejected. An entry is amended only when its decision is superseded. What the language *is* belongs to [SYNTAX.md](SYNTAX.md), what exists or is pending belongs to [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md), local architecture belongs to crate and module rustdoc, and contributor rules belong to [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) — link there, do not restate here.

## Objectives

Curios is a small, fully dependent language: types depend on values, proofs live beside ordinary code, and one pure pipeline compiles everything to WebAssembly-GC, serving a native product and a browser product from the same backend. The long-term objective is a self-hosting compiler — every language-specific stage from source text to raw Wasm bytes written in Curios — running on the retained Rust host.

Non-goals: Curios is not a foundational proof assistant — it claims consistency (see "Totality of the erased program") and it is neither strongly normalizing nor canonical; it does not target multiple backends; it does not pursue self-hosting below the language-specific stages.

An *independent* checker is no longer among those non-goals and is under construction; see "An independent kernel re-checks what the elaborator accepts". A *small* one still is. The term language has native inductive families, structures, an implicit universe hierarchy, and a primitive roster with folds, and any checker for it is thousands of lines rather than hundreds — a consequence of choosing a language that is pleasant to write proofs in over one that is cheap to verify.

## The language

### Implicit cumulative universes, general recursion

**Decision.** Surface `Type` remains nullary, but Core assigns every occurrence an inferred level in an infinite cumulative hierarchy: `Type u : Type (u + 1)`. Reusable declarations generalize their inferred universe inputs and instantiate them freshly at external uses; recursive calls remain monomorphic within their shared group instance. `rec` still provides unrestricted general recursion, uniformly spelled across the type and value layers; totality is required only where erasure deletes the result, as "Totality of the erased program" sets out.

**Rationale.** Implicit levels remove the inconsistency specific to `Type : Type` without burdening programs with universe syntax or changing runtime code. Curios remains a programming language first: `rec` is the honest marker of unrestricted general recursion, and type-level computation is bounded operationally by the reduction deadline rather than by a termination proof.

**Rejected.** Retaining `Type : Type`; exposing universe variables or level expressions in surface syntax; specializing runtime code by universe instance; and gating the whole language on termination checking. Soundness did not arrive as a second language of accepted-versus-rejected recursion imposed on all code, but it did not arrive as an opt-in subset either: the obligation is keyed to *position* rather than to a mode or an annotation, so a definition is checked for totality exactly when something erases it. General recursion needs no opt-out because it was never restricted where it is used.

**Generated declarations.** Lowering records whether a definition is authored or generated and records a generated definition's owner explicitly; flattened path spelling is never used to infer ownership. Registry entries and their actual type-former definitions share one finalized context. Generated constructor and concept-method wrappers independently generalize their own schemes because they may use only a subset of, or additional independently instantiated dependencies beyond, the owner's scheme; their explicit owner metadata validates provenance without conflating the wrapper's binders with the nominal registry's binders.

**Inexpressible paradox.** Every declaration generalizes over the levels it infers, so each occurrence of a name instantiates at fresh ones, and the surface language offers no universe variables, level expressions, or explicit universe arguments with which to force two occurrences to share a level. A program therefore cannot write the self-application Girard's paradox needs: a self-reference such as `Box/wrap(Box)` stratifies into two distinct levels and is admitted, which is stratification working rather than a hole. This is the accepted outcome, not a gap to close — the hierarchy exists to make the paradox underivable, and being unable to spell it at all is the strongest form of that. It does leave open whether any surface program can reach `UniverseInconsistency`; the check guards Core construction either way, and no end-to-end rejection test is claimed, because one would pin behavior that holds for the wrong reason.

### Strict Prop under Type

**Decision.** Alongside the cumulative `Type` hierarchy, Curios has a strict `Prop` with definitional proof irrelevance. `Prop : Type 0` holds, proposition types are admitted at `Type` through the existing subsumption and cumulativity, and large elimination out of `Prop` is guarded.

**Rationale.** Proof irrelevance is what makes proofs erasable by construction: any two proofs of a proposition are definitionally equal, so no program can depend on which proof it received, and erasure drops them wholesale. The large-elimination guard is what keeps that erasure sound.

### Strict positivity, modulo polarity

**Decision.** Every `induct` and `struct` declaration must be strictly positive. The check computes a per-parameter polarity vector for each declaration over a five-point lattice (`Unused ⊑ Strict ⊑ Pos ⊑ Mixed`, `Unused ⊑ Neg ⊑ Mixed`, with `Strict` and `Neg` incomparable), builds an occurrence relation recording at what polarity each declaration appears inside each other, closes it transitively under composition, and accepts exactly when no declaration reaches itself through a non-strict path. The vectors persist on the registry entries and ride the prelude archive; the relation is transient.

**Rationale.** An `induct` declaration claims its functor has an initial algebra, and the eliminator it hands back is the statement that the algebra is initial. Strict positivity is the syntactic sufficient condition for that functor being polynomial, hence accessible, hence for the algebra existing — and it is also the construction that produces the eliminator with its computation rule. Without it, `induct Bad | c(f : (Bad) -> False) end` inhabits `False` in four lines with no recursion at all.

The "modulo polarity" qualifier is load-bearing on day one, not a later refinement. Two thirds of the standard library's parameterized declarations are not covariant — the concepts genuinely are not, since `Show(A)` carries `(A) -> Str` and `Add(A)` carries `(A, A) -> A` — while `/std/Toml` recurses `Toml → Map(Toml) → Option(Node(V)) → Node` and `/std/Json` recurses through `Lst` and an anonymous tuple. A check that only recognizes recursive occurrences in immediate payload positions rejects `Toml`, `Json`, and `Map` outright. Polarity does not weaken the rule: every functor it admits was polynomial already. It only lets the checker recognize that through abstraction.

Loosening acceptance to admit merely-positive occurrences is closed by Cantor together with the impredicativity of `Prop`: with both present the Coquand–Paulin construction applies. That is recorded as a test rather than as prose.

**Rejected.** Running the check during elaboration, which would have to decide what an unsolved metavariable might become; it runs post-zonk instead, beside `validate_universes`, where telescopes are final and meta-free. Treating `Mixed` as a stop rather than a result, which would let a recursive occurrence hidden inside a stuck `match` arm pass unseen. A provenance tag on `Mixed` for diagnostics, which would cost `Polarity` its derived equality to say what the reported site type already says. Any surface syntax for polarity: the property is computed and the obligation is inferred, so an Agda-style `@++` modality is never spelled in Curios source.

**Indices are not parameters, and the two index positions are not each other.** An inductive is not uniform in its indices, so the index *arguments* at an occurrence site are walked opaquely, at `Mixed`. A declaration's own index *binder types* are not walked at all: they describe the family's arity rather than its carrier, existing to typecheck the index arguments a constructor targets and storing nothing, so what a declaration contains is witnessed entirely by its constructor payloads. `Eq(@A : Type) : (x : A, y : A)` is therefore `Strict` in `A` — from `refl(@z : A)`'s payload alone.

Treating both positions alike was the first implementation, and it cost precision for nothing: `Eq` came out `Mixed`, which rejected the sound `induct Wit | base() | tied(a : Wit, b : Wit, p : Eq(a, b)) end`. The guard it appeared to provide is unnecessary, because a declaration cannot reach itself through its own index binder type. `induct Foo : (x : Foo) -> Type` does not elaborate: `x : Foo` requires `Foo` to already be a type, and `Foo` is a family until applied to the very index being declared. The elaborator rejects it on kinding grounds before positivity runs.

**Deferred.** A declaration taking a *type-former* parameter — `induct Mu(F : (Type) -> Type) | fix(F(Mu(F))) end` — cannot be checked from its own body, because `F` is a binder with no known polarity. The missing concept is an inferred per-binder obligation: recorded on the declaration, generalized onto any enclosing signature binding the parameter, instantiated freshly at each external occurrence, and discharged against the argument's own vector — the architecture `UniverseContext` already implements over a simpler domain. It must live in a side store keyed by declaration and binder, never as a field on `FuncType`: unlike a universe level a polarity never appears inside a term, and keeping it out of the node is what stops `compare_func_type` from acquiring a polarity comparison. Nothing in the corpus takes a type-former parameter, so this is co-scheduled with `Mu`.

### Erased positions are non-strict

**Decision.** An expression in an erased value position — an argument to an erased parameter, an erased structure or constructor field, an operand slot instantiated at a proposition or a type — is not evaluated: its effects, traps, and divergence do not occur. Statement positions evaluate under call-by-value regardless of their result's sort: a top-level item, a local `let` binding, and a direct call of a never-returning function all run even though their results erase. Type-level positions are guarded rather than non-strict: forcing an effectful primitive — a host call, `IoExit`, or a `Cell` operation — during type-level reduction is a compile error (`EffectAtTypeLevel`); an effectful subterm a type never forces is erased with the type.

**Rationale.** Not evaluating erased value positions is what erasure is for: proofs are certificates whose computation must cost nothing at runtime, which is what lets the `/std` lemma corpus decorate data structures without runtime traversals. Host effects make the gap observable, because an effectful never-returning expression can inhabit a proposition, and no type-level rule can separate it from pure proof content. The boundary is therefore drawn by position rather than by type: what the program sequences as a statement runs; what erasure removes does not; what type checking would have to perform itself is refused outright.

**Rejected.** A transitive host-effect analysis that evaluates an erased position when its expression may reach a host operation: real machinery that still cannot preserve divergence, so it buys only a partial strictness guarantee. Retyping never-returning operations at an **empty** `Type`-sorted carrier was also rejected here, because it narrows the observable gap without closing it — an elimination immediately launders the effect back into an erased position, and an empty carrier is precisely what an elimination into a proposition passes through unguarded.

That rejection stands, and the carrier it rejected is gone: `/std/proc/exit` is now `(Nat) -> {}`. Retyping at an **inhabited** carrier is a different fix and it is the one that landed, because there is nothing to eliminate and so nothing to launder — see "Totality of the erased program" below. (V) still rejects an exit reachable from a `Prop`-sorted term, now for the operational reason alone: the call sits where erasure deletes, so it would not fire where the author wrote it.

### Totality of the erased program

**Decision.** Everything erasure deletes must be total; what it retains may diverge. Erasure deletes types and it deletes `Prop`-sorted proofs, so two obligations enforce that over one partiality relation on zonked Core, seeded twice: **(T)** every definition reachable from a term in a type position must be total, and **(V)** every definition reachable from a term at a `Prop`-sorted type must be total. Totality is decided per `rec` group by size-change termination. A group that fails is classified `Partial` rather than rejected, and remains usable in programs: `rec` and `/std/proc/exit` keep their unrestricted meaning in every position erasure retains.

**`exit` returns unit, and that is a typing rule rather than an obligation.** A term that never returns is unsound exactly when it inhabits a type nothing total inhabits — the forgery is the problem, not the non-return — so `Prim::Exit` is typed at `{}`, which `()` already inhabits. Nothing is left to forge and the rule carries no side condition.

Restricting *which* type `exit` may be given cannot achieve this, and the reason is worth recording because two weaker designs were tried against it. Refusing a `Prop`-sorted result leaves `Exit(Empty, 0)` at a constructor-free `Empty : Type`, and a `Type`-sorted empty inductive eliminates into `Prop` unguarded — `guard_large_elimination` returns at its first check, and zero constructors leave no arms to verify. Making `Never` a core bottom type with `Never ≤ A` in subsumption relocates the same permissiveness into the relation every check goes through, and the restricted clause admits `Never ≤ Empty` for the same reason. Only removing the choice works.

This is also the shape every other host operation already had: `Prim::Foreign` reads its result off an ABI `WireSignature`, so it is structurally confined to inhabited types. `Exit` was the sole exception, and it no longer is.

**`/std/Never` is removed.** It existed only as `/syn/False`'s `Type`-sorted twin, and that twin existed only so `exit` had a `Type`-sorted carrier erasure would retain. With `exit` at `{}` nothing can inhabit `Never`, `Never/absurd` had no callers, and the module's entire rationale was a mechanism that no longer exists. What is given up is `Never/absurd(@A, proc/exit(1))` — passing an exit off as a value of arbitrary type, so a branch that cannot produce one need not. The replacement idiom is `let _ = proc/exit(1); …`, which is marginally worse and more honest: the type system stops claiming a branch produced a value it never produced. A user who wants an empty `Type`-sorted type can declare one, and it stays harmless, because inhabiting it is what the route needed.

Totality still has both clauses for `exit`, on operational rather than soundness grounds. (T) is unchanged: a `Nat` defined through an exit does not terminate, so a type mentioning it does not form. (V) keeps it because an exit reachable from a `Prop`-sorted term sits where erasure deletes, so the process would not exit where the author wrote that it should — a wrong program rather than a false proof.

**Both obligations read the elaborator's record of what each term settled at; (T) additionally walks.** The elaborator records every term with the type it settled at. (V) keeps the `Prop`-sorted ones and needs nothing else: a term is a proof exactly when its type is a proposition, which is a typing question elaboration has already decided for every term in the program, and which a later walk can only re-derive incompletely. (T) keeps the ones that settled *at a sort* — a term whose own type is `Type` or `Prop` is a type wherever it appears — and unions that with a syntactic walk over written type positions.

**(T) needs both halves, because neither contains the other.** The walk seeds the body of every definition whose declared type ends in a sort, which is how it reaches through `/std/BigNat/Canonical` into `is_trimmed`, a dependency no annotation records and no typing judgment classifies as a type position. The settle records catch a type passed as an *argument*, which the walk cannot find — it has no case for an application's parameters, and giving it one would mean re-deriving the head's telescope, the re-derivation that cost (V) six defects. Counting each seeding is what obscured this: the records are fewer than the walk's positions, which says nothing about containment. `ignore(@Shape(inf), 5)` — a total type-level function applied to a partial value, in argument position — was accepted until the records were added, while the same type in an annotation was not.

Coverage for both is therefore a consequence of elaboration being a typechecker rather than a property anyone maintains: a position it never settles is a position the program was never typed at.

**The claim, and what it is worth today.** The intended claim is that there is no closed term of type `/syn/False`. What the compiler *enforces* is narrower and should be read as the actual statement: nothing in a type position and nothing at a `Prop`-sorted type is partial, and every rule in "The soundness perimeter" below holds. Those coincide only if the perimeter is complete and each of its entries is correct, which is not established — three of its entries cannot be probed at all, and one of them, definitional proof irrelevance, is both load-bearing and unargued. The trusted base is the elaborator and its whole kernel together with the Rust implementation of all of it. An independent kernel is being built in `curios-core` and does not yet re-check anything, so it subtracts nothing from that base today; what it is intended to be worth, and what it cannot be worth, is set out in "An independent kernel re-checks what the elaborator accepts". Curios is not a foundational proof assistant and does not claim to be one.

The honest summary is that three of the four known routes to a closed inhabitant of `False` are closed — two by regression tests and one, the exit behind an erased proof, by having become *inexpressible* rather than rejected — that every perimeter entry reachable from source has been probed once, and that **one route is open**: the large-elimination guard's singleton side condition, recorded under the perimeter below. That is a much weaker statement than consistency and is deliberately written as one.

The exit route's fixture was deleted rather than retargeted, and that is the intended reading: `match proc/exit(1) : (_) => /std/False end` is now refused during elaboration as a match on a non-inductive type, so there is no longer a totality verdict to assert. A route closed by a typing rule needs no regression test for the obligation that used to catch it.

**Rationale.** A divergent type breaks type formation and a divergent proof proves anything, but a program that loops is a program rather than an unsoundness. Drawing the line at erasure rather than at the whole language is what keeps general recursion honest and unrestricted where it is actually used, while removing it from exactly the positions where it is a logical hole. The prelude pays almost nothing for this. Of its 1063 definitions, 169 classify partial — `/std/Toml`, `/std/Async`, `/std/tcp`, `/std/Json`, `/std/Flt`, and the rest of the productive-but-not-descending corpus — and **not one of them is reachable from a type or from a proof**. `/syn`, the module tree that carries the certificates, has no partial definition at all. The single source change the obligations forced was a descending `fuel` on `/std/Fmt`'s three parsers, which sit in a type position because `format_type_with(Str, parse(s))` is the result type of `render` and `print`.

Three independent routes to a closed inhabitant of `False` motivated it, and closing any one closes neither other. A partial `rec` at a `Type`-sorted carrier releases a `Prop` through a projection or a `match` arm binder, so the escaping certificate need not mention the partial definition syntactically. A `Prim::Exit` behind a nullary `Prop`-valued function is dropped by erasure, so the exit never fires and the program continues with a forged invariant. And a type-level `rec` ties the negative knot positivity exists to forbid — `check_positivity` walks `induct` and `struct` registry entries, and a `rec` is neither, so `rec Bad : Type = Sink(Bad)` is never analyzed. The third route is why (V) alone is insufficient: its exploit contains no `rec` and no `exit`, only a lambda, a constructor application, and one application.

**(T) is the aggressive reading.** Everything reachable from a type must be total, not merely the values that reach a type-level eliminator. The narrow reading is unsound, and not marginally: a *total* type-level function applied to a *partial value* reties the knot, with `rec Shape(f : F) -> Type` structurally recursive and `rec inf : F = F/more(inf)` a partial value of ordinary data type. Exempting the index positions of nominal declarations leaks the same way through `induct Trap(f : F) | mk(x : Shape(f)) end`, where the dangerous type arises only by instantiation and is written nowhere a syntactic walk could see it. Closing that precisely needs a per-parameter, interprocedural "scrutinized at the type level" summary computed as a fixpoint over the call graph — a second positivity analysis — and it would buy nothing, because (V) independently rejects every nontrivial way to state a proposition about a partial program. Aggressive rejects strictly more, so refining it later breaks nothing already accepted.

**Consistency, not canonicity.** Partial `rec` survives in retained positions, so a closed `s : /syn/Str` may be bottom and projecting `s.bytes` may diverge. The certificate idiom is preserved anyway, because the `valid` field is `Prop`-sorted: (V) forces every constructed `Str` to carry a total proof, so divergence is observed as divergence rather than as a valid-looking wrong answer. What the claim rules out is a *value* that lies, not a computation that fails to produce one.

**The obligation is enforced twice, at two different times, and both are needed.** The whole-module gate runs post-zonk — *after* elaboration has performed the type-level reduction (T) exists to make safe. That ordering is unavoidable for a whole-module pass, and it leaves a window: a type that reaches a partial definition and happens to be **productive** survives elaboration and the gate rejects it, while one that is **not** productive spins until the step budget dies. The program is refused either way, but in the second case it is refused for apparently exhausting a resource that no amount of would have helped, naming neither the definition at fault nor the reason.

So each definition's verdict is recorded as it is defined — items arrive in dependency order, so everything a definition mentions is already classified and no fixpoint is needed — and a *written* type that mentions a definition already known partial is refused before it is elaborated. Both shapes then report the same thing. This subsumes the earlier local form at `rec` elaboration, which existed for the same reason in the narrower case where the partial definition is the one being defined. The post-zonk gate still runs and still sees everything elaboration produces; the early net is not a replacement, because it reads written mentions rather than elaborated ones.

**Both obligations were superlinear, and the cause was not in them.** (V)'s local-partiality walk cost 2002ms of a 2148ms compile on a 640-byte string literal, and (T)'s written-position walk cost 1813ms of 3482ms at 12KiB; both are now iterative and memoized on node identity, at 0.89ms and 13.4ms. Neither was a defect of the obligations as specified. A string literal's UTF-8 derivation threaded its scanner state symbolically, so link `i` carried a term of depth `i` — `O(n)` distinct nodes but `O(n²)` paths through them — and any walk that neither deduplicated nor iterated paid the square, while any walk that recursed per node ran out of stack. Five defects traced to that one shape, the two obligations among them, and `/syn/Str` now proves a literal valid by computation rather than by derivation. The lesson is narrower than "walks are dangerous": these gates were measured for *coverage* and never for *cost*, and cost is where a representation's shape shows up.

**Decidability is machine-dependent, by the same choice reduction already made.** Conversion is bounded by the reduction deadline rather than by a termination proof, so acceptance depends on how much reduction a run affords. That is deliberate and predates this work; totality does not change it. The descent analysis reads a term's shape syntactically and falls back to a small bounded number of weak-head steps when it cannot, which is what lets it see through a cons-former written as an ordinary `let` — `/std/Bits/cons` is one, and `raw_trimmed`'s descent is invisible without it. That fallback and (V)'s sort decision are the two places totality contacts the budget — the latter asked once per *distinct* recorded type at the gate rather than once per term, which hash-consing keeps far below the term count.

**Definitional K is permanent.** `Eq : Prop` plus definitional proof irrelevance makes the system definitionally K, which is what licenses the deletion rule in index inversion (`curios-core/src/invert.rs`, shared by both checkers) and what makes proof erasure sound by construction. Because inversion is shared rather than duplicated, that deletion rule — an *acceptance*, unlike the unifier's refusals — now sits inside the trusted base while its premise stays graded *auditable only* in the perimeter below. Relocating it re-grades nothing; it changes who holds the grade. It also rules out univalence forever: under K all proofs of an identity are definitionally equal, so a univalent equality carrying nontrivial content cannot be added later. Curios takes that trade knowingly — proofs are certificates to be erased, not data to be computed with.

**Rejected.** Structural recursion on a single argument, which rejects `add/raw` (descending on either of two `Bits` depending on the arm), `raw_assoc` (the same over three), and `raw_comm` (which needs the mutual closure); a fold cannot express them either, because a fold cannot short-circuit. Rejecting partial definitions outright rather than classifying them, which would delete `/std/Async`, `/std/Json/decode`, and every productive-but-not-descending program for a soundness property none of them threaten. New surface syntax for the checker to lean on — an induction hypothesis on `Cases::Induct` would convert about five of the forty-eight must-pass proofs and helps none of the ones that matter. And making descent structural rather than size-change *without* match-refinement expansion, which is not an optimization but a correctness requirement: without it `add/raw`'s two nil-arm matrices compose to an idempotent matrix with no descending diagonal entry, and the group is rejected on a call path that is infeasible.

### The soundness perimeter

**Decision.** The claim above quantifies over *every* rule that can admit a term, so it is stated against an enumerated perimeter rather than against prose. Each entry names what it assumes and how far it has been checked, and the vocabulary is deliberate: **probed** means an adversarial program was written and the compiler rejected it; **argued** means a written justification exists; **auditable only** means no surface program can exercise the rule, so reading the implementation is the only available evidence. An entry may be probed and unargued, or argued and unprobed; they are independent.

The perimeter has two halves, and they differ in how coverage is obtained. The **whole-module passes** run once, post-zonk, from `finalize_and_check` in `curios-elab/src/elaborate/module.rs` — the single site every entry point that produces an elaborated module comes through, which is what keeps a check from reaching one configuration and not the other. The **per-term rules** run during elaboration, so their coverage follows from the elaborator visiting every term rather than from a pass enumerating positions.

| Whole-module pass | Assumes | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `zonk_module` | No unsolved metavariable survives, so nothing downstream can later be solved to a partial or negatively-occurring term | auditable only |
| `validate_universes` (inside `zonk_module`) | The accumulated level constraints are satisfiable | auditable only |
| `check_positivity` | Every `induct`/`struct` declaration is strictly positive modulo polarity | **probed** |
| `record_totality` + (T) | Nothing reachable from a type position is partial | **probed** (four routes) |
| (V) | Nothing at a `Prop`-sorted type is partial | **probed** (two routes) |

| Per-term rule | Assumes | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coverage | Every elimination enumerates its constructors | **probed** |
| Large-elimination guard | A proposition eliminates into a relevant result only when empty or singleton | **probed**, both directions |
| `Prop` non-informativeness | A `Prop`-sorted structure or concept carries only non-informative fields | **probed** |
| Witness coherence and the orphan rule | One witness per key, declarable only by a root owning the concept or a rigid head | **probed** |
| Foreign wire contract | A `foreign` declaration's type is drawn from the wire grammar, so an embedder cannot supply an inhabitant of a proposition | **probed** |
| Definitional proof irrelevance | Any two inhabitants of a proposition are interchangeable | **argued** (fixpoint on (V), below); not directly observable, but its side condition — the guard above — is probed |
| Index inversion and K | `Eq : Prop` licenses deleting a redundant constraint | auditable only |
| Conversion recurrence | Absence of finite disagreement is sufficient for equality | **argued** (fixpoint on (T), below); auditable only otherwise |

**Three entries can never be attacked, only read.** The universe hierarchy has no surface syntax for levels — they are implicit and inferred — so no program can be written that forces a violation. Index inversion and the conversion recurrence rule need constructed internal state that no source text reaches. These will never accumulate the kind of evidence positivity and the elimination guard have, and that asymmetry is a permanent property of the claim rather than a gap awaiting work.

**Two entries are load-bearing and unargued, and their dependency on (V) is a fixpoint rather than a circle.** Definitional proof irrelevance accepts without inspecting either term, which is correct precisely because every `Prop` inhabitant is total — that is (V)'s job. The conversion recurrence rule accepts on the absence of finite disagreement, and aggressive (T) is what removes its exposure to bottom-typed terms. Both therefore depend on the obligations, and elaboration uses both *while* the obligations are still unchecked.

**The dependency does not close, so it is a fixpoint rather than a circle.** `convert` consults `Sort::of`, which reaches only `reduce` and `synth_neutral`, and neither reaches `convert`. (V)'s own pipeline — record at settle, zonk, decide sort-hood, classify by size-change, close over reached definitions — never invokes conversion at any point, and neither does `zonk`. So elaboration may use both rules optimistically, and *acceptance validates them after the fact*: a program is accepted only if (V) passes, and if (V) passes then no `Prop`-typed term in it is partial, so every irrelevance step taken while checking it was applied to total terms. Rejected programs are rejected; accepted ones satisfy the precondition their acceptance relied on. The recurrence rule stands the same way on aggressive (T), which is what removes its exposure to bottom-typed terms in the positions it compares.

**The premise that argument needs is that (V) inspects every `Prop`-typed term in the accepted module.** Seeding at settle gives that for everything `elaborate` builds, since every node of its output settled with its type. The one route around it is a metavariable *solution*, which reaches the module through unification or witness resolution rather than through settling. That route is exercised and closed: a partial proof supplied as the witness of a `Prop`-sorted concept — arriving entirely by resolution, written nowhere as an argument — is rejected by (V), and `tests::soundness::a_partial_proof_cannot_arrive_through_witness_resolution` is the regression. Measurement agrees: across the corpus no solved metavariable sits at a `Prop`-sorted type at all, and in a program constructed to force one, its solution was among the recorded terms.

**What is still missing is the model, not the reasoning.** The fixpoint above is an argument about the implementation's dependency structure and about coverage; it is not a proof that definitional proof irrelevance is sound, which needs a model in which `Prop` is a genuine subsingleton and only total terms are interpreted. That is the first properly metatheoretic work this project would undertake, and it is what an observational-equality layer would rest on directly — but it is not required to state, or to check, the fixpoint.

**The erasure premise is a containment, not an equality, and stating it as an equality was wrong.** Erasure deletes strictly more than types and `Prop`-sorted proofs. It has five deletion sites: the signature-mask skip in `masked_fields`, the erasable operand in `kept_operand_at`, a proof-valued callee that is not a direct function reference, a `Prop` family's constructor, and an erasable scrutinee. The last three drop a whole construct *including its arguments*, and those arguments are ordinary values — neither types nor proofs. Erasure also skips dead code after a terminator, which is a reachability decision rather than a sort-driven one.

None of that is a hole, because the obligations do not need to seed each deleted term. What they need is that everything deleted lies **within** a term one of them covers, and the reachability closure and the local-partiality walk both descend through whole terms. A partial computation hidden inside any of those three constructs is reached through the enclosing proof position, not through a seed of its own; `tests::soundness` has one fixture per site. Dead code is covered for a different reason — the obligations are reachability-blind, seeding every `Prop`-typed term whether or not it can run.

So the premise to check, and the one now checked, is: *everything erasure deletes lies within a term (T) or (V) covers.* The equality form is false and would have sent an audit looking for the wrong thing.

**A second opinion is being built against this perimeter, and it is not yet load-bearing.** The kernel in `curios-core` re-decides reduction, conversion, typing, and elimination from the terms alone; see "An independent kernel re-checks what the elaborator accepts". No entry in the table above may be re-graded on account of it, because it does not yet walk a real module to completion — it stops at index inversion, recorded in that entry. What it *will* cover when it does run is the per-term half of the table plus the whole-module obligations' inputs; what it will not cover, by construction, is totality, positivity, and witness coherence, which are whole-module analyses rather than typing rules.

**One entry is known to be graded too generously.** The large-elimination guard is marked *probed, both directions*, and the probes exercised the rungs rather than the singleton rung's side condition. That condition asks whether a payload component is *determined* by the constructor's index targets, and `singleton_eliminable` decides it with `terminal.free_vars()` — a syntactic occurrence test. Occurring in an index target is not being determined by one: `mk(a : Nat) : (blur(a))` mentions `a`, but a constant `blur` leaves `Loose(0)` inhabited by `mk(0)` and `mk(7)` alike. Proof irrelevance then identifies the two while an eliminator observes them apart, and a closed inhabitant of `False` follows. `tests::perimeter::a_non_injective_index_target_does_not_force_its_binder` is the fixture, ignored because the route is open; its control, `an_unmentioned_payload_binder_is_not_forced`, is ignored alongside it so the pair is read as a pair. The kernel decides the same condition correctly — by whether the targets *pin* the component — so wiring it in is one of the two things that closes this.

**One thing sits outside the perimeter entirely.** The *Rust implementation* must fail closed — a panic, an abort, or a stack overflow is only acceptable where it refuses a program rather than admits one.

**Rejected.** Stating the trusted base as prose, which is what this replaces: it could not be checked, could not be falsified, and gave no way to tell a rule that had been attacked from one that had merely been assumed. Also rejected: treating a passing corpus as evidence of coverage. Every defect this design has produced was found by counting or by probing, and none by the corpus failing — the corpus passed throughout the period (V)'s argument rule was inert at 6010 of 6041 sites.

### An independent kernel re-checks what the elaborator accepts

**Decision.** The compiler is split so that the rules live apart from the machinery that makes them convenient. `curios-core` holds the term representation and a kernel that decides, from a finished term alone, whether it is well-typed. `curios-elab` holds elaboration, unification, zonking, witness resolution, the universe solver, and erasure. The dependency runs one way — `curios-elab` depends on `curios-core` and never the reverse — so the kernel *cannot* consult a metavariable store, a refinement layer, a parked goal, or a cached elaboration. Independence is a property of the crate graph rather than of anyone's discipline.

**Rationale.** The perimeter above enumerates every rule that can admit a term, and it is honest about what that enumeration is worth: three entries can never be probed, one load-bearing entry is unargued, and the whole of it rests on one implementation being right. A second checker does not remove any of those weaknesses. What it changes is the cost of being wrong about one: a rule the elaborator gets wrong is caught unless the kernel gets it wrong in the same way, and the two are written against different information. That is a weaker guarantee than a proof and a much stronger one than a single implementation reviewed twice.

The second motivation is that the rules are currently not written down anywhere they can be read. They exist, but distributed through a program whose job is something else: implicit-argument insertion, metavariable invention, postponement and re-validation, overload resolution. A rule cannot be checked against a specification that does not exist, and it cannot be discussed by people reading nine hundred lines of `elaborate/module.rs`. The kernel is where each rule is stated once, in a form that can be read as the rule it is.

**Sharing the representation is not sharing a judgment.** The kernel and the elaborator share `Term`, its locally-nameless binder discipline, the primitive roster, and the primitive folds — what a term *is*, and what `2 + 2` evaluates to. They share no strategy and no rule. Two checkers that agree on what a term is while deciding independently what it means still catch each other's mistakes; two that share the rule admitting a bad program catch nothing. `Reducer` is where that line is drawn: primitive folding is arithmetic over the representation and is shared, while how far an operand reduces before a fold sees it is a strategy each side supplies for itself. The concrete consequence is that reduction is written twice, and the crate boundary is what stops the duplicate from quietly collapsing back into a call.

**Rejected.** A third crate for the kernel alone, with the representation shared through `curios-base`: the extra boundary buys nothing that the one-way dependency does not already give, and it would put the representation in a crate whose purpose is stage-independent utilities.

Also rejected: **a fresh, fully explicit, metavariable-free IR for the kernel to check.** It is the textbook answer and it was the initial plan. Against it: the elaborator would need a translation into it, that translation would itself be trusted and unchecked, and every construct in the language would acquire a second representation to keep in sync. The three constructors a kernel input must never contain — `Metavar`, `Infix`, `NumLit` — are better excluded by a validation pass at the kernel's boundary, which is a few lines and is checkable, than by a parallel type whose only real content is the absence of those three.

Also rejected: **sharing the reduction driver**, parameterized over a trait supplying variable lookup and metavariable resolution. It would remove a few hundred lines of near-duplicate code. It would also mean that a mistake in the scrutinee stack, in `rec` forcing, or in the eta side condition is a mistake in both checkers simultaneously — which is the one outcome this whole split exists to prevent.

**What exists today, precisely.** Reduction (beta, delta, zeta, iota, projection, universe instantiation, eta, `rec` forcing), with agreement against the elaborator's reducer asserted as tests. A sort query. Conversion, with proof irrelevance, eta at Π and Σ, and a coinductive recurrence rule. The typing judgment: one rule per term form, plus the primitive table, with each match arm's context specialized by its case's forced equations and coverage of absent arms decided by the same unifier. And subsumption, stated as its own relation beside conversion. Universe constraints run in both directions: a definition is checked *under* its own constraint set — subsumption and conversion decide levels by entailment over those hypotheses — and every instance it contains must *satisfy* the instantiated scheme's set, which is the choosing-versus-verifying distinction made checkable. Declaration acceptance re-derives the constructor and field size conditions from the registry telescopes under the same entailment, closing the route by which an inductive contains the universe it lives in.

**Subsumption is a relation, not a traversal order.** `Γ ⊢ t : A` together with `A ≤ B` gives `Γ ⊢ t : B`, where `≤` is `Type u ≤ Type v` whenever the level algebra proves `u ≤ v`, `Prop ≤ Type v`, and `Π(x:A).B ≤ Π(x:A').B'` when `A ≡ A'` and `B ≤ B'` under `x` — conversion otherwise. **Domains are compared by conversion and codomains cumulatively**, which is the choice Coq makes. The fork is asymmetric and that is why it was taken conservatively: widening to contravariant domains later accepts strictly more, so it breaks nothing already accepted, while shipping contravariance and withdrawing it would break programs.

The elaborator reaches the same verdicts by a different route, and the difference is the point. It is bidirectional, so checking a λ against a Π pushes the comparison down to the leaves where both sides are sorts and a head-only rule suffices; it never forms the Π being subsumed, and `expect` in `curios-elab/src/typing.rs` accordingly applies cumulativity only when the expected type is itself a sort. That is not a statable rule — it is an emergent property of where a traversal bottoms out, and it would change silently if elaboration's order ever did. The kernel is synthesis-directed precisely because a finished Core term has no omitted annotations to recover, and deciding subsumption structurally is what forces the rule onto the page where it can be read and disagreed with.

**Cumulative inductive types are an open fork, deliberately not yet taken.** Nominal constructors are invariant in their parameters today: `Vec(Type u, n)` and `Vec(Type v, n)` are unrelated for `u ≤ v`. Taking the other side needs a per-parameter *universe-variance* lattice (irrelevant / covariant / invariant), which is a different analysis from `Polarity`'s strict-positivity lattice even though it would ride the same registry entries and the same archive. Three things argue for deciding it later rather than now: the corpus shows no demand — `/std` and `/syn` contain no lifting workaround of any kind; the retrofit cost that forced Coq's hand is largely absent here, since the prelude archive is explicitly not a stable interchange format and the standard library is entirely in-tree; and cumulativity in parameters changes which instantiations of a family are related, which is the input the large-elimination guard reasons about — and that guard currently has an open forgery route. The sequencing that follows is to close the guard first. The kernel's disagreement count over the whole prelude is the evidence this should be decided against.

Nominal eliminations are verified in full: each arm is checked in a context *specialized* by its case's forced equations — see the index-inversion entry below — and must inhabit the motive at *its own* constructor's index targets, and the large-elimination guard is enforced with its side condition decided rather than approximated. A payload component counts as determined when the index targets *pin* it — when matching a target against a value recovers the component — which holds for a binder standing as a target and, recursively, for one inside a constructor application, since constructors are injective with disjoint tags. It does not hold for a binder under an arbitrary function: `mk(a : Nat) : (blur(a))` mentions `a`, but `blur` need not be injective, so knowing `blur(a)` recovers nothing. That distinction is the whole of the rule. The walk is total by construction — a target it cannot decompose contributes no determined binders, yielding a stricter guard rather than a looser one.

**What is not checked, and what that costs.** Three things, in descending order of importance.

*Index inversion is shared, and the kernel's arm rule specializes the context with it.* Unifying a scrutinee's actual indices against a case's targets is one implementation in `curios-core/src/invert.rs`, run by both checkers: it is a total function of post-zonk terms on both sides, so a second copy would be a second run of the same function rather than a second opinion. What each checker owns is *applying* the solution, and this is where the two had differed. The elaborator applies it through its refinement store (`refine_head` for the scrutinee and its variable indices, inversion for the arm binders). The kernel holds no store, and until this landed it applied nothing — opening the motive at the case's targets teaches the *goal* the case's equations, and the ambient locals, the body's occurrences of outer variables, and the scrutinee variable itself all stayed unrefined. The convoy pattern, `refine_head`, and both inversion rungs are all machinery for routing the missing half through the half that existed.

The rule now built in is the Goguen–McBride–McKinna specialization step stated directly: an arm is checked with the most-general solution of `actual indices ~ case targets` substituted — into the body and the expectation, and into the type of every local it mentions, re-assumed innermost and retracted with the arm. Both unification directions run the shared unifier: pinning an arm binder to the rigid actual it must equal is the call as the elaborator makes it, and refining an outer variable to the target it is forced to equal is the same call with its sides swapped — which lands the guards exactly right, the occurs check refusing the parameter cycle (`b := b + 1` through a family parameter) and the top guard leaving the variable-variable case to the first direction. A variable scrutinee is additionally substituted by the case's constructed value, in nominal, `Bool`, and `Switch` arms alike. Definitional K licenses the substitution, as it already licensed the deletion rule.

Measured on landing: 90 of 1050 whole-prelude kernel refusals became 79, with no regression. `/std/Nat/Lte/trans` — the item every earlier revision of this entry named — re-checks, as do `/std/Vec/rest`, `/std/Str/utf8/drop_valid` and `take_valid`, and a cluster of `/std/BigNat` equality lemmas. The alternative this supersedes was widening the elaborator's convoy so an arm-used hypothesis rides into the motive: measured, it cleared `trans` and broke obligation (V) for `drop_valid`, because a convoy *re-binds* the hypothesis while size-change termination measures descent against the original parameter identities. Specializing in the kernel touches no emitted term, so (V) never sees a change — and the items the widening broke are among the ones this rule cleared.

**Amended — the universe blocker this entry used to record did not exist.** It said that a zonked module reaches the kernel still carrying unsolved universe *metavariables*, that `validate_bound_universes` inspects only the nested contexts on `Rec`/`RecMember` nodes and never a term's own levels, and that defaulting at declaration finalization was the next thing to build. All three were wrong. `recheck_module` was being handed `Stage::Core`, which the pipeline emitted *before* elaboration: the metavariables were the lowering's own universe seeds, and the module the kernel kept refusing had never been type-checked at all. `validate_bound_universes` does walk a term's own levels and does reject a metavariable. A zonked module's levels are ground, and always were. The pipeline now emits `Stage::CoreElab` for the elaborated module.

The correction is recorded rather than deleted, because of how it was reached. The diagnosis had been derived by reading the refusal and reasoning back to the pass that must have admitted it, and it accused a mechanism that was working correctly — the same failure mode as every defect the totality obligations produced. Printing the module settled it in one command. Reading is where this project's wrong answers come from; counting and printing are where the right ones do.

*Coverage is verified; two arm-sets are not.* An absent arm must justify its absence: with no catch-all, every constructor with no arm must be *impossible* at the scrutinee's indices — its targets must clash with the actuals, decided by the same shared unifier that specializes the present arms, with an undecided case refused rather than passed. Measured on landing, the whole prelude satisfies this with zero new refusals. What remains unverified is a `Switch`'s default and the free-monoid carriers' arms, whose binders would have to be typed against the carrier's own successor structure — those arms are typed by their bodies but not against the motive.

*Totality is not decided by the kernel*, and deliberately so: `rec` is general recursion, and the obligation that keeps it sound is positional and whole-module. (T) and (V) stay where they are.

The conversion checker is also incomplete in several positions — an elimination's motive and arms, `rec` groups, and spine arguments compared at `Type` — each of which refuses programs rather than admitting them. That direction is the point: a refusal is a visible disagreement between two checkers, an over-eager acceptance is silent. ROADMAP.md tracks the status.

### Concepts resolve with global coherence

**Decision.** Ad-hoc polymorphism is concepts and witnesses. Witness resolution consults one program-wide table under global coherence checks, and anonymous witnesses fill structure the goal already determines.

**Rationale.** Coherence makes the chosen witness a fact about the program rather than about the scope of the call site, so moving code or reorganizing imports cannot silently change which witness runs.

### Concept representations may be sealed

**Decision.** Concepts carry the same declaration-local representation visibility as structs and inductives: `: pub Type` is transparent, `: Type` is sealed. A sealed concept's representation is private to its declaring module's subtree — witness declarations, dictionary literals, structure updates, and raw field projections are permitted only there — while resolution, `use` parameters, and the generated method wrappers work identically for both. A sealed `pub` concept's fields are not interface, so they may reference private names: a private superclass is a hidden obligation resolution discharges without the consumer naming it.

**Rationale.** Sealing lets an owner control the full instance set of a concept — a guarantee otherwise inexpressible, since the auto-generated public wrappers plus explicit implicit arguments leak any field value, defeating private-token workarounds. It reuses the enforcement that representation privacy already provides, adding no new checks, and it is honest about mechanism: a concept is a record, so its visibility story is the record's. Enforcement is scoped to surface elaboration (an island in `Context`); machinery that re-derives types from already-elaborated terms — erasure, the metavariable oracle — suppresses it through the one bracket that clears the island, because compiler-built projections (witness splices, eta-expansions) were never subject to surface privacy.

**Rejected.** Coherence-only opacity (blocking dictionary literals while exempting `satisfy`): its motivating hazard dissolved once `Map` was recognized as byte-keyed by design, leaving no coherence-sensitive concept. Sealing through orphan-rule tightening: representation privacy already provides the gate, at the construction site where it belongs.

### Privacy is scoped to a subtree

**Decision.** A declaration written without `pub` in module `M` is visible exactly within `M`'s subtree — `M` and its descendants at any depth; a `pub` declaration is additionally visible wherever `M` itself is. The same rule governs both namespaces and the declaration-local representation marker, so a private representation is transparent throughout its declaring subtree. Reachability along a path is the conjunction of the rule at each hop, and the root's subtree is the whole program. Globs are excluded: `use M/*` imports the exported surface only, so reaching a subtree-private declaration always requires naming it. The interface audit compares audiences rather than declaration paths, which makes the facade — a public module re-exporting selected names out of a private child — expressible for the first time.

**Rationale.** The unit of trust was the unit of file organization, so an abstraction that outgrew one file had to choose between a monolith and a fully public representation. Splitting `/std` under the old rule would have added roughly 170 smart constructors and helpers to the public API, so the stricter rule produced the *larger* exported surface. Subtree scoping decouples the two while keeping the property worth having: the trusted set is a directory the author owns, and it coincides with the boundary the facade already hides behind. The asymmetry is deliberate and strictly weaker than ancestor privilege — descendants gain, ancestors and siblings gain nothing — so a sibling subtree still cannot open a representation, and `pub` retains one meaning instead of acquiring a family of scoped variants. Privacy remains a rule about what surface elaboration may *write*, never about what conversion may see.

**Rejected.** Rust-style ancestor privilege, where privacy means "visible in the defining module and all descendants": it answers "who can break this invariant" only by reading a whole subtree the declaration does not own. `pub(<path>)` targeted export: a strict widening still available later, but it inverts the dependency direction by making a declaration name its consumers. ML-style signature ascription: modules are namespaces here, and the visibility story belongs on the declaration.

### Plicity is part of function identity

**Decision.** A binder's plicity — explicit, implicit (`@`), or witness (`use`) — is part of a function type's identity and calling convention. Function types that differ only in plicity are not convertible, every written function binder and constructor-pattern argument is checked against the plicity of the slot it claims, and an omitted implicit or witness *lambda* binder is inserted automatically from the expected function type, mirroring application-side hidden-argument insertion. Reduction stays positional and erasure stays sort-driven, so plicity has no runtime effect.

**Rationale.** Plicity controls elaboration-visible calling behaviour, so it must be stable under conversion: without it a convertible annotation or alias could reinterpret which binders get inserted. Lambda-side insertion lets a polymorphic definition omit the hidden binders it does not name while still writing exactly the ones it does, and keeping the rule positional-by-plicity (never by binder label) avoids named-argument selection. Constructor patterns are exempt from insertion because the type-blind match matrix lays out payload columns before elaboration knows the constructor signature; that omission is deferred rather than approximated.

**Rejected.** Plicity-blind function-type conversion; silently binding an implicit or witness slot with a plain binder; inserting omitted hidden constructor-pattern arguments by analogy with lambdas.

### Matching is total by enumeration

**Decision.** Every match must cover its scrutinee. Arms enumerate constructors without row priority, nested patterns compile through the pattern matrix by full enumeration, and an omitted arm is legal only when index inversion proves it impossible.

**Rationale.** Arm order never changes meaning, coverage is a checked property rather than a runtime default, and impossibility is discharged by the type system instead of an unreachable-arm convention.

**Rejected.** First-match-wins row priority with catch-all defaults.

### Literals are library values

**Decision.** Character and string literals lower to transparent `/syn/Char` and `/syn/Str` values — proof-certified library structures — while the erased runtime carriers remain `Nat` and packed `Bytes`.

**Rationale.** The kernel stays free of ad-hoc literal types, literals arrive already carrying the structure and certificates library code wants to consume, and erasure guarantees those certificates cost nothing at runtime.

**Rejected.** Kernel-primitive character and string types.

### A motive is a term, not a grammar

**Decision.** A match motive is a single construct: a term checked against the eliminator's motive type, `(ī : Ī(p̄)) -> I(p̄, ī) -> Sort`. It binds one name per index and then the scrutinee. Parameters are never abstracted, and the eliminated family is never written.

**Rationale.** The motive is morally a lambda, so making it literally one removes every question a bespoke grammar had to answer badly. Binder annotations become ordinary types in ordinary positions, so plicity is expressible — a family declared `induct Eq(@A : Type)` is annotated `Eq(s, t)`, the same spelling it has everywhere else. Whether a position binds or is checked is decided by the syntax rather than by whether a name happens to resolve to a module binding. Parameters were abstracted to the identical term in the match's type and in every arm, so removing them costs nothing and settles the plicity question by deleting the positions that raised it. A malformed motive becomes a type error at the motive instead of a silent reinterpretation.

Dropping the constant rung is what buys the absence of a disambiguation rule: a constant motive may itself be a Π type, and when its domain is the scrutinee's type both readings check and mean different things. With one rung the parser calls `parse_term` and needs no backtracking, because `|` is not an infix operator and a motive term therefore ends at the first arm.

**Rejected.** The three-rung ladder — constant, scrutinee-bound, and an annotated type-pattern over the inductive's flat parameter-then-index slots. Also rejected: making eliminators first-class functions with the motive as an ordinary argument, which would require generating a bespoke matcher per match site; `Match` stays a primitive node whose arms carry per-constructor index refinement. Making the motive a checked term keeps that door open without walking through it.

### No expression-level ascription

**Decision.** `term : type` is not an expression form and is not planned; `:` appears only in binder, signature, and motive positions.

**Rationale.** The whole-term forms — `let`, `rec`, `match`, lambdas, function types — already extend to the end of the enclosing term, so a postfix ascription would compete with them for the same tail. Where a type must be forced mid-expression, a `let` binding is the idiom.

## The toolchain

### WebAssembly-GC is the only target

**Decision.** The pipeline emits Wasm-GC exclusively. Program values live in GC references, never linear memory, and the same backend serves the native and browser products.

**Rationale.** A functional dependently typed language needs a garbage collector, and targeting Wasm-GC inherits a production collector instead of hand-rolling a runtime system. One backend yields both products, and portability comes with the ecosystem. The mechanism — the symbolic module builder and the GC-only, memory-less instruction roster — belongs to `curios-wasm`'s rustdoc.

**Rejected.** Native code generation, and Wasm over linear memory with a shipped garbage collector.

### Curios owns the language, Rust owns the host

**Decision.** Rust owns the native host — Binaryen optimization, Wasmtime precompilation and execution, bundling, the CLI, and operating-system services — and the self-hosting objective claims only the language-specific stages. The frozen Rust baseline compiler remains as bootstrap seed and differential oracle.

**Rationale.** Self-hosting pays off exactly where the language defines itself — parsing through Wasm generation. Reimplementing Wasmtime, Binaryen, or host integration in Curios would add risk without teaching the language anything.

**Rejected.** Making the bootstrap objective reach past the Curios toolchain. Going further stays open for later — with a more robust FFI story, host components could link directly through WebAssembly imports and exports compiled from other languages — but for now, the main toolchain in Curios itself is the objective.

### Distribution is ahead-of-time

**Decision.** The native product precompiles modules to `.cwasm` with Cranelift at build time and bundles them with the slim runtime launcher into standalone executables. The launcher deserializes and runs; it cannot compile. Other native distribution modes are not foreclosed; none is planned yet.

**Rationale.** User startup does no compilation work, the launcher stays slim precisely because Cranelift and Binaryen are excluded from it, and pinning the compiler and runtime to one Wasmtime version guarantees every `.cwasm` matches the engine that deserializes it.

### Deep shared terms are cached, not special-cased

**Decision.** The lowerer emits `Rc`-shared DAGs — a string literal's certified UTF-8 derivation shares every per-byte scan-state chain — and the compiler scales to them through generic mechanisms only, none of which recurses on input depth: an elaboration cache for metavariable-free, minted-name-free subterms whose result names only already-defined globals — so it survives, within one item, the `#`-minted definitions reduction and the frame elaborators mint (`Context::get_or_init_elaborated` / `elaboration_cacheable`, split into probe/record halves for the driver); the reduction cache's probe/record pair (`Context::cached_reduced` / `Context::reduce`); eliminator scrutinees reduced on an explicit frame stack inside `reduce`'s trampoline; elaboration's `elaborate → elaborate_apply → check` cycle defunctionalized onto its own frame stack for ground, all-explicit applications, so a data-shaped constructor spine elaborates at native depth bounded by the written program's binder nesting (`elaborate`'s driver and `ElabFrame`); and each term's memoized derivations (`hash`/`reach`/`free_vars`/`has_local_free`/`has_metavar`) carried on the shared `Rc` node (`Node`) and filled by an iterative post-order walk, so a subtree memoizes once across every occurrence, on a bounded native stack. No stage carries `Str`- or literal-specific machinery; eligibility gates and invalidation discipline belong to the `curios-elab` rustdoc of those methods.

**Rationale.** Sharing-oblivious elaboration cost O(N²) work and O(N) native stack in a literal's byte length, and the depth surfaced wherever the checker ran on a bounded thread. The defect was the compiler's — recursive tree walks over DAG-shaped input — so the cure had to be compiler-general: the next proof-carrying literal or generated spine benefits identically, and `/syn`, `/std`, and the emitter stay ordinary library code.

**Rejected.** Emitter-side pre-reduced scan states (duplicates `/std/Str` semantics inside the compiler); a canonical-spine special case in conversion (a nominal `Str` exception to definitional equality); redesigning the `/syn/Str` evidence to sidestep depth (the language may write deep certified data; the compiler must carry it).

**Deferred.** The elaboration cache now survives a fresh `define` — the insert refuses any result naming a not-yet-defined global (the name analogue of the reduction cache's unsolved-metavariable rule, which lets reduction entries survive `define` selectively), so `define_entry` no longer clears it wholesale — but `set_island` still clears it at every top-level item boundary, so the survival is within-item. Extending it across items would key the cache on the item's actual island rather than merely whether privacy checks were live, so `set_island` need not clear; the reduction cache, island-independent, already spans items.

### A name is an identity, not a record

**Decision.** A compiler name distinguishes one binding from another and renders for a human. Nothing else. Behavior never branches on a name's characters, its prefix, or its collation order; a fact known where a name was built is carried as a value, never recovered by taking the name apart. The vocabulary that enforces this — `Free`, `Global`, `Mint`, `WitnessId` — belongs to `curios-elab`'s `names.rs` rustdoc, and the property is upheld by the *absence* of accessors: no path leads from a `Free` to a `&str` outside the printer, so reintroducing behavior-from-spelling requires adding a method to a name type.

**Rationale.** A name's spelling had become an undocumented wire format between stages. Five structured facts were flattened into one `String` — namespace path, global-versus-local, minted-versus-authored, a minted binder's authored hint, and the owner/family relation — and each consumer that needed one back deserialized it with a hand-rolled parser no other site shared. Every such parser was sound only under an invariant enforced in a different crate and stated nowhere near the parse, which is a failure mode rather than untidiness: one had already misfired (a `#` test for minted locals that a `#`-bearing global silently defeated, discarding more than 4,096 cache entries per prelude build), and the tree contradicted itself in writing about a second, where the consequence of a misread would have been a silently dropped call. A sixth fact was not in the characters at all but in the collation order over them — a constructor's runtime tag was its alphabetical rank — so renaming a case renumbered the tags of every case it sorted past.

**Rejected.** Fixing the readers. A decoder is half a format; leaving the encoders in place preserves the merge and the next consumer writes a sixth parser. Also rejected: a marker character reserving a sigil namespace, which trades one wire-format field for another and was adopted only as a temporary measure to fix the live bug before the types existed; and a flat `Global { qualifier, disambiguator: Option<u32> }`, whose `qualifier` would have meant *module plus item name* for an authored global but *declaring module alone* for a witness — one field with two readings, which is exactly the merge being removed.

**Rejected — de Bruijn levels for free variables.** Bound variables are already de Bruijn indices and stay so; the proposal was to identify *free* variables — opened binders — by level instead of by minted identity, making context-dependence structural rather than a property to be tested. Four structures already store binders in order and then key them by name, so levels are the representation they approximate. Not adopted: every correctness benefit is delivered by typed names, while the risk concentrates in the two least forgiving parts of the tree. Conversion's `history_key` renames minted opening labels in mint order to collapse successive rounds of an unfolding cycle onto one history entry; mint order is not depth order and siblings at equal depth share a level, so whether the two schemes are equivalent could not be settled by reading — and a false history hit is unsoundness in the conversion checker. Parked work (`FrozenFrame`, `ParkedWork`) freezes a local frame and reapplies it at a possibly different depth, which identities make free and levels would require rebasing. And the lowerer would have to track depth through decision-tree compilation, the most intricate lowering in the tree. Two earlier objections did *not* survive scrutiny and are recorded so they are not raised again: `Sort::of`'s `Opened` avoids the `Context` to keep from bumping `mutation_stamp`, but a level is `context.depth() + position` and depth is readable without mutation; and the reverse linear scan it would replace with indexing holds one entry per telescope binder, so the win was never measured. Reopen only on a concrete problem, not on aesthetics — and if the kernel is reworked for another reason, settle the `history_key` question first.
