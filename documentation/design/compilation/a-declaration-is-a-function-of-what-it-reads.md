# A declaration is a function of what it reads

**Decision.** What a declaration compiles to is determined by its own text and by what it reads of other declarations, never by where either is written, by which of them was elaborated first, or by how many came before. The lowering hands a unit's items on as they are written, and sorts nothing. They are elaborated on demand (`curios-elab/src/elaborate/module/demand.rs`): each after every item its lowered form reaches, and after every declaration it turns out to read while it elaborates, the witness a goal resolves to. A read of a declaration that has not elaborated voids the attempt, whatever is read of it — its name, its body, a witness's key, or a registry entry, which is registered as lowered before the unit's first item: the read is recorded (`Context::need`), what the attempt wrote is undone, the declaration it needed is elaborated, and the attempt is made again. An attempt is never suspended to run another inside it, and never waits on one. Declarations that need one another are refused together, once, at the first of them as written, naming every member. A witness is known before it elaborates by the key its signature is spelled at (`curios-elab/src/concept/spelled.rs`), so what a goal, the auto-lift's monad check and a report's survey of a concept's edges each ask — whether a witness stands at a key — is answered from every witness the unit declares, and registration refuses a witness whose elaborated signature registers anywhere else.

Nothing but those reads flows into a declaration. The declaration is the unit of work: an item with every item the lowering generates from it, a type former with its constructors and a concept with its method wrappers. Its identities — binders, metavariables, universe levels — count from zero for it, in its lowering (`curios_core::Minted`) and above that in its elaboration, and it elaborates in a state of its own: its metavariables with their solutions, its universe solver and its caches are replaced where an attempt at it begins (`Context::attempt`). Each of its items is published as it finishes, zonked, into what every other declaration reads — the bindings of its names, its registry entries, the terms it recorded — as a unit in scope is replayed, so what is read of it holds nothing of the state it was elaborated in. A declaration that wrote a goal is published by its types alone and keeps that state, in which the goal is reported once every declaration of the unit has finished.

A declaration's signature is published with its body: a declaration that reads another's type reads it once that declaration's elaboration has finished. And a body another declaration needs is read, never withheld: no body is opaque.

A proof the elaborator writes applies lemmas its declaration never names, the prover's vocabulary (`SyntaxRegistry::entailment`), so no read says which of them a declaration has. In the unit that declares the vocabulary, the walk asks for its declarations first, which brings in every declaration they read, with no lemma of the unit on offer, and offers all of them from then on (`Demand::all`, `Context::proof_may_apply`): a declaration the vocabulary reads is proved without it, and every other finds the whole of it elaborated. Compiled over a baseline, the declarations the vocabulary reads through a reused item are asked for with it (`vocabulary_reach`, `curios-elab/src/elaborate/module.rs`). Which lemmas a declaration's proofs have is thereby a function of what the vocabulary reads, and of nowhere anything is written.

What is decided of a declaration over its final terms is decided as it is published, once, each judgment on a budget restored for it (`settle_published`, `curios-elab/src/elaborate/module.rs`): the polarity vectors of the registry entries it declares, that the variance vectors they carry claim no more than their telescopes grant, each definition's totality, and its two erasure obligations. No pass over a unit decides any of them again, and a recompile writes none of a reused declaration's. Erasure restores its budget for each item, and the replay of a scope restores it for each witness it registers, so a unit takes a declaration under every budget the declaration takes alone, whatever else the unit or its scope holds.

The kernel judges a declaration the same way (`verdicts_within`, `curios-cert/src/recheck.rs`): an item's check, its classification and its erased positions, then the registry entries it declares — their strict positivity, their variance and their sizing — each on a budget restored for it and over those entries alone. A family they mention answers from the polarity vector the certifier filed for it, this walk's as it accepted the family or a unit in scope's from the record its walk left (`curios_core::Certification`). The vector an entry carries is elaboration's, and no judgment of the kernel reads it.

A unit's items are stored in one order, read off what they name: every item after the items its elaborated form reaches, and where that leaves a choice, by name (`curios_core::Module::ordered`). The module is put in that order once, where the elaborator makes it, whole or over a baseline, and every later walk takes the items as they come: the certifier, which refuses an item that names one stored after it, and erasure. A witness is named by its place among its module's witnesses, so witnesses keep the order their module writes them in, and tests are scheduled in the order they are written.

**Rationale.**

- **It is the correctness of a build, stated for declarations.** Mokhov, Mitchell and Peyton Jones call a build correct when "the result of recomputing the corresponding task matches the value stored in the result" ([Build Systems à la Carte](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf), definition 3.1). A declaration recompiled alone over its finished unit is that recomputation, so [a stored unit is a baseline](a-stored-unit-is-a-baseline-for-an-item-level-recompile.md) only as far as this holds, and so is any schedule that elaborates two declarations at once.
- **The surface already promises it.** A witness may follow the code that resolves through it ([Witness resolution](../../syntax.md#witness-resolution)), so where a declaration is written is no part of what it means; a program accepted in one order of its declarations and refused in another keeps that promise by accident. rust-analyzer's account of [three architectures](https://rust-analyzer.github.io/blog/2020/07/20/three-architectures-for-responsive-ide.html) keeps the snapshot-after-headers design for languages with a "declaration before use" rule, and answers the rest by demand, where "we compensate for the deficit of laziness with incrementality".
- **An answer that follows what has elaborated is an answer about the schedule.** A goal retried after every later item resolves once its consumer's levels have closed, so the consumer keeps the levels it happened to be left with; a monad check that reads the table finds no monad where the witness is written lower down; and a goal that never resolves is reported after its item has finished, which means taking finished items back. Each is one question asked at the wrong time, and none of them remains once the question is answered before any declaration that asks it is kept.
- **A signature is not final before its body.** `finalize_definition` settles a written signature's universe context with the constraints its body raised (`finalize_universe_metas(interface, internal)`), and the surface has no syntax for a level, so a signature read before its body has finished could still move.
- **A proof body is not opaque.** Eliminating `Accessible` into data reduces the proof, and a relevant `match` on an `Eq` proof reduces it to `refl` ([`Prop` is strict, proof-irrelevant, and definitionally K](../theory/prop-is-strict-proof-irrelevant-and-definitionally-k.md)), so a later declaration may need any body.
- **A void attempt costs one elaboration and needs nothing new.** Undoing an attempt is what refusing an item already does (`ItemMark`), and an attempt made again enters nothing: one context serves every attempt, where elaborating the needed declaration inside the attempt that needs it would need a context per declaration, and a worker that waits for one needs a deadlock detector. Mokhov, Mitchell and Peyton Jones name it *restarting*: "whenever a task calls fetch on an out-of-date key dep, abort the task, and switch to building the dependency dep", which is "not minimal in the sense that a task may start, do some meaningful work, and then abort" ([Build Systems à la Carte](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf), section 4.1.2). Bazel's Skyframe is the industrial case: it hands a function `null` for a dependency that is not built, "the function itself is expected to return `null`", and "the dependent node is evaluated, and then the original node builder is invoked again" ([Skyframe](https://bazel.build/reference/skyframe)).
- **The record voids the attempt, not the error.** A read of an unfinished declaration returns an error that cuts the attempt short, and elaboration turns errors into decisions in dozens of places — a candidate tried and dropped, a diagnosis that is simply absent. The need is recorded where the read happens and asked for once the attempt ends, so no such site can make an attempt that read an incomplete answer count.
- **A key that elaboration computes cannot be asked for early.** Read off the elaborated signature on demand, a key is itself a read of everything the signature names, and the monad check is asked of nearly every checked application: a declaration that some witness's signature is built from would ask for that witness's key while the key waits on it, a cycle that is none, since such a witness could never have been the answer. Read off the spelling, the key of every witness is in hand before the first item, a goal needs exactly the one witness spelled at its key, and nothing else waits. All 278 concept parameters of `/std`'s 252 witnesses spell their head — a type former, a name for an intrinsic type, a tuple type or a function to an applied former — counted at `8bc6868d8` by reading each lowered signature. GHC makes the same cut: it "collects information" from every instance head in one pass and checks the bindings in a second, "when the class-instance envs and GVE contain all the info from all the instance and value decls" ([`GHC.Tc.TyCl.Instance`](https://hackage.haskell.org/package/ghc-9.6.4/docs/src/GHC.Tc.TyCl.Instance.html)).
- **The two readings of a key are held to each other.** A goal is keyed by what its parameters reduce to, so a witness has to register where reduction takes its signature; the spelling is what answers before that. Registration computes the first and requires the second to equal it, so a witness is refused rather than known under one key and registered under another.
- **A cycle is a fact about the program, and is reported as one.** Declarations that each need another have no order to elaborate in under any schedule, so the refusal names them and the way out — one group — where the kernel would name an identity the reader cannot find in their own program.
- **A counter two declarations share is a read neither wrote.** A binder's identity is a position in a counter, and an atom's rank is its structural hash (`curios-core/src/atoms.rs`), which reads it: a product's factors are ordered by rank, and a product rebuilt from its normal form is spelled in that order. With one counter for a unit, how an inferred implicit was spelled, and which proof the elaborator found first, followed how many declarations had elaborated before. Counted from zero for each declaration, in its lowering and above that in its elaboration, an identity says nothing of any other declaration, and an edit to one renumbers no other.
- **A state replaced whole cannot carry what a list forgets.** Solutions, the universe solver and the caches are replaced where an attempt begins, never cleared field by field: a field added later would flow from one declaration to the next unless its author remembered the list.
- **Publishing is replaying.** A finished item is bound in the context from its zonked form, as a unit in scope is replayed, so a declaration elaborated beside it in a whole compile and one elaborated over it in a recompile read the same terms, and a hole it left unsolved is refused where it stands.
- **A declaration's items share one state because they share one written declaration.** The lowering copies a declaration's written types into the items it generates from it, a constructor's payload and a method wrapper's field, and a written type is one level wherever it is copied; a constructor and a method wrapper are finalized at the instance of the declaration they belong to. So they are attempted together, and no other declaration is elaborated between two of them.
- **A goal is reported in the state that knows it, over the unit that is written.** A declaration that wrote a `?` keeps the state it elaborated in (`Attempted`), and its goals are reported in that state once every declaration of the unit has finished, so the candidates a report offers are drawn from the whole unit, a definition written below the goal among them, whichever declaration elaborated first. Until then it is published by its types alone, a constant: what reads it elaborates and reports its own goals, where withholding it would hide them until the first was filled. A type former, a concept and a witness are read by more than a type, so one that holds a goal is taken out, and what reads it is withheld.
- **A lemma no declaration names is a read no walk can order by.** Offered by position, to the declarations written after it, a lemma makes where a declaration sits part of what its proofs are built from, and an item the lowering moves behind one of its dependencies gains the lemmas written between the two. Offered as each proof wants it, a lemma is asked for by a declaration it may itself read through a witness, which closes a cycle found only once both have begun. Asked for before everything else and offered whole from then on, the vocabulary waits on nothing that could wait on it.
- **What a declaration reads is found by elaborating it.** A sort made before elaboration orders by what is written, and a witness a goal resolves to, a lemma a proof applies and a body a derivation writes are written nowhere: to stand in for them the sort grows edges that over-approximate, deadlock on the library's own cycles and are dropped a node at a time. The walk needs none of it, since a read of a declaration that has not elaborated asks for it, and a cycle is refused once, by the one place that sees every edge.
- **An order every stage sorts for itself is an order none of them owns.** Sorted by the lowering over the names an item writes, by the certifier again over the elaborated terms and by erasure a third time, elaboration adding the edges a witness and a lemma make, each sort breaks its ties on the order it is handed, and where a declaration is written reaches the items a unit stores and the module a program compiles to. One order, a function of what the items declare and reach, is the same for the same declarations wherever they are written, and a walk that takes it sorts nothing and can only refuse what it finds out of order.
- **A budget two declarations share is a read neither wrote.** A pass that runs over a unit after its items spends one budget on all of them, so whether a declaration's verdict fits follows what the others cost: sixteen families that name nothing of one another are refused under a budget each is taken under alone, the refusal names whichever the budget ran out on, and a program that declares nothing has to afford the key of every witness in its scope. Decided as the declaration is published, on a budget of its own, a verdict is the declaration's. And a cell written once has one value: a pass wrote every reused declaration's vectors and stamp again on each recompile, where nothing a reused declaration read had moved.
- **A judgment over the scope is a read of everything in it.** Strict positivity decided for every declaration in scope at once spends one budget on all of them: a program that declares nothing has to afford the analysis of every family of the library, a unit of sixteen families sixteen times one, and the refusal names whichever family the budget ran out on. Variance answers for a family in scope from a vector held to that family's declaration when its unit was certified, and a polarity vector filed by the walk that computed it is the same answer to the same question ([Cached verdicts](../soundness/cached-verdicts.md)), so judging a declaration reads the vectors of the families it names and nothing else of the scope. The peers' kernels judge a declaration as it is added: Lean's reference says "Before new inductive types or definitions are added to the environment by the command or term elaborators, they must be checked by the kernel" ([Elaboration and Compilation](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Elaboration-and-Compilation/)), and Agda checks "that the datatypes in the mutual block containing the given declarations are strictly positive", a block at a time, and adds "information about positivity and recursivity of records to the signature" ([`Agda.TypeChecking.Positivity`](https://hackage-origin.haskell.org/package/Agda-2.6.0/docs/Agda-TypeChecking-Positivity.html)).
- **A verdict is a closure, so a declaration asks only what it names.** A definition is partial where anything it mentions is, and every declaration a published one mentions has finished, so its erasure obligations read the verdicts of the definitions their positions write and walk into none of them.
- **Evidence.** `curios`'s `tests::universes::settlement_tests::a_declarations_levels_do_not_follow_where_its_witnesses_are_written` holds a declaration's levels in both orders, through `!` and through a `use` premise; `tests::concepts::monad_tests::an_action_is_embedded_wherever_its_monads_witnesses_are_written` the monad check; `tests::concepts::recursion_tests` the cycles, between two witnesses and between a definition and the witness it resolves to. `curios-text`'s `a_declaration_lowers_the_same_whatever_is_written_before_it` and `curios-elab`'s `an_attempt_mints_above_what_its_own_declaration_lowered` hold a declaration's identities to it; `curios-pipeline/src/tests/incremental_tests.rs` requires a unit compiled over a baseline to equal the one compiled whole, an inserted declaration among its fixtures; `curios-elab`'s `context::tests` hold a read of an unfinished declaration's name, body or registry entry to a need and the offer before and after the walk makes it, and `elaborate::module::tests::the_vocabulary_reaches_what_it_reads_through_a_baselines_items` what a recompile asks for first; `curios-pipeline`'s `tests::order_tests` hold a unit to the same elaborated module, record and erased arena wherever its declarations are written; `curios-pipeline`'s `tests::budget_tests` hold a unit to the budget one of its families is checked under, by the elaborator and the kernel together, and one of its values erases under, and a program to the budget it takes once its scope holds a unit it never reads; `curios-cert`'s `recheck::scope_tests::a_mounted_familys_vector_is_the_certifiers_and_not_its_entrys` holds a later walk to the vector the certifier filed, against an entry whose stamp lies, and `recheck::budget_tests::positivity_is_judged_on_its_own_budget` the budget a declaration's positivity is judged on; and `curios-pipeline`'s `std_replay_census` measures the property over `/std`, each declaration elaborated alone against the finished library, with and without an unrelated definition elaborated first: none of its 1,926 declarations differs from the one the library holds under either, and none is refused, counted at `f866213ff`.

**Rejected.**

- **Deferring a goal with no entry and retrying it after every later item.** It is the schedule answering: the goal resolves when a witness happens to register, after its consumer has closed.
- **Reading a witness's key off its elaborated signature when a question first asks for it.** A question would wait on everything every candidate's signature names, and close cycles through declarations that only build those signatures.
- **Hiding a witness from the declarations its signature is built from**, so that a key read that would cycle is skipped: which keys are skipped where two such reads meet follows which declaration was asked for first.
- **Ordering uses after their witnesses in the lowering**, with more or harder edges: which witness a use resolves to is a typing fact the lowering cannot know, so the edges over-approximate and deadlock.
- **Requiring a witness before its use**, as Lean and Rocq do: `/std`'s order does not follow its witnesses, and the surface says it need not.
- **Elaborating the unfinished declaration inside the attempt that needs it**, Shake's suspension: it needs a context per declaration, and under several workers it leaves the cell another worker holds, which waits.
- **Waiting on the task that holds a declaration**: a worker that waits needs a deadlock detector, which is what rustc's parallel front end lists open: of its threads it says "there are some known bugs, including deadlocks" ([Faster compilation with the parallel front-end in nightly](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2023/11/09/parallel-rustc/)).
- **A witness whose head only computation reaches**, keyed where reduction takes it: its key would be known only once everything it computes through has elaborated, which is the on-demand read again for that witness.
- **Clearing a shared context between declarations**, field by field: a field added later flows from one declaration to the next unless its author remembers the list, where a state replaced whole cannot carry one.
- **Renumbering a declaration's identities as its elaboration begins**, in place of minting them per declaration: every table keyed by a lowered identity — unbound names, a level's origin — would follow the renaming.
- **One state for a unit that holds a goal**, so its goals are reported from one place as they once were: two declarations' identities are the same numbers, and a declaration elaborated after a goal would follow the declarations before it again.
- **Reporting a goal as its declaration finishes**: what fits it would be drawn from the declarations that had elaborated by then, which is the order they were asked for in.
- **Withholding what reads a declaration that holds a goal**: a reader's own goals and refusals would stay unreported until the first goal was filled.
- **Keeping the lowering's order as what a proof's lemmas are read by**, with the sort computed for that alone: nothing moves, and an edit elsewhere still decides which lemmas an item has.
- **A lemma applied by the declarations written after it**: where a declaration is written is no part of what it means, and the order of `/std`'s `mod` lines would decide which of its bounds are proved, proofs in `/std/Str` applying `/std/Eq/sym`, which is written far below them.
- **A lemma applied by every declaration it does not reach, the vocabulary's own among them**: neither of two unrelated lemmas reaches the other, and a proof of each written with the other is a cycle through two declarations that name nothing of one another.
- **A lemma withheld from the declarations the vocabulary's written names reach, and asked for by any other as its proof wants it**: a witness a lemma resolves to is reached by no name the lemma writes, so a proof in that witness would ask for the lemma while the lemma waits on the witness, a cycle found only once both had begun.
- **Giving a proof no lemma of the unit being compiled**: order-free, and `/std`'s own bounds past its `Nat` lemmas would need written proofs, or the lemmas a unit of their own.
- **An order kept by where a declaration is written**, as the tie among items that do not name one another: a unit would store another module for the same declarations moved, and [a stored unit says what its items mean, not where they were written](../../../curios-unit/README.md#a-stored-unit-says-what-its-items-mean-not-where-they-were-written-or-how-they-were-come-by).
- **An order read off a record of what elaboration read**: what the elaborated terms name is enough to order them, is in the module a baseline already holds, and needs nothing stored beside it.
- **Lean's signature rule**, that a signature alone fixes a declaration's universe parameters: it needs level syntax the surface does not have.
- **Opaque proof bodies**, the reason the peers that check in parallel can fix a statement without its proof — Lean 4.19 elaborates theorem bodies in parallel ([lean4#7084](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/pull/7084)), and Rocq processes only opaque proofs asynchronously, needing the theorem's statement without looking at its proof ([Asynchronous and Parallel Proof Processing](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/v9.1/refman/addendum/parallel-proof-processing.html)): Curios reduces proofs where it eliminates `Accessible` and matches on `refl`.
- **Publishing a signature speculatively and rolling back dependents** when the body disagrees: the answer would be deterministic and the work would not, and a dependent's diagnostics would be computed against a signature that never existed.
- **Passes over a unit after its items**, for the vectors, the totality stamps and the erasure obligations: one budget for every declaration, each cell written again by every recompile, and one refusal for the unit where each declaration has its own.
- **Recomputing the polarity vectors a declaration reaches**, believing no filed one: the trusted base is unchanged, each verdict records more reads, and variance and positivity keep two answers to one question.
- **Restoring the budget per family inside one judgment of positivity over the scope**: the verdict is then the declaration's, and every compile still walks every family of every unit before it.
- **One budget for everything decided of a declaration**, its elaboration and the judgments of its final terms together: a declaration that elaborates under a budget would be refused under it for what deciding its vectors and its obligations adds.
