# Curios is coloured from one class set

**Decision.** The language's words are stated once, in `curios-common`'s `Keyword`: every word the surface grammar reads as itself rather than as a name, each with where it is one — reserved wherever it stands, a word at an item's head or between a group's members and a name elsewhere, or a sort where a term stands and a path segment elsewhere. The parser reads its words there, and `is_reserved`, its reserved part, is what refuses a word as a name, a path segment or a package's name. The wire words of a `foreign` signature are `curios-abi`'s, which owns the wire: it writes each (`WireType`'s `Display`) and reads each back (`WireType::read`, `WireType::elemented`, `WireLeaf::read`, `NEVER`), and the parser and the printer read them there. Every other statement of a word is held to `Keyword` by `curios-surface`'s `parse::tests::editors`: each tree-sitter grammar's word tokens, read off its committed `src/node-types.json`; the words each TextMate grammar paints as keywords, booleans and sorts; the words each Zed query captures as keywords, with those its grammar reads as booleans and sorts; and the reserved, contextual and sort words `documentation/language.md` states.

**Rationale.** A word the language adds reaches whichever list its author remembers, unless one statement exists and every copy is held to it. The parser and a name's legality are Rust and read the table; the editors' grammars cannot, and their patterns are written for what a highlighter needs, so each keeps its own statement and a test holds it, as `editors/grammar`'s own tests hold the queries to the tree-sitter grammars. The peers do the same: rustc states every keyword once, used, unused and weak, in its `symbols!` table ([`symbol.rs`](https://github.com/rust-lang/rust/blob/master/compiler/rustc_span/src/symbol.rs)), Swift states each as a `KeywordSpec` its parser is generated from ([`KeywordSpec.swift`](https://github.com/swiftlang/swift-syntax/blob/main/CodeGeneration/Sources/SyntaxSupport/KeywordSpec.swift)), and both keep their editor grammars by hand; what breaks is a copy nothing holds, as Zig's documentation carried "an implementation of Zig language tokenization in JavaScript, despite Zig already exposing its own tokenizer" until it was deleted for the compiler's own ([ziglang/zig#19208](https://github.com/ziglang/zig/pull/19208)).

**Rejected.** Generating the grammars from the table: their patterns stay hand-written, and only their words, which the test holds as exactly, could be generated. The words in the parser, where `curios-package` cannot read the reserved ones. The wire words in `Keyword`, which would state again what `curios-abi`, the wire's owner, already spells.
