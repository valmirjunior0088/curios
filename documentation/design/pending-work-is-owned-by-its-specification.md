# Pending work is owned by its specification

**Decision.** Pending work lives under [`roadmap/`](../roadmap/), with its status in the finding or specification that describes it, save a proof of `False`, whose status is its ticket's on [the soundness board](soundness/the-soundness-board.md). Implemented contracts live in the owning reference, rustdoc, crate `README.md` and tests; rationale and rejected alternatives live in a design decision or the owning crate's `README.md`. Neither pending work nor landed capabilities have a second inventory.

## Filing and order

The areas follow this directory's subjects, one directory per area under `roadmap/`. Work belonging to the workspace as a whole is filed loose at its root. A file's name opens with the order its work is taken in:

- `000-findings.md` holds the fixes that can be taken now in two commits at most, and the points still to be investigated before they become one or a specification. Ready fixes come first; an uncertain entry is an investigation, with its own question and stopping condition, and does not hold ready work behind it.
- A specification numbered from `100` describes code that breaks a rule a document states, and opens by linking that rule. A design decision states the intended rule; a gap is a specification here, never a caveat there.
- A specification numbered from `200` describes a removable cost for which no document states a rule.
- A specification numbered from `300` describes a missing capability for which no document states a rule.

Order applies to work whose prerequisites are met. Each specification states whether its next step is a ready fix or delivery, a bounded investigation or decision, or blocked on a named capability or consumer. An investigation states the question it answers and the result that ends it; a blocked step names what releases it. Within each hundred a lower number is taken first among eligible steps. Across areas, a demonstrated conversion inconsistency or other violation that refuses an intended program comes before a removable cost; focused compiler feedback and dependency-record validation come before architectural expansion, and an attributed cost with a consumer comes before a capability with none. A prerequisite decision may be taken ahead of the work it releases, without committing to the whole project that owns that decision. A number is a place in that order, never a permanent identifier: a retired specification leaves its number free, and one filed ahead of others takes a free number at its place or moves the later specifications up with every path that cites them. A filename spells its subject out, and references cite the file directly. Directory listings provide discovery without a maintained index.

## Findings and specifications

A finding is a defect shown. Its entry in a findings file is a bullet led by the finding in bold and naming where, what is wrong, the fix, its check and its size in commits. A finding stays an entry while its fix can be taken now and lands in two commits at most, the second only where a first must land before it. A fix can be taken now where it is stated, or is a choice among stated fixes an answer on the spot settles, and where whoever takes it can produce all it waits on, such as a reading on a quiet machine or another entry landing first. Any other finding is a specification: one whose fix takes three commits or more states its stages, and one that waits on a consumer, on a measurement deciding whether it is worth doing, or on a design no one has drawn is filed not yet refined. A fix that in the making needs a third commit, or rests on a question an answer on the spot cannot settle, is undone and lifted into a specification, never stretched. The commit that fixes a finding deletes its entry.

An entry marked uncertain holds a point seen but not yet shown, to be investigated before it becomes a finding or a specification: it names what was seen and by what, what is not yet known and what would settle it, and its size is unknown until then. Its investigation restates it as a finding, lifts it into a specification, files it as a ticket where it builds a proof of `False`, or drops it where the code does not bear it out.

A proof of `False` is no entry: whoever meets one files its ticket open at once, in a commit of its own, and fixes it in the change at hand only where that change refuses it. A fix of it past the line an entry's is held to is a specification of `soundness/` numbered from `100`, linking the ticket, and retired when the ticket turns fixed.

A specification states what is missing or wrong today and where, its context from the code, its goal, decisions already settled and questions still open, stages each with its own check, its verification and its retirement. One not yet refined is marked **Not refined yet** and states what is known and what it waits on. A specification that waits for a consumer says so. Independent deliveries state separate prerequisites and completion checks: a consumer releases the delivery it needs, and no unrelated stage holds it back. A measurement it depends on states a protocol precise enough to rebuild its scripts; the scripts are not checked in. A scaling fix states its algorithmic bound and counts the work its cause adds over a geometric size grid; a broad regression threshold alone establishes no asymptotic bound. A representation change states which stored bytes may change and which verdicts and executable artifacts must remain equal.

## Landing and retirement

When a stage lands, rewrite the specification it extends to describe the remaining work; do not stack a new specification beside it or leave completed stages as pending. Contracts go to their authoritative owners and rationale to the owning decision as the work lands. Once all the work has landed, update references to those owners, verify that nothing references the specification and delete it. No checked summary replaces it. Open new work only for a limitation named from the code: what is missing, refused or wrong, and where.

**Rationale.** A finding or specification already holds its own status and the context a contributor starts from. A second summary has to change whenever the first changes, and its existence cannot ensure agreement. A landed capability's contract is held by its documentation and tests, so maintaining a second account of what exists adds another place for it to drift. The directory structure exposes pending work and its order without making a reader synchronize an index. An entry and a specification part where a specification pays for itself: writing one and retiring it are a commit each, earned by carrying context, settled decisions and stages across sessions, while an entry holds one fix under one check, which a commit landing before it stretches and a third outgrows. An entry is taken whenever its file is worked, so work waiting on what its taker cannot produce needs a place that states what it waits on.

**Rejected.** A maintained checklist beside the specifications, which duplicates status; a replacement inventory of landed capabilities, which duplicates their owners; filing gaps as caveats in design decisions, which makes an intended rule depend on the current implementation; putting the filing rules only in agent instructions, which leaves contributors without their workspace policy; and sizing a fix by the time it takes or the lines it changes, neither of which can be read off an entry as it is filed or tells a fix that waits.
