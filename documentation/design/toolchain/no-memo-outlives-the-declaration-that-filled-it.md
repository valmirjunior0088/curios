# No memo outlives the declaration that filled it

**Decision.** Every memo either checker keeps is cleared wherever the work budget is restored, and a hit on one is free: the kernel's reduct and type tables at `Memos::begin_declaration` in `curios-cert`, and the elaborator's reducts, canonical and settled refinement keys and elaborations at `Caches::begin_declaration` in `curios-elab`. No table outlives a declaration, so nothing a declaration compiled earlier left behind can change what a later one spends, and a cache can change how fast a compilation runs and never what it decides. Each checker's `what_a_declaration_spends_does_not_depend_on_what_was_reduced_before_it` holds it, and fails once any reduct table is kept across a declaration boundary.

**Rationale.** The budget is restored at every declaration boundary so that whether a declaration type-checks is a fact about the declaration. A table outliving the budget undoes that from the side: a free hit on an entry an earlier declaration filled lets that declaration pay for this one's work, so a declaration can exhaust its budget when compiled after different neighbours. The serial compiler hides that, and a schedule that runs items in parallel would turn it into a verdict that changes between runs. Aligning every table's lifetime with the budget's removes the dependence by construction, where pricing the hits could only argue it away. It is also the rule a compilation of item tasks needs: an item's context holds its own caches, and nothing mutable is shared between items.

**What it costs is reuse across declarations, and there was little.** The closed machine reads a definition's body directly, so the kernel's name-keyed unfold table — the one table that outlived a declaration — served only occurrences inside a local-bearing term or under a case equation, and held 209 entries over the whole standard library when it was removed. Every item's finalization already cleared the elaborator's reducts as it rewrote the item's universe levels. Measured over `/std` on 2026-09-29 against the build before the rule, through the `budget::consumed` sample both checkers take where the budget is restored: the elaborator's heaviest declaration held at 1 712 446 units, and its units summed over every declaration moved by 3 022, from 21 329 257 to 21 332 279. The kernel's heaviest went from 214 925 to 212 852 and its sum from 14.27 M to 13.31 M, recomputing an unfold in its declaration's company costing less than the price a hit on the table had been charged. Over the programs in `programs/`, every diagnostic was unchanged, as was the elaborator's heaviest declaration in every program, and the kernel's was unchanged or up to 2 050 units lower. Wall clock was not separable from the load the machine carried.

**The retention allowance went with it.** A compilation-wide counter bounded what the caches retained, because a table surviving item boundaries times a budget restored at each is no bound at all. With no such table left, every entry is bounded by the budget that built it, so the allowance, its quota and the footprint every term carried to price an insertion were removed.

**Rejected — a table outliving the declaration, its hits free.** The elaborator's reducts of closed terms, until this. Finalization cleared them at the end of every ordinary item, so what crossed an item boundary was what the work after finalization reduced — retried witness goals, drained parked ones — and a declaration that hit such an entry spent less than it would have compiled on its own.

**Rejected — a name-keyed table outliving the declaration, a hit charged what its first computation spent.** The kernel's unfold table, until this. Charging a hit keeps a declaration from spending less than its work, but the recorded price counted that first computation's own free hits on its declaration's tables, so which declaration unfolded a name first decided what every later one was charged for it.

**Rejected — such a table in each checker, each unfold priced alone.** Computing an unfold with its declaration's tables and reduction depth set aside makes its price a function of the name, so a hit and a miss spend the same and the dependence goes. It was implemented in both checkers and measured over `/std` and `programs/` before being withdrawn: every unfold paid its whole cold cost, where a declaration unfolding several names that share work had paid that work once. The heaviest declaration went from 1 712 446 to 3 396 277 units on the elaborator and from 214 925 to 499 599 on the kernel, the units summed over every declaration from 21.3 M to 81.4 M on the elaborator, and the elaborator's heaviest declaration over the programs corpus from about 28 000 units to between 106 000 and 172 000 — to keep a table the closed machine had left 209 entries.

**Rejected — evaluating each definition's head form when it is defined**, charged to its own item and published with it. The price is order-independent, and it fits the summaries a published declaration carries; but it changes acceptance, since a divergent or very expensive constant nothing uses would now be refused, and it spends on every definition rather than on those unfolded.

**Rejected — charging a hit by the size of its result.** The charge is deterministic, but a miss must then cost the same, which leaves the computation behind an unfold unbudgeted and the budget no longer a guarantee that reduction ends.

**Rejected — keeping the warm caches and ordering the schedule** to reproduce the serial order. It serializes what a schedule of item tasks runs in parallel, and keeps the defect.
