# A sum is merged when it is forced, not when it is built

**Decision.** A product of two symbolic sums is its own weak-head form. `NatMul` distributes as it always did when either operand is a literal or carries a single symbolic summand — `2 · (x + 1)`, `x · (y + z)` — and leaves `(a + b) · (c + d)` as a stuck node. Sums and differences are untouched: `NatAdd` merges like terms and hoists its floor, `NatSub` cancels, both in the fold, both on every demand, as before. Distribution of a stuck product is a demand of its own, `Nat::normalize`, asked for by name where a comparison needs the value — `compare_nat` and every bound that rides on it, and the two converters' rule for a pair of `Nat`s one of which holds a stuck product and neither of which is a literal. A literal against anything is the peel's, as it always was: sums and differences are merged and cancelled by the fold, so a side with a symbolic summand — and a stuck product is one — is never a literal, and distributing it would build the polynomial to answer what the first summand settles. `reduce_forced` and every one of its callers are unchanged.

**Rationale.** The normal form has one quadratic step. Merging a sum is linear in its summands; cancelling a difference is linear; distributing a product is every summand of one operand against every summand of the other. On a web of definitions each naming the one before it twice, `rₙ = rₙ₋₁·(1 + rₙ₋₂)`, that step built 1 222 222 monomials to keep 25 412 at ten definitions — 87 seconds and six gigabytes, after three other fixes, to decide `top(n) ≡ 0`, which the first summand settles. With the product stuck, the same normal form is linear in the number of definitions, and the peel decides the comparison on a small term with no new rule: ten definitions in four seconds and a tenth of a gigabyte, where the morning's compiler never finished.

**Why only the product, and not the demand.** The first version of this decision deferred everything — sums, differences, floors — to weak-head form and put normalization behind `Forced`. It reached the same four seconds and cost three held laws (`x − x = 0`, `(x + y) − y = x`, `(x + y) − x = y`), a refinement key that no longer matched `n + n` against `2 · n`, a folded recursion inside a difference that the eager fold used to unfold as a side effect, and every `reduce_forced` on a *type* paying for a polynomial it never asked for. Each of those was a consumer relying on a sum being merged the moment it was built, and each was true: merging is cheap and everything downstream assumes it. Deferring the one expensive step keeps every assumption and every law; deferring the cheap ones bought nothing and needed six mechanisms to repair. The class this belongs to is "a fold does quadratic work past its demand", and only one fold does.

**Nothing definitional is lost.** `(a + b) · (c + d)` converts with `a·c + a·d + b·c + b·d` because a converter meeting a stuck product on either side normalizes both before the peel. A bound `(a + b) · (c + d) <= n` discharges because `compare_nat` normalizes both operands before cancelling. A refinement keyed on a product distributes it as `canonical_scrutinee` reduces its arguments — the fold's `NatMul` arm is what that reduction runs. Two literals, a literal against a sum, and like terms are decided in the fold exactly as before, so the fixture inventory of held laws is unchanged.

**A stuck product is priced where it is distributed.** `operand_bound` at the fold prices a product by its operands' literal widths, and a symbolic operand read as zero bits — a cross product of a million monomials cost one unit, which `Cost`'s own contract names as the case it exists for. `Nat::normalize` charges one collection and one node per product before the first is built, in the concat fold's idiom.

**Rejected — deferring sums and differences as well.** Measured: it reached the same four seconds and lost the three laws above, needed a floor hoist to keep eliminators stepping, a re-force of `rec` operands, a literal-versus-stuck rule that was wrong for differences, and a net in the kernel's structural comparison for a `Nat` shape against a bare application. Every one of those repaired something merging in the fold had provided for free.

**Rejected — a `Reducer::demand()` read by the folds.** A `reduce` call's demand is weak-head either way; the accessor would have been seven implementations of a fact the call site already states, and normalization bound to a shared demand taxes every caller of that demand — which is what happened when it was bound to `reduce_forced`.

**Rejected — a cheaper monomial representation.** Exponent-form monomials make each product O(distinct factors) rather than O(factor count), a real constant the canonical-monomial table already halves; the count of products is the square of the survivors under any representation.

**Precedent.** The `&&`/`||` cliff `documentation/roadmap.md` records among those closed: a fold normalized a tree whole to decide what a stuck operand had already settled. `documentation/soundness/per-term-rules/intrinsic-fold-laws-and-the-free-monoid-peel.md` records the linear combination as the normal form and its soundness; it now states that a product of two symbolic sums reaches that form on demand. [`a-reduction-step-costs-what-it-builds.md`](a-reduction-step-costs-what-it-builds.md) is why the product is priced where it is built.
