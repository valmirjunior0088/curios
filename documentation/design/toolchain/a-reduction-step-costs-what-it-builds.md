# A reduction step costs what it builds

**Decision.** One counter decides whether a declaration type-checks, and it prices construction as well as transitions. A reducer transition costs one unit; a value a reduction builds costs its logical size; a level of guarded reducer recursion costs the native frame it takes. Every fold that can allocate reducer-owned storage charges *before* allocating, through `Reducer::spend`, and a size that overflows while being computed is refused without being compared. The unit is machine-independent — eight logical bytes of scalar payload, or one abstract reference slot, computed in `u64` on every target — and the formulas and constants live in `curios-core`'s `cost`, below both checkers, so the elaborator and the independent kernel price the same program the same way.

A second, compilation-scoped counter bounds what the optional caches *retain*. It is not a second acceptance budget: exhausting it refuses an insertion and leaves evaluation correct and cold. `curios-core`'s `retention` owns it.

**Rationale.** Reduction is the only stage a well-typed program can drive to arbitrary cost, because it is the only stage a *type* can call; every other stage's work is bounded by what elaboration produced. A counter that charged one unit per transition therefore bounded the one thing that could not run away and nothing else — a `PackedBin::concat` copying half a megabyte cost what a `Bool` fold cost. `Nat/shl(1, 400000000)` is three lines with no loop in them and built fifty megabytes of magnitude in under a second while the counter observed a handful of steps.

**The budget is a fact about the program, and that is what every constraint here protects.** It is machine-independent because `curios-js` compiles to wasm32, where `usize` and a bignum's digit width both differ, and the shipped default promises that a program compiling in the playground compiles at the command line. It is *build*-independent for the same reason, which is why the frame row charges the release figure rather than the debug one although both were measured. And it is charged before the allocation rather than after, because a charge taken afterwards is a report.

**Depth is charged per new peak, not per call.** A level's native frame is reclaimed when the level returns, and reduction re-enters itself once per operand of a nested intrinsic and once per link of a spine, so charging every call prices a stack the reduction is not holding — measured, the fixed prelude will not build even at a large multiple of the shipped budget. A high-water mark charges the peak the reduction actually reaches, never refunds, and costs a wide shallow computation nothing.

**One budget goes to two checkers, so they have to cost the same, not merely decide the same.** The compile path hands the same figure to the elaborator and then to the kernel, and a user meets whichever demands more. Two evaluators written separately are expected to differ a little; a *multiple* is two price lists differing rather than two evaluators differing, and it reaches a user as a program one checker accepts and the other refuses for exhaustion with no disagreement about any rule. That has now happened twice — first when a kernel memo hit was charged what the computation it replaced had cost, and again when the kernel's memo was consulted only at its crate boundary while the elaborator's was consulted at every reduction level, which cost 5.3× on the same terms at the same depth. Neither was a rule; both were reach. So cost parity between the two is a property to measure rather than to assume, which is what `curios`' `str_literal_cost_measurements` and `kernel_memo_charge_measurements` are for, and what makes the per-declaration `Consumption` both checkers report worth having at all.

**What one weighted limit buys and what it costs.** Independent transition and construction limits are genuinely more expressive: a transition-heavy and a construction-heavy program can get different verdicts under the two designs. One limit was chosen anyway — it preserves the existing configuration shape, needs one default and one threshold to calibrate, and gives two independent checkers one deterministic quantity to reproduce. The fixed price list states the exchange rate rather than hiding it, and a refusal names the row it was refused on. The cost is visible in the shipped default: it must clear the heaviest prelude declaration, which puts the ceiling on a single construction far above what anyone would call reasonable. `curios-elab`'s `DEFAULT_STEP_BUDGET` carries both ends of that with their measurements.

**Rejected — an operating-system memory limit or an allocator hook.** Platform-dependent, applied too late to say anything useful, and inclusive of memory reduction did not cause. They would also diverge between the native and wasm32 targets, which is the one thing the unit exists to prevent.

**Rejected — exact heap-byte accounting.** A deterministic logical unit refuses pathological construction predictably across platforms; process memory remains subject to ordinary implementation overhead, measured at about 28 bytes per logical unit — see `curios-elab`'s `DEFAULT_STEP_BUDGET`, which records the measurement and what it admits. Pricing the overhead would price the allocator.

**Rejected — bounding the caches by eviction rather than by admission.** Eviction needs a replacement policy and shared-payload lifetime accounting, and makes warmth less predictable. The monotone retention quota refuses new insertions after a conservative allowance is consumed and never refuses evaluation.

**Rejected — charging the recursion row when `recurse` actually grows the stack.** It prices the 32 MiB segment exactly, and makes acceptance depend on the host thread's stack size: a two-megabyte test thread grows on its first call where an eight-megabyte main thread does not. A charge that a thread's stack size can change is not a fact about the program.

**Precedent.** Lean's deterministic heartbeats are precedent for an allocation-sensitive deadline and instructive about where such a counter stops: it increments per small-object allocation rather than per logical byte, so a few large objects are cheap in heartbeats — the same blind spot a transition counter has, reached by another route. Rocq is the closer precedent for measuring allocation *volume*, exposing `Timeout` separately from `AllocLimit` in machine words, which shows the usefulness of volume accounting and also why Curios cannot adopt the mechanism across native and wasm32. Curios remixes the two: Lean's deterministic accounting and stable primary deadline, Rocq's sensitivity to volume, with implementation-dependent allocation units replaced by preflighted logical ones that both checkers reproduce.
