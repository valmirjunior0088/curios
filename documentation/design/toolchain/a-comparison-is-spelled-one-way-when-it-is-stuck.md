# A comparison is spelled one way when it is stuck

**Decision.** `gt` and `ge` are spelled as their `lt` and `le` mirrors from the moment they enter Core: the `/sys` rows for `Nat`, `Byte`, `Int` and `Flt` build `a > b` as `b < a` and `a >= b` as `b <= a`, and no representation below the rows has a `Gt` or `Ge` head at all — Core's intrinsic roster, the erased operations, the continuation intrinsics and the emitter carry four comparison heads, not six. The surface language and the `/syn` propositions keep every spelling they had: `Int/NonNeg` still reads `Int/ge(a, 0)` and `a >= b` is still written that way; each is one term with its mirror.

**Rationale.** `a >= b` and `b <= a` denote one proposition and were two terms — `NatGe(a, b)` and `NatLe(b, a)` — that neither conversion nor scrutinee refinement related, so a decided bound had to be spelled exactly as its `/syn` proposition spelled it. `/std/Flt/finite` was written `Flt/le(min, a)` for no reason but that `Finite` says `Flt/le(min, a)`, and a user's guard `0 <= a` would not discharge `Int/NonNeg(a)`, which says `Int/ge(a, 0)`. That is a spelling deciding a judgment, which is what a normal form exists to prevent. The ground is the one `compare_nat` already states for its own operands: it hands back a stuck comparison with the shared floor peeled so that *`cmp(x + m, y + m)` and `cmp(x, y)` reduce to the same term, which conversion needs*. Mirroring is the same rule at the head rather than the operands.

**Soundness.** `Ge`'s reading of `compare(a, b)` is `Le`'s reading of `compare(b, a)`, and `Gt`'s of `Lt`'s, because the comparison is antisymmetric; on `Flt` every ordered comparison against the NaN is false on both sides, so the mirror holds there too — the binary32 model states both readings and the fixture grid closes them at every closed instantiation. Nothing false is decided that was not decided before; two spellings that were unrelated are now one term.

**Why at the boundary and not only in the reducer.** The first cut mirrored in the reducer alone and broke `/std/Char/of_nat`: a case equation is recorded on its guard *as written*, and the proposition it discharges met the same guard reduced — one spelling in the key, the mirror in the occurrence, no hit. A spelling that never exists cannot be keyed on, so the rows build the mirror. For a while the reducer also kept a rewrite as a net for a `Gt` built by hand; with the heads gone from every roster there is no such term to net, and the rows are the one admission.

**What it costs.** A `>=` written in source renders as `<=` with its operands swapped in a diagnostic and in `Module` display. No fixture moved: the two that build a `>=` decide it to a literal, and a folded comparison folds the same. It is a fact about the roster rather than a reduction rule — there is no rule left for the two checkers to share — so no perimeter entry grades it.

**Rejected — teaching conversion the mirror instead.** A rule in both converters relating `Ge(a, b)` to `Le(b, a)` keeps the spellings apart and adds a case to the trusted comparison, where a mirrored row is one line per head and is exercised by every reduction. Keeping the `Gt`/`Ge` heads below the rows was the same mistake in a smaller coin: a variant no producer reaches is a typing row, a fold rule and an emission path no test can exercise. Normal forms belong to the boundary that admits a spelling and to the reducer, not to the judgment that compares.

**Rejected — rewriting the `/syn` propositions to the canonical spelling.** They would still be one spelling a user has to match; the reducer makes the match unnecessary, and leaves `Int/NonNeg` readable as `0 <= a` in the direction its doc states it.

**Precedent.** `Nat::cancel_common`, which normalizes a stuck comparison's operands "which conversion needs"; `Nat::sum` and `Nat::spine`, which put a sum and a product in one order so `x + y` and `y + x` are one term; the free-monoid peel, which flattens concatenation. Each is a stuck term spelled one way so that conversion compares values and not spellings.
