# A law is decided where it neither respells nor invents

**Decision.** An equation over the intrinsic carriers is taken at one of three places, and which one is settled by what the law would have to *do* there. A **fold** takes it when the reduct is a term the fold can build outright, charged before it is built: `shl(x, k) = 2ᵏ · x` for a literal `k`, a comparison whose two sides are apart modulo the gcd of their coefficients folding to `false`, and `map(xs, f) = xs` where `f`'s body weak-head reduces to its binder. The **peel** takes it when it is an equation between two stuck values that no rewrite of either could state without deriving a proof: two `get`s that read one position of one root, however many windows either reads it through, and a window of a window against the window it names. A **probe-side** decision, asked for by name in both converters, takes it when deciding needs a reducer and rewriting would respell a term a guard was keyed on: two `Bool` terms that agree at every assignment of their atoms. Every one answers `Equal` or declines; none answers a disequality it did not already have.

**Rationale.** The grid of `curios/src/tests/laws.rs` kept these rows refused, and the reasons written beside them turned out to be three obstacles rather than five, each of which one of the places above walks around rather than through. *A reducer may not invent a proof*: rewriting `get(slice(xs, s, l), i)` to `get(xs, s + i)` owes `s + i < len(xs)`, which follows from the two bounds in hand by transitivity and is convertible with neither — but a comparison builds no term, so it owes nothing, and the bounds are never read, which is proof irrelevance. *A fold may not respell*: a guard's refinement is keyed on its written spelling ([A comparison is spelled one way when it is stuck](a-comparison-is-spelled-one-way-when-it-is-stuck.md)), and a fold that normalized a `&&`/`||` tree whole paid for the whole tree at every leaf, so a normal form for `Bool` is closed twice over — and a decision made where two trees are compared needs no normal form. *What a rule builds must be priced*: a left shift is the one fold whose result size its operands do not bound, which kept it out of the laws beside the fold, where there is no reducer; the rule sits one call later, where there is one. Two of the recorded reasons were not obstacles at all. The shift's said the run time truncates, which [Numeric carriers narrow by refusing, never by changing a value](numeric-carriers-narrow-by-refusing-never-by-changing-a-value.md) forbids and the emitter does not do; the map's said extensionality, where the fold was testing a lambda's spelling and both checkers already converted `(v) => v + 0` with `(v) => v`.

**What each lift owes, which is the bar the five were held to.** The rows are stated first, at every carrier the rule reaches, with a control beside them — a claim that is *not* a law and that the lift must leave refused, so a rule widened past its soundness moves a control to the held side and the goal test names it. The row moves from refused to held, where both checkers see it as an `Eq/refl()` proof. A value grid in `curios-core` holds the rule to ground truth at closed instantiations — a fold pinned to its stated reduct so that a law which stops firing fails, a verdict under its own obligation with its tally asserted so that a grid deciding nothing fails too. One mutation is run and caught, and the perimeter entry names the mutation and the instantiation that caught it, so it can be run again. Whatever the rule builds is charged before it is built, and a count or a cap is the theory's and never the host's. The rule sits where the two checkers cannot disagree about it. The evidence for each is its perimeter entry's — [Open fold laws and the sum normal form](../../soundness/per-term-rules/open-fold-laws-and-the-sum-normal-form.md) for the folds and [Intrinsic fold laws and the free-monoid peel](../../soundness/per-term-rules/intrinsic-fold-laws-and-the-free-monoid-peel.md) for the verdicts — and is not repeated here.

**What it costs.** The `Reducer` seam gained `fresh_binder`, moved up from `ClosedHost`, because `is_identity` is the one fold that looks under a binder. The prelude's build cost did not move: 89.4 s of wall after the last of the five against 89.3 s before the first, 55.3 s against 55.4 s inside declarations, one instrumented debug sample each, read off the profile the build files beside its archive — in which the truth table runs five times, for a millisecond in all. `Comparison` gained the one non-empty set of orderings it could not spell, forced unequal with the order open. The truth table is declined past eight atoms between the two sides, which costs completeness and never a verdict; the grid states the same law held at eight and refused at nine. And a law may now move a *refusal* between two spellings, never a value: at a run-time zero and a count past the carrier, `shl` answers `0` where `2ᵏ · x` refuses on materializing the coefficient, which matters only where a term solved by unification reaches the program in its reduced spelling.

**Rejected — a normal form for `Bool`.** A reduced ordered BDD over the atoms in structural-hash order is canonical, and as a fold it is the rewriting both records above reject; probe-side it is the truth table with a different inner loop under the same cap. Algebraic normal form over GF(2) is canonical and exponential exactly where the common case is not: an `n`-way `||` is `2ⁿ - 1` monomials.

**Rejected — reducing a tautology to `true`.** A comparison-time decision closes `Eq(e, true)` by `refl`, and `Bool/holds_of_eq` turns that into `Bool/Holds(e)` in one written step, but an obligation the elaborator fills *by reduction* is not filled. Filling it would take the table into the fold, where a whole-tree walk at every reduction is the cliff the record already names.

**Rejected — reducing through a window with a library lemma.** A fold could rewrite the node and build the bound from a `/std` lemma reached through the `SyntaxRegistry`, adding no axiom. The trusted reducer's output would then name a library declaration, and the rule could not fire where that slot is unfilled — the standard library compiling itself is such a place.

**Rejected — asking conversion whether a mapped function is the identity.** Probe-side, both converters would compare the function with the identity lambda and then the list with the other side. It only equates, so `len` and `get` would not see through the map; it needs an arm for a `map` against a term that is no intrinsic; and the elaborator's worklist converter has no way to ask a yes-or-no question without a sandboxed sub-conversion that may solve metavariables on the way.

**Rejected — a right shift through a division node.** `shr(x, k)` is `⌊x / 2ᵏ⌋` and could join the division family, but `NatDiv` carries a proof that its divisor is nonzero and a reducer may not invent one. The route that remains is handing the dividend to the Euclidean split directly; it is a refused row of the grid with that reason.

**Deferred — parity as a clash.** The gcd test is a fold verdict and the peel was left alone: conversion needs only "not equal", which it already had. Read by `classify_nat` as a `Clash` it would reach inversion as *impossible* and let `match h end` close a hypothesis `Eq(x * 2 + 1, y * 2)` with no arm — useful, and the vacuous-elimination route, so it is a row of its own with [Coverage](../../soundness/per-term-rules/coverage.md)'s evidence behind it rather than a by-product of this one.

**Not this decision's.** Hypotheses feeding the algebra: with none in scope `compare_nat` is already complete for `<` and `<=`, a side still holding a symbol being unbounded, so beyond the gcd test what an omega-style procedure adds is hypotheses, which [A bound is stated in a decided proposition and discharged by reduction](../language/a-bound-is-stated-in-a-decided-proposition-and-discharged-by-reduction.md) parks as a larger and separate capability. Summands paired up to conversion, map fusion, the symbolic shift count and the table past its cap are candidates, and each is a refused row of the grid with its reason, which is where a candidate lives.
