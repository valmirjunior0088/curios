# Numeric carriers narrow by refusing, never by changing a value

**Decision.** `Nat` and `Int` are unbounded in Core — the free-monoid `Nat` carries no width and `nat_bound` computes in `curios-num`'s unbounded `Natural` — narrow to `u32` and `i32` at the erased stages, where `curios-num`'s `scalar` states their exact arithmetic, and ride a 31-bit `i31ref` at runtime. An operation whose meaning needs a width is therefore not one of these: rotation and leading-zero counts were `Nat` and `Int` operations that narrowed through `to_u32` to answer, which made them 32-bit notions wearing an unbounded type and left them *stuck* above `2³²` — partial in the theory. They are gone from the surface; a width-carrying `/std/Bits` is where they belong. A narrowing may *refuse* a value it cannot represent; it may never change one. So the emitter panics on a result that leaves the i31 envelope rather than truncating it — naming the carrier and `BigNat` or `BigInt` as where larger values live, per [a refusal is a panic the emitter renders](a-refusal-is-a-panic-the-emitter-renders.md), and a stage's constant folder must decline rather than wrap past its own carrier. Values beyond a carrier belong to `/std/BigNat` and `/std/BigInt`, not to a wider primitive.

**Rationale.** The stratification is what lets the theory be unbounded and the backend be fast, and refusal is the only narrowing discipline that keeps the two consistent: a proof stated over Core's `Nat` remains true of the compiled program, because that program either computes the proved answer or computes none. Wrapping breaks exactly that, making a proved theorem false of the running program — which is why the trap is not a wart to be optimized away but the mechanism holding the layering together. Each layer refuses at the boundary it owns and needs no knowledge of the ones beneath it: the erased carrier is `u32` whatever the target holds, and `31` appears in `curios-cont`'s emitter alone.

**Where each refusal lives.** There are three boundaries, not one, and each refuses at its own width. `curios-elab`'s narrowing into the erased carriers reports an overflowing numeral as an error rather than wrapping it. `curios-num`'s `scalar` then refuses a *computed* value past `u32`/`i32` rather than wrapping it, so a folder records a trap where it used to record a different number. And `curios-cont` refuses at materialization on every path a constant can take — boxed, held in a register, and by excluding an out-of-range scalar from constant hoisting, since a trap is not a constant instruction and has to stay at its execution point. A *materialized* constant and the same expression computed therefore agree on which programs trap, and only a materialized value is subject to the third boundary: `1073741824 + 1073741824` is `2³¹`, a legitimate `u32`, so a fold carrying it through `to_str` builds no i31 and the program prints it, while the same expression with a runtime operand materializes one and traps. Those are two programs, one of which crosses the third boundary and one of which does not, and a value that never needs a runtime representation is no more subject to the runtime's width than a Core-level value is. `curios`'s `closed_computation_through_the_envelope_folds_in_u32` and `folded_literal_outside_the_envelope_traps_at_materialization` are the two halves, asserted separately.

**What refusal costs is associativity, and a transformation that reorders must pay it.** Wrapping is a total group operation, which is why a compiler for a wrapping carrier may reassociate freely; refusing makes the operation *partial*, and a partial operation can be associative wherever both sides are defined while differing in **where** it is defined. So an algebraic law licenses value-equality only: a pass that changes the *order* operands are combined in owes a second check — that no partial result of the new order falls outside the carrier when the total is inside it. `curios-ersd`'s monoid rebase is the one pass that reorders, and it is licensed on exactly that, not on associativity alone. The rows that have it are `NatAdd` (non-negative operands, so every partial sum is at most the total) and `NatOr`/`IntOr` (total inside the envelope); the rows that do not are multiplication, whose zero annihilator makes the total smaller than a partial, and signed addition, which cancels — `((1 * 0) * 2¹⁶) * 2¹⁶` computes `0` and its reversal traps. Both were registered, and both turned a program that computed into one that trapped until the licence was stated.

**What the boundaries are not allowed to do is disagree about a value.** A `scalar` that reduces a shift count modulo 32 as Wasm does folds `Nat/shl(1, 40)` to `256` while Core computes `2⁴⁰`, and an emitter guard that tests a product `i32` has already truncated lets it through: a *changed* value rather than a representable one meeting a limit, which is what the first paragraph forbids. **Core is the oracle**: an erased stage must produce Core's value or refuse, never a third. `curios`'s `a_shift_past_the_carrier_width_answers_the_arithmetic` holds the computing half — `⌊v / 2^k⌋` is zero past the width, not `v >> (k mod 32)` — `overflowing_computations_trap_at_the_backend_boundary` the emitter's refusal, and `a_shift_past_the_widened_intermediate_traps_folded_and_executed` the fold's, which compiles both the tainted and the folded tables — a fixture built on `runtime_traps` alone exercises the emitter and never the fold, and cannot witness the disagreement this paragraph forbids. The fold clamps the count at the carrier's width rather than widening the intermediate to `u64`, which closes a large value with a small count and a small value with a large count but not their product, `2⁷⁰`'s low sixty-four bits being zero and reading back as a representable answer.

**Rejected.** Boxing the overflow — `i31ref` with a wide struct fallback — which preserves the range but pays a two-way test at every unbox to buy one bit, `2³¹` to `2³²−1`, that `BigNat` already covers. Declaring the carrier, making `Nat` a 31-bit wrapping natural as OCaml's `int` and Rocq's `Uint63` do, which deletes every check and the 64-bit multiply widening but writes a backend-derived width into a theory deliberately built unbounded, and makes `n + 1 > n` false of the type theorems are stated over. Making `Nat` arbitrary-precision with a small-value fast path, as Lean does, which duplicates `/std/BigNat` and moves a bignum implementation onto the trusted side of the perimeter — where [the soundness perimeter](../../soundness) grades intrinsic signatures *auditable only* and holds their fold laws by value grids rather than proofs — while `BigNat` over `Bits` is proved.
