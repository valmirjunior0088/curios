# Equality is decided where a decider exists and is `Eq` everywhere, joined by one law

**Decision.** Curios has two equalities and gives each its own work. `Eq`, the identity type, is equality at every type: what a law states of values no decider compares — a `Map` lookup's `Option(V)`, a function, a type, a proof, an `Io` description — and what a term is rewritten along by `Eq/subst`. `==`, `Eql`'s decider, is equality where one exists, and a fact about such values is stated decided, `Bool/Holds(a == b)`, as [A bound is stated in a decided proposition and discharged by reduction](../arithmetic/a-bound-is-stated-in-a-decided-proposition-and-discharged-by-reduction.md) states every relation a decider exists for: a length, a count, a code. No fact is stated both ways. A law that concludes two values are one concludes the equation, an order's tie (`Ord/eq_of_ord`) and an encoding's injectivity (`Map/Key/injective`) as `Nat/le/antisym` does: it is what a proof rewrites along and matches to `refl()`, and it crosses from no decided equality. The two meet in one place, the law `Eql` carries that `a == b` holds exactly where `Eq()(a, b)` does, so the bridge between them is one lemma of the concept and no carrier states its own. The elaborator closes a goal whichever way it is written: a decided one by reduction and the arithmetic over the facts in scope, and an `Eq` goal at a carrier with a lawful `Eql` by deciding it and crossing the law.

**Rationale.**

- **Neither equality can do the other's work.** `Eq` holds at types no decider compares — equality of two functions from `Int` would need every input — and is what `Eq/subst` rewrites along; a decided equality is what reduction decides and the elaborator discharges, which no `Eq` is. F\* keeps both for this reason: its decidable `=` is restricted to `eqtype`, and its propositional `==` holds at every type.
- **Two equalities fragment where they can disagree or state one fact twice.** Lean's `BEq` and `DecidableEq` form a diamond, and a type deriving both loses `decide`, since the law joining them, `LawfulBEq`, is opt-in; MathComp states facts as `x == y` and as `x = y` and crosses between them with `eqP` at each boundary. `Eql` carries its laws, no lax face being offered, so `==` cannot disagree with `Eq`, and a fact has one form by its kind.
- **Which form is written buys no automation.** The elaborator closes each, so a statement takes the form its kind calls for, not the one a procedure reaches.

**Rejected.** `Eq` dropped, `==` the only equality: no law over a value without a decider could be stated, and nothing rewritten along an equation. The decided equality dropped: a bound at a decidable carrier would be an `Eq` the elaborator never discharges, written by hand at each literal. A bridge per carrier: one law stated once for each, a copy each to keep. A law beside a decider that may break it, as `LawfulBEq`: the diamond Lean's community proposes to close by making the law mandatory. An order's tie stated decided, as Lean's `LawfulBEqCmp` states it beside `LawfulEqCmp`: the statement names `Ord`'s own `Eql` superclass, and inside the witness of a family nested through a container that superclass is the one of the witness under definition, so the container's `==` written in the body and the one in the law's type do not convert there, and every derived proof would cross to `Eq` and back to say it.
