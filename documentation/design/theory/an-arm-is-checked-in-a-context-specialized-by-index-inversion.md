# An arm is checked in a context specialized by index inversion

**Decision.** An arm of a nominal elimination is checked in its context specialized by the most-general solution of `actual indices ~ case targets`, the Goguen–McBride–McKinna specialization step: each variable the solution solves is bound to its solution as a `let` binds a local, at the solution's own type, the body is checked as written against the expectation — typing reads a solved variable at that type, and every other reader reads the arm by value, the solution substituted — and every local whose type mentions a solved variable is assumed again at its specialized type, innermost and retracted with the arm. One unifier, `curios-analysis`'s `invert`, solves the equations as one problem, in order, over the arm's binders and the outer variables the scrutinee's indices name. Its rules are deletion, solution, injectivity, conflict and cycle, and each equation is read through the solutions before it, each side as the arm's typing reads it, by the checker's judgment reduction, so an index a case equation in force answers up to conversion is read as the case value it assumes. Where both sides are variables, the scrutinee's is refined to the case's. The occurs check, read through the definitions and refinements in force, refuses a cycle, and an equation none of these decides stands aside and specializes nothing. A variable scrutinee is bound to the case's value in nominal, `Bool`, `Switch`, `Nat`, `List` and `Bytes` arms alike, which definitional K licenses; an expression scrutinee, which is no variable to bind, stands as a case equation held beside the arm's binders ([Case equations and their key](../soundness/case-equations-and-their-key.md)). Each arm inhabits the match's goal at its case. An arm may be absent only where the unifier proves it impossible or an arm that writes a binder stands for the cases no arm names, one it proves impossible is typed by no rule of the certifier's, and the large-elimination guard's side condition is decided, not approximated. A case the facts in scope refute is no absent arm: the elaborator writes its arm, the zero-arm match on the contradiction it proves there ([Patterns are compiled where their types are known](../surface/patterns-are-compiled-where-their-types-are-known.md)), and the guard counts it as any written arm.

A match is typed at its goal, the type expected where it stands, stated once: each arm inhabits it under its case's solution, a variable scrutinee and its variable indices substituted by the case, and over an expression scrutinee the goal's occurrences of that expression standing for the case, as with-abstraction reads them (`curios_core::goal_at`). Where nothing is expected the goal is a metavariable the arms solve, so the result depends on no case. No match writes its result: one that depends on the scrutinee beyond what the expected type writes is stated where the match stands, by a declared result, a `let`'s annotation or a helper whose signature states the family, and where nothing states it the convoy, `match … end(evidence)`, is written by hand.

The zero-arm match is the one way to eliminate an empty type: `match contradiction end` wherever a type is expected, and `/std` exports no `absurd`. Where the facts in scope refute each other and no written term says so, the elaborator proves the contradiction ([A bound that follows from the facts in scope is proved by the elaborator](../arithmetic/a-bound-that-follows-from-the-facts-in-scope-is-proved-by-the-elaborator.md)): at a case of a match no arm reaches it writes the match that eliminates it, and elsewhere `/std/proved(@False)` produces it for a match written there.

**Rationale.**

- **One unifier for both checkers.** Unifying indices against targets is a function of the terms it is handed, so a second copy would be a second run, not a second opinion. Both checkers hand it the indices as written and it reads each side by the checker's judgment reduction, since a side one checker reads plainly and the other by its judgment is an arm one excuses and the other demands. What differs is how each checker applies the solution — the elaborator through its counterfactual refinement store, the certifier by binding each solved variable to its solution — and which locals are re-typed at what type is shared again, `curios_analysis::retyped`. The two applications are one answer only where the solution has the variable's own type: a variable, outer or a binder, is checked at its index's type by subsumption, so a value typed at the index's may sit above it, and binding the variable to it there is ill-typed. So the unifier solves such a variable only where the judge holds the value's sort below the variable's (`Judge::sort_below`): the certifier decides it under the declaration's constraints, and the elaborator, which infers a declaration's levels, records the relation where nothing refuses it, so the declaration states what its arm needs and the certifier finds it there; a variable left unsolved stands as it is to both. The convoy pattern is what an elaborator writes when the certifier applies nothing; specializing in the certifier makes it unnecessary in an arm taken once.
- **One problem, as the step states it.** The rules are the step's, as [Cockx and Devriese](https://doi.org/10.1017/S095679681800014X) state them and Agda's and Lean's unifiers run them, over one set of variables, so an equation that needs a binder and an outer variable at once is solved like any other. A variable forced twice becomes, read through its first solution, one equation between its two forcings. An undecided component leaves its siblings to be decided, since injectivity makes each component an equation of its own. A free monoid cancels a generator, so two words that each start with a known element are one value exactly where the elements are one and so are the words after them. The occurs check closes a value's locals under the solutions, the definitions in scope and the refinements in force, since a reader of the solution meets a refined term's value in its place: under an arm that refined `p.0` to `node(t)`, the solution `t := node(p.0)` would read back to `t`.
- **A solved variable is bound, as a `let` is.** A `let`-bound local is read by value by every reader ([`curios-cert`'s README](../../../curios-cert/README.md#a-let-is-bound-for-typing-and-read-by-value)), so binding a solved variable to its solution hands every reader the substituted arm the specialization step states, while typing walks the arm as written, reading the variable at the type its solution was given once rather than typing a copy of the solution at each occurrence, and a reader of the finished term reads the arm in the scope the certifier checked it in. The binding is at the solution's type rather than the variable's: a case value is typed at its case's targets, which an index equation the unifier leaves standing, between forms it does not take apart, keeps apart from the scrutinee's own type, so bound at the variable's type the `let` would hold a value of another type, and at its own each occurrence is typed as the substituted solution was. The elaborator assumes a variable scrutinee again at its case's type, so both type an occurrence of it in its own arm alike, and what the type it was declared at states, an equation left standing among it, is a fact where the match stands and none in the arm. The elaborator keeps its store apart from its definitions because its judgments about a metavariable born outside the arm must not see the arm's solution; the certifier holds no metavariable, so there the solution is an ordinary binding.
- **A goal stated once is what a hypothesis mentioning the scrutinee can meet.** A family over fresh index and scrutinee binders is typed under those binders, at which a hypothesis about the scrutinee is not typed; the goal is typed where the match stands, where the hypothesis is.
- **Inversion decides nothing at a `Prop`-valued position.** Injectivity and tag disjointness are claims about values a program can tell apart, and a proposition's inhabitants cannot be told apart: reading `Two/a()` against `Two/b()` as a clash, or decomposing `Tag/t(a)` against `Tag/t(7)` into a relevant `a := 7`, each inhabits `False`. The rule is read off the declaration's `result_sort` and only ever refuses (`curios`'s `tests::rules::index`).
- **Two forcings of one binder that clash make the case impossible.** `refl(@z) : (z, z)` against `Eq()(false, true)` forces `z` two ways by injective steps, which excuses the arm as two constructors in one position would; short of a definite clash — a variable that may yet be the constructor, an opaque function, a disagreement only arithmetic sees — the arm stays mandatory. A variable forced twice is one equation between its two forcings, so a variable of the scrutinee's indices forced two ways is the same conflict met from the other side, `mk() : (0, 1)` against `Two(n, n)`, and an absent arm is asked by the call a written arm is specialized by: an arm may be absent exactly where one written is typed by no rule of the certifier's.
- **A variable met against a value that holds it as a proper part makes the case impossible.** No finite value is its own proper part, which the conflict rule's sibling in Agda's unifier, *Cycle*, states. Here the part is read beneath the layers injectivity takes apart: a relevant family's constructor, a tuple, an element a word spells out. It is read the same way at the top, as a stretch of a word that spells out an element, or as a summand of a `Nat` above a positive floor. So `Eq()(l, [1, ..l])`, `Eq()(n, n + 1)` and `Eq()(t, node(t, t))` have no arm. A proposition's constructor breaks the chain, since irrelevance makes `wrap(p)` one value with `p`. So does an application, whose value is no part of it as written, and so does a stretch beneath nothing, which may be the whole word.
- **An arm the indices rule out is the elaborator's to type and not the certifier's.** No value of the scrutinee's type is that case, so no reduction takes the arm, which is what excuses its absence. The elaborator types an arm a reader wrote and refuses there what does not type; the certifier also meets the arm in a type the elaborator derived, a `let`'s annotation or a goal at an instance, where a substitution fixed an index and the arm was typed under a solution the instance does not have.
- **A case equation is an elaboration convenience, not a certification contract.** It fires only where the reducer meets the recorded spelling whole, and β-substitution manufactures respellings no finite key set covers. A definition whose typing depends on one the reducer cannot meet has the convoy, `match … end(evidence)`, as its strategy-independent spelling.
- **The match is the one elimination of an empty type.** A function from one is a zero-arm match with a parameter in front, so `absurd` would be a second spelling. The match eliminates any type that reduces to an empty inductive under the arm's refinements — `/std/Str/Valid/from_bad`'s arm, where the proof's type is `False` only inside the arm — and `refuted` states no result and eliminates nothing.

**Rejected.**

- **Substituting the solution through the arm's body and expectation**: typing is handed a copy of the solution at each occurrence of its variable, and what the certifier checks is not the arm written, so a reader of the finished term, which reads the arm as written, reads it in no scope the certifier checked it in.
- **A convoy the elaborator generates, so an arm-used hypothesis rides into a family**, which re-binds the hypothesis and touches emitted terms where specializing in the certifier touches none.
- **A written motive kept beside the goal, `match x: (i, s) => P(i, s)`.** It is a second statement of a match's result, with a binder count, a typing rule and a grammar position of its own in both checkers, for the results the expected type does not write; a helper's signature states each of those as any declaration states its type, and a convoy where nothing does, so with the motive gone a match is typed by one rule.
- **Eliminators as first-class functions** taking the result family as an argument, which would need a generated matcher per match site: `Match` is an intrinsic node whose arms carry per-constructor index refinement.
- **First-match-wins row priority.** Two arms may match one value only where the arm written first is a case of the other, so reading the arms first to last and reading each as its pattern less the cases written before it give one answer, and a match means the same in every order of its arms that is accepted. Two arms that match one value with neither a case of the other are refused, since their order would decide what the match means, and so is an arm no value reaches; a reader who assumes first-match order is never misled, the general arm standing after its exceptions ([Patterns are compiled where their types are known](../surface/patterns-are-compiled-where-their-types-are-known.md)). What priority buys is one arm for `(_, nan(_))` written after `(nan(_), _)`, where the rule asks for an arm for each remaining case of the first column; what it costs a proof is clauses that hold only past the ones before them.
- **An `absurd` convenience**, a second spelling of a primitive.
- **A fold, a `Nat`, `List` or `Bytes` arm binding an induction hypothesis about the tail.** The fold at the tail is the same arms over the tail, so reduction takes each arm at every tail of the value, where the scrutinee is not that arm's case: specialized to its case, an arm under `h: Holds(n == 2)` whose zero case answers `h` is `h` at `2`, typed at `0`, and what an arm knows of the scrutinee at a tail, that the tail is one of its suffixes, no equation states. So a fold's arm is specialized by nothing, and a hypothesis about the scrutinee rides in a family restated per tail and applied back, the convoy. A recursion on the tail through a case split states the same induction with every arm specialized, and the size-change check grades it ([Totality of the erased program](../soundness/totality-of-the-erased-program.md)).
