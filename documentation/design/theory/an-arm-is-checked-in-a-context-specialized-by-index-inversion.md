# An arm is checked in a context specialized by index inversion

**Decision.** An arm of a nominal elimination is checked with the most-general solution of `actual indices ~ case targets` substituted into the body, the expectation and the type of every local it mentions, re-assumed innermost and retracted with the arm: the Goguen–McBride–McKinna specialization step, stated directly. Both unification directions run one unifier, `curios-analysis`'s `invert` — pinning an arm binder to the actual it must equal, and refining an outer variable to the target it is forced to equal — with the occurs check refusing a parameter cycle. A variable scrutinee is substituted by the case's value in nominal, `Bool` and `Switch` arms alike, which definitional K licenses; an expression scrutinee, which no substitution can name, stands as a case equation held beside the arm's binders ([Case equations and their key](../soundness/case-equations-and-their-key.md)). Each arm inhabits the motive at its constructor's targets. An arm of a fold — an elimination of `Nat`, `List`, `Bits` or `Bytes` whose cons arm reads its induction hypothesis — is specialized by nothing: it inhabits the motive at its case value, with the scrutinee left as it stands. An arm may be absent only where the unifier proves it impossible or an arm that writes a binder stands for the cases no arm names, one it proves impossible is typed by no rule of the kernel's, and the large-elimination guard's side condition is decided, not approximated.

Where a motive is elided in a checked position, the result is stated once, at the ambient goal (`MatchResult::Ambient`), and each arm inhabits it under its case's substitution; over an expression scrutinee the goal's occurrences of that expression stand for the case, as with-abstraction reads them. A fold whose arm reads its induction hypothesis, a written motive and an elimination in inference position keep the family.

The zero-arm match is the one way to eliminate an empty type: `match contradiction end` in checking position, `match contradiction : (_) => A end` where the result must be spelled, and `/std` exports no `absurd`. Where the facts in scope refute each other and no written term says so, `/std/Bool/False/refuted()` produces the contradiction, its bound proved by the elaborator ([A bound that follows from the facts in scope is proved by the elaborator](../arithmetic/a-bound-that-follows-from-the-facts-in-scope-is-proved-by-the-elaborator.md)), and the match eliminates it.

**Rationale.**

- **One unifier for both checkers.** Unifying indices against targets is a function of the terms it is handed, so a second copy would be a second run, not a second opinion. What differs is how each checker applies the solution — the elaborator through its refinement store, the kernel by substitution under the case equation — and which locals are re-typed at what type is shared again, `curios_analysis::retyped`. The convoy pattern is what an elaborator writes when the kernel applies nothing; specializing in the kernel makes it unnecessary in an arm taken once.
- **An arm of a fold is taken where its scrutinee is not its case.** The fold at the tail is the same arms over the tail, so reduction takes each arm at every tail of the value. An arm specialized to its case reads a hypothesis about the scrutinee at a value the scrutinee never has: under `h: Holds(n == 2)`, `match n: (_) => False | 0 => h | k + 1; ih => ih end` is `h` at `2`, typed at `0`. No partial specialization is sound either: what an arm knows of the scrutinee at a tail is that the tail is one of its suffixes, which no equation states. A hypothesis about the scrutinee therefore rides in the motive, the one place it is restated per tail, and a case split, whose reduct never holds the elimination again, keeps the specialization.
- **A stated ambient result is what a hypothesis mentioning the scrutinee can meet.** Once the context is specialized per arm, a closed motive's remaining obligation is its own typing under fresh binders, which such a hypothesis cannot satisfy.
- **Inversion decides nothing at a `Prop`-valued position.** Injectivity and tag disjointness are claims about values a program can tell apart, and a proposition's inhabitants cannot be told apart: reading `Two/a()` against `Two/b()` as a clash, or decomposing `Tag/t(a)` against `Tag/t(7)` into a relevant `a := 7`, each inhabits `False`. The rule is read off the declaration's `result_sort` and only ever refuses (`curios`'s `tests::board::index_tests`).
- **Two forcings of one binder that clash make the case impossible.** `refl(@z) : (z, z)` against `Eq()(false, true)` forces `z` two ways by injective steps, which excuses the arm as two constructors in one position would; short of a definite clash — a variable that may yet be the constructor, an opaque function, a disagreement only arithmetic sees — the arm stays mandatory. A variable of the scrutinee's indices forced two ways is the same conflict met from the other side, `mk() : (0, 1)` against `Two(n, n)`, and an absent arm is asked of both directions, by the call a written arm is specialized by: an arm may be absent exactly where one written is typed by no rule of the kernel's.
- **An arm the indices rule out is the elaborator's to type and not the kernel's.** No value of the scrutinee's type is that case, so no reduction takes the arm, which is what excuses its absence. The elaborator types an arm a reader wrote and refuses there what does not type; the kernel also meets the arm in a type the elaborator derived, a `let`'s annotation or a goal at an instance, where a substitution fixed an index and the arm was typed under a solution the instance does not have.
- **A case equation is an elaboration convenience, not a certification contract.** It fires only where the reducer meets the recorded spelling whole, and β-substitution manufactures respellings no finite key set covers. A definition whose typing depends on one the reducer cannot meet has the convoy, `match … end(evidence)`, as its strategy-independent spelling.
- **The match is the one elimination of an empty type.** A function from one is a zero-arm match with a parameter in front, so `absurd` would be a second spelling. The match eliminates any type that reduces to an empty inductive under the arm's refinements — `/std/Str/Valid/from_bad`'s arm, where the proof's type is `False` only inside the arm — and `refuted` names no motive and eliminates nothing.

**Rejected.**

- **Widening the elaborator's convoy so an arm-used hypothesis rides into the motive**, which re-binds the hypothesis and touches emitted terms where specializing in the kernel touches none.
- **First-match-wins row priority.** Two arms may match one value only where the arm written first is a case of the other, so reading the arms first to last and reading each as its pattern less the cases written before it give one answer, and a match means the same in every order of its arms that is accepted. Two arms that match one value with neither a case of the other are refused, since their order would decide what the match means, and so is an arm no value reaches; a reader who assumes first-match order is never misled, the general arm standing after its exceptions ([Patterns are compiled where their types are known](../surface/patterns-are-compiled-where-their-types-are-known.md)). What priority buys is one arm for `(_, nan(_))` written after `(nan(_), _)`, where the rule asks for an arm for each remaining case of the first column; what it costs a proof is clauses that hold only past the ones before them.
- **An `absurd` convenience**, a second spelling of a primitive.
