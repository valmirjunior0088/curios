# `Prop` is strict, proof-irrelevant, and definitionally K

**Decision.** Beside the cumulative `Type` hierarchy, Curios has a strict `Prop` with definitional proof irrelevance: `Prop : Type 0`, a proposition is admitted at `Type` through subsumption, and large elimination out of `Prop` is guarded. `Eq : Prop` under irrelevance makes the theory definitionally K, permanently: proofs are certificates to be erased, not data to compute with. The checkers decide a sound, budgeted fragment of that theory. Both decide a comparison at a `Prop`-sorted type by irrelevance before either side is reduced, wherever neither side is flexible. K-like reduction — firing a relevant match on a proof of a one-constructor proposition whose payload the indices pin, without reducing the proof — is not a rule, so a cast along a hypothetical proof, `Eq/subst(P, p, v)` with `p` a hypothesis, stays stuck, and matching on `p` first is the spelling that computes. A proposition whose constructor carries a proof payload, as `Accessible` does, eliminates into data by reducing that proof, Lean's position rather than Rocq's.

**Rationale.**

- **Irrelevance makes proofs erasable by construction.** Any two proofs of a proposition are equal, so no program depends on which it received, and the large-elimination guard keeps that erasure sound. K licenses index inversion's deletion rule and the substitution of a variable scrutinee by its case value; inversion is shared, so that rule, an acceptance, sits in the trusted base with its premise graded in [Index inversion and K](../soundness/elimination/index-inversion-and-k.md).
- **Reducing a proof to learn it equals another proof is work whose answer was known**, and it need not end. Abel and Coquand (LMCS 16(2:14), 2020) show an impredicative `Prop` with an irrelevant equality loses normalization once a cast along a proof reduces without inspecting it, and in Curios the loop is reachable with no K at all, inside an arm whose case equation makes the proof `refl`. The budget refuses it, but a checker that reduces proofs first spends its budget there (`tests::board::a_proof_is_not_reduced_to_compare_it_with_another`).
- **Completeness is not the goal, and cannot be.** An impredicative, definitionally irrelevant `Prop` with singleton elimination into data is Lean's position, where Carneiro (*The Type Theory of Lean*, 2019, §3.1) shows definitional equality undecidable and any checker for it non-transitive.
- **K reduction has no program to serve.** Every `Eq/subst` and `refl` match in `/std` lands in a proposition, where irrelevance decides it, and a match's refinement serves where it would not. Erasure already takes a proof match's single arm without evaluating the proof, reading each pinned payload from the scrutinee's index (`tests::erasure::value_tests`).

**Rejected.**

- **Univalence**, forever: under K all proofs of an identity are equal, so a univalent equality with content cannot be added.
- **Rocq's K criterion**, firing when the motives at the recorded and constructor indices convert: Rocq's `seq` binds nothing in its arm, while `refl(@z)` binds a payload the step would have to choose.
- **Recording the scrutinee's family instance on the `match`**, as Rocq's `CaseInvert` does: the kernel reduces a `match` in a type position before typing it, so the record would be believed before it is checked.
- **Restricting relevant elimination to empty and pinned-payload propositions**, as Rocq, Agda and Gilbert, Cockx, Sozeau and Tabareau (POPL 2019) do for decidability: it would cost `/std/WellFounded/recurse`, which eliminates `Accessible` into data, and Curios makes no decidability claim for it to restore.
- **A loop detector in reduction**, where the step budget already refuses deterministically.
