# A witness body may be written by the compiler

**Decision.** A witness declaration may leave its body to the compiler — `satisfy Spell(Point) { .. }`, or `satisfy (@A: Type, use Spell(A)) => Spell(Tree(A)) { .. }` under a telescope, alone or in an `and` group — and the compiler writes the body from the declaration of the type in the key. The block holds `..` alone: the dots are no entry, nothing is written beside them and they take no comma, so a body is written whole or derived whole and every witness ends at its `}` (`curios-surface`'s `a_witness_takes_a_block_of_its_entries_or_of_the_dots_alone`). The signature stays the programmer's: it registers, keys and meets the orphan and one-witness-per-key rules as a written witness does (`curios`'s `the_signature_refusals_fire_on_a_derived_witness_as_on_a_written_one`). Lowering puts `Transient::Derive` in body position, so the telescope's binders and premises are in scope; `curios-elab`'s `elaborate_derive` refuses a sealed concept outside its module, looks the concept up in the `DerivationSyntax` roster (`curios-common/src/syntax.rs`) and elaborates the Core the derivation returns under the same expectation, so the body is typed, resolved, zonked and certified like an authored one, and the certifier refuses the transient as `NotCore`. `Spell`, `Eql`, `Ord`, `Hash` and `Hash/Key` derive; every other concept refuses the form by name. A roster row carries the names its body applies, and `curios-module`'s scheduler reads the same row, so the writer and the ordering cannot disagree about what a derived body references.

The key must reduce to a registered `induct` or `struct`, transparent at the declaring island, fully applied and not `Prop`-sorted. Each constructor's telescope is opened at the key's parameters; a type payload is refused, a proof payload takes no part beyond what the derivation states for it, and every other payload goes through the concept's own method with a `use` goal the body mints, so resolution finds a telescope premise, the witness's own entry or an `and` sibling, and an unresolved goal names the constructor, the payload and the premise to add.

- **`Spell`**: one arm per constructor, each one application of a `/std/Spell` renderer — `call` for a constructor over its payloads, `record` for a struct's fields — so a value spells as its constructor qualified by its type's own name, `Ordering/lt()`, which re-parses wherever the type's name is visible.
- **`Eql`**: `eql` is a lockstep match with a `| _ => false` default, comparing payloads pairwise under `&&`, and the concept's two laws are proved of it. `eq` matches both arguments, an arm for every pair of constructors: two that differ are closed by the hypothesis, which reduces there to a proposition nothing proves, and the same one by each payload's own equation, from the concept's law at that payload, matched to `refl()` in turn. `refl` joins each payload's own under the same `&&`. A struct has no arm to bind its fields, so its `eq` is a congruence written over them in a helper, taking an equation for each field, and applied at the projections, where the law is applied too: a field is below its record, so a family nested through the struct descends through the law as through `eql`.
- **`Ord`**: `ord` by ordinal first — a match per argument answering its constructor's position, handed with the lockstep comparison to `/std/ops/Ord`'s `by_tag` — which is linear in the constructors where an arm per pair is quadratic, and `lt` and `le` that comparison's `Ordering/is_lt` and `Ordering/is_le`, each over the comparison built again, so each agrees with `ord` by reduction, which is the whole of the two laws that say so. The order's other three laws are proved of the comparison built again too, each payload's part by the concept's own law at that payload. The payloads' answers fold by a step, `Ordering/then`, whose laws carry `flips` from one payload to the next, and the chaining of an order's three answers (`/std/ops/Ord`'s `chains`) that `trans` closes on; where the fold is `eq` each step's two answers are, so each payload's two sides are one by the concept's law there, each equation matched to `refl()` in turn as `Eql`'s are. A step's law is handed the answers it is over, the fold so far and the payload's own, written at its call. An inductive goes through `by_tag`'s laws, which ask for the payloads' part where two positions are one: under that equation two values at two constructors are closed by it, so a law over three values takes arms for pairs of constructors where it would take them for triples. A struct's `eq` hands its fields' equations to the congruence `Eql`'s law is written by. Its superclass slot is left to resolution, as every registered witness's is ([Concepts resolve with global coherence](concepts-resolve-with-global-coherence.md)).
- **`Hash`**: an arm a constructor, its encoding the constructor's position as `/std/Nat/hash` writes it where the type has more than one, then each payload's encoding in declaration order, every one but the last through `/std/Hash/delimited`, as one packed literal of spreads. A position's encoding and a delimited run each part at a byte from any other of their kind and stand in the order of what they encode, so two values' encodings stand in the order the derived `Ord` puts them in, by position and then a payload at a time, which is what a key's law asks of them; the last payload stands as it is, so a type of one field encodes as its field. It omits a proof payload: two values differing only in a proof are one value, and erasure leaves no bytes to encode.
- **`Hash/Key`**: `mono`, that the derived order of two values is `Bytes/ord` of their derived encodings, proved of the order and the encoding a derivation writes, so where the `Ord` or the `Hash` its superclasses resolve to computes otherwise — read by comparing each, as a function of fresh values, with what its derivation writes, committing nothing — the derivation refuses, naming it. A struct's proof, and a constructor's against itself, steps through the payloads from the last back: the last by `/std/Hash/Key/last` and each before it by `then`, each payload's part its own key's `mono`, the fold so far and the encodings after it written at the call, as the order's laws write a step's answers. Two different constructors are closed by `Eq/refl()`, each side reducing to its positions' order there, an arm a pair as the order's laws over two values take. A payload whose type holds the family, directly or through another key's type, takes its part through the witness's own entry, whose superclasses read there as the registered order and encoding, so a key derives over a family that holds itself (`an_inductive_that_holds_itself_derives_its_key`, `a_family_holding_itself_through_another_keys_type_derives_its_key`).

`/std` derives many of its witnesses, so every workspace check exercises the derivations; the intrinsic carriers' witnesses are written, and tuples take `/std/Tuple`'s per-arity ones.

**Rationale.**

- **The body is determined by the declaration, and writing it by hand is where the two drift.** `Test/equal` reports through `Spell`, and a report that misspells a constructor is worse than none.
- **Deriving in the compiler keeps the body a term**, checked and certified, never a template. In body position the derivation has the telescope, the concept's reduced application and the elaborated constructor types, none of which exist before elaboration, and an unresolved payload witness is an ordinary deferred goal.
- **The renderers are Curios**, so the re-parse grammar is spelled once, in `/std/Spell`, and certified rather than restated by an emitter.
- **Sealing is checked before the lookup**, so the compiler's body is no door through a representation's privacy, and the refusal is the written form's (`a_sealed_concept_refuses_a_derived_witness_outside_its_module`).
- **The registry is read as it stands.** A constructor spells by its family's own final name and tag, so `Option/some` stays the spelling of `/sys/Option` exposed through `/std/Option`. An unlabeled field reaches Core under the lowerer's `_{position}` hint, which is read back as no label. Inside the prelude's unit the scheduler cannot see through the transient, so the row's vocabulary — the concept whose method it calls, the renderers and the string machinery a rendered piece names — is a hard edge of a derived witness; a concept the body only dispatches through, such as `&&`'s, names no global and is no edge.
- **A derived encoding delimits its parts, and no carrier proves anything for it.** A `Hash` body sees each payload through its `Hash` alone, so the one way to compose encodings that keep their order is a form every encoding can be written in: each zero byte written `0x00 0xFF` and the run closed by `0x00 0x01`, as CockroachDB's ordered keys close a byte string and FoundationDB's tuple layer escapes a zero in one, so a text that begins a longer one encodes below it, and `/std` proves once that such a run orders as it did and parts at a byte from any other (`/std/Hash/delimited/ord`).

**Rejected.**

- **Each part behind its length**, the frame a derived encoding once wrote: no two values share an encoding, but two encodings order by their parts' lengths before their contents, so `("b", 1)` encodes below `("ab", 1)`, which its order puts above, and no key can be derived over it.
- **A law a key proves for each part**, that a smaller value's encoding is below a larger one's at a byte, a text meeting it by an escaped form: a `Hash` body over a payload sees its `Hash` alone, so it could choose which parts to escape only by their types, and a payload with no key would have no law to ask for.
- **Delimiting the last part too**: every encoding grows by a terminator, and a type of one field no longer encodes as its field.
- **A key encoding beside `Hash`**: two encodings of one value, a reader asking which one a map holds.
- **Refusing a derived key to a type whose text or bytes part is not its last**: a rule a program's author meets as a refusal about the order of its fields.
- **`;` in the block's place** (`satisfy Spell(Point);`). It is the one witness that takes a terminator, and in a group the one `;` an `and` follows.
- **One `;` closing a group whose last member is derived**, as a `let` group's does. Whether a group takes it turns on which member is written last.
- **An empty block**, Swift's `extension Token: Hashable {}`. `{}` is the written body of a concept with no fields of its own, so one text would say that nothing is to be written and that everything is.
- **A `derive` word**, in the block or on the head as GHC's and Lean's `deriving instance` is. It costs a contextual word where `..` is already the language's spelling of the rest, and on the head a mixed group's members would differ in their keyword.
- **Entries beside the dots**, the rest derived. A derivation writes a body whole, each method over the one comparison it wrote and each law's proof of the method it wrote, and a written entry beside the rest would part them from it.
- **A derived body recursing through a local function of its own**, as Lean's deriving handlers define the bare functions in a `mutual` block and build the instances from them: it leaves a written key over the same family refused, gives derivation two recursion shapes where every derived law recurses through its own entry, and refuses a payload nested through another key's type.
- **Deriving clauses on the type** (`induct Tree … deriving Spell`). A clause could only put the witness at the type, and the orphan rule's other half — a program deriving a standard concept for its own type, or its own concept for a standard type — needs a `satisfy`, which also keeps the telescope where the programmer writes it.
- **Inferring the premises.** A telescope is the programmer's statement of what the witness needs; inferred premises would be binders nobody wrote, and a phantom parameter would look like a forgotten premise. The report names the premise to add.
- **A Text-stage expansion.** Surface syntax has neither the reduced key — a `let Alias: Type = Tree(Nat);` key — nor the elaborated constructor types, and the text would sit outside the module's binder discipline.
- **Deriving `Show`.** `Show` is the display chosen per type; a derived one would be `Spell` under another name.
- **A description-based route**, a `Generic` concept describing constructors as data. An indexed family's payload types mention earlier payloads and indices, which a first-order description cannot express, and a description that could is the elaborator's registry. What a later concept needs — proofs for law fields, the harness's generator — is one more roster row at the same seam, as `Ord` and `Hash` are.
