# A witness body may be written by the compiler

**Decision.** A witness declaration may leave its body to the compiler — `satisfy Spell(Point) { .. }`, or `satisfy (@A: Type, use Spell(A)) => Spell(Tree(A)) { .. }` under a telescope, alone or in an `and` group — and the compiler writes the body from the declaration of the type in the key. The block holds `..` alone: the dots are no entry, nothing is written beside them and they take no comma, so a body is written whole or derived whole and every witness ends at its `}` (`curios-text`'s `a_witness_takes_a_block_of_its_entries_or_of_the_dots_alone`). The signature stays the programmer's: it registers, keys and meets the orphan and one-witness-per-key rules as a written witness does (`curios`'s `the_signature_refusals_fire_on_a_derived_witness_as_on_a_written_one`). Lowering puts `Transient::Derive` in body position, so the telescope's binders and premises are in scope; `curios-elab`'s `elaborate_derive` refuses a sealed concept outside its module, looks the concept up in the `DerivationSyntax` roster (`curios-utilities/src/syntax.rs`) and elaborates the Core the derivation returns under the same expectation, so the body is typed, resolved, zonked and certified like an authored one, and the kernel refuses the transient as `NotCore`. `Spell`, `Eql`, `Ord` and `Hash` derive; every other concept refuses the form by name. A roster row carries the names its body applies, and `curios-text`'s scheduler reads the same row, so the writer and the ordering cannot disagree about what a derived body references.

The key must reduce to a registered `induct` or `struct`, transparent at the declaring island, fully applied and not `Prop`-sorted. Each constructor's telescope is opened at the key's parameters; a type payload is refused, a proof payload takes no part beyond what the derivation states for it, and every other payload goes through the concept's own method with a `use` goal the body mints, so resolution finds a telescope premise, the witness's own entry or an `and` sibling, and an unresolved goal names the constructor, the payload and the premise to add.

- **`Spell`**: one arm per constructor, each one application of a `/std/Spell` renderer — `call` for a constructor over its payloads, `record` for a struct's fields — so a value spells as its constructor qualified by its type's own name, `Ordering/lt()`, which re-parses wherever the type's name is visible.
- **`Eql`**: a lockstep match with a `| _ => false` default, comparing payloads pairwise under `&&`; `neq` negates it.
- **`Ord`**: by ordinal first — a match per argument answering its constructor's position, handed with the lockstep comparison to `/std/Ord`'s `by_tag` — which is linear in the constructors where an arm per pair is quadratic. Its superclass slot is left to resolution, as every registered witness's is ([Concepts resolve with global coherence](concepts-resolve-with-global-coherence.md)).
- **`Hash`**: `Spell`'s shape with the ordinal where the path is, through `/std/Hash`'s `tagged`. It omits a proof payload: two values differing only in a proof are one value, and erasure leaves no bytes to encode.

`/std` derives many of its witnesses, so every workspace check exercises the derivations; the intrinsic carriers' witnesses are written, and tuples take `/std/Tuple`'s per-arity ones.

**Rationale.**

- **The body is determined by the declaration, and writing it by hand is where the two drift.** `Test/equal` reports through `Spell`, and a report that misspells a constructor is worse than none.
- **Deriving in the compiler keeps the body a term**, checked and certified, never a template. In body position the derivation has the telescope, the concept's reduced application and the elaborated constructor types, none of which exist before elaboration, and an unresolved payload witness is an ordinary deferred goal.
- **The renderers are Curios**, so the re-parse grammar is spelled once, in `/std/Spell`, and certified rather than restated by an emitter.
- **Sealing is checked before the lookup**, so the compiler's body is no door through a representation's privacy, and the refusal is the written form's (`a_sealed_concept_refuses_a_derived_witness_outside_its_module`).
- **The registry is read as it stands.** A constructor spells by its family's own final name and tag, so `Option/some` stays the spelling of `/sys/Option` exposed through `/std/Option`. An unlabeled field reaches Core under the lowerer's `_{position}` hint, which is read back as no label. Inside the prelude's unit the scheduler cannot see through the transient, so the row's vocabulary — the method wrapper, the renderers and the string machinery a rendered piece names — is a hard edge of a derived witness; a concept the body only dispatches through, such as `&&`'s, names no global and is no edge.

**Rejected.**

- **`;` in the block's place** (`satisfy Spell(Point);`). It is the one witness that takes a terminator, and in a group the one `;` an `and` follows.
- **One `;` closing a group whose last member is derived**, as a `let` group's does. Whether a group takes it turns on which member is written last.
- **An empty block**, Swift's `extension Token: Hashable {}`. `{}` is the written body of a concept with no fields of its own, so one text would say that nothing is to be written and that everything is.
- **A `derive` word**, in the block or on the head as GHC's and Lean's `deriving instance` is. It costs a contextual word where `..` is already the language's spelling of the rest, and on the head a mixed group's members would differ in their keyword.
- **Entries beside the dots**, the rest derived. A derivation writes a body whole: `Eql`'s `neq` negates the `eql` it wrote, and beside a written `eql` it would negate another function.
- **Deriving clauses on the type** (`induct Tree … deriving Spell`). A clause could only put the witness at the type, and the orphan rule's other half — a program deriving a standard concept for its own type, or its own concept for a standard type — needs a `satisfy`, which also keeps the telescope where the programmer writes it.
- **Inferring the premises.** A telescope is the programmer's statement of what the witness needs; inferred premises would be binders nobody wrote, and a phantom parameter would look like a forgotten premise. The report names the premise to add.
- **A Text-stage expansion.** Surface syntax has neither the reduced key — a `let Alias: Type = Tree(Nat);` key — nor the elaborated constructor types, and the text would sit outside the module's binder discipline.
- **Deriving `Show`.** `Show` is the display chosen per type; a derived one would be `Spell` under another name.
- **A description-based route**, a `Generic` concept describing constructors as data. An indexed family's payload types mention earlier payloads and indices, which a first-order description cannot express, and a description that could is the elaborator's registry. What a later concept needs — proofs for law fields, the harness's generator — is one more roster row at the same seam, as `Ord` and `Hash` are.
