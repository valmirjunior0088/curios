# A literal is realized by its expected type

**Decision.** A literal is an ordinary term the elaborator realizes against its expected type, and every value of a carrier has one literal spelling.

- **A numeral** realizes at `Nat`, `Int`, `Byte`, `Flt` or `Bool` under its range rules, `Byte` and `Bool` only where expected.
- **A character literal is a numeral spelled by its scalar**: `Transient::NumLit`, whose `Character` variant carries a `char` beside `Number`'s numeral, a natural where no sign is written and an integer where one is. It realizes as the certified `/std/Char` wherever nothing pins it and as its code point at `Nat`, `Byte` or `Int`, never at `Bool` or `Flt`. In a match it is a dispatch case of a `Char`, a `Nat` or a `Byte`.
- **A string literal** is a transparent, certified `/std/Str` value; the erased carriers stay `Nat` and packed `Bytes`. In a match it takes a `Str` apart by its bytes.
- **A `Bytes` literal** is bracketed behind the prefix `0x` glued to the bracket, which no name or numeral begins: `0x[0x48, b, ..suffix]`, `0x[]`, and as a pattern the run `0x['%', high, ..rest]`. Each entry is an ordinary term contributing one atom, a constant atom is a numeral realized as a `Byte`, `..` spreads a packed value, and adjacent constant atoms lower to one packed constant (`lower_bytes_literal`, pinned by `constant_atoms_fold_into_the_packed_run`).
- **A literal whose type is still open waits for the term that awaits it.** A numeral checked at a type stuck on an unsolved metavariable that a term awaits — a call, a list or a structure literal, a match, an operator reading its parts — is realized where a solution gives the type, and otherwise where that term has read every part: the numerals that wait on one type take one default there, `Int` where one of them is signed, `Nat` where one is an unsigned number, and `Char` only where every one is a character. A tuple literal that waits gives the type its shape there, a product of one open type a field, at which every other literal waiting on it is checked. Where no term awaits the type the default is taken at once.
- **A float's non-finite values** are `Flt` literals with a required sign: `+inf.0` and `-inf.0`, and `+nan.0` and `-nan.0`, the default quiet NaN with its sign clear and set. Every other NaN is spelled by the call that builds it, `Flt/of_le_bytes(0x[…])`, and a decimal too large for `Flt` is refused, never rounded to an infinity.

**Rationale.**

- **The certifier stays free of literal types**, and a literal arrives carrying the structure and certificates library code consumes, which erasure makes free at run time.
- **One mechanism realizes every literal**: one table, `realize`, which realizes a literal term at its expected type and a literal pattern at its column's, with a shape default for a term, the `switch` the match compiler emits keyed by its carrier, and the certified-value emitter. An enum rather than a flag makes a signed, radixed or out-of-range character unrepresentable.
- **A literal pattern is realized by its column's type**, as a literal term is by its expected one: a `Nat` column's literals are naturals, an `Int` column's its keys written with their sign, a `Bool` column's `0` and `1` its truth values, and a `Byte` column's its bytes, dispatched on the byte itself. A `Char` and a `Str` are the structures their literal terms build, so a column of either is read at the field its literal fills: a character's code point, dispatched as a natural, and a string's bytes, taken apart as the closed run they are. The certifier is handed a natural's `switch` and a packed sequence's runs, and nothing new, and the arm learns the field.
- **A numeral pattern written with a sign is an `Int`'s, and an `Int`'s is written with one.** A term keeps its realization by its expected type, `let i: Int = 1`, while a pattern is held to its spelling: it writes a value as every report prints it, `+1`, and a natural's numeral and an integer's take a value apart two ways that the sign keeps apart where the scrutinee's type is out of sight, `| 0 => … | _ => …` a natural's case split whose catch-all is the successor's arm, and `| +0 => … | _ => …` an integer's dispatch.
- **A signed numeral is the integer it spells, so a `-` leads a numeral past zero.** A numeral holds the natural it spells where no sign is written and the integer where one is (`curios_core::Numeral`), and the grammar has no negative zero: `-0` is no numeral, and the zero of an `Int` is `+0`. A numeral `-0` would spell two values by its type, the integer zero `+0` spells and a float's negative zero apart from `+0.0`, which the decimal `-0.0` spells alone; and as a pattern it would be a second key for one value.
- **A dispatch is keyed by its carrier.** `Cases::Switch` holds its keys by the carrier they are values of (`curios_core::Keys`), a natural's, an integer's or a byte's, so a dispatch has one form, one typing rule and one reduction rule over every carrier: each arm is checked at its key's literal with the scrutinee refined to it, and the catch-all under each key's failed test, by the carrier's equality ([Case equations and their key](../soundness/case-equations-and-their-key.md)). The keys are one at least and strictly ascending, held by the constructor of the keys and not by each builder, and each carrier's keys are of its own type, so a negative key of a natural's switch is unrepresentable. Core text spells a key as it spells the value, `| 2 =>`, `| -1 =>`, `| #Byte(65) =>`, so a switch's carrier is read off its keys with no type at hand. A byte is dispatched on itself, so its arm is refined to the byte where a view through `Byte/to_nat` would refine the code. The closed machine and the elaborator's reducer read one statement of the reduction in `curios-core`, and the certifier's `whnf` keeps its own, held to one answer by a differential case. Erased, a switch stays keyed by its carrier, a byte's erased as its code's, `ByteToNat` and then a natural's switch, the erased alphabet's explicit conversion; and `into_cps` tests each key no branch table indexes by its carrier's equality, every one of an integer's among them.
- **A default is no fact about the order a term is written in.** Taken where the numeral is read, `g(5, b)` defaults the numeral to `Nat` and refuses a `b` of another type, where `g(b, 5)` compiles; taken once the call has read both, the argument that states the type gives it in either order, and two numerals that must share a type are defaulted as one.
- **Every value reads back.** A report prints what its reader can write ([A diagnostic spells what its reader can write](../tools/a-diagnostic-spells-what-its-reader-can-write.md)), so a carrier's literals cover its values, and reading back is a property of the grammar rather than of library names a printer must know. The non-finite spelling is [R7RS Scheme's](https://standards.scheme.org/corrected-r7rs/r7rs-Z-H-12.html); unlike R7RS, `-nan.0` is its own value, since term identity is bitwise and a NaN's sign is observable. An overflowing decimal is far more often a mistake than a request for an infinity.
- **Delimiters make an entry an ordinary term**, printed by the ordinary printer, and the three bracketed carriers share one grammar differing in their prefix. A radix is presentation the writer chooses per site — `0x48` where bytes read hex, `1` where bits read binary.

**Rejected.**

- **Certifier-intrinsic character and string types.**
- **A default held to the end of the declaration**, as Lean's and Rust's are: a value stands in the types after it here, and whatever reads the type before then, a match, a projection, a call, would have to wait in its turn. **The first of two literals at one type synthesized and the second held to it**, which answers a call's literals in the order of its slots.
- **A `character: bool` beside the numeral's fields**, which admits the bogus pairings.
- **An unsigned numeral or a character as an `Int`'s pattern**, as the term realizes one at an `Int` and Rust's literal pattern takes its scrutinee's type: `| 0 => … | _ => …` would be a natural's case split and an integer's dispatch in one spelling, and a key would be written otherwise than every report prints its value. **The sign required of a term too**, OCaml's literals typed by their form: a term computes a value at the type its context gives and states no case to keep apart from another.
- **A dispatch through a view**, an integer's sign beside its magnitude, as a `Char` is read at its code point: it would need nothing of the certifier, but it refines the view and never the integer, where `Nat`, the carrier `Int` extends, has its own switch.
- **An integer's two constructors as a Core case split**, `Int` read as Lean's `ofNat | negSucc` or Agda's `pos | negsuc`. Core holds an integer as a sum normal form over its literal (`curios-core/src/int.rs`), no tree of constructors, so the split would be a second reading of every integer; a literal would be two dispatches, the sign's and the magnitude's, the catch-all copied under both signs, and a negative key's pattern a constructor of the magnitude's predecessor.
- **A twin `Cases::IntSwitch`**, and a `SwitchInt` beside a natural's switch below it: one rule stated twice in each checker, a third time for a byte, and each walk over a match or a statement writing the same arm twice. **One key type for every carrier**, an `Integer` beside a carrier tag, where a negative key of a natural's switch is representable.
- **A branch table over an integer's keys**: it needs a signed narrowing of the scrutinee the emitter does not have, and each of `/std`'s dispatches on an integer writes one key. The keys are tested in `into_cps`, where a natural's table is built, so a table is a change to that one function.
- **Character spellings for `Bool` and `Flt`**, and **equality-guard patterns**, which a guard with `==` already owns; a string pattern is no such thing, since it takes its `Str` apart by its bytes.
- **Floating-point patterns.** A pattern the arm learns from tests the value's identity, which is bitwise, where `==` is IEEE's, and the two disagree at the zeros and the NaNs: Agda's float literal patterns compared by IEEE equality proved a contradiction from it ([agda/agda#2169](https://github.com/agda/agda/issues/2169)) and now compare bit patterns, and Rust's compare by `==`, refuse a NaN and refine nothing. Either way a pattern spells what a guard already says, or says what `==` does not.
- **`x[…]`, the letter `x` glued to the bracket**: it claims a bracket glued to a name, where an index reads an element, and `x` is the commonest name a binder takes.
- **A tight packed spelling**, `b\1\..rest\0`: with no delimiters, operands need a glued grammar and a printer of their own, and whitespace ends the literal.
- **An escape marking constant atoms beside numerals**, two spellings of one constant whose constancy lowering already guarantees; **bare `[` for every carrier**, which leaves an empty literal's carrier unrecoverable. The price accepted is that postfix `[` is spent.
- **Named float constants**, as OCaml's `infinity` and Julia's `Inf`, which make each value two spellings and reading back rest on names; **printing a computation such as `Flt/div(+1.0, +0.0)`**, which shows a division where the reader has a value.
