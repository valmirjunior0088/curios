# A command narrates on one transcript and reports in blocks

**Decision.** What a command says takes one of four forms, each with one writer, and which form a line takes says whose it is.

- **What a command does is a transcript**, written on standard error and nowhere else: events, each naming the subject it is of, drawn by whoever tells one as it is told, in one of two drawings chosen once as the command starts — `--progress`, else `CURIOS_PROGRESS`, else live where standard error is a terminal, `TERM` is not `dumb` and `CI` is unset, and plain everywhere else. Live, a subject stands alone on its line, its tree parted from what stands above it by a blank line, as the line under no subject that ends a run is, and each step is a node of the tree, `label; outcome`: the label written before the work, the outcome after it — a time where the step succeeded, an ending and a time where it did not, `refused` where a rule said no and `failed` where the machine did, or the place its product stands. The oldest open subject's tree is written as its nodes close, and the rest stand in a region beneath it, redrawn as they run: each running node as it will read when it closes, its time after `;` and of a check how far its walk over its declarations has come behind `·`, each later subject's tree so far, and what is done, cut to the terminal and gone before anything else is written there; a subject that ends behind an older one waits there and is written in its turn. Plain, each event is a line standing alone — a verb, the subject's name in backticks, what the event carries —, a step's two lines sharing its verb, `Checking` and `Checked`, and a step said at once, its outcome known as it begins, one line. Nothing written above the region is erased or redrawn, a unit taken from the cabinet prints nothing, and the plain drawing writes the same bytes in a pipe as on a terminal, colour apart. A test is a node opened before it runs; what it writes is relayed as it writes it, each line quoted behind `>` — in the tree beneath its node, plain behind the test's path —, and a test that wrote nothing closes on its own line. In the tree a test's ending leads with its mark, `✓` where it passed and `✗` where it did not, which stands in the quote's column beneath what the test wrote, so one rule beside the tree's marks what is quoted; beneath the ending stands what only the runner holds, a trap's text and what a failing test found, which the test answers as data rather than writes, a block no other line splits. A total is the last line, flush left.
- **What a command finds or refuses is a report, and a report is a block.** Its first line is a tag: one to three lower-case words that name the kind of thing reported, with nothing of the program in them, the same whichever stage or command meets it; a goal's block and a query's answer open instead on what they answer, the goal's type, the path the query asked about, or the term written at the place it asked about. Beneath it, on a trunk, stand its facts, a statement a line in one order — what stands here, each value at issue behind a label, the rule, the way out —, and whatever a fact shows of the program, a term, a name, a pattern, a label or a value as written, stands in backticks, behind a label or inside a sentence (`curios_common::Code`); then each further place it is about, its line shown and closed by what stands there, then its own place. A block that lists holds an entry behind a `├─` for each thing it lists, above its ways out, the rest of an entry on the trunk beneath: a line of the program as written, which no `;` joins to an outcome. A way out that is a command stands behind `try` on the line above the close. The block closes on its kind in brackets and what it is about: `[error]`, `[goal]` or `[lint]`, or for an answer `[declaration]`, `[module]`, `[local]`, `[field]`, `[term]` or `[trace]`, then a place in a text, a file or a directory as a whole, or a thing by the name that was written.
- **A report is data until the edge that writes it** (`curios_common::Report`): a tag, facts, further places, ways out and what it is about, with no severity and no reader in it. Whoever prints decides the kind and the reader — where they stand, which a path is spelled from, and whether they see colour, which changes no byte beneath it — and an editor is handed the same data, never a rendering. A place is spelled by the file that holds its text, and by its source's name, its package and its path within it, where no file on the reader's machine does, as in the library a compiler carries.
- **What a query lists is rows**, on standard output, after the block of what was asked where there is one: a row a line, its fields joined by tabs and as many in every row of the listing, `-` where a field has nothing to say, so a script splits any listing alike. What a module exposes is no such listing: each thing is a declaration as its source writes it, and stands as an entry on the module's block, as a declaration's own source, its body left out, stands on its own; and each witness a declaration takes part in stands on the block of its `--witnesses` view, by its head as its author wrote it, closed by its place as a further place is.
- **A refusal of what a manifest states stands at the row that states it**, named by the keys that lead there and found in the manifest's text when the refusal is reported (`curios_package::Refused`). The records a manifest is read into keep no positions.
- **What a running program stops on is its own, and is one line**: the kind of stop, a colon, and what there is to say of it (`curios_native::Stop`). It is no block, it carries no frame of the machine, and a built executable says it as `curios run` does. What could not be linked is a block where the compiler meets it and that same line where a built executable does, from one value (`curios_native::Unlinked`).

**Rationale.**

- **One writer per form keeps the forms from drifting.** The transcript's two drawings draw one record of events, so neither can say what the other does not; a report drawn in one place has one shape for a parse failure, a type mismatch, a lint, a manifest row and a mistyped flag, so a reader learns it once.
- **A plain line stands alone because subjects stop taking turns.** A tree is written for one open line at a time: once two subjects are open, the second's label lands inside the first's open line. A line that names its subject and ends where it is written interleaves with any other, reads the same in a pipe, a log and a continuous-integration page, and serves a screen reader on a terminal, so it is what is drawn wherever no person watches a terminal. A step that hangs is the line begun and never ended, as it is the line left open in a tree.
- **The live scrollback is the oldest open subject streaming.** Written as its nodes close, the oldest subject reads as the tree always has, a test's lines quoted beneath its node, and a subject that begins later waits in the region, where it is seen as it runs, until its turn: so the scrollback is the trees in the order their subjects began, whatever order the work ran in, which no schedule moves. The region is what a person watching a terminal needs while work runs — what is running, for how long, and what is left —, and nothing of it is kept: a frame is never written to a log or a page, and the plain drawing is what a reader who cannot watch a redraw is given.
- **The transcript is drawn by whoever tells it, on the thread that tells it**, one value behind a lock: a label reaches the reader before the work it names, and a process that dies mid-step has written its last line, where a drawing thread fed through a channel would lose what is queued on an abort or an early exit and let a child given a test's stream write ahead of the test's own line.
- **A tag is what a reader, a search and a tool can hold on to.** A sentence that carries the program's names is different every time it is met; a tag is the same, and the facts beneath it are where this program's names go. Statements a line each, in one order, let the eye find the value it wants without parsing prose.
- **The kind stands in brackets because `;` is taken.** On every other line of a tree `;` joins a label to what it came to, and a kind is no step whose outcome is a place: `check; refused in 0.3s` is a step, and `[error]` closes a report.
- **A place is the most useful thing a refusal can end on**, so every block ends on what it is about, in a form a terminal opens where it is a place, and which `wonder --at` asks of as it is printed ([A place in a file is answered from the declaration that writes it](a-place-in-a-file-is-answered-from-the-declaration-that-writes-it.md)). A manifest's refusal is most useful at the row, and reading the text again for where the row stands costs nothing on the one path that reports.
- **An answer opens on what was asked, as a goal opens on its answer**: the kind it closes on is what a tool holds on to, and the path is what its reader looks for first. **A listing is read by a script as often as by an eye**, so its fields are told apart by one character no field holds and stand in the same column in every row.
- **A program's stop belongs to the program.** Once a program runs, what it prints is its own, and so is the line it stops on: a frame around it would be the compiler speaking for a program that, built into an executable, runs with no compiler beside it. The frames the engine can list name machine code, which the program's author did not write.
- **Structure is data because every consumer needs a different drawing.** A terminal, a pipe, an editor, a served test page and a test of the compiler each show a report their own way; a rendering parsed back for its parts would hold each to the others' wording. What a test found is such data too: written by the test as a sentence, it could be quoted and never drawn, and would read as one more line of what the test wrote.

**Rejected.**

- **The live scrollback in the order subjects finish**: a test's lines would stand above a tree printed whole after them, so not even one subject at a time would read as the tree, and the scrollback's order would be the schedule's.
- **A full-screen interface**, as Turborepo's since 2.0: some terminals have shown it blank or fallen back, and it leaves nothing in the scrollback.
- **Capturing what a test writes and showing it on failure**, which hides the output of a test that hangs, and reorders it against the test's own timing; and **holding any subject's lines until it ends**, which gives silences and then bursts.
- **One stream for both drawings**, closing a node left open with `…` and writing it again with its outcome: a stream of fragments on a terminal and in a log alike.
- **A drawing thread fed through a channel**: every write asynchronous, what is queued lost on an abort or an early exit, and a test's line overtaken by a child given its stream unless every such path waits on the queue.
- **A stream of results beside what a test reports**, each check as it happens: a test runs in an instantiation of its own, so its runner knows a trap, an exit and the time without one, and what the test found is what `/test` reports of it once it has run, through a row only its runner answers and its body cannot name.
- **A severity before the headline**, `error: type mismatch`: the kind is known to whoever prints and is the same for every report of a run, so it closes the block and the tag leads.
- **A sentence as the headline**, which no reader can match and which pushes the facts into clauses.
- **`kind; subject` as the closing line**, which reads as a step of the tree and collides with the step `format`.
- **A note for a file no `mod` reaches, or for a cabinet that took nothing**: neither is a finding about the program, and a reader who asked about a file is not asking where it hangs.
- **A separator of a listing's own**, a ` → ` between a witness and its place: a block closes a further place behind `;`, which a witness's entry takes, and a second mark would be a second rule to learn.
- **A block for a run-time stop**, under a kind of its own, and **the engine's frames beneath it**.
- **Positions carried through the manifest's records**, a span on every field for a refusal most reads never make.
- **The parser library's own message for a line that does not parse**: its wording is another program's, and a report is built from the parts it keeps of its refusal, never from its text.
