# Index inversion and K

**Assumes.** `Eq : Prop` licenses deleting a redundant constraint, two forcings of one binder that definitely clash make the case unreachable, and inversion's use of its driver's conversion is as sound as that conversion.

**Status.** The clash between two forcings is **probed** from the surface (`curios/src/tests/board/index_tests.rs`); the deletion rule's answers and the clash rule's license are reached by construction in `curios-analysis`'s `tests/driven.rs`; the deletion rule's semantic half is **auditable only** from the surface, since across the corpus every acceptance has syntactically identical sides.

- **A binder forced twice survives only when its forcings convert.** Inversion reconciles two forcings by asking conversion, an acceptance where a syntactic non-linearity test would refuse. `tests/driven.rs::a_binder_forced_twice_survives_only_when_its_forcings_convert` forces one binder to two different inhabitants and varies only the family's sort: reconciled at a proposition, where irrelevance identifies them, and impossible at a relevant family.
- **A definite clash excuses the arm, and the verdict is guarded twice.** `refl(@z) : (z, z)` against `Eq()(false, true)` forces `z` two ways by injective steps; after conversion refuses, the first-order walk decides a definite clash, the tag test licensed by the family's sort. An `Impossible` wrongly read at a `Prop`-sorted family is the vacuous-elimination route to `False` ([Coverage](coverage.md)): `Eq/refl()` inhabits `Eq()(Two/a(), Two/b())` by irrelevance. The sort test and asking conversion first each suffice alone, as a mutation of either at the unifier shows; with both gone the fixture above fails at its proposition case. Its third case, one opaque function at two arguments, holds answering `Impossible` for every pair that does not convert. From the surface, `a_clash_between_two_forcings_of_one_binder_excuses_the_arm` states the refutations both checkers accept, beside `two_proofs_forced_on_one_binder_do_not_clash`, `an_open_forcing_does_not_clash`, `two_applications_of_one_opaque_function_do_not_clash` and `a_parity_disagreement_is_not_a_clash`.
- **Blindness never excuses.** A binder whose type is out of scope drops its forcings and asks no clash (`tests/driven.rs::a_binder_whose_type_is_out_of_scope_drops_its_forcings`, at a proposition and at a relevant family, mutation-checked both ways); both callers open a case's payload through the one `assume_payload`. A family the registry cannot answer for does not clash (`a_family_the_registry_cannot_answer_for_does_not_clash`), since the sort that licenses a clash is exactly what a missing entry hides.
- **Inversion reads the algebra only as a `Deduction`**, which cannot carry a merely sufficient residual: the product-factor peel's `f` against `g` from `x · f` against `x · g` is a `Conclusion` conversion asks for and inversion cannot receive (`curios-core`'s `spine::nat_tests::a_shared_factor_leaves_conversion_a_residual_and_inversion_nothing`). Freeness modulo the theory licenses the peel's `Impossible`, checked at every intrinsic case inversion distinguishes by `tests::laws::audit::distinct_constructors_stay_distinct`, with an equation that may hold as the control.
- **Inversion takes its driver's word on convertibility.** The `Env`/`Judge` seam splits environment queries from conversion, and `invert` is `Judge`'s only consumer, so the elaborator's and the kernel's runs of it are two functions of two convertibility relations — which [Conversion recurrence](../conversion/conversion-recurrence.md) shows differ in their key. Positivity and totality take `Env` alone.
