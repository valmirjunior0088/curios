# Conversion recurrence

**Decision.** Absence of finite disagreement is sufficient for equality: a goal met again in the conversion history is assumed rather than unfolded forever. It stands on a fixpoint with (T), as proof irrelevance does with (V) ([Totality of the erased program](totality-of-the-erased-program.md)), and a recurrence excuses no finite disagreement.

- **The key is where this rule admits.** A history hit on two goals that are not one goal turns absence of disagreement into absence of a comparison. The kernel's key records the local context, the type and both terms, each renamed by position, and omits the case equations in force and the item's assumed universe constraints — safe because conversion mutates neither and a history lives for one top-level conversion, so both are constant while it stands. A `let`'s binder stands in that context like any other and names nothing in a goal: conversion reads its terms by value and opens no `let`, so the binders a `let` added are the same for every goal of one history. A conversion taught to consult a case equation would make the key incomplete without touching it. A binder an elimination's comparison opens stands in that context at the type its position gives it, as any binder does; where no lookup types the scrutinee it is assumed at `Type`, and there the key's context carries no information and the terms alone separate goals.
- **A question reduction asks runs under a history of its own.** Where a judgment's reduction puts a question to conversion — which atoms of a pair or of a stuck fold are one, whether a stuck form is an equation's scrutinee — the comparison opens a history of its own, as the elaborator's classing does, so it takes no assumption from the goal reduction was serving and leaves it none. It is a conversion like any other: a goal met again inside it is assumed by the rule above, on the same fixpoint. What the key omits stays constant while a history stands: a question withholds an equation and the equations inside it for its own duration and restores them before it returns, and reduction is plain from its first step to its last. A question is answered by plain reduction, which asks none, so reduction asking conversion asking reduction ends at one conversion the budget bounds.
- **The elaborator's key differs.** It renames the labels it minted in mint order and records no local context, and its walk parks goals, so two goals distinct under their binder types can share one key. It cannot move a verdict, because an opening is typeless everywhere in the elaborator, so a collided goal is its twin under an injective relabeling and any disagreement it hides surfaces through the twin's children; a rule that read an opening's type would invalidate that in silence.
- **Two instances of one recursive group are a level question**, decided before either recurrence rule is reached. The kernel decides two projections of one group that agree modulo levels by their level vectors under the item's hypotheses (`rec_instances`, through `Term::level_differences`, which keeps every level's position). A family's former is the one member whose refusal is no verdict: its two instances reproduce nothing under unfolding, so they are left to the nodes they build ([A nominal type's levels compare by variance](a-nominal-types-levels-compare-by-variance.md)). The elaborator sends a family's former applied in full on both sides to its two nodes as well, and identifies every other pair's levels at the applied head or at the recurrence (`level_question`), parking a pair only an unsolved metavariable keeps from being a level question.
- **The same member of two recursive groups is decided by the groups compared as the binders they are**, before either checker unfolds one. Two groups of one shape are one where, member for member, their types convert and then their bodies at those types, every member of both opened at one shared binder: the congruence of a binder, as two lambdas whose bodies convert under one binder are one function. No member is unfolded while two groups are compared, so nothing recurs and the rule rests on no assumption; two calls of a pair it holds are compared by their spines. Groups are matched by position, which is what makes one index of two a pair, and only where neither binds a universe of its own. A pair it does not hold is left to the unfolding, which is taken once.
- **A pair of calls is unfolded against itself once.** Two folded calls no rule before this one decides are opened one definitional step, both of them, and compared again, and their two heads are a pair being unfolded for as long as that comparison stands. The same two heads met again inside it, whatever their spines, are apart. A second unfolding poses what the first posed one binder further in, so it decides nothing the first did not, and the chain is no goal met again: each round stands in a scope no round has met. What would hold such a pair is an induction, which a lemma states. The rule withholds an unfolding, so it accepts nothing, and what it refuses that more unfoldings would hold is two calls a second round reconciles. A side opened against a term with nothing to open is no pair, and two bare members forced against each other are one as two calls are. The kernel's pairs are the path of its walk. The elaborator's are carried with each problem, as its binders are: its walk parks what it cannot decide, so a problem retried is retried inside what it was posed inside, and a comparison a run opens for a part of the problem in hand — an argument, two groups as binders, a classing's atoms — is posed inside that problem's pairs. There a pair met again waits, parked, where an unsolved metavariable is what keeps a rule from deciding its two heads, that they are one member or one as binders: refused, it would be refused by the order its metavariables were solved in.
- **A classing does not take the assumption for the pair it is asked about.** Where the intrinsic chain hands a pair's atoms back, the kernel compares two of them under the history of the goal the pair belongs to, and a goal met again inside that comparison is assumed as it is under any operand of the congruence: the verdict that follows rests on the comparisons a pairing congruence would have as premises. The atom pair itself is the exception. A classing that found it in progress and took it as held would spell one atom as the other and decide the pair with nothing compared, so two atoms whose comparison the history already holds are left apart (`classes`, in `kernel::convert::intrinsic`), and a pair neither side of which the readers read through hands back no atoms at all, since the only pair to class would be the goal. The elaborator's classing opens a conversion of its own for each pair, with a history of its own, so it has no assumption to take and rests on the second limit alone.
- **A verdict that rested on the rule is not remembered.** The kernel remembers a comparison's verdict by its type and its two sides, for as long as what it read of the scope stands ([`curios-cert`'s README](../../../curios-cert/README.md#the-kernel-memoizes-its-own-evaluation-and-a-memo-changes-only-resource-verdicts)), and only where deciding it met no goal in progress and left no pair apart inside its own unfolding: `History::assumed` counts every goal met again — assumed, or in a classing left apart — and every such pair, and `compare` files a verdict where the count stood still across it. An accepted goal that assumed one holds on that path alone, and a refused one may have been refused by a classing that left two atoms apart because their comparison was in progress, or by a pair left apart that is one where it is met outside its own unfolding, so neither is a fact. A remembered verdict answers before its goal is entered, whatever goals are then in progress, since it rested on none of them. The key's two omissions stand for the table as they do for a history: it is cleared wherever the case equations in force change, and with the declaration, whose universe constraints are assumed once. The sets are plain, with no closure taken over accepted pairs, as Lean's kernel keeps its own and for the reason it gives: a closure's result would depend on the order goals were met in.
