# Cached verdicts

**Decision.** A unit filed under an address naming its mounts, the prefixes it declared it could see and the compiler binary that judged it may be admitted without being judged again, provided its record still agrees: every file it read holds the text it read, and every unit it could have read contains what it contained. It is the one rule that admits by declining to judge again. The fixed prelude needs no key — the only crate handing its image out is one whose build script walked it with the kernel — so a package's key carries the argument alone.

- **The address names a place; a record decides whether what is in it may be believed.** The address carries the compiler, the mounts and the declared prefixes, and no file contents and no unit before its own, so an edit, to its source or to a unit before it, changes what is verified rather than where it is looked for. An entry claims the empty prefix whichever program it is, so the unit of its own declarations is addressed by the executable it is, its package's name and its own, where a unit's mounts stand (`curios_package::entry_slot`), and has no address where it is no package's executable; its own file is among the files its record names, logged where the entry is opened, from the text that was parsed (`curios_text::RootSource::opened`). Beside the unit sits a record of every file the compilation read, by canonical path and a digest of the text parsed from it, of what each unit it could have read contained, by the prefixes that unit claims and a digest of the bytes its slot holds, and a digest of the unit itself; a hit requires all of it to hold, and any disagreement, unreadable record or vanished file is a miss. The record and the unit are one file, written beside the slot and renamed into place, so an interrupted write leaves the old slot, the new one or none; the unit's digest makes a unit damaged after filing a miss, where bytecheck confirms only its structure.
- **Each part of the address is load-bearing.** The compiler is a content digest of the running binary, memoized against its size, modification time, device, inode and status-change time — a version string does not move while a compiler is developed, and a per-build nonce discards what a reproducible rebuild should keep. The mounts are how a unit's names are spelled. The declared prefixes decide which references resolve. The prelude and the syntax registry are inside the binary, so a changed standard library changes the address.
- **A hit rests on every unit its unit could have read.** A hit is believed with nothing compiled, so it stands on a set that is closed by how a unit is compiled and not by any record of what it read: the units it may name, and the ones those may name in turn. Resolution is narrowed to the mounts a unit declared; a term handed in from another unit names what that unit could name; a witness is declared with its concept or with the type it is at, both of which whoever states the goal can name. A record names what each of those contained, in the order of their prefixes, and a hit requires each to contain it still and no other to have joined them. A unit outside them is not named: an edit to it leaves the hit standing, and a unit two folds reach after different others is filed once. Rejected is a record of only the units a compile read anything of, which turns a recompile that redoes nothing into a hit for a dependency a unit could read and did not: the lowering's passes over a whole unit read the units before it through no one door, so that set is a claim about every one of them, and a read it missed is a stale unit believed.
- **A slot whose record disagrees is a baseline, whatever moved.** Its unit is compiled over, and each stage asks again what its own record says it read. Of the unit's own text that is the lowering's. Of a unit the record names that contains what it did, nothing is asked. Of any other unit in scope, each stage recorded the digest of every part it read — the elaborator's and erasure's of a declaration's signature, body, kind, totality, vector and entry, as a unit stores them, and of how a name is bound in an erased form (`curios_elab::Foreign`), the certifier's own of what a signature read and a body read are (`curios_core::Abroad`) — and asks the part again by what it digests to now. The fixed prelude's units are the compiler's own, which the address names, so nothing is recorded of a read of one.
- **What was read is recorded, never predicted.** The record is taken at the one seam every module read passes through, so an input of a kind nobody anticipated is covered. It needs no account of what was looked for and not found because the read set is closed: a module enters a unit only through a `mod` in a header that is itself a read, and cross-unit references read nothing, resolving through a compiled predecessor. A stored unit carries no identity meaningful only in the compilation that made it: `curios_core::validate_stored_identities` refuses a free local, a term metavariable and a witness declared under a mount the unit does not own where a unit is written, and a universe metavariable is refused before, by `curios-elab`'s `validate_universes`, and again by the kernel.
- **One verdict at a time is admitted on the certifier's own record.** A unit compiled over a baseline ([A stored unit is a baseline for an item-level recompile](../compilation/a-stored-unit-is-a-baseline-for-an-item-level-recompile.md)) is judged by a walk that is handed the baseline whole, the module an earlier walk judged and the record it filed, and that adopts the earlier verdict on a declaration where the declaration's terms and registry entries are the baseline's and every declaration its filed reads name stands in the part that was read: the type, universe scheme, registry entries and verdict a signature read reads, or what a body read was answered with, a definition's body and, of a proof, nothing, whatever its body is (`curios_cert::Baseline`). It decides in the order a module is stored, so everything a declaration read is settled, adopted or judged, before the declaration is asked about, and every other declaration is judged. The key of an adopted verdict is the declaration's own terms and what the certifier recorded reading while it judged them, and nothing outside the certifier computes any part of it: which declarations the fold elaborated again decides what the kernel is handed and never what it believes, so a fold that kept a declaration it should have made again costs a judgment of it and admits nothing. A read of a unit in scope stands where that unit is the one the baseline's walk read, which whoever offers the baseline says of each unit by what its slot holds, or, for a session's, by its being the unit in hand then. Of any other the walk filed the digest of the part it read, its own digest of the terms in its scope and of its verdict on them, and a walk over the baseline digests the part again and adopts where the two are one: nothing another component computed is compared, and a part that differs in a binder's name alone is judged again, which admits nothing. Whole and baseline compiles are required to agree, resource verdicts excluded, and to be stored as the same bytes. A language server's baseline may itself be a recompile (`curios-verdicts`'s `Session`), which extends by induction, an adopted verdict's record being the one filed by the walk that judged it. No unit a fold compiles from a text the disk does not hold is filed, nor any unit after one. A unit a question compiled from the text on disk, after units the disk answers for, is filed as a build files it, whole or over a baseline: the same bytes are stored either way, so neither who filed a slot nor how its unit was compiled is part of the key.
- **A unit's totality record, its reads and its families' polarity and variance vectors are admitted with it**, as parts of the unit under its one digest, the reads stored apart and restored where a walk over the unit as a baseline first asks ([`curios-cert`'s README](../../../curios-cert/README.md#a-later-walk-reads-the-certifiers-own-totality-record-never-elaborations-stamp)), and a walk over a baseline files the baseline's entry for each verdict it adopts. A filed vector stands as a filed classification does: the walk over a later unit composes strict positivity through the vector the walk over this one computed, where it would otherwise analyse every family in scope again for each unit it judges, and it reads the vector elaboration stamped on an entry nowhere.
- **The budget is not in the key.** A verdict reached under one budget is reused under another, including a smaller one, because exhausting a budget refuses and never accepts ([the soundness board](the-soundness-board.md)); a `Partial` classification and a `Mixed` in a filed polarity vector are what a budget can shape, each in the refusing direction.
- **Where it is weakest.** The compiler's identity is the running binary, which for a library embedder is its whole application, so an embedder's verdicts invalidate whenever it rebuilds, and a binary that cannot be identified disables caching. A record names files by canonical path, so moving a project invalidates its records. Both over-invalidate.
