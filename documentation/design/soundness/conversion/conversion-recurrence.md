# Conversion recurrence

**Assumes.** Absence of finite disagreement is sufficient for equality: a goal met again in the conversion history is assumed rather than unfolded forever.

**Evidence.** Argued, on the fixpoint with (T) that [Definitional proof irrelevance](definitional-proof-irrelevance.md) records beside its own; in the kernel the rule is reached only by `kernel::convert::recursion_tests`, where `a_recurring_goal_is_assumed_rather_than_unfolded_forever` reaches it on two spellings of a recursive type equation and `a_recurrence_does_not_excuse_a_finite_disagreement` is the control a blanket accept would fail.

- **The key is where this rule admits.** A history hit on two goals that are not one goal turns absence of disagreement into absence of a comparison. The kernel's key records the local context, the type and both terms, each renamed by position, and omits the case equations in force and the item's assumed universe constraints — safe because conversion mutates neither and a history lives for one top-level conversion, so both are constant while it stands. A `let`'s binder stands in that context like any other and names nothing in a goal: conversion reads its terms by value and opens no `let`, so the binders a `let` added are the same for every goal of one history. A conversion taught to consult a case equation would make the key incomplete without touching it. Where a comparison is grounded, its binders are assumed at `Type` ([Eta and untyped child positions](eta-and-untyped-child-positions.md)), so the key's context carries no information there and the terms alone separate goals.
- **The elaborator's key differs.** It renames the labels it minted in mint order and records no local context, and its walk parks goals, so two goals distinct under their binder types can share one key: `curios-elab`'s `convert::solve_tests::two_goals_distinct_under_their_binder_types_share_one_history_key`. It cannot move a verdict, because an opening is typeless everywhere in the elaborator, so a collided goal is its twin under an injective relabeling and any disagreement it hides surfaces through the twin's children (`a_goal_assumed_by_key_collision_cannot_move_the_verdict`); a rule that read an opening's type would invalidate that in silence.
- **Two instances of one recursive group are a level question**, decided before either recurrence rule is reached. The kernel decides two projections of one group that agree modulo levels by their level vectors under the item's hypotheses (`rec_instances`, through `Term::level_differences`, which keeps every level's position), held by `two_instances_of_one_recursive_group_convert_when_their_levels_are_equal_under_the_hypotheses`, `two_instances_of_one_recursive_group_at_unequal_levels_are_refused_without_unfolding` and `two_calls_of_one_recursive_group_with_different_spines_still_retry_through_an_unfolding`. The elaborator identifies the levels at the applied head or at the recurrence (`level_question`), parking a pair only an unsolved metavariable keeps from being a level question (`convert::conversion_tests::a_recurrence_whose_sides_differ_only_in_levels_is_identified_rather_than_assumed`, `a_recurrence_blocked_on_a_metavariable_parks_and_commits_once_it_is_solved`), and `a_signature_instantiating_one_recursive_definition_twice_certifies_with_its_levels_identified` puts the program to the compile path.
- **A classing does not take the assumption for the pair it is asked about.** Where the intrinsic chain hands a pair's atoms back, the kernel compares two of them under the history of the goal the pair belongs to, and a goal met again inside that comparison is assumed as it is under any operand of the congruence: the verdict that follows rests on the comparisons a pairing congruence would have as premises. The atom pair itself is the exception. A classing that found it in progress and took it as held would spell one atom as the other and decide the pair with nothing compared, so two atoms whose comparison the history already holds are left apart (`classes`, in `kernel::convert::intrinsic`), and a pair neither side of which the readers read through hands back no atoms at all, since the only pair to class would be the goal ([Intrinsic fold laws and the free-monoid peel](intrinsic-fold-laws-and-the-free-monoid-peel.md)). The elaborator's classing opens a conversion of its own for each pair, with a history of its own, so it has no assumption to take and rests on the second limit alone. `conversion_tests::two_stuck_operations_on_different_operands_do_not_convert` holds the second limit. No test reaches the first: no fixture is known that puts an atom pair's own comparison in progress once the second limit stands.
- **A verdict that rested on the rule is not remembered.** The kernel remembers a comparison's verdict by its type and its two sides, for as long as what it read of the scope stands ([The evaluation memo](the-evaluation-memo.md)), and only where deciding it met no goal in progress: `History::assumed` counts every goal met again — assumed, or in a classing left apart — and `compare` files a verdict where the count stood still across it. An accepted goal that assumed one holds on that path alone, and a refused one may have been refused by a classing that left two atoms apart because their comparison was in progress, so neither is a fact. A remembered verdict answers before its goal is entered, whatever goals are then in progress, since it rested on none of them. The key's two omissions stand for the table as they do for a history: it is cleared wherever the case equations in force change, and with the declaration, whose universe constraints are assumed once. The sets are plain, with no closure taken over accepted pairs, as Lean's kernel keeps its own and for the reason it gives: a closure's result would depend on the order goals were met in. `kernel::convert::recursion_tests::a_verdict_that_assumed_a_goal_in_progress_is_not_remembered`, mutation-checked: the two equirecursive spellings converge and are filed nowhere, where a comparison that meets no goal in progress is.
