# A float's non-finite values are literals

**Decision.** `+inf.0`, `-inf.0`, `+nan.0` and `-nan.0` are `Flt` literals: the two infinities, and the default quiet NaN with its sign clear and set. The sign is required, so `inf.0` without one stays field `0` of a binder named `inf`. A decimal too large for `Flt` is still refused rather than rounded to an infinity. Every other NaN has no literal: a report spells one as the call that builds it from its eight bytes, `Flt/of_le_bytes(x[…])`. Every `Flt` a report prints therefore reads back as itself, bit for bit. The literals are these values' only spelling: `/std/Flt` declares no constants for them.

**Rationale.** A report prints what its reader can write where it stood ([A diagnostic spells what its reader can write](../toolchain/a-diagnostic-spells-what-its-reader-can-write.md)), and `Flt` had values the grammar could not write. The printer spelled an infinity `+inf.0` and the parser refused it, so the paste-back sweep over `/std` failed at `/std/Flt/exponent`, whose bound `Finite(a)` prints reduced as `(-inf.0 < a) && (a < +inf.0)`. Every other carrier's literals already cover its values — `Nat`, `Int`, `Char` and `Str`, and `Bits` and `Bytes` through `b[…]` and `x[…]` — and giving `Flt` the same makes reading back a property of the literal grammar, owned where literals are parsed and printed, rather than of library names a printer has to know. The spelling is [R7RS Scheme's](https://standards.scheme.org/corrected-r7rs/r7rs-Z-H-12.html), which [most implementations accept and print](https://small.r7rs.org/wiki/NonFiniteNumbers/), and the printer already emitted it. Unlike R7RS, `-nan.0` is its own value rather than a synonym, since term identity is bitwise and a NaN's sign bit is observable through `copysign` and `to_le_bytes` ([`Flt` is specified by a model and the runtime conforms](flt-is-specified-by-a-model-and-the-runtime-conforms.md)).

**Rejected.** Printing named constants, which `/std/Flt` declared as `pos_inf`, `neg_inf` and `nan`, as OCaml's toplevel prints `infinity` and Julia prints `Inf`: readable, but reading back would rest on library names the printer reaches through the syntax registry, and the grammar would still refuse what the printer emitted before. Once the literals existed the constants were a second spelling of each value, and they went. Printing a `/sys` computation, `Flt/div(+1.0, +0.0)`: it needs no syntax and always reads back, but a report would show a division where its reader has a value. Exempting non-finite values from the sweep, which bends the check to fit the output. Reading an overflowing decimal such as `1.0e309` as an infinity: a literal whose meaning rests on the reader's rounding, which an author writes by mistake far more often than on purpose.
