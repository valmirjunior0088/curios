# `Prop` is strict, proof-irrelevant, and definitionally K

**Decision.** Alongside the cumulative `Type` hierarchy, Curios has a strict `Prop` with definitional proof irrelevance: `Prop : Type 0` holds, proposition types are admitted at `Type` through the existing subsumption and cumulativity, and large elimination out of `Prop` is guarded. `Eq : Prop` under that irrelevance makes the system definitionally K, and Curios takes that permanently: proofs are certificates to be erased, not data to be computed with. That is a property of the theory the checkers decide a fragment of: its equality closes irrelevance under congruence and reduction, which equates a cast along any proof of `Eq(x, x)` with its argument, while neither checker has the K-like reduction that would compute it — [A proof is never reduced to decide what irrelevance decides](a-proof-is-never-reduced-to-decide-what-irrelevance-decides.md) defers that rule with its shape stated, and a cast along a hypothetical proof stays stuck until the proof is matched on.

**Rationale.** Proof irrelevance is what makes proofs erasable by construction: any two proofs of a proposition are definitionally equal, so no program can depend on which proof it received, and erasure drops them wholesale; the large-elimination guard is what keeps that erasure sound. K is what licenses the deletion rule in index inversion (`curios-analysis/src/invert.rs`, shared by both checkers) and the substitution of a variable scrutinee by its case value. Because inversion is shared rather than duplicated, that deletion rule — an *acceptance*, unlike the unifier's refusals — sits inside the trusted base while its premise stays graded *auditable only* in [Index inversion and K](../../soundness/per-term-rules/index-inversion-and-k.md). Relocating it re-grades nothing; it changes who holds the grade.

**Rejected.** Univalence, forever: under K all proofs of an identity are definitionally equal, so a univalent equality carrying nontrivial content cannot be added later.
