# A witness premise is smaller than its head

**Decision.** A `use` premise is admitted when it is strictly smaller than the witness's own concept application under Paterson's conditions: every variable in the premise is one of the witness's binders, no variable occurs in the premise more often than in the head, and the premise has fewer nodes than the head, counting constants, applications and variables alike. `register_witness` in `curios-elab/src/resolve.rs` measures both sides and refuses the rest with `non-regular premise`.

**Rationale.** The rule exists for one property, that resolution through a premise is structurally decreasing so the resolver needs neither fuel nor tabling, and the conditions above are the weakest textbook statement of it: each recursive goal is a strictly smaller term than the goal that raised it, and the ordering is well founded. The rule it replaced, that a premise applies its concept to binders only, is a special case that refused for nothing the one shape the standard library's effect tier needs, a premise naming a constant beside a binder. `Lift(Io, (A) => Try(M, E, A))` needs `Lift(Io, M)` as a premise to embed an action into the transformer over any base, and a witness keyed on a variable base cannot be written because keys stop at rigid heads, so without this relaxation the transformer needed one nominal family per base monad. The kernel does not see the rule: witness resolution happens in the elaborator alone, and the certifier re-checks the finished terms.

**Rejected.** Keeping variables-only and writing one transformer per base monad, which multiplies the library's types by its monads. Fuel or tabling in the resolver, which would admit any premise at the price of a bound that turns a well-founded search into a budget-relative one. Keying witnesses on the full parameter shape rather than the rigid head, which would let `Lift(Io, Try(Io, E))` and `Lift(Io, Try(Async, E))` coexist but reopens the coherence argument that one witness occupies one key.
