# A bound is stated in a decided proposition and discharged by reduction

**Decision.** A precondition on a `/sys` operation is a *decided* proposition — one whose body is a `match` on the machine comparison, `True` in range and `False` outside it — and the elaborator fills it wherever the goal has already reduced to truth. So is every other relation a decider exists for, including the ones proofs recurse over: such a proof recurses on the values the decider reads rather than on evidence, and the hypothesis follows it by reduction, since the decider unfolds one step on a constructor-headed argument — cancellation makes `Nat/Le(a + 1, b + 1)` *be* `Nat/Le(a, b)`, and `Str/Valid/from(s, x[c, ..t])` is `from(step(c, s), t)`. `/std/Nat/Le`'s `trans` and `mul_mono_r` and `/std/Str/Valid`'s `drop` and `cont_len` are written that way. An inductive family is kept only where no decider exists or where its evidence carries more than its indices, as `/std/Cli`'s `Values` carries the parsed values; `Nat/Le/Ind` and `Str`'s `Utf8` were retired under this rule.

**Rationale.** The two forms look as if they fail at opposite ends: a decided proposition has no constructors to induct on, and an inductive one does not reduce, so nothing can discharge it for free. Only the second loss is real. Induction never needed the evidence's constructors, only a smaller instance to call itself at, and the values supply that — a recursive call names the smaller values, with `@` where they are implicit, since unification would otherwise solve them from the unreduced application. The reducer's cancellation is what makes the decided form self-sufficient rather than parasitic on an inductive twin — `x < x + k + 1` is now settled by the comparison itself, where it previously took `Nat/Le/Ind/add_r` to build evidence by recursion and `Nat/Lt/succ_of_ind` to bridge it back across.

That bridging was the tell. Three folds in the standard library built an inductive proof of an arithmetic fact solely to convert it into a decided one and discard it — correct, deliberate, and compensating for a mechanism that did not exist. Left alone it was becoming the idiom: it is what the folds taught by example, and every proof-carrying primitive added after them would have copied it. The alternative to the mechanism is not "slightly longer proofs", it is a standard library where bounds are established by lemma chains, which is the thing this language's obligations exist to avoid.

**Filling it is not proof search.** A metavariable whose type reduces to the trivially true proposition is solved with its constructor. The answer is unique, so there is no choice, no backtracking and no incompleteness — it is the introduction-side dual of the singleton condition the elaborator already applies to elimination, and of proof irrelevance, which already holds every inhabitant interchangeable. The rule fires exactly when the obligation is definitionally discharged and refuses exactly when it is not: `n / 256` fills silently, `n / m` demands evidence. A bound inserted before its subject is known — pinned by a later argument, by the expectation, or, in a witness's telescope, by the goal — is tried again once the subject is, and filled if it has come to truth; that later fill is a metavariable solution, which may be spliced past the arm the bound was written in, so it is decided without the arm's refinements, which the attempt at insertion may use. The kernel is untouched, because it rechecks an ordinary application.

**The escape hatch.** When the evaluator cannot reach a fact, the answer is to prove it by induction and pass it as `@` — which is what `/std/Str`'s `drop_width_within` does. For that route to be open to a bound whose recursive function returns one of its own parameters in the base arm, the ordinary shape of an accumulator, an unfolding is kept when it exposed a head **or** lost the group it was recursing on, so `f(0, acc)` reducing to `acc` is an answer rather than a discarded `Var`. A reducer that discards a reduct on its head constructor alone — a test for "made no progress" that also catches "finished at an answer that happens to be a variable" — makes the base case of every such lemma unprovable in decided form, so `Eq(Bytes/len(go(0, acc)), Bytes/len(acc))` checks while `Nat/Le` over the same terms does not. Both reducers carry the rule, written separately, and `tests::numeric`'s `a_bound_over_a_recursion_returning_a_parameter_discharges` is the fixture with its literal-returning control beside it.

**A guard feeds the reducer and a hypothesis does not, and the asymmetry is the elimination rule's rather than a gap.** An arm's equation is licensed by the case it is in — within the arm the scrutinee *is* that case's value — so what enters the store is one term standing for one constructor-shaped value, keyed on the scrutinee as written and retracted with the arm. A hypothesis in scope carries no such licence: `sized: Eq(List/len(l), n * k)` says two terms are equal without saying which stands for which, an ordering says less still, and a context holding one may be inconsistent, where an arm's is reachable by construction. Entering either into conversion is the step CoqMT restricted itself to weak elimination to avoid, and it is a larger change than it looks — the store would need an orientation, an occurs check across everything already in it, and the same rule in both checkers. So the route for a fact the reducer cannot reach is transport: `Eq/subst` rewrites the bound's subject and the obligation discharges on the rewritten spelling, as `/std/Vec`'s `get` and `/std/List`'s `drop` do. What the probe *will* cross is a spelling — the `<`/`<=` seam and a comparison's dual, both recorded in [A comparison is spelled one way when it is stuck](../toolchain/a-comparison-is-spelled-one-way-when-it-is-stuck.md) — and the line between the two is exactly that: a second spelling of the fact a guard already decided is a lookup, while a fact that follows from two hypotheses by arithmetic is a proof, and `tests::matching`'s `a_bound_a_hypothesis_only_implies_is_still_stuck` is the fixture that holds it there.

**What still constrains these obligations is evaluation, not provability.** A decided bound is discharged by reducing its subject, so a bound over a *computed* value runs that computation at elaboration time, and the step budget is what decides whether it finishes. `Bytes/slice(built, 0, 10)` over an accumulator built in a few thousand steps discharges its own bound at the ordinary budget; the same spelling over a hundred thousand refuses, because a hundred thousand iterations asks for more work than the default budget affords. The three fixtures pin the contrast at one budget rather than the figures, which move with the price list. Keeping a subject opaque — a parameter, refined once behind a guard — remains the way to write a bound that computes nothing at all, but it is now a choice about cost rather than the only route that survives. `tests::numeric`'s `a_bound_on_a_small_computed_subject_discharges`, `a_bound_on_a_computed_subject_evaluates_it` and `a_bound_behind_a_parameter_evaluates_nothing` pin all three points under stated budgets.

**The budget is a fact about the program, and it sees construction.** A fusion of every all-literal concatenation into one packed value makes an accumulation recopy everything accumulated so far on every step — a linear number of transitions performing a quadratic volume of construction, which a counter of transitions cannot see, so the obvious spelling neither refuses nor times out but exhausts the machine. `curios-core`'s `FUSION_CAP` stops fusing past a documented operand size, the free monoid's measure reads a length off the resulting spine instead of rebuilding a `len` per operand, and the counter prices what a step builds — [A reduction step costs what it builds](../toolchain/a-reduction-step-costs-what-it-builds.md) — so a bound over a computed subject is constrained by a budget rather than by a host's memory.

**Rejected: stating the decision as an equation, `Eq(b, true)`, instead of reflecting it through `Holds(b)`.** The equation subsumes the reflection logically — `Eq(true, true)` is filled by `refl` as uniquely as `True` is by `qed`, `Eq(false, true)` is empty by the inverter's literal clash as `False` is by reduction, and the equation rewrites by `Eq/subst` where `Holds(b)` only case-splits — so the reflection is not kept for what it can prove. It is kept for where a bound is *used*: at a guard. `Holds(a < b)` is consumed by `match a < b`, and a guard over an expression is exactly what the refinement store records and serves, in both checkers, whereas an `Eq(a < b, true)` hypothesis is consumed either by transport with a written motive or by matching on it, which asks the inverter to refine an expression index rather than the variable it is built for. The two closed cases being reductions rather than a fill rule and an inversion is the smaller half of the argument. What the equation offers on demand, `Bool`'s `eq_of_holds` and `holds_of_eq` give in one match each.

**Rejected.** Stating preconditions in an inductive order such as the retired `Nat/Le/Ind` (every call site then builds evidence constructor by constructor, which is what the folds were already doing). Keeping such an order beside the decided one for proofs to recurse over: two statements of one relation need bridges in both directions, every proof that uses both pays a crossing, and the twin does nothing a recursion on the values does not. Adding a general arithmetic decision procedure first (an `omega` equivalent is a larger and separate capability; the facts these obligations need are settled by cancellation, and the ones that are not — a bound on a recursive function — want structural induction rather than a solver). Giving a partial operation a total `/std` face beside its proof-carrying one, so callers who cannot prove take the total face: both faces return the same type, so nothing pushes a caller toward the checked one, and the bound reverts to discipline — the defect the obligations were introduced to close. Leaving the obligation to witness resolution instead of filling it: metavariables are solved by unification alone and reach `zonk` unsolved, proof irrelevance equates inhabitants but manufactures none, and `HeadKey` keys only on type heads — so a value-indexed `NonZero(b)` is unkeyable and cannot be a concept at all.
