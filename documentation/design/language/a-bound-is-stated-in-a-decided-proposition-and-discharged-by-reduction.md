# A bound is stated in a decided proposition and discharged by reduction

**Decision.** A precondition on a `/sys` operation is a *decided* proposition — one whose body is a `match` on the machine comparison, `True` in range and `False` outside it — and the elaborator fills it wherever the goal has already reduced to truth. The inductive families keep their own job: `Nat/Lte` exists to be *matched on*, in laws like `trans` and `add_mono_l` that recurse over the relation's constructors. Establishing a bound is not that job.

**Rationale.** The two forms fail at opposite ends. A decided proposition has no constructors, so nothing can induct on it; an inductive one does not reduce, so nothing can discharge it for free. Splitting them by purpose gives each the end it is good at, and the reducer's cancellation is what makes the decided half self-sufficient rather than parasitic on the inductive one — `x < x + k + 1` is now settled by the comparison itself, where it previously took `Nat/Lte/add_r` to build evidence by recursion and `Nat/Lt/succ_of_lte` to bridge it back across.

That bridging was the tell. Three folds in the standard library built an inductive proof of an arithmetic fact solely to convert it into a decided one and discard it — correct, deliberate, and compensating for a mechanism that did not exist. Left alone it was becoming the idiom: it is what the folds taught by example, and every proof-carrying primitive added after them would have copied it. The alternative to the mechanism is not "slightly longer proofs", it is a standard library where bounds are established by lemma chains, which is the thing this language's obligations exist to avoid.

**Filling it is not proof search.** A metavariable whose type reduces to the trivially true proposition is solved with its constructor. The answer is unique, so there is no choice, no backtracking and no incompleteness — it is the introduction-side dual of the singleton condition the elaborator already applies to elimination, and of proof irrelevance, which already holds every inhabitant interchangeable. The rule fires exactly when the obligation is definitionally discharged and refuses exactly when it is not: `n / 256` fills silently, `n / m` demands evidence. The kernel is untouched, because it rechecks an ordinary application.

**The escape hatch, and what it cost to open.** When the evaluator cannot reach a fact, the intended answer is to prove it by induction and pass it as `@` — which is what `/std/Str/utf8`'s `drop_width_within` does. That route was shut for a bound whose recursive function returns one of its own parameters in the base arm, the ordinary shape of an accumulator, because the base case of any such lemma was unprovable in decided form and the induction could not be started. It is open now: an unfolding is kept when it exposed a head **or** lost the group it was recursing on, so `f(0, acc)` reducing to `acc` is an answer rather than a discarded `Var`. Both reducers carry that rule, written separately, and `tests::numeric`'s `a_bound_over_a_recursion_returning_a_parameter_discharges` is the fixture with its literal-returning control beside it.

**The cause was never what this entry first guessed, and the guess is worth keeping as a warning.** It read the discriminator as "a program-local `rec`", speculating that such a term elaborates to an inline `Rec` with slots where a prelude global does not. It was neither: `force_rec` granted one definitional unfolding and then discarded the reduct on its *head constructor* alone, a test for "made no progress" that also caught "finished at an answer that happens to be a variable". `drop_width` was not the exception that disproved the rule but an instance of it — its arms return `0` and `1 + drop_width(…)`, `Intrinsic`-headed in every case, so the discard never fired. Two paths reached the same unfolding and only one discarded, which is why `Eq(Bytes/len(go(0, acc)), Bytes/len(acc))` checked while `Nat/Le` over the same terms did not.

**What still constrains these obligations is evaluation, not provability.** A decided bound is discharged by reducing its subject, so a bound over a *computed* value runs that computation at elaboration time, and the step budget is what decides whether it finishes. `Bytes/slice(built, 0, 10)` over an accumulator built in a few thousand steps discharges its own bound at the ordinary budget; the same spelling over a hundred thousand refuses, because a hundred thousand iterations costs about seventeen million steps and the default allows one million. Keeping a subject opaque — a parameter, refined once behind a guard — remains the way to write a bound that computes nothing at all, but it is now a choice about cost rather than the only route that survives. `tests::numeric`'s `a_bound_on_a_small_computed_subject_discharges`, `a_bound_on_a_computed_subject_evaluates_it` and `a_bound_behind_a_parameter_evaluates_nothing` pin all three points under stated budgets.

**That used to be a claim about memory, and the correction is the reason this paragraph is worth reading twice.** Reduction fused an all-literal concatenation into one packed value, so an accumulation recopied everything accumulated so far on every step: a linear number of transitions performing a quadratic volume of construction, which the step counter could not see. The obvious spelling therefore did not refuse and did not time out — it exhausted the machine. `curios-core`'s `FUSION_CAP` stops fusing past a documented operand size, and the free monoid's measure reads a length off the resulting spine instead of rebuilding a `len` per operand, so what remains is an ordinary bounded computation. A bound is now constrained by a budget, which is a fact about the program, rather than by a host's memory, which was a fact about the machine.

**Rejected.** Stating preconditions in the inductive `Nat/Lte` (every call site then builds evidence constructor by constructor, which is what the folds were already doing). Adding a general arithmetic decision procedure first (an `omega` equivalent is a larger and separate capability; the facts these obligations need are settled by cancellation, and the ones that are not — a bound on a recursive function — want structural induction rather than a solver). Giving a partial operation a total `/std` face beside its proof-carrying one, so callers who cannot prove take the total face: both faces return the same type, so nothing pushes a caller toward the checked one, and the bound reverts to discipline — the defect the obligations were introduced to close. Leaving the obligation to witness resolution instead of filling it: metavariables are solved by unification alone and reach `zonk` unsolved, proof irrelevance equates inhabitants but manufactures none, and `HeadKey` keys only on type heads — so a value-indexed `NonZero(b)` is unkeyable and cannot be a concept at all.
