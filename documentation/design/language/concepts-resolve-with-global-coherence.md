# Concepts resolve with global coherence

**Decision.** Ad-hoc polymorphism is concepts and witnesses. Witness resolution consults one program-wide table under global coherence checks, and anonymous witnesses fill structure the goal already determines. A registered witness never writes a superclass slot: a `use` entry in a `satisfy` body is refused by the grammar, so resolution fills every one and the superclass a witness carries is the one the table holds.

**Rationale.** Coherence makes the chosen witness a fact about the program rather than about the scope of the call site, so moving code or reorganizing imports cannot silently change which witness runs. A data structure whose invariant is stated in terms of a witness needs exactly that: `/std/Map` is a crit-bit trie whose identity *is* the byte string a `Key` witness produces, so an insert and a lookup resolving to two different `Key(K)` witnesses would corrupt the trie with no type error anywhere — the guarantee is not a convenience, it is what lets a container name a concept in its own operations at all.

**Rejected.** Scope-local coherence, where the witness is whatever is in scope at the call site — Scala's implicits and OCaml's modular implicits. It buys the ability to select a witness locally, which the anonymous-witness design already covers for the cases worth covering, and it costs the property above: the same call means two things in two files, and a `Map` built in one and read in the other is silently wrong rather than rejected.

**Rejected.** A written superclass slot in a `satisfy`, which the grammar once admitted because a witness body was read as a concept literal. With `satisfy Eql(Mine);` registered and `satisfy Ord(Mine) { use always, … }` beside it, `x == y` answered `false` through the table and `true` through the superclass projection of a local `Ord(A)` — two implicit resolutions of one equality on one type, which is exactly what coherence rules out, reached with no explicit dictionary anywhere. Two weaker rules were weighed and lost. Requiring the written slot to convert with the registered witness accepts only the programs where writing it was pointless, and conversion is intensional, so an equal witness spelled differently is refused. Refusing the written slot only where resolution would find a witness keeps a superclass that exists solely inside its subclass's witness, reachable through the projection and not through the table — and both rules have to wait for the end of the unit, since a witness may be declared after the code that resolves through it. Filling by resolution needs neither a comparison nor a deferral of its own, and costs the standard library nothing: none of its witnesses wrote the slot. A concept *literal* keeps `use value`: that is an explicit dictionary, handed over by name, and a different question.

**Rejected.** Global coherence *without* the orphan rule, resolving a collision when the program is linked. Two unrelated roots may then each legally `satisfy` the same `(concept, key)` pair, and that collision is unfixable once both are linked into one program: neither party is wrong on its own, neither can withdraw its witness without breaking its own consumers, and the person who hits it is a third party who wrote neither. Requiring a witness to be declared where its concept or a rigid head of its key is already declared makes the collision unrepresentable instead of arbitrated — `curios-elab/src/resolve.rs` states the argument at the check, and [Witness coherence and the orphan rule](../../soundness/per-term-rules/witness-coherence-and-the-orphan-rule.md) records how far it has been probed.
