# A witness body may be written by the compiler

**Decision.** A witness declaration may omit its body — `satisfy Spell(Point);`, or `satisfy (@A: Type, use Spell(A)) => Spell(Tree(A));` under a telescope, alone or as a member of an `and` group beside written ones — and the compiler writes the body from the declaration of the type in the key. The signature stays the programmer's: it registers, keys, and meets the orphan and one-witness-per-key rules exactly as a written witness does, since registration reads the signature alone. Lowering carries the declaration as the same anonymous definition a written witness becomes, with `Transient::Derive` in body position (`curios-core/src/term/shape.rs`), so the telescope's binders and `use` premises are in scope when the body is checked; `elaborate_derive` (`curios-elab/src/derive.rs`) reads the concept application the transient is checked against, refuses a sealed concept outside its module with the rule a written literal meets, looks the concept up among the registered derivations by registry slot, and elaborates the Core the derivation returns under the same expectation — so a derived body is typed, resolved, zonked and certified like any authored one, and the kernel refuses the transient as `NotCore` should one ever reach it. Derivability is a property of the concept, registered against its slot: `Spell` (`SpellSyntax` in `curios-utilities/src/syntax.rs`) and `Eql` (`OperatorSyntax::eql`) derive, and every other concept refuses the form by name, so the hand-written witness remains the norm.

The derivations read a declaration one way. The key must reduce to a registered `induct` or `struct` — not an intrinsic carrier, a tuple or function shape, or a concept's own record — transparent at the declaring island, fully applied, and not `Prop`-sorted. Each constructor's telescope (or the struct's field telescope) is opened at the key's parameters with one binder per payload, and each explicit payload is classified by its type: a type is refused, a proof takes no part beyond what the derivation states for it, and every other payload goes through the concept's own method — `Spell/spell`, `Eql/eql` — applied with a `use` argument the body mints itself as a witness goal carrying a provenance of its own, so ordinary resolution finds a telescope premise, the witness's own table entry, or an `and` sibling, and an unresolved goal reports the constructor and the payload, with the `use` premise to add when the payload's type is a variable of the telescope. `Spell` emits one match arm per constructor in declaration order, each a single application of a `/syn/Spell` renderer — `call` for a constructor over its spelled explicit payloads, `record` for a struct over its labeled fields — so a value spells as its constructor's absolute path applied to its payloads and a struct as its literal, labeled where the field has a label; the renderers are Curios, re-certified by the kernel on every prelude build, and the compiler only ever emits an application of one. `Eql` matches its two arguments in turn, an arm per constructor on the first and a one-arm match on the second with a `| _ => false` default, comparing payloads pairwise under `&&`; `neq` negates the same comparison. `/std` derives both witnesses for `Option`, `Result` and `Order`, so every workspace check exercises both derivations over the prelude; the intrinsic carriers stay written by design, and tuple shapes take `/std/Tuple`'s written per-arity witnesses.

**Rationale.** A witness body for `Spell` or `Eql` is determined by the declaration it is written for, and writing it by hand is where the two drift: the harness's `Test/equal` reports through `Spell`, and a report that misspells a constructor is worse than none. Deriving *in the compiler*, against the registry the elaborator already reads, is what keeps the derived body inside the ordinary soundness story: it is a term, checked, resolved and certified, never a template. Putting the transient in body position rather than expanding at the Text stage is what gives the derivation the declaration's telescope, the concept's reduced application, and the registry's elaborated constructor types — none of which exists before elaboration — and what makes an unresolved payload witness an ordinary deferred goal, retried between items and drained with the rest. The renderers live in `/syn` because a compiler-emitted name lives there, and they are Curios rather than Rust so the re-parse grammar is spelled once and certified rather than restated in an emitter. Sealing is checked at the declaration, before the derivation lookup, so that the compiler's body can never become a door through a concept's representation privacy, and so the refusal lands on the declaration with the written form's exact report.

Three details follow from reading the registry rather than the source. The constructor path is the declaration's — `/std/Option/Option/some`, the induct `Option` in the module `/std/Option` — rather than the facade's `/std/Option/some` the written witnesses once spelled; both re-parse, and the derivation spells the one identity Core carries. An unlabeled field or payload reaches Core under the lowerer's `_{position}` hint and no other mark, so that hint is read back as "no label" and the field spells positionally, which the literal grammar admits for any struct. And within the prelude's own unit the scheduler cannot see the names a derived body will reference through the transient, so `order_flat_items` adds the derivation's vocabulary — the method wrapper and the renderers — as hard edges for a body-less witness of the concept its signature names.

**Rejected.** *Deriving clauses on the type declaration* (`induct Tree … deriving Spell`). A witness is declared where its concept or a type in its key is, and a clause on the type could only ever put it at the type; the orphan rule's other half — a program deriving a standard concept for its own type, or its own concept for a standard type — needs the declaration to be a `satisfy`, which is also what keeps the telescope and its premises where the programmer writes them.

*Inferring the premises.* `satisfy Spell(Tree(A));` could mint `use Spell(A)` for every parameter the payloads mention. A telescope is the programmer's statement of what the witness needs; inferred premises would make the key's parameters implicit binders nobody wrote, and a payload needing no premise (a phantom parameter) indistinguishable from one the programmer forgot. The report names the premise to add instead.

*A Text-stage expansion.* Generating surface syntax from the surface declaration has neither the reduced key nor the elaborated constructor types — a `let Alias: Type = Tree(Nat);` key, a universe-instantiated payload — and would leave the derived text outside the module's binder discipline. The transient costs one Core variant and gives the derivation everything elaboration knows.

*Deriving `Show`.* `Show` is the human-facing display and deliberately underivable: its renderings are chosen per type, and a derived one would be `Spell` under another name.

*A description-based route* — a `Mirror`/`Generic` concept describing a type's constructors as data, with derivations written in Curios over it. The types here have dependent telescopes: an indexed family's payload types mention earlier payloads and the family's indices, which a first-order field description cannot express, and a description rich enough to express them is the elaborator's registry. What a later derivable concept needs beyond these two — derivations that use other derivations (`Ord` over `Eql`), derivations that produce proofs for law fields, the harness's generator — is one more arm and one more slot at the same seam.
