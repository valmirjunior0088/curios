# A proof is never reduced to decide what irrelevance decides

**Decision.** Both checkers decide a comparison at a `Prop`-sorted type by definitional proof irrelevance before either side is reduced, wherever neither side is flexible — the kernel's order from the start, and now the elaborator's. K-like reduction — firing a relevant match on a proof of a one-constructor proposition whose payload the indices pin, without reducing the proof — is not a rule of either checker: a cast along a hypothetical proof, `Eq/subst(P, p, v)` with `p` a hypothesis, stays stuck, and matching on `p` first is the spelling that computes. When a program needs the rule, it takes this shape: tried before the scrutinee is forced, never after; decided by `invert_indices` with the constructor's payload binders flexible, firing only when every binder is solved and no position is refused; the scrutinee's instance synthesized from its neutral spelling; the step written in each reducer, the classification shared. A proposition whose constructor carries a proof payload — `Accessible` is one — keeps eliminating into data by reducing that proof, which is Lean's position rather than Rocq's.

**Rationale.** The kernel's reason is the whole of the ordering: reducing a proof in order to discover it equals another proof is work whose answer was already known. That work is not always finite, and not only in principle. Abel and Coquand (*Failure of Normalization in Impredicative Type Theory with Proof-Irrelevant Propositional Equality*, LMCS 16(2:14), 2020) show that an impredicative `Prop` with an irrelevant equality loses normalization once a cast along a proof reduces without inspecting it, and in Curios that loop is reachable with no K rule at all: inside an arm of `match h(Top, Top)` the case equation makes that proof `refl`, so their `Omega(h)` unfolds forever. It is refused by the budget and threatens no consistency, since it lives only under an absurd hypothesis, but a checker that reduces proofs before asking what they are spends its budget there — the elaborator did, and `tests::perimeter::a_proof_is_not_reduced_to_compare_it_with_another` is the program.

**Completeness is not the goal, and cannot be.** Curios's `Prop` is impredicative, definitionally irrelevant, and admits singleton elimination into data including a recursive family, which is Lean's position; Carneiro (*The Type Theory of Lean*, 2019, §3.1) shows definitional equality there is undecidable and any checker for it non-transitive. Curios reproduces the triple: `f(0, lt, a)` converts with `f(0, lt, intro(inv(0, a)))` by irrelevance, that converts with its one-step reduct, and the two ends stay apart. So [`Prop` is strict, proof-irrelevant, and definitionally K](prop-is-strict-proof-irrelevant-and-definitionally-k.md) describes the theory; the checkers decide a sound, budgeted fragment of it.

**K is deferred rather than refused because nothing asks for it.** Every `Eq/subst` and every `refl` match in `/std` lands in a proposition, where irrelevance already decides it; nothing in `programs/` or the test fixtures needs a cast along a hypothesis to compute while the proof is still a hypothesis; and the refinement a match installs is the workaround wherever something would. `curios-cert`'s conversion adds an acceptance only against a program that needs it. The shape is recorded so the rule arrives as designed. It is tried before the scrutinee is forced because that is the loop it can remove: Lean's kernel asks `to_cnstr_when_K` about the major premise's type before reducing it, and Rocq's `case_inversion` never reads the scrutinee at all. It is decided by inversion rather than by a new unifier because inversion already refuses metavariables — Lean's elaborator likewise refuses to fire K by assigning `?x := y` — and its deletion rule is already licensed by K. And its runtime half exists: erasure takes a proof match's single arm without evaluating the proof, reading each pinned payload from the scrutinee's index, and `tests::erasure::value_tests` holds that it computes the value the checkers prove.

**Rejected.** Rocq's criterion, which fires when the motive at the recorded indices converts with the motive at the constructor's even where the indices differ: Rocq's `seq` binds nothing in its arm, while Curios's `refl(@z)` binds a payload, so with unequal indices the step would have to choose which one `z` is, and reduction would depend on the choice. Recording the scrutinee's family instance on the `match` node, as Rocq's `CaseInvert` does, so an untyped reducer could fire without synthesizing anything: the kernel reduces a `match` in a type position before anything types it, so the record would be believed before it was checked. Restricting relevant elimination to empty and pinned-payload propositions, as Rocq, Agda and Gilbert, Cockx, Sozeau and Tabareau (*Definitional Proof-Irrelevance without K*, POPL 2019) do — the restriction behind their decidability results, and one that costs the corpus nothing, `Accessible` never being eliminated into data: Curios makes no decidability claim for it to restore, and a language restriction should arrive on a program's evidence. A loop detector in reduction: the step budget already refuses, deterministically.
