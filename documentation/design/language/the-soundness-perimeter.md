# The soundness perimeter

**Decision.** The claim above quantifies over *every* rule that can admit a term, so it is stated against an enumerated perimeter rather than against prose. That perimeter is [soundness.md](../../soundness.md): every entry, what it assumes, its grade, and the evidence standing behind it.

**It is a separate document because the two halves have different lifecycles.** A decision changes when someone decides something, and rarely. An entry's grade changes every time its rule is attacked, and the evidence under it accumulates run by run — which is why the ledger had grown to rival this file, and why someone here for a design rationale was paging through an audit log to find one. The split is by what churns, not by what the topic is: the decisions this perimeter rests on stay here, beside the rest of the language's.

**Rejected.** Stating the trusted base as prose, which is what this replaces: it could not be checked, could not be falsified, and gave no way to tell a rule that had been attacked from one that had merely been assumed. Also rejected: treating a passing corpus as evidence of coverage. Every defect this design has produced was found by counting or by probing, and none by the corpus failing — the corpus passed throughout the long period when (V)'s argument rule was firing at almost none of the sites it was written for.

**An enumeration that is not itself checked can be short, and this one was.** Three rules that admit terms had no entry: sort formation, subsumption, and conversion's eta together with its untyped child positions. None of them was a defect — the first two are argued and the third only forfeits rules — but a table that reads as *every* rule and holds all but three is making a stronger claim than it can support, and the way that happened is worth naming. Entries arrived by being attacked. A rule nobody had thought to attack therefore had no entry, so the file recorded the history of the hunt where it claimed to record the surface, and the two coincide only until someone asks the question this paragraph came from.
