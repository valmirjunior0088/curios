# A bound that follows from the facts in scope is proved by the elaborator

**Decision.** An omitted proof the fill leaves open is proved by the elaborator where it follows by linear arithmetic from the facts in scope: the hypotheses, their proof fields one level down, and the guards of the arms around it, each read through the local definitions and refinements in scope. A disequality among them — an `==` guard's false arm, a `!=` guard's true one, a hypothesis or a field that states either — is read last, where the search over the others and their products has left an assignment, as the order comparison conversion holds it to be: `n != 0` is `0 < n` at `Nat`, proved by what states it; and one neither of whose sides bounds the other is the two cases their order makes, `a < b` where `a <= b` holds, by `lt/of_le_ne`, and `b < a` where it fails. The bound itself is any of `<`, `<=`, `!=` and `==`: a disequality is proved by refuting the equation, and an equality by refuting its disequality. The elaborator searches for a certificate — non-negative multipliers under which the facts and the negated goal combine into a false constant — and writes it as an ordinary term, a sum of the facts through `le/add`, which elaboration checks as written code and the certifier rechecks. A decision conversion equates with `true` where reduction does not, `b || Bool/not(b)`, is proved without a search, by `Bool/holds_of_eq` over `Eq/refl()`. A proposition a fact in scope states outright is proved by that fact, whatever the proposition is: the first hypothesis, or proof field one level down, whose statement converts with the bound with nothing solved. Where no certificate is found, the bound is refused with a report naming the facts considered, the facts left unread and, where the search produced one, an assignment of the atoms that satisfies the facts and falsifies the goal. Three `/std` entry points reach the one procedure: an omitted `@` bound, `/std/Bool/False/refuted()` in a contradiction arm, and `/std/Bool/True/proved()` for a proposition no bound demands. A proof form is written only from names in scope, and inside `/std`, which declares them, they are offered to every declaration they do not themselves read ([A declaration is a function of what it reads](../compilation/a-declaration-is-a-function-of-what-it-reads.md)). The procedure is `curios-elab`'s `entailment`, whose documentation is its contract, and `curios`'s `tests::bounds` is its grid.

**Rationale.**

- **Nothing is trusted that was not.** The proof is a term, so a wrong certificate is a term that does not check, and the procedure's mistakes are refusals. No conversion rule, refinement or solving choice changes, and the procedure assigns only the hole it was asked about, so it adds nothing to the trusted base.
- **Hand-written arithmetic is this bridge.** A chain of order lemmas carries a fact in scope to the fact a term needs — an `@` bound, a contradiction arm, a fact handed to another lemma — and such a chain is linear arithmetic over the facts in scope, products aside, so the procedure writes the chains `/std` would otherwise write; what stays by hand is a chain reading a proof field two levels down, and an `Eq/subst` transport that rewrites an equation or under a function.
- **A fact that states the bound is found by conversion alone.** It is asked before the search, the fact being then the whole proof, and it is the one step that needs to read nothing in the bound — a decision the linear view does not open, as `/std/Str/Valid`'s scan of its bytes is, or an inductive proposition such as an equation — which is what an invariant a structure's hidden field states often is. A proposition is proof-irrelevant, so which fact is taken decides nothing. Each statement in scope is compared with the bound and nothing is solved by it, so the step makes no solving choice: a bound still waiting on its subject is stated by no fact yet, and is asked again when the subject is known.
- **It stands beside the fill, not in it.** The fill is a unique answer and this is a search ([A bound is stated in a decided proposition and discharged by reduction](a-bound-is-stated-in-a-decided-proposition-and-discharged-by-reduction.md)).
- **The search is Fourier–Motzkin, capped by the rows it derives.** Mathlib's `linarith` is the model — an untrusted oracle finds the certificate and normalization checks it — with conversion's cancellation as the normalizer. `linarith` keeps Fourier–Motzkin as the engine "sometimes faster on small states" ([`Mathlib.Tactic.Linarith.Frontend`](https://florisvandoorn.com/carleson/docs/Mathlib/Tactic/Linarith/Frontend.html)), and the states here are small: over the whole standard library a search derives 6.5 rows on average and 27 at most (the `entailment::derived` sample), against a cap of 4 096. The cap counts derived rows, never host time, so a program finds the same certificate on every machine, and exhausting it refuses as the reduction budget does.
- **The fragment is the linear one, extended by definitions read as facts.** A consequence over the rationals is found within the cap, a strict integer fact strengthened to its successor; one that holds only over the integers and needs a cut is refused, as `linarith` and `omega` without its shadows refuse it. Truncated subtraction enters through the case split `omega` makes, a `min` or a `max` through the split on its operands' order, and an `abs` or a `sign` through the splits on its operand's sign, none of which conversion reads a hypothesis for. What both cases of a split state is asked with no case opened where conversion decides it — `a - b <= a`, a `min` at most each operand, a `max` at least each — after the facts alone and before the first case, so a bound the facts prove is proved as it was, one that turns on no case opens none, and a sum of such operations is not a case for each way their orders fall. A `Nat` quotient and remainder enter through the quotient's bounds `d * (x / d) <= x < d * (x / d) + d`, whatever the divisor, a multiple by a divisor that is no literal being a product; at `Int` conversion decides neither of a remainder's bounds, so there a quotient is no fact. A power of a base conversion holds at least one, as the `pow(2, m)` a shift by a symbolic count reads as a product by is, enters as at least one, which conversion decides. A variable multiplier enters through the pairwise products Mathlib's `nlinarith` adds, asked only where linear arithmetic finds an assignment; a product of two sums, which the fold leaves standing until a comparison asks, is read distributed, as a comparison reads it.
- **A disequality is read last, and its split opens nearest guard first.** Read where the guards are, it hands out atoms, classes and spellings every search over the scope shares, and a proof it takes no part in is written another way; read by a reader of its own once the search over the other facts and their products has left an assignment, as the products are, a bound that was proved before a disequality was read at all is proved as it was. `linarith` leaves a disequality out because the runs double with each, and splits on request ([`Mathlib.Tactic.Linarith.Frontend`](https://florisvandoorn.com/carleson/docs/Mathlib/Tactic/Linarith/Frontend.html)); here the splits open in the order they were read, each where the search without it left an assignment, which is the order `omega` takes its disjunctions in ([`Lean.Elab.Tactic.Omega`](https://lean-lang.org/doc/api/Lean/Elab/Tactic/Omega.html)), and the cap on derived rows bounds what a bound under many of them costs.
- **A disequality and an equality are goals with no lemma of their own.** The proof that refutes a goal splits on the goal's own decision, and in the arm where it came back `false`, `qed` proves what the negation states. For `a != b` that is the equation, whose two bounds the certificate weighs apart, as `omega` introduces `x = y` for a goal `x ≠ y` ([`Lean.Elab.Tactic.Omega`](https://lean-lang.org/doc/api/Lean/Elab/Tactic/Omega.html)); for `a == b` it is the disequality, read as one in scope is, its split the first the search opens. One conversion holds to be an order, as `n != 0` is `0 < n` at `Nat`, is asked as that order, so the facts' sum is its proof as it stands. So `Int`'s division, which asks that its divisor is not zero, is licensed by an order check of the divisor as `Nat`'s is.
- **Two operations whose guards are one comparison share one split.** An arm of a split on `a + c <= b + c` decides `a <= b`, the equation it records answering every term conversion holds equal to its scrutinee or to its negation, so a second split inside it would have a case no value reaches, where `qed` proves no fact of that case's guard. A guard the linear view reads as an open split's comparison joins that split, and one it reads as the comparison's negation joins it with its cases exchanged.
- **It runs where the elaborator would otherwise report the bound, and nowhere else.** At insertion the arm's guards are live and the proof is written into the arm; at a parked bound's retry the guards are those its slot was born under, which is what re-validation judges a solution by. So a program the fill alone accepts never reaches it. It does not run after the item closes, as a Lean tactic block does, because a guard here is a refinement rather than a binder.
- **A proof is written over the spellings in scope and read through their reducts.** The search reads each statement reduced, as conversion computes it, but writes its operands as stated: a local definition's value names locals the statement does not, and a call's reduct carries arms that elaborate only where they were written. A guard's fact is the exception, written over the guard's own reduct, since `qed` checks against a guard wherever the arm's equation answers the statement, which is wherever conversion holds it equal to the guard's scrutinee; a range check's two bounds alone are read from its written spelling, `le/of_in_range` giving them where no reduct does. What the facts invent over atoms is written through the spelling each atom was read from, and the whole proof with the metavariables elaboration has solved substituted, so it names an operand one way throughout: a fold pairs by identity, and an operator's witness stands as a metavariable in one statement and as the witness in another.
- **An atom is a class of terms the elaborator's conversion holds one.** The linear view pairs atoms by identity, so each operand is classified among those read before it, its opaque atoms put to conversion pair by pair with nothing committed and its operations held by their own readers, and the operand is read one spelling to a class, as the conversion chain reads a pair ([A term is one where conversion says so](a-term-is-one-where-conversion-says-so.md)). A call a hypothesis states at `k + 1` and the goal states at the successor an arm refined a variable to is then one unknown to the search, where reduction opens neither inside the stuck call. The proof names each operand as it was stated, and conversion holds the two spellings one where it checks the sum.
- **The lint credits what a proof reads**, by declaration and place, so a hypothesis only the written proof sums is used rather than reported, and keeps the name that documents its precondition ([A lint is an exact finding read off the compilation](../tools/a-lint-is-an-exact-finding-read-off-the-compilation.md)).
- **The proofs are small and cheap.** The standard library's 73 proofs average 104 nodes, the largest 518; checking them takes 2.2 s of the standard library's 85.9 s instrumented elaboration. The figures are `cargo xtask workspace lint`'s profile streams folded by `curios profile`: the `entailment::written` and `entailment::derived` samples and the `entailment::check` span.

**Rejected.**

- **Hypotheses in conversion**, for the reason [A bound is stated in a decided proposition and discharged by reduction](a-bound-is-stated-in-a-decided-proposition-and-discharged-by-reduction.md) gives. The Calculus of Congruent Inductive Constructions' metatheory holds for the weak recursor only, and `Holds` is a large elimination. A mistake there is a false definitional equation; a mistake here is a term that does not check.
- **A search over the facts for a bound that is no comparison** — congruence, symmetry, or a fact's consequence such as one conjunct of an opaque conjunction. A fact that states the bound is found by conversion alone, and anything further is a search with no fragment to be complete for.
- **Reflection first.** A checker written in Curios and proved sound once would make each proof an application to a closed certificate, trading a one-time proof for smaller terms; Chaieb and Nipkow measured it one to two orders of magnitude faster in Isabelle (2008). The proofs are small enough that nothing asks for it yet.
- **An external solver.** Linear arithmetic needs no SMT solver, and a dependency outside the workspace would buy nothing the search does not do.
- **A tactic syntax.** [Syntax forms are closed](../surface/syntax-forms-are-closed-semantics-extend-by-witness.md); the entry points are `/std` declarations with `@` bounds.
- **Running after the item closes.** It would lose the guards.
- **A heuristic search.** Acceptance would depend on the heuristic; the procedure is complete for its fragment within a deterministic cap.
- **A split one of whose cases the facts refute, read as its other case before any is opened.** It would spare a bound the guards around the one it needs, at the cost of a search a split and of a certificate that names its splits out of order; the nearest guard opens first, and the cap answers the rest.
- **A default arm read as its siblings' literals ruled out.** `match count | 0 => 0 | _ => total / count end` is refused, and the spelling that is read is `| k + 1 =>`: an arm is typed under the cases its own pattern states and nothing else ([Patterns are compiled where their types are known](../surface/patterns-are-compiled-where-their-types-are-known.md)), and what an arm is typed under is that decision's to change, for a consumer that asks.
- **A simplex engine.** A Bland's-rule simplex over integer tableaus would replace Fourier–Motzkin behind the same interface if a row exhausted the cap; none does.
