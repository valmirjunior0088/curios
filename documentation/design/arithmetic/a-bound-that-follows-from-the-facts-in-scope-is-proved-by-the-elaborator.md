# A bound that follows from the facts in scope is proved by the elaborator

**Decision.** An omitted proof the fill leaves open is proved by the elaborator where it follows by linear arithmetic from the facts in scope: the hypotheses, their proof fields one level down, and the guards of the arms around it, each read through the local definitions and refinements in scope. The elaborator searches for a certificate — non-negative multipliers under which the facts and the negated goal combine into a false constant — and writes it as an ordinary term, a sum of the facts through `Le/add`, which elaboration checks as written code and the certifier rechecks. A decision conversion equates with `true` where reduction does not, `b || Bool/not(b)`, is proved without a search, by `Bool/holds_of_eq` over `Eq/refl()`. Where no certificate is found, the bound is refused with a report naming the facts considered, the facts left unread and, where the search produced one, an assignment of the atoms that satisfies the facts and falsifies the goal. Three `/std` entry points reach the one procedure: an omitted `@` bound, `/std/Bool/False/refuted()` in a contradiction arm, and `/std/Bool/True/proved()` for a proposition no bound demands. The procedure is `curios-elab`'s `entailment`, whose documentation is its contract, and `curios`'s `tests::bounds` is its grid.

**Rationale.**

- **Nothing is trusted that was not.** The proof is a term, so a wrong certificate is a term that does not check, and the procedure's mistakes are refusals. No conversion rule, refinement or solving choice changes, and the procedure assigns only the hole it was asked about, so it adds no board entry.
- **Hand-written arithmetic is this bridge.** A chain of order lemmas carries a fact in scope to the fact a term needs — an `@` bound, a contradiction arm, a fact handed to another lemma — and such a chain is linear arithmetic over the facts in scope, products aside, so the procedure writes the chains `/std` would otherwise write; what stays by hand is a chain reading a proof field two levels down, and an `Eq/subst` transport that rewrites an equation or under a function.
- **It stands beside the fill, not in it.** The fill is a unique answer and this is a search ([A bound is stated in a decided proposition and discharged by reduction](a-bound-is-stated-in-a-decided-proposition-and-discharged-by-reduction.md)).
- **The search is Fourier–Motzkin, capped by the rows it derives.** Mathlib's `linarith` is the model — an untrusted oracle finds the certificate and normalization checks it — with conversion's cancellation as the normalizer. `linarith` keeps Fourier–Motzkin as the engine "sometimes faster on small states" ([`Mathlib.Tactic.Linarith.Frontend`](https://florisvandoorn.com/carleson/docs/Mathlib/Tactic/Linarith/Frontend.html)), and the states here are small: over the whole standard library a search derives 6.5 rows on average and 27 at most (the `entailment::derived` sample), against a cap of 4 096. The cap counts derived rows, never host time, so a program finds the same certificate on every machine, and exhausting it refuses as the reduction budget does.
- **The fragment is the linear one, extended by definitions read as facts.** A consequence over the rationals is found within the cap, a strict integer fact strengthened to its successor; one that holds only over the integers and needs a cut is refused, as `linarith` and `omega` without its shadows refuse it. Truncated subtraction enters through the case split `omega` makes. A `Nat` quotient and remainder enter through the quotient's bounds `d * (x / d) <= x < d * (x / d) + d`, whatever the divisor, a multiple by a divisor that is no literal being a product; at `Int` conversion decides neither of a remainder's bounds, so there a quotient is no fact. A variable multiplier enters through the pairwise products Mathlib's `nlinarith` adds, asked only where linear arithmetic finds an assignment.
- **It runs where the elaborator would otherwise report the bound, and nowhere else.** At insertion the arm's guards are live and the proof is written into the arm; at a parked bound's retry the guards are those its slot was born under, which is what re-validation judges a solution by. So a program the fill alone accepts never reaches it. It does not run after the item closes, as a Lean tactic block does, because a guard here is a refinement rather than a binder.
- **A proof is written over the spellings in scope and read through their reducts.** The search reads each statement reduced, as conversion computes it, but writes its operands as stated: a guard's `qed` checks only against the key the arm recorded, which both checkers hold as written; a local definition's value names locals the key does not; and a call's reduct carries arms that elaborate only where they were written. What the facts invent over atoms is written through the spelling each atom was read from.
- **The lint credits what a proof reads**, by declaration and place, so a hypothesis only the written proof sums is used rather than reported, and keeps the name that documents its precondition ([A lint is an exact finding read off the compilation](../tools/a-lint-is-an-exact-finding-read-off-the-compilation.md)).
- **The proofs are small and cheap.** The standard library's 73 proofs average 104 nodes, the largest 518; checking them takes 2.2 s of the standard library's 85.9 s instrumented elaboration. The figures are `cargo xtask clippy`'s profile streams folded by `curios profile`: the `entailment::written` and `entailment::derived` samples and the `entailment::check` span.

**Rejected.**

- **Hypotheses in conversion**, for the reason [A bound is stated in a decided proposition and discharged by reduction](a-bound-is-stated-in-a-decided-proposition-and-discharged-by-reduction.md) gives. The Calculus of Congruent Inductive Constructions' metatheory holds for the weak recursor only, and `Holds` is a large elimination. A mistake there is a false definitional equation; a mistake here is a term that does not check.
- **Reflection first.** A checker written in Curios and proved sound once would make each proof an application to a closed certificate, trading a one-time proof for smaller terms; Chaieb and Nipkow measured it one to two orders of magnitude faster in Isabelle (2008). The proofs are small enough that nothing asks for it yet.
- **An external solver.** Linear arithmetic needs no SMT solver, and a dependency outside the workspace would buy nothing the search does not do.
- **A tactic syntax.** [Syntax forms are closed](../surface/syntax-forms-are-closed-semantics-extend-by-witness.md); the entry points are `/std` declarations with `@` bounds.
- **Running after the item closes.** It would lose the guards.
- **A heuristic search.** Acceptance would depend on the heuristic; the procedure is complete for its fragment within a deterministic cap.
- **A simplex engine.** A Bland's-rule simplex over integer tableaus would replace Fourier–Motzkin behind the same interface if a row exhausted the cap; none does.
