# A bound is stated in a decided proposition and discharged by reduction

**Decision.** A precondition is a *decided* proposition: `Holds` of a decision, a `match` that is `True` in range and `False` outside it. Every relation a decider exists for is stated so, including the ones proofs recurse over, and an inductive family is kept only where no decider exists or where its evidence carries more than its indices, as `/std/Cli`'s `Values` carries the parsed values. The elaborator fills an omitted bound whose proposition reduces to `True` with its constructor. One it does not fill is proved from the facts in scope where linear arithmetic reaches it ([A bound that follows from the facts in scope is proved by the elaborator](a-bound-that-follows-from-the-facts-in-scope-is-proved-by-the-elaborator.md)), and is otherwise proved by the author and passed with `@`.

**Rationale.**

- **Induction never needed the constructors.** A proof recurses on the values the decider reads, and the hypothesis follows by reduction, since the decider unfolds one step on a constructor-headed argument: cancellation makes `Nat/Le(a + 1, b + 1)` *be* `Nat/Le(a, b)`, and `Str/Valid/from(s, x[c, ..t])` is `from(step(c, s), t)`. `/std/Nat/Le`'s `trans` and `mul_mono_r` are written that way.
- **The fill is not proof search.** Its answer is unique, so there is no choice, no backtracking and no incompleteness: it is the introduction-side dual of the singleton condition on elimination, and proof irrelevance already holds every inhabitant interchangeable. It fills exactly the bounds that are definitionally discharged, as `n / 256` is. A bound inserted before its subject is known, or whose proposition is itself still a metavariable, as `p` is in `@P: Prop, @p: P`, is parked and tried again once it is, judged under the refinements its slot was born under, as re-validation judges any solution.
- **The refinement store is a lookup, not a theory.** An arm's equation is licensed by the case it is in and retracted with the arm, so the store holds one term standing for one value, keyed on the scrutinee as written, and two contradictory entries compose into nothing. A fact that *follows from* two entries is a proof rather than a lookup: under `match i < n` and `match List/len(l) == n * k`, `List/slice(l, i * k, k)` needs a monotonicity step over both, which the elaborator writes (`curios`'s `tests::bounds`), while a bound the guards do not imply stays stuck (`tests::matching`'s `a_bound_the_guards_do_not_imply_is_still_stuck`). What a probe crosses is a spelling: the `<`/`<=` seam and a comparison's dual ([A law is decided where it neither respells nor invents](a-law-is-decided-where-it-neither-respells-nor-invents.md)).
- **A hypothesis reaches a bound through a proof, never through the store.** It carries no case's licence: `Eq()(List/len(l), n * k)` does not say which side stands for which, an ordering says less, and a context holding one may be inconsistent. Entering hypotheses into conversion is the design of the Calculus of Congruent Inductive Constructions (Blanqui, Jouannaud and Strub, 2007), and CoqMT keeps a decidable theory in conversion only by keeping the context's hypotheses out (Strub, 2010; Jouannaud and Strub, 2017). A rule both checkers held would let an equation escape the context that licensed it.
- **What constrains a bound is evaluation, not provability.** A bound over a computed subject runs that computation at elaboration time, and the step budget decides whether it finishes ([A reduction step costs what it builds](../soundness/a-reduction-step-costs-what-it-builds.md)). A subject kept opaque behind a parameter computes nothing: `tests::numeric`'s `a_bound_on_a_small_computed_subject_discharges`, `a_bound_on_a_computed_subject_evaluates_it` and `a_bound_behind_a_parameter_evaluates_nothing` pin the three at one budget.
- **An unfolding that finishes at a variable is an answer.** Both reducers keep an unfolding that exposed a head or lost the group it recursed on, so an accumulator's `f(0, acc)` reducing to `acc` discharges its base case; a reducer that discarded it as "no progress" would leave every such lemma unprovable in decided form (`tests::numeric`'s `a_bound_over_a_recursion_returning_a_parameter_discharges`).

**Rejected.**

- **The decision as an equation, `Eq()(b, true)`.** It subsumes the reflection logically, but a bound is used at a guard: `match a < b` is what the refinement store records and serves in both checkers, while an equation hypothesis is consumed by transport with a written motive, or by matching on it, which asks the inverter to refine an expression index. `Bool`'s `eq_of_holds` and `holds_of_eq` convert between the two.
- **An inductive order such as `Nat/Le/Ind`**, alone or beside the decided one: call sites build evidence constructor by constructor, and two statements of one relation need bridges both ways.
- **A decision procedure as the discharge rule.** The fill would become a search; the procedure stands beside it instead.
- **A total `/std` face beside a proof-carrying one.** Both return the same type, so nothing pushes a caller to the checked one, and the bound reverts to discipline.
- **Witness resolution for obligations.** Metavariables are solved by unification alone, proof irrelevance manufactures no inhabitant, and `HeadKey` keys only on type heads, so a value-indexed `NonZero(b)` cannot be a concept.
