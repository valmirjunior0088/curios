# The soundness perimeter

Every rule that can admit a term, what it assumes, and how far it has been checked. The *decision* to state the trusted base this way — and what it replaced — belongs to [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md), "The soundness perimeter"; this file is the evidence, and it is where a hunt records what it found.

Each entry names what it assumes and how far it has been checked, and the vocabulary is deliberate: **probed** means an adversarial program was written and the compiler rejected it; **argued** means a written justification exists; **auditable only** means no surface program can exercise the rule, so reading the implementation is the only available evidence. An entry may be probed and unargued, or argued and unprobed; they are independent.

The perimeter has two halves, and they differ in how coverage is obtained. The **whole-module passes** run once over a module: the elaborator's from `finalize_and_check` in `curios-elab/src/elaborate/module.rs` — the single site every entry point that produces an elaborated module comes through, which is what keeps a check from reaching one configuration and not the other — and, since the certifier's walk decides *which* items are judged at all, the boundary that walk draws. The **per-term rules** run during elaboration, so their coverage follows from the elaborator visiting every term rather than from a pass enumerating positions.

An entry earns its place by *assuming* something, not merely by admitting. The discriminator is whether the rule rests on anything beyond the shape of the term in front of it — a declaration, a carried number, a cached verdict, an equation stated elsewhere. A rule that only destructures inherits its correctness from the representation and is not listed; `Var`, `Let`, the bare `Func` rule and the `Type`/`Prop` head rules are the ones that fall the other side of that line, and they inherit [Telescope instantiation](#telescope-instantiation) and [Binder identity](#binder-identity) rather than earning rows.

### `record_totality` + (T)

**Assumes.** Nothing reachable from a type position is partial

**Status.** **probed** (four routes), and separately at the size-change engine's accepting side condition: a `Nat` dispatch licenses the arithmetic decrease `n - 1` only where its arms rule zero out. Those arm facts carry every decrease the corpus proves — 4117 grade upgrades over 1365 gradings, none downward — so they are the accepting direction throughout. A dispatch is not the only source of that fact, and the second had no fixture: a *boolean* arm establishes it too, from a comparison against a literal, which is what `/std/Nat/to_str` descends on. Attacked in the three spellings that reach different halves of the reader — the bare intrinsic, the operands flipped, and the comparison behind a definition that only unfolding exposes — plus an accepting control in two spellings. All held; the null is recorded so the route is not re-attacked. The engine's *structural* accepting rule was attacked next and did admit a term, and the defect was in the walk's coverage rather than in the size order: `Walk::walk` gives a member reference — a `rec` node whose tail selects one member — an arm above the general `rec` one, because `RecGroup::member_body` materializes every member reference as a projection carrying the whole group, so descending into the group being analyzed would regenerate its own bodies without end. That arm answered for every projection and not only this group's, so a projection of a *different* group matched no branch and left that group's bodies unwalked — while the general arm below exists for exactly what it thereby skipped, an inner group classified on its own whose bodies may still call *this* group. ### Prefix identification

**Assumes.** The module's first `checked_from` items are the archived prelude's

**Status.** **auditable only**, and benign by construction today rather than by any rule. `verdicts_from` takes `checked_from` as `prefix.module.items.len()` — a length off the *archive* — applies it as an index into the module it was handed, and defines everything below it into the kernel's environment at its declared type without typing it. ### Subsumption and level entailment

**Assumes.** A term typing at `A` may be admitted at `B` whenever `A ≤ B`, decided under the item's assumed constraints

**Status.** **probed** at the oracle and **auditable only** from source, which is why it belongs with the entries that can never be attacked: surface Curios has no level syntax, so no program forces a subsumption the elaborator did not itself choose. `Kernel::level_leq` decides the universe rung as `Level::structurally_leq(..) || entails(..)`, and `entails` carries seven fixtures of which two are the rungs that matter — `a_hypothesis_does_not_gain_an_offset`, because reading `u ≤ v` as `u + 1 ≤ v` is a level gained for free, and `a_constant_is_not_bounded_by_a_bare_parameter`, because a parameter ranges over every natural and nothing bounds it from below. ### Intrinsic signatures

**Assumes.** Every intrinsic's declared operand and result types are the ones its carrier actually has

**Status.** **auditable only**, and unprobed. `infer_intrinsic` hands each intrinsic a type off a roster and has nothing to check it against: the roster *is* the statement of what `Bin/slice : (Bytes, Nat, Nat) -> Bytes` means, so a wrong row is not a disagreement with anything, it is the definition. No surface program can force one, because the spelling and the signature are read from the same table — which is why this is graded with the three entries that can only be read. The class is not hypothetical. `curios-elab`'s `Sort::of` is non-exhaustive over parameterized intrinsic formers, so a newly added former is classified at level 0 by falling through rather than by anyone deciding it should, and the only thing that notices is the prelude's kernel recheck; the same shape one position over produced a demonstrated route, recorded in [`Prop` non-informativeness](#prop-non-informativeness), when the typing rule for a parameterized former reported its element's sort as the former's and `List(True)` inferred at `Prop` on both sides. What an audit consists of is one comparison per row against the operand model the erased IR gives the same operation, which is the only other place those arities and carriers are written down

### Intrinsic fold laws and the free-monoid peel

**Assumes.** Every fold is a valid equation on the carrier's values, and a `Peel::Clash` is a genuine disequality

**Status.** **argued in code comments only**, which is the weakest position anywhere on this perimeter, and it carries the worst asymmetry available: `reduce_intrinsic` and `peel_intrinsic` live in `curios-core`, so both checkers run the same function on the same input and no disagreement is structurally possible — the class that produced `satisfiable`'s decision over the integers, recorded in [`validate_universes`](#validate_universes-inside-zonk_module). It admits by two routes this file already records as having produced closed inhabitants of `False` from elsewhere. `convert` reduces both sides before comparing, so a wrong fold is a false definitional equation, and `Eq/cong` with `Eq/subst` carries one to `False` — the derivation `tests::perimeter::a_list_of_proofs_is_not_a_proposition` records. And `peel_intrinsic`'s `Peel::Clash` reaches `invert_indices` as `Invert::Impossible`, which excuses an omitted arm: the vacuous-elimination route both [Coverage](#coverage) and the [Large-elimination guard](#large-elimination-guard) record. `BinEql` folds straight to a literal off `peel_bin`, so a wrong peel is also a wrong *value*.

The equations are asserted in comments across `curios-core/src/reduce/intrinsic.rs` and stated nowhere else — the window-fusion law `slice(b, s, m) ++ slice(b, m, e) = slice(b, s, e)`, the empty-window drop, the full-window collapse, `append(base, e) = base ++ [e]`, `peel_nat`'s successor cancellation, the `(il + sl) - k` borrow, and the `(it + st) · c` distribution among them. Exactly two are checked rather than asserted, and they are the model the rest lack: `Nat/to_int`/`Int/to_nat` round-tripping, and `Nat` comparison agreeing with the host ordering on every pair of literals, each carry a unit test exhaustive over samples that the file labels *Soundness gate*. Neither is a fold that reaches conversion, so the two laws with executable backing are not the two that decide equations. What stands in for this row today is one sentence in `curios-cert/src/kernel/whnf.rs`, which classifies both shared functions as "representation rather than judgment" and concludes that neither "can admit an ill-typed program on its own". That is true only in the narrow sense that neither *types* anything; both decide equations that typing then believes.

One law has been looked at. `tests::aggregates::bin_slice_is_a_monoid_citizen` states window fusion at *arbitrary* `s`, `m` and `e`, with no `s ≤ m ≤ e` side condition, and it holds — but for a reason outside the law: `PackedBin::slice` requires `start <= end && end <= len`, so every spelling that would break it is a reduction error over a literal base and a trap at runtime, leaving the law correct on its whole defined domain. That is an argument made while reading, not a fixture. The technique this row wants first is counting — how often each law fires, and which fire nowhere — because a fold that never fires is a rule whose mistakes no program can reach

### Telescope instantiation

**Assumes.** A dependent telescope is opened at the actuals already checked, in order, and plicity is part of a function type's identity

**Status.** **probed incidentally, never directly.** One assumption serving five typing rules — an application's arguments, a projection's field, and the three literal forms — and four conversion sites: `compare_binders`, `eta_tuple`, `induct_type_args`, and `struct_eta`. Each opens the rest of a telescope at what it has already accepted, and each is sound only because the earlier positions were established first; `eta_tuple` opens at the *left* side's projection and says so in its own doc, `induct_type_args` at the left instance's actuals. Nothing states the shared rule, so nothing holds the four spellings to it, and a fifth added later would be judged by whichever neighbour its author read. The plicity clause is the half most obviously able to admit: conflating `(A) -> A` with `(@A) -> A` would let a value be applied through the wrong convention, and erasure masks by plicity, so the two differ in what survives. It is guarded by hand in three places — `convert`'s `Func` and `FuncType` arms and `subsumes` — each with a comment, and no row saying why the guard has to be there

### Binder identity

**Assumes.** No minted identity aliases an existing one, so opening a binder instantiates exactly the intended occurrences

**Status.** **argued**, with one positive control. Every rule on this perimeter is stated over terms whose binders are distinct, and the kernel mints binders of its own — comparing under a telescope, eta-expanding, opening a motive or an arm — into an identity space the lowerer, the elaborator and the archived prelude have already minted into. The discipline is right and is written down three times: `recheck.rs`'s `set_local_floor` combines `Module::binder_floor` with a *derived* mark by maximum and says the carried one is unchecked, `obligation.rs`'s `derived_binder_floor` says the same, and `curios-core`'s name module documents why a floor may be derived at all — `Entropy::seed` only raises. ### A type is a pure term

**Assumes.** Nothing at the type level performs an effect

**Status.** **argued**, and the observation that it had no entry has been sitting in a test file rather than here — `tests::perimeter` calls it "the premise every rule above is stated over and no entry of `SOUNDNESS.md` names". Three demonstrated routes through it are recorded in the [scrutinee refinement](#scrutinee-refinement) row, the third of which compiled, passed the compile-path recheck, and trapped at runtime on the `unreachable` `False/absurd` erases to. What discharges it now is a typing fact rather than an analysis, which is why that row's walk could be deleted with no successor: every host operation returns `Io(T)`, and there is no operation taking an `Io(T)` to a `T`, so a term standing at a type position cannot have performed anything. It is a property of *which names* `/sys/Io` exports — `/std/Io` re-exports `pure` and `bind` and nothing else — so an eliminator added later would reopen all three routes with no test failing, and the premise is one library file wide

## Across the perimeter

**Three entries can never be attacked, only read.** [Subsumption and level entailment](#subsumption-and-level-entailment) is decided over a hierarchy with no surface syntax for levels — they are implicit and inferred — so no program can be written that forces a violation. Index inversion and the conversion recurrence rule need constructed internal state that no source text reaches. These will never accumulate the kind of evidence positivity and the elimination guard have, and that asymmetry is a permanent property of the claim rather than a gap awaiting work.

**One of those two is load-bearing in the elaborator and inert in the kernel, which the count above separates for the first time.** Definitional proof irrelevance never fires in `curios-cert` — not once in 86,547 goals — because no conversion goal there is ever at a proposition. That does not make the kernel unsound: an accepting rule that never runs cannot admit anything, and incompleteness is the safe direction. What it does mean is that the second opinion buys *nothing* about this entry, so "load-bearing" describes the elaborator's copy alone — where it is now measured at 37 firings in 84,826 goals, none of them decidable by syntactic equality, and 35 of them at one site shape. The two checkers see comparable work (84,826 goals against 86,547), so the difference is not that the kernel sees less: it is that the kernel compares proofs in untyped child positions, which removes exactly the goals irrelevance would answer. The paragraph below argues about the rule as the language has it; the argument is unaffected, but which checker it is an argument about is now recorded rather than assumed.

**Two entries are load-bearing and unargued, and their dependency on (V) is a fixpoint rather than a circle.** Definitional proof irrelevance accepts without inspecting either term, which is correct precisely because every `Prop` inhabitant is total — that is (V)'s job. The conversion recurrence rule accepts on the absence of finite disagreement, and aggressive (T) is what removes its exposure to bottom-typed terms. Both therefore depend on the obligations, and elaboration uses both *while* the obligations are still unchecked.

**The dependency does not close, so it is a fixpoint rather than a circle.** `convert` consults `Sort::of`, which reaches only `reduce` and `synth_neutral`, and neither reaches `convert`. (V)'s own pipeline — record at settle, zonk, decide sort-hood, classify by size-change, close over reached definitions — never invokes conversion at any point, and neither does `zonk`. So elaboration may use both rules optimistically, and *acceptance validates them after the fact*: a program is accepted only if (V) passes, and if (V) passes then no `Prop`-typed term in it is partial, so every irrelevance step taken while checking it was applied to total terms. Rejected programs are rejected; accepted ones satisfy the precondition their acceptance relied on. The recurrence rule stands the same way on aggressive (T), which is what removes its exposure to bottom-typed terms in the positions it compares.

**The premise that argument needs is that (V) inspects every `Prop`-typed term in the accepted module.** Seeding at settle gives that for everything `elaborate` builds, since every node of its output settled with its type. The one route around it is a metavariable *solution*, which reaches the module through unification or witness resolution rather than through settling. That route is exercised and closed: a partial proof supplied as the witness of a `Prop`-sorted concept — arriving entirely by resolution, written nowhere as an argument — is rejected by (V), and `tests::soundness::a_partial_proof_cannot_arrive_through_witness_resolution` is the regression. Measurement agrees: across the corpus no solved metavariable sits at a `Prop`-sorted type at all, and in a program constructed to force one, its solution was among the recorded terms.

**What is still missing is the model, not the reasoning.** The fixpoint above is an argument about the implementation's dependency structure and about coverage; it is not a proof that definitional proof irrelevance is sound, which needs a model in which `Prop` is a genuine subsingleton and only total terms are interpreted. That is the first properly metatheoretic work this project would undertake, and it is what an observational-equality layer would rest on directly — but it is not required to state, or to check, the fixpoint.

**The erasure premise is a containment, not an equality, and stating it as an equality was wrong.** Erasure deletes strictly more than types and `Prop`-sorted proofs. It has five deletion sites: the signature-mask skip in `masked_fields`, the erasable operand in `kept_operand_at`, a proof-valued callee that is not a direct function reference, a `Prop` family's constructor, and an erasable scrutinee. The last three drop a whole construct *including its arguments*, and those arguments are ordinary values — neither types nor proofs. Erasure also skips dead code after a terminator, which is a reachability decision rather than a sort-driven one.

None of that is a hole, because the obligations do not need to seed each deleted term. What they need is that everything deleted lies **within** a term one of them covers, and the reachability closure and the local-partiality walk both descend through whole terms. A partial computation hidden inside any of those three constructs is reached through the enclosing proof position, not through a seed of its own; `tests::soundness` has one fixture per site. Dead code is covered for a different reason — the obligations are reachability-blind, seeding every `Prop`-typed term whether or not it can run.

So the premise to check, and the one now checked, is: *everything erasure deletes lies within a term (T) or (V) covers.* The equality form is false and would have sent an audit looking for the wrong thing.

**A second opinion now runs against this perimeter, on the compile path in production.** The kernel in `curios-cert` re-decides reduction, conversion, typing, and elimination from the terms alone; see "An independent kernel re-checks what the elaborator accepts" for the decisions and their boundaries. No entry in the table above is re-graded on account of it — a second checker changes the cost of being wrong about a rule, not the perimeter's own coverage — and the whole-prelude disagreement count, closed at 0 of 1052, is tracked by ROADMAP.md.

**One analysis on this perimeter fails open, and every other one fails closed.** Positivity answers `Mixed` for a name outside its set, `whnf` goes stuck on any shape it cannot fire, inversion derives nothing at a `Prop`-valued position, the size reader answers *unread* on a term it cannot decode, and an under-applied call is graded `Matrix::unknown`. Each turns blindness into a refusal. The size-change engine's *call-site discovery* does not: a position `Walk::walk` never visits contributes no edge, and a group with no edges is accepted. That asymmetry is why the `record_totality` row's coverage audit was the one piece of prose here worth converting into a test, which it now is — and the conversion found the prose undercounting its own whitelist by one. It used to have a second consequence: `Subterm::any_child_term` and `Intrinsic::for_each_operand` being exhaustive matches was what made the *other* fail-open walk, `fixes_no_value`, complete by compiler enforcement instead of by inspection. That walk is deleted, so the size-change engine's call-site discovery is now the only rule here that fails open.

**What the two checkers are held to each other on, and what they are not.** Five tests compare them: `kernel_disagreements` over the whole prelude at 0 of 1052, `kernel_memo_parity` over the evaluation memos, `both_checkers_decide_non_informativeness_alike`, `both_checkers_decide_universe_context_validity_alike`, and `tests::perimeter::the_two_checkers_agree_as_recorded` per fixture. `convert` is not among them. It is the rule `curios-cert`'s own module documentation calls the most consequential thing in that crate, the two implementations are known by the entry above to differ in their recurrence key, and the only place their verdicts meet today is wherever the corpus happens to send them. **Missing test:** a goal-level differential on conversion, over goals chosen to reach the rules the two spell differently rather than over whatever the prelude contains.

**Two things are named on this perimeter and not yet attacked.** The recurrence-key collision in `curios-elab`, and the goal-level conversion differential. The position-coverage differential for `Walk::walk` was the third and is written, in the `record_totality` row above; the fourth was `compile_entrypoint`'s `checked_from`, listed here for a while as belonging to no row on the grounds that it is not a rule — it is one, since it decides which items are judged at all, and it now has [its own entry](#prefix-identification). Each is written into the entry it belongs to; this paragraph exists so the count is visible from the top rather than assembled by reading. A sixth is a discipline rather than a test: several accepting rungs above are reached by exactly one fixture — `carries_information`'s excuse, inversion's `consolidate` acceptance, the kernel's irrelevance — and only some of those fixtures record a mutation-check, which is the only thing separating a sole-reach fixture from dead code.
