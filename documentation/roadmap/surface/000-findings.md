# Surface: findings

- **A successor pattern beside literal arms is refused, so a proof by cases past `1` nests a split a level.** A successor arm takes any step of one or more, `p + 2`, but one match does not mix a successor arm with literal arms past `0` (`MatrixMixedNatDispatch`, `naturals`, `curios-elab/src/elaborate/matrix.rs`), a rule no other pattern has. A successor arm refines its scrutinee and names its predecessor, which a proof by cases needs and a dispatch's catch-all does not give, so `/std` writes the nesting by hand fifteen times: `/std/Map/digits`' `at_place` three levels deep for `| 0 | 1 | 2 | _`, and `/std/Str/utf8`'s `advanced` lemma four, one proof case a level, beside `Str`, `Str/Valid` and `time/iso8601`. Lean 4 writes `n+2` as a pattern beside literal patterns, its match compiler expanding a literal into successors where a constructor pattern stands beside it and splitting by value past them, an overlapped arm's equation side-conditioned ([Theorem Proving in Lean 4](https://lean-lang.org/theorem_proving_in_lean4/induction_and_recursion.html), [`Match.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/master/src/Lean/Meta/Match/Match.lean)); Agda reads a number literal as successors over zero, its `--exact-split` warning where an arm holds only off an earlier clause; Haskell 2010 dropped its n+k patterns as a special case overloaded over every `Integral` type that matched no negative, an objection a `Nat` alone does not meet ([haskell-prime](https://mail.haskell.org/pipermail/haskell-prime/2009-July/002822.html)). Fix, settled with the user on 2026-11-18: numbers are matched by the rules every pattern answers to and none of their own — a numeral read as the successors it builds over zero, and `p + k` as `k` successors over the predecessor `p` — so the three refusals of [Binders beside patterns](../../language.md#binders-beside-patterns) decide every match: `| 7 => … | p + 1 => …` takes `7` as an exception written before its general arm, `| p + 1 => … | 7 => …` is refused as misplaced, and the rule against a successor arm beside literal arms goes. The match compiler peels to the step and tests a literal past it on the predecessor, so a large literal stays one test, and Core, both checkers, erasure and the back end meet the forms they meet today; a literal written before a successor arm makes that arm hold only off it, as `some(_)` beside `some(some(x))` does, which no lint reports. Rejected: literals beside a successor arm held to a run below its step, a rule of naturals' own beside the ordering every pattern has (the user, 2026-11-18). [An `Int` is taken apart by its literals](100-an-int-is-taken-apart-by-its-literals.md) reads an `Int` column's literals in the same `naturals` at its third stage, so whichever lands second reads the column as the first left it. Check: `| 0 => 0 | 1 => 1 | p + 2 => fib(p) + fib(p + 1)` and its law by `Eq/refl()` through both checkers; `| 0 | 7 | p + 1`, `| 0 | 1 | 7 | p + 2`, `| 5 | p + 3 | _`, `some(p + 2)` beside `some(0)` and `some(1)`, and `| 1000 | p + 2 | _` answering as stated; `| p + 1 | 3` refused, naming what is wrong; `documentation/language.md`'s two `Nat` sections made one; `/std`'s hand-nested splits rewritten where they read as one match. Size: one commit, then `/std`.
- **A missing `;` is refused at whatever stands next, lines below where it is written.** `pub let a: Nat = 1` followed by a blank line and `pub let b: Nat = 2;` is refused at `5:1`, "expected: `;`" and "obtained: `pub`", with the caret under the next declaration's `pub`; a `use`, a `test` and a `foreign` missing theirs are refused alike at the next item or at the program's final term (probed on standard input at `61bee406e`). Each item reads its `;` past the whitespace and comments after its last token (`parse_literal(";")` in `curios-surface/src/parse/top_level.rs`, `take_exact(";")` in `curios-surface/src/parse.rs`), so the furthest failure stands at the next word. That departs from [`curios-surface`'s README](../../../curios-surface/README.md#a-refusal-is-worded-at-the-commitment-the-alternative-owes), "a refusal stands where its mistake is", and from [A diagnostic spells what its reader can write](../../design/tools/a-diagnostic-spells-what-its-reader-can-write.md), whose report is read to be acted on by writing something at its position: the reader is sent to a line that is right. Fix: an item's terminator is read by one parser that refuses its absence just past the item's last character, where the `;` is written, naming the `;` it ends with and what the item is, and no longer the word that stands next. Check: a parser test for each item a `;` closes — a top-level `let`, a `use`, a `test`, a `foreign` — refused at the column after its last character with a blank line and a comment before what follows; a `;` that stands on a later line after a comment still read. Size: one commit.
