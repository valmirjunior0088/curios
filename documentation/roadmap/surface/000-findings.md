# Surface: findings

- **A dispatch on a natural's literals neither reduces on an open successor nor tells its catch-all what it was reached past.** A match of literal arms and a catch-all is Core's `Cases::Switch`, which steps only where its scrutinee's value is known whole (`Nat::decompose`, `curios-core/src/machine.rs`; `curios-cert/src/certifier/whnf.rs`), so on `k + 2` it stops though no key below `2` can match: probed on 2026-11-18 at `e6b54a764`, a Fibonacci written `| 0 => 0 | 1 => 1 | _ => fib(n - 1) + fib(n - 2)` has `fib(k + 2) == fib(k) + fib(k + 1)` refused, `fib(k + 2)` stuck, where the same body under two successor splits proves it by `Eq/refl()`, subtraction reducing on an open successor. And the catch-all is typed under nothing: with `need(n: Nat, @ok: Bool/Holds(1 < n))`, `| 0 | 1 => 0 | _ => need(n)` and `| 0 => 0 | 1 => 0 | _ => need(n)` are refused, "it fails at `n = 0`", where `| m, m < 2 => 0 | _ => need(n)` checks, a failed lone guard being a fact in the arms after it ([An arm is taken by its patterns, then its guards](../../design/surface/an-arm-is-taken-by-its-patterns-then-its-guards.md)) and a failed pattern none. Fix, settled with the user on 2026-11-18, with no length anywhere: a switch on `k + s` whose every key is below `s` takes its catch-all, in both checkers' statements of the reduction with a differential case; and a dispatch's catch-all is typed under `n != l` for each literal it was reached past, the decision's sentence restated so a literal arm of a natural's dispatch is a lone test failed on the one way there, where a constructor pattern's remaining case is still the tree's. Rejected: lowering a run of literals from zero as that many successor splits, which gives both at once but only up to the length where a long run, a byte table's, keeps the value switch, a cliff at whatever length is chosen. Check: the Fibonacci's law by `Eq/refl()` through both checkers; `need(n)` discharged in the catch-all after `| 0 | 1` and after alternatives; a key at or past the step left stuck on `k + s`. Size: two commits, the reduction and the facts.
- **A successor pattern takes a step of one, so a proof by cases past `1` nests a split a level.** `| p + 2 =>` is refused where it is written, "a successor pattern takes a step of one, `p + 1`" (`curios-surface/src/parse/patterns.rs`), and one match does not mix a successor arm with literal arms (`naturals`, `curios-elab/src/elaborate/matrix.rs`), a rule no other pattern has. A successor arm refines its scrutinee and names its predecessor, which a proof by cases needs and a dispatch's catch-all does not give, so `/std` writes the nesting by hand fifteen times: `/std/Map/digits`' `at_place` three levels deep for `| 0 | 1 | 2 | _`, and `/std/Str/utf8`'s `advanced` lemma four, one proof case a level, beside `Str`, `Str/Valid` and `time/iso8601`. Lean 4 writes `n+2` as a pattern, addition carrying `[match_pattern]` and normalized to `succ (succ n)`, beside literal patterns, an overlapped arm's equation side-conditioned ([Theorem Proving in Lean 4](https://lean-lang.org/theorem_proving_in_lean4/induction_and_recursion.html)); Agda reads a number literal as successors over zero, its `--exact-split` warning where an arm holds only off an earlier clause; Haskell 2010 dropped its n+k patterns as a special case overloaded over every `Integral` type that matched no negative, an objection a `Nat` alone does not meet ([haskell-prime](https://mail.haskell.org/pipermail/haskell-prime/2009-July/002822.html)). Fix, settled with the user on 2026-11-18: numbers are matched by the rules every pattern answers to and none of their own — a numeral read as the successors it builds over zero, and `p + k`, for a numeral `k` of one or more, as `k` successors over the predecessor `p` — so the three refusals of [Binders beside patterns](../../language.md#binders-beside-patterns) decide every match: `| 7 => … | p + 1 => …` takes `7` as an exception written before its general arm, `| p + 1 => … | 7 => …` is refused as misplaced, `| 0 => … | p + 2 => …` names `1` as a value no arm matches, and the rule against a successor arm beside literal arms goes. The arm refines its scrutinee to `p + k`, and the step is a pattern wherever a pattern stands, `some(p + 2)` among them. The match compiler peels to the step and tests a literal past it on the predecessor, so a large literal stays one test, and Core, both checkers, erasure and the back end meet the forms they meet today; a literal written before a successor arm makes that arm hold only off it, as `some(_)` beside `some(some(x))` does, which no lint reports. Rejected: literals beside a successor arm held to a run below its step, a rule of naturals' own beside the ordering every pattern has (the user, 2026-11-18); and the Fibonacci written by literals as this entry's case, which the dispatch's own entry above settles without a pattern. [An `Int` is taken apart by its literals](100-an-int-is-taken-apart-by-its-literals.md) reads an `Int` column's literals in the same `naturals` at its third stage, so whichever lands second reads the column as the first left it. Check: `| 0 => 0 | 1 => 1 | p + 2 => fib(p) + fib(p + 1)` and its law by `Eq/refl()` through both checkers; `| 0 | 7 | p + 1`, `| 5 | p + 3 | _`, `some(p + 2)` beside `some(0)` and `some(1)`, and `| 1000 | p + 2 | _` answering as stated; `p + 0`, `| 0 | p + 2` and `| p + 1 | 3` refused, naming what is wrong; the tree-sitter grammar with the Zed pin moved; `documentation/language.md`'s two `Nat` sections made one; `/std`'s hand-nested splits rewritten where they read as one match. Size: two commits, the grammar and the match compiler, then `/std`.
- **A missing `;` is refused at whatever stands next, lines below where it is written.** `pub let a: Nat = 1` followed by a blank line and `pub let b: Nat = 2;` is refused at `5:1`, "expected: `;`" and "obtained: `pub`", with the caret under the next declaration's `pub`; a `use`, a `test` and a `foreign` missing theirs are refused alike at the next item or at the program's final term (probed on standard input at `61bee406e`). Each item reads its `;` past the whitespace and comments after its last token (`parse_literal(";")` in `curios-surface/src/parse/top_level.rs`, `take_exact(";")` in `curios-surface/src/parse.rs`), so the furthest failure stands at the next word. That departs from [`curios-surface`'s README](../../../curios-surface/README.md#a-refusal-is-worded-at-the-commitment-the-alternative-owes), "a refusal stands where its mistake is", and from [A diagnostic spells what its reader can write](../../design/tools/a-diagnostic-spells-what-its-reader-can-write.md), whose report is read to be acted on by writing something at its position: the reader is sent to a line that is right. Fix: an item's terminator is read by one parser that refuses its absence just past the item's last character, where the `;` is written, naming the `;` it ends with and what the item is, and no longer the word that stands next. Check: a parser test for each item a `;` closes — a top-level `let`, a `use`, a `test`, a `foreign` — refused at the column after its last character with a blank line and a comment before what follows; a `;` that stands on a later line after a comment still read. Size: one commit.
