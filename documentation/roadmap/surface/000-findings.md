- **Uncertain: a derived key over a mutual family is refused, its law's two sides reaching one stuck `match` in different numbers of definitional steps.** Over `induct Tree: pub Type | leaf(Nat) | node(Forest) and Forest: pub Type | nil() | cons(Tree, Forest) end`, with `Eql`, `Ord` and `Hash` derived in `and` groups, `satisfy Hash/Key(Tree) { .. } and Hash/Key(Forest) { .. }` is refused at `Hash/Key(Tree)` (probed at `c9bdf46a3`, `a5be0cbe6` and `9a3f8b34c`), where a key over a family that holds itself alone derives (`an_inductive_that_holds_itself_derives_its_key`); `Ord/ord(Tree/node(x0), Tree/node(x1))` against `Ord/ord(x0, x1)` at `x0, x1: Forest` is refused, though the same equation at `Forest/nil()` is proved by `Eq/refl()`. Published, `Ord(Tree)`'s `node` case reads `Forest`'s order by its witness's name, so the two sides are one function on one pair: what parts them is that the left reaches `Forest`'s stuck `match x0` in two definitional steps, `Tree`'s member and then `Forest`'s, and the right in one. Reduction keeps the left call folded, `force_rec`'s test reading any member of the group in the reduct as the call coming back (`curios-elab/src/reduce.rs`, `restuck` and `finished`), and conversion opens two folded calls one definitional step each ([A pair of calls that re-sticks is unfolded against itself once](../../design/soundness/conversion-recurrence.md)), after which one side is a call and the other a `match`. A conversion that spells a stepped member's siblings by their names changes nothing (probed at `9a3f8b34c`). Two routes, each against a recorded decision: the guard read at the member being unfolded, so a call that moves to its sibling is progress, which is the decision's rejected "whether forcing moves a call" and its `first(T/node(k))` example, in both reducers with a differential case and a termination argument for two siblings that alternate on stuck arguments; or a folded call opened past the one step against a side that is no call, which is the one-step rule itself. Settled by the decision weighed again for a call that moves to a sibling, and the chosen route proved on this probe and over `/std`.
- **Uncertain: a guard holds no action, though an acting guard could be useful.** A `!` in a match's guard is refused, `action in a guard` (`Error::GuardHoldsAction`, raised in `curios-module/src/into_core/lowerer.rs` at the `!`, which `lowerer/match_compile.rs` lowers each guard under `Lowerer::guard` to tell), as [A function's body follows its arrow](../../design/surface/a-functions-body-follows-its-arrow.md) decides, so an action a guard would test is bound inside the arm that tests it. Rust, OCaml, F# and Scala try a guard in arm order once its arm's pattern has matched, its effects happening exactly when it is tried, Rust's `.await` among them, and in Curios a `!` would mark the one guard that acts, where Rust's reader cannot tell an acting guard from a pure one; Lean's nested actions, hoisted ahead of the match, run the actions of arms the match never reaches. Not yet known: whether programs want an acting guard, and how the match compiler would keep one — a barrier in the Curios/Core decision matrix, the arms before it decided first, its arm's pattern then its action, and the arms after it decided again in its failing branch, where coverage stays precise only if that branch knows the scrutinee's case. Settled by a survey of `/std`, `programs/` and the fixtures for arms that bind an action only to test it, then the barrier prototyped in a scratch copy and held to coverage's precision, or this entry dropped.
