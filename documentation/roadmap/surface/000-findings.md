- **A literal's field written over parameters in the old form is refused for another token than its arrow.** A field over parameters takes `=>` ([A function's body follows its arrow](../../design/surface/a-functions-body-follows-its-arrow.md)), which states that an old form is refused by the grammar as `expected: =>`; a head, a test, a premise and a witness's field are, but a tuple's and a structure literal's are not: `(twice(n: Nat) = n * 2, ten = 10)` is refused at the parameter's `:` as `expected: )`, its reader falling back to a positional element and reading `twice(n: Nat)` as a call, and `Step { next(n) = n + 1 }` at its `=` as `expected: }`. - **A hidden binder may stand nameless in a type, a payload and a structure's field, where a definition must name one.** A definition's `@` parameter is named, `@x: T` or `@_: T`, and a nameless one is refused, `a definition names its plain and @ parameters` (`documentation/language.md`, the signature of a `let` or a `satisfy`); a structure's hidden field is written `as @label: type or its type alone` (`language.md`, fields), and a constructor's payload `at(n: Nat, @Holds(0 < n))` and a function type's parameter `(n: Nat, @Holds(0 < n)) -> Nat` each take one nameless. So what follows `@` is told only by what comes after it: `@Holds` names a binder `Holds`, and `@Holds(0 < n)` types a nameless one, which reads as a binder `Holds` over parameters, or a call — the clash a `let`'s `name(…) =` was, a case or a function by the token after it. The rule the user set on 2026-11-27: one rule throughout, a binder nameless only under `use`, a concept's and a witness's, after which a concept application always stands and never a name. It reaches `@` alone: a plain binder is told by its arrow, a lambda's `(x) => …` naming `x` where a function type's `(Nat) -> …` types a nameless slot, and keeps its nameless form, a payload's `success(A)` among them. Not yet known: what the sweep costs over `/std`, the corpus and the fixtures; and each nameless `@` refused where it is written, worded as a definition's is. Size: unknown.
- **Uncertain: a guard holds no action, though an acting guard could be useful.** A `!` in a match's guard is refused, `action in a guard` (`Error::GuardHoldsAction`, raised in `curios-module/src/into_core/lowerer.rs` at the `!`, which `lowerer/match_compile.rs` lowers each guard under `Lowerer::guard` to tell), as [A function's body follows its arrow](../../design/surface/a-functions-body-follows-its-arrow.md) decides, so an action a guard would test is bound inside the arm that tests it. Rust, OCaml, F# and Scala try a guard in arm order once its arm's pattern has matched, its effects happening exactly when it is tried, Rust's `.await` among them, and in Curios a `!` would mark the one guard that acts, where Rust's reader cannot tell an acting guard from a pure one; Lean's nested actions, hoisted ahead of the match, run the actions of arms the match never reaches. Settled by a survey of `/std`, `programs/` and the fixtures for arms that bind an action only to test it, then the barrier prototyped in a scratch copy and held to coverage's precision, or this entry dropped. Size: unknown.
