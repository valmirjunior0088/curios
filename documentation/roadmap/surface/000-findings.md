# Surface: findings

- **A string, `Int` or `Flt` literal written as a pattern is refused as a token the parser did not expect, not by name.** [A literal is realized by its expected type](../../design/surface/a-literal-is-realized-by-its-expected-type.md) gives literal patterns to `Nat` and `Byte` columns alone, and rejects `Str` patterns as what "a guard with `==` already owns"; `parse_match_pattern` (`curios-surface/src/parse/patterns.rs`) says none of it. Probed at `1effd4217`: `| "source" =>`, `| -1 =>` and `| +1 =>` are each a `syntax error` with `expected: an identifier` and no `obtained:` at the literal, and `| 1.5 =>` reads `1` as a natural's pattern and stops at the `.` with ``expected: `=>` ``. Neither names the form written or the form the language has, as [`curios-surface`'s README](../../../curios-surface/README.md#a-refusal-is-worded-at-the-commitment-the-alternative-owes) asks of a form the grammar does not have, so a reader who writes `match name | "source" => … end` is not told of the guard that tests it, `match | name == "source" => … | _ => … end`. Fix: an alternative of `parse_match_pattern` that commits at a string, signed or decimal literal in a pattern's place and refuses it by name, a literal of that type being no pattern, with the guard written in its place. Check: a parse test for each of the four spellings holding the wording and the place at the literal. One commit.
