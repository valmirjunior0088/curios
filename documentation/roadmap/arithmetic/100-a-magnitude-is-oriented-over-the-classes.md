# A magnitude is oriented over the classes

**Not refined yet.** [A term is one where conversion says so](../../design/arithmetic/a-term-is-one-where-conversion-says-so.md) decides that no verdict follows how a term is spelled, and [Conversion is one relation in both checkers](../../design/soundness/conversion-is-one-relation-in-both-checkers.md) that a verdict is a function of the two terms. `Int/abs` and `Int/sign` hold an operand and its negation one by orienting it, which holds two magnitudes one only where their atoms are spelled alike, so a verdict follows the spelling an atom reached the fold in.

## What is missing

`Int/abs` and `Int/sign` fold a combination read from one end (`read`, `curios-core/src/reduce/intrinsic/magnitude.rs`): its monomials stand in the order of their factors' structural hashes and the first leads, so `abs(i - j)` and `abs(j - i)` are one term. Two spellings of one factor that convert without being one term hash apart — a `match` whose motive is written beside one that takes it from where it stands — so an operand spelled two ways is led by one monomial in one magnitude and by the other in the next. Conversion classes the pair's atoms and reads it again as the classes spell it (step 7, `read_classed`, `curios-analysis/src/conversion.rs`), but no reader reads a magnitude, so the classed pair keeps both orientations, and the congruence compares `A - B` with `B - A` and refuses.

Under `use /std/{Eq, Nat, Int, Bool, Io};`, `let one(a: Nat, b: Nat, c: Bool) -> Nat = Int/abs(Nat/to_int(match c | true => a | false => b end) - Nat/to_int(match c | true => b | false => a end));`, `let other`, the same with each `match c` written `match c: (_) => Nat`, and `let claim: Eq()(one, other) = Eq/refl();`, elaboration accepts and the compilation ends at ``the kernel refused /claim: expected `Eq.{Type; 0}(…)(one, other)`, found `Eq.{Type; 0}(…)(one, one)` ``. Of the six orders of the three binders, the two with `c` last are refused and the other four accepted, and `Int/sign` in `Int/abs`'s place answers alike; with the two functions spelled one way, and with no magnitude over the difference, both checkers accept; probed on top of `68ca73258`. No `False` follows: a term elaboration accepts is refused. `/std/Flt/of_decimal_in`, printed as a report prints it, is refused so: with a local's index hashed as a 64-bit word, elaboration infers its bound's proof with the `Int/abs` operand led by the denominator's `bit_len`, where the type it is checked against leads it with the numerator's; probed on top of `03e32ba40` (`REFUSED`, `curios-pipeline/src/standard/tests.rs`).

No order of atoms removes it: the order puts the two spellings of one factor where their hashes put them, and an order that reads no binder's identity still reads the spelling.

## Design

- **The classification spells a magnitude by its own fold over its spelled operand.** It is the combined canonizer Shostak's method rests on: the canonical form of a term is the theory's canonizer applied once its subterms are replaced by their representatives — Rueß and Shankar's `S⟨⟨a⟩⟩ = σ(S[a])` ([*Combining Shostak Theories*](https://www.csl.sri.com/people/shankar/rta02.pdf), 2002). The classification already replaces each atom by its class's representative and the readers canonize what they read; a magnitude's canonizer is its fold, which the classification does not take, so the two sides of the pair are canonized over two spellings of one class. Taken over the representatives, both are led by one monomial, `sign`'s negation leaving it as its law says.
- **Which operations.** Those whose fold writes a choice it reads off its atoms' ranks and no reader reads again: today `Absolute` and `Signum`, found by surveying every reader of the structural hash (`atoms.rs`, `magnitude.rs`, two sorts in `int.rs`): every other site orders a product's factors or a combination's monomials, which each reader reads again as a combination. Declared once, with the operation in `curios-algebra`'s declaration, and read by the classification, so no list says it a second time.
- **The decision changes first.** The term decision rejects "a node's own fold taken by the classification, wherever an operand of it moved", for two reasons. That every fold a program needs is one a reducer takes again at its stuck fold fails here: the reducer's refold classes the atoms within one magnitude, and the orientation needs the classes across the pair, which no reduction sees. That a fold the classification took would decide, inside a question, what plain reduction leaves stuck does not apply: over representatives a magnitude's fold only reorients, a collapse among the atoms of one magnitude being the reducer's own refold. The rejection is narrowed to the operations whose fold reads no rank.

**Rejected.**

- **Two magnitudes held one where their operands are one or each other's negation**, a residual of two alternatives: no outcome hands back one of two residuals, the elaborator would have to try the first without committing, and `sign` reaches the linear reader as `sign(X)` beside `+0 - sign(Y)`, which a rule over two magnitudes does not meet.
- **A reader of magnitudes in the chain**: it states the orientation a second time beside the fold, and the two drift.
- **Leaving the orientation to the readers alone**, the fold keeping the operand as reduced: `abs(i - j)` and `abs(j - i)` become two terms to every fold that compares them and to every key a guard is recorded under, and every reader gains the orientation.

## Check

The claim above accepted by both checkers in each of the six orders of its binders, with `Int/abs` and with `Int/sign`; `/std/Flt/of_decimal_in` leaves `REFUSED`; and `curios`'s `tests::laws` holds every law with an atom respelled by a convertible spelling that ranks apart — a `match` with its motive written and taken from where it stands — so stability under respelling is held where it failed. Taken supervised; it lands before [An identity is read by its equality and its age](101-an-identity-is-read-by-its-equality-and-its-age.md), which reorders the atoms of every magnitude `/std` writes and could surface this refusal elsewhere.
