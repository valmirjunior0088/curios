# A classing reads an atom as its reader meets it

**Not refined yet.** [A term is one where conversion says so](../../design/arithmetic/a-term-is-one-where-conversion-says-so.md) decides that no verdict follows how a term is spelled, and that a classing asked for inside a classing is held inside the two atoms being compared. A call forced inside a classing's question stands inside neither atom as written, so it stays two atoms, and each checker refuses two functions that differ only in whether a type argument is written or solved for. It is not an implementation plan, and no design is chosen.

## What is missing

`Classes::join` (`curios-analysis/src/classify.rs`) classes an atom met while another classing is asking only where it stands, as it is spelled, inside one of the two atoms asked about (`stands_inside`), which is what keeps classings from nesting deeper than a term does. An atom is handed out in weak-head form and the two asked about hold their parts as written, so a call inside one of them, forced where a reader beneath meets it, is a fold or a `match` that stands inside neither: it stays two atoms, a commutative operation over it has no operand paired (`Outcome::Unpaired`, `curios-analysis/src/conversion.rs`), and the pair is unequal to the kernel and to elaboration alike. The rule's doc gives such atoms as the refusing direction and means those a recursive call unfolds to; this call recurses nowhere.

Two sides that are one term are never classed, so it takes two spellings of one value to meet the rule, and a type argument gives them: a family's name written is its former, a member of the family's recursive group, a type argument solved for is the `InductType` the former unfolds to, and both print `T`. Under `induct T: Type | b(n: Nat) end`, `induct Mode: Type | near() | far() end`, `let weigh(ts: List(T)) -> Nat = List/fold(ts, 0, (t, acc) => match t | b(n) => acc + n end);` and `let step(m: Nat, mode: Mode) -> Int = let up = match mode | near() => m * m > 5 | far() => false end; Nat/to_int(match up | true => 1 | false => 0 end);`, the functions `let written(ps: List(Nat), mode: Mode) -> Int = step(weigh(List/map(@_, @T, ps, (n) => T/b(n))), mode);` and `let solved(ps: List(Nat), mode: Mode) -> Int = step(weigh(List/map(ps, (n) => T/b(n))), mode);` are one to elaboration, by `let claim: Eq()(written, solved) = Eq/refl();`, and the compilation ends at ``the kernel refused /claim: expected `Eq(…)(written, solved)`, found `Eq(…)(written, written)` ``. With `let m = weigh(…); step(m, mode) + step(m + 1, mode)` as each body, elaboration refuses the claim itself: a type mismatch between those two types, under the note that no operand of one side of a commutative operation is an operand of the other, which names nothing its reader can change. Both checkers accept the first claim with the two functions written alike, either way, with the fold written where `weigh` is called, with `step` answering its second `match` and no `Nat/to_int` over it, and with no `match` on `mode`; and the second with the fold written in place. Probed on top of `d01e9f291`, and read off a line added for the runs at each comparison the kernel refuses and at each atom `join` passes over: the kernel's first refusal is the product `m * m`, unpaired while a classing asks (`Kernel::asked`), eight atoms passed over before it.

## What is known

- **Why elaboration accepts the first claim is read and not traced.** `one_definition_by_its_spines` (`curios-cert/src/kernel/convert.rs`) says elaboration compares the spines of one definition before it unfolds either call, and the kernel, posed two names, forces both through `step` first (`forced`).
- **Four `let`s of `/std/Flt` the read-back lists as refused by the kernel alone are this** (`UNCERTIFIED`, `curios-pipeline/src/standard/tests.rs`). The printed form of `signals/scaled_prod_sum` and of `signals/scaled_prod_diff` writes `List/map`'s result type, `@Term`, where the source leaves it solved for, and in a copy of the library each `let` written again with that one argument added is refused by the kernel as its printed form is, where the first with the argument left out is accepted. In the printed form of `scaled_prod_sum` the operation left unpaired is an `||` of `signals/scaled`, 352 atoms passed over before it. `to_str` and `significant` carry this rule's marks and are not bisected to their pair of spellings: each is refused at the proof that it is its printed form, at every one of eight starts of the kernel's identities, with 328 and 288 atoms passed over and an operation unpaired while a classing asks, probed on top of `939b82db1`. None of the four is [An atom is ranked by where its binder stands](100-an-atom-is-ranked-by-where-its-binder-stands.md)'s, where an operand is led by another monomial.

## Refinement

- **Which rule moves.** One of three, each a rule of the shared analysis or of the kernel, designed in its decision first (`.claude/rules/checking.md`): `stands_inside` reads the two atoms asked about as their readers meet them, a term's weak-head form standing inside what the term stands inside, with what bounds the nesting stated for it; the kernel unfolds two calls of two definitions one definition at a time and tries their spines at each, as it says elaboration does; or a family is spelled one way where its name is written and where a type is solved for, which removes this pair of spellings and leaves the rule.
- **Check**: both claims above accepted by both checkers, and the four `let`s leaving `UNCERTIFIED`. Taken supervised.
