# An atom is one where conversion says so

Working specification for what an atom of a carrier's algebra is, and for when two of them are one. It is held to [A term is one where conversion says so](../../design/arithmetic/a-term-is-one-where-conversion-says-so.md): no verdict and no solution follows how a term is spelled, the order it holds its operands in, or the arm it stands in. The code keeps that rule for an atom every reader takes whole, a variable or a call, and breaks it for an operation that one reader reads and the reader above it does not, and for two calls of one definition apart in a universe level and in an argument that converts.

## What this builds on

- **One table says what an operation is.** `Intrinsic::algebra` (`curios-core/src/intrinsic/algebra.rs`) gives each intrinsic the operation `curios-algebra` declares for it, its carrier and the operands its meaning reads, or says it is opaque. The match is exhaustive.
- **The readers decide over handles.** Each reader of a carrier's algebra — the linear views of `Nat` and `Int` (`curios-core/src/linear.rs`), the truth table of `Bool` (`reduce/intrinsic/truth.rs`), the peels of a commutative operation, a connective and a lattice's leaf (`spine.rs`), the fold laws (`reduce/intrinsic/laws.rs`) — reads the operations it knows and hands `curios-algebra` a handle for whatever else stands at an operand, its atom. `curios-algebra` names no term, and decides over handles by normal forms.
- **The chain hands a pair's atoms back, and is entered again with a partition.** Where the readers decide nothing of a pair, `convert_intrinsics` (`curios-analysis/src/conversion.rs`) ends on `Pass::Atoms`; the checker says which atoms convert, the elaborator with nothing committed (`same_uncommitted`, `curios-elab/src/convert.rs`) and the kernel leaving apart what is already in progress (`classes`, `curios-cert/src/kernel/convert/intrinsic.rs`); and `convert_classed` reads the pair once more with each atom spelled as its class's representative. `Classes` (`curios-core/src/reduce/intrinsic/classed.rs`) is that partition, and four more consumers take the same turn each on its own: `connectives_agree`, an arm's equation (`answers`, `curios-analysis/src/case_equation.rs`), the stuck fold of each reducer (`curios-elab/src/reduce.rs`, `curios-cert/src/kernel/whnf.rs`), and the bound prover's reading of a fact (`Facts::reading`, `curios-elab/src/entailment/facts.rs`).
- **An atom's identity is its carrier's, stated once.** `same_atom` and `Atoms::handle` (`curios-core/src/atoms.rs`) say what an atom of a carrier is known by, and the readers of a number, a truth value and a float take their handles and their direct comparisons from them, a fold's test of one operand against the other among them: an idempotence, a division of a value by itself, a packed sequence against itself. The word readers match a chunk as written, of a packed sequence as of a list (`curios-algebra`'s `Alphabet::Symbol`). At a carrier whose values hold no type — `Nat`, `Int`, `Bool`, `Byte`, `Flt`, a packed sequence — a universe instance is no part of it, in both checkers, on the argument [Case equations and their key](../../design/soundness/case-equations-and-their-key.md) states for a number: Core has no elimination from a type or a level into such a value. Two applications of one operation that produce such a value are one where they are one up to instances (the chain's ninth step). A list's element and a product's factor are known by the term as written, since a list holds values of any type and a product's order is a hash of the term.
- **Peers.** The word problem of a union of equational theories over disjoint signatures is decided from each theory's own procedure, by abstracting a term's alien subterms from its leaves to its root, removing a collapse and identifying two variables equated to equivalent pure terms; the combination is polynomial past the theories' own cost (Baader and Tinelli, [*Deciding the Word Problem in the Union of Equational Theories*](https://homepage.cs.uiowa.edu/~tinelli/papers/BaaTin-RR-98.pdf), after Pigozzi, 1974). Coq Modulo Theory's conversion collects a term's aliens under its algebraic cap and abstracts convertible aliens by one variable, comparing them two by two (Strub, slides of the ADT Coq meeting, 2010; Jouannaud and Strub, 2017). Canonizers of disjoint theories combine where solvers do not (Rueß and Shankar, [*Combining Shostak Theories*](https://www.csl.sri.com/people/shankar/rta02.pdf), 2002). Lambdapi keeps a term over a commutative or associative-commutative symbol in a canonical form under a total order it leaves unspecified ([commands](https://lambdapi.readthedocs.io/en/latest/commands.html)), and Lean's `grind` sorts the operand lists of an associative-commutative operator, `And`, `Or` and `Iff` excepted ([Associativity and Commutativity](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/The--grind--tactic/Associativity-and-Commutativity/)). Lean compares two instances of a constant by assigning level metavariables ([`Lean/Meta/LevelDefEq.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/master/src/Lean/Meta/LevelDefEq.lean)), and Mathlib's `AtomM` compares two atoms by `isDefEq` ([`Mathlib/Util/AtomM.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Util/AtomM.lean)).

## The gap

Each program is read at `1d5004da7`, as the goal of an `Eq/refl()` or a hypothesis handed to a goal, by `cargo run --release --package curios -- run -`; *refused* is refused by the elaborator, and where it is the kernel's refusal the row says so.

**Nothing says what an atom is.** Each reader states which operations it reads by reading them, and `read_through` (`classed.rs`) states the union of those once more for the walk that hands a pair's atoms out: a sum, a product, a connective, a comparison, every operation declared commutative, a fused multiply-add, a `Nat`'s successor floor, `Int/abs`, `Int/sign`, a float's negation and `copysign`. The walk reads through each and hands out only what stands beneath all of them. So an operation one reader reads and the reader above it takes whole is handed out to no one: the reader above pairs it by its spelling, and its two spellings are one only to its own reader.

Over `x, y, z, w, u, v: Nat`, `i, j, k, l: Int`, `a, b, c, d: Flt`, each of these is refused, where `Eq()(Nat/and(x, y), Nat/and(y, x))` closes and each closes with one operand spelled alike on both sides:

- A commutative operation under a difference, a sum, a product or an equality: `Nat/and(x, y) - Nat/and(y, x)` against `0`; `Int/and(i, j) - Int/and(j, i)` against `+0`; `Nat/or(x, y) + Nat/xor(z, w)` against `Nat/xor(w, z) + Nat/or(y, x)`, and the same pair under `*` and under `==`; `Int/or(i, j) + Int/xor(k, l)` against `Int/xor(l, k) + Int/or(j, i)`; `Nat/and(x, y) + Nat/and(z, w)` against `Nat/and(w, z) + Nat/and(y, x)`; `Nat/and(x, y) == Nat/and(z, w)` against `Nat/and(w, z) == Nat/and(y, x)`.
- A commutative operation under another, or under itself: `Nat/and(Nat/or(x, y), Nat/xor(z, w))` against `Nat/and(Nat/or(y, x), Nat/xor(w, z))` and against `Nat/and(Nat/xor(w, z), Nat/or(y, x))`; `Nat/min(Nat/or(x, y), Nat/xor(z, w))` against `Nat/min(Nat/xor(w, z), Nat/or(y, x))`; `Nat/min(Nat/and(x, y), Nat/and(z, w))` against `Nat/min(Nat/and(w, z), Nat/and(y, x))`; `Nat/and(Nat/and(x, y), Nat/and(y, x))` against `Nat/and(x, y)`.
- A sum under a commutative operation: `Nat/and(x + y, z + w)` against `Nat/and(y + x, w + z)` and against `Nat/and(w + z, y + x)`; `Int/and(i + j, k + l)` against `Int/and(l + k, j + i)`; `Nat/min(x + y, z + w)` against `Nat/min(y + x, w + z)` and against `Nat/min(w + z, y + x)`; `Nat/and(x + y, z + w) - Nat/and(y + x, w + z)` against `0`; `Nat/xor(x + y, z) + Nat/xor(w, x + z)` against `Nat/xor(w, z + x) + Nat/xor(y + x, z)`; `Nat/max(x + y, z + w) + Nat/min(x + z, y + w)` against `Nat/min(w + y, z + x) + Nat/max(w + z, y + x)`.
- A comparison under a connective: `Nat/eql(x, y) && Nat/eql(y, x)` against `Nat/eql(x, y)`; `(x == y) && (y == x)` against `x == y`; `(x == y) && (z == w)` against `(w == z) && (y == x)`; `(x != y) || (z != w)` against `(w != z) || (y != x)`; `(x + y < z) && (w + v < u)` against `(v + w < u) && (y + x < z)`.
- A float operation under another: `Flt/min(a + b, c * d)` against `Flt/min(b + a, d * c)`; `(a + b) + (c * d)` against `(d * c) + (b + a)`; `Flt/fma(a + b, c * d, a)` against `Flt/fma(d * c, b + a, a)`; `Flt/max(Flt/min(a, b), Flt/min(c, d))` against `Flt/max(Flt/min(d, c), Flt/min(b, a))`; `Flt/min(a + b, c + d) + Flt/max(a * c, b * d)` against `Flt/max(d * b, c * a) + Flt/min(d + c, b + a)`.
- A sum that is a factor of a product no reader expands: `(x + y) * (z + w) - (y + x) * (w + z)` against `0`, the same beside a summand `n` on both sides, and at `Int`; `Nat/and((x + y) * (z + w), (x + z) * (y + w))` against `Nat/and((w + y) * (z + x), (w + z) * (y + x))`.
- An operation whose operand holds one in turn, over `f, g: (Nat) -> Nat`: `Nat/and(f(x + y), z) + Nat/xor(g(z + w), x)` against `Nat/xor(x, g(w + z)) + Nat/and(z, f(y + x))`, and the same two under `Nat/and`; `Nat/and(x + y, z) == Nat/or(z + w, x)` against `Nat/or(x, w + z) == Nat/and(z, y + x)`; `Nat/and(x, y) * Nat/and(z, w) + Nat/and(x, z)` against `Nat/and(z, x) + Nat/and(w, z) * Nat/and(y, x)`; `Nat/and(x, y) + Nat/and(z, w) + Nat/and(x, z)` against `Nat/and(z, x) + Nat/and(y, x) + Nat/and(w, z)`; and a comparison beside its dual, `(Nat/and(x, y) < n) && Bool/not(n <= Nat/and(y, x) + z)` against `(Nat/and(y, x) < n) && (Nat/and(x, y) + z < n)`.

**The same reading is taken at five places, and each misses it.**

- A side's own root against an operation inside the other side: `Nat/eql(x, y) || (Nat/eql(x, y) && q)` against `Nat/eql(y, x)` is refused.
- A stuck fold: `match Nat/and(x, y) == Nat/and(y, x) | true => 0 | false => 1 end` against `0` is refused.
- An arm's equation: under `match Nat/and(x, y) + n == 3 | true => …`, `let fact: Holds(n + Nat/and(y, x) == 3) = True/qed();` is refused, and accepted with `Nat/and(x, y)`.
- The bound prover: with `low: Holds(Nat/and(x, y) < n)` in scope, `Holds(Nat/and(y, x) < n + 1)` is not proved by `True/proved()`, and the report says it fails at `Nat/and(y, x) = 2, n = 1, Nat/and(x, y) = 0`, an assignment no value reaches.
- Conversion is not transitive: `held: Holds(Nat/or(x, y) + Nat/xor(z, w) == 7)` is refused at `Holds(Nat/xor(w, z) + Nat/or(y, x) == 7)`, and accepted by both checkers through `let step: Holds(Nat/or(y, x) + Nat/xor(z, w) == 7) = held;`.

**A classing asks about two calls only where their heads are one term.** `may_be_one` (`classed.rs`) reads two applications as possibly one where their heads are identical, universe instances included, so two calls of one definition apart in an instance and in an argument that converts are never put to the checker. Each written call of a universe-polymorphic definition takes a level of its own, open until its declaration closes: over `induct Tree(K: Type): Type | tip(K) | join(Tree(K), Tree(K)) end` and `let size(@K: Type, t: Tree(K)) -> Nat = match t | tip(_) => 1 | join(l, r) => size(l) + size(r) end;`, with `f: (Nat) -> Tree(K)`, `held: Eq()(size(f(x + y)) + size(f(z + w)), n)` is refused at `Eq()(size(f(w + z)) + size(f(y + x)), n)`, and its twin over a type that takes no parameter is accepted.

**What stays refused.** Each of these is false or is no law the algebra declares, is refused today, and stays so: `Nat/and(x, y) + Nat/and(z, w)` against `Nat/and(x, w) + Nat/and(z, y)`; `Nat/and(x + y, z)` against `Nat/and(x, y + z)`; `(x == y) && (z == w)` against `(x == w) && (z == y)`; `Nat/and(x, y) - Nat/or(y, x)` against `0`; `Flt/min(a + b, c)` against `Flt/min(a, b + c)`; `Nat/and(Nat/and(x, y), z)` against `Nat/and(x, Nat/and(y, z))`; `Nat/or(x, y)` against `Nat/and(y, x)`; `(x < y) && (y < z)` against `(y < x) && (z < y)`; `(a + b) + c` against `a + (b + c)`; `Nat/and(x, y) + Nat/and(y, z)` against `Nat/and(y, x) + Nat/and(x, z)`; `(x == y) || (p && q)` against `(y == x) && (q || p)`; and, over `let twice(c: Nat, n: Nat) -> Nat = match n == 0 | true => c | false => twice(c, n / 2) * twice(c, n / 2) end;`, `twice(x, y)` against `twice(2, y)`, `twice(x, y) * x` against `x * twice(2, y)`, `Nat/shl(x, y)` against `x * twice(2, y)`, `twice(x, y + 1)` against `twice(x, y) * x`, and `Nat/and(twice(x, y), twice(2, y)) + Nat/or(twice(x, y), x)` against `Nat/or(x, twice(2, y)) + Nat/and(twice(2, y), twice(x, y + 1))`.

## Decisions

**A term at a carrier is layers of the algebra over opaque atoms, classified once, from its leaves to its root.** What a layer is, and which operands it reads, is `Intrinsic::algebra`'s to say and no reader's: an intrinsic the table declares an operation is a node, and anything else is an opaque atom. The table declares every intrinsic a reader reads, the successor floor, `Int/abs`, `Int/sign`, a float's negation and `copysign` among them, so no second list says what is read through.

**An opaque atom is classed by the checker.** Two of them are one where the checker's own conversion says so, each checker under its own state, as today.

**A node is classed by the readers, over its operands' classes.** A node's operands are classified first, and the node is spelled over their representatives. Its own fold is then taken: a fold that answers an operand, a literal or another operation is the node's class, the collapse; one that leaves the node standing is not kept. A node left standing is held to the earlier nodes of its operation by the shared readers alone, the chain's `read` with every residual followed and no judgment asked, and takes the first one's representative. So an operation one reader reads and the reader above it takes whole is an atom of the reader above by construction, and the checker is asked nothing about what a node's operands are. One question is left to it: two nodes of one operation spelled alike in every operand the algebra reads and apart in one it does not, a quotient's proof of its divisor, are put to the checker as two atoms are, and the comparison meets identical operands and that one pair, so it classes nothing in turn.

**Two calls of one name apart in an instance are asked about by their arguments.** `may_be_one` holds two applications of one head up to its universe instances, and where the instance is all the heads differ in, the classing puts each pair of arguments to the checker and joins the two where every pair converts. An atom the classification hands out is an operand of an operation the table declares, and it declares none over a list, so the two stand at a carrier whose values hold no type and the instance is no part of what they are.

**One entry classifies, and every consumer calls it.** The classification lives in `curios-analysis`, beside the chain, and conversion, the connectives, an arm's equation, each reducer's stuck fold and the bound prover's reading take a term's spelling from it.

## Open questions

**Which nodes are held to the earlier nodes of their operation.** Holding one costs a reading against each earlier node of its operation, so what is held decides what a large term costs. Three rules give every program above its verdict: every node; every node but one standing under a node of its own operation, where the law table declares that operation associative, since a reader that holds an association flattens it; and a node the reader of its parent does not read through. The first states nothing of the readers and holds the most, quadratic in the summands of a long sum; the second reads the law table alone; the last holds the fewest and restates, per operation, what its reader flattens, which *Rejected* turns down as a list. Measured before the second stage lands, as *Verification* states, and the first rule within the cost stated there is taken.

## Verification

- Every program under *The gap* is a row put to both checkers, the kernel asked alone wherever the harness allows, and every program under *What stays refused* a refused row.
- **Every declared law, generated.** `tests::laws::audit` puts each law of each carrier's table to both checkers reversed, chained, at a compound operand and over calls apart in a universe instance; the second stage adds the grid at an operand spelled two ways, one operand and two.
- **Nested terms.** `L(0) = x0`, `L(k) = Nat/and(L(k - 1), Nat/or(xk, yk))`, against its mirror, every operand pair commuted at every level, as the goal of an `Eq/refl()`: accepted at depth 32 as a fixture, and timed at depths 8, 16, 32 and 64 by an ignored measurement, where a classing that put two nodes to the checker would nest one classing a level.
- **Which nodes are held** is measured in a copy of the tree with the three rules behind one switch: the prelude's elaboration and its certification profiled under each (`cargo xtask profile-prelude`), comparing the counted `budget::consumed`, `convert::same_uncommitted` and the classing spans with the commit before, then the nested terms above and a sum of 256 products of two atoms against its reversal under each. A rule that grows either budget by more than a tenth, or under which the long sum takes more than a second, is not taken.
- **The image compared**, where a stage claims it unchanged: a release build of the commit before the stage and one of the stage, `std.rkyv` under `curios-prelude-archive`'s `OUT_DIR` and `certification.rkyv` under `curios-prelude`'s, by digest.
- `cargo xtask workspace lint` at every stage, which elaborates, erases and certifies `/std`, and `cargo xboard report`.

## Rejected

- **A node put to the checker**, two operations of one kind asked about as two atoms are: comparing them classes their operands in turn, so the cost doubles with each level of nesting, and a wrong equation between two nested terms waits on its budget to be refused.
- **Every node the readers read through handed out as an atom as well.** The questions put over `/std` grow several times over before anything new is decided.
- **Only a node standing under a node of another operation held.** `Nat/and(Nat/and(x, y), Nat/and(y, x))` holds two nodes under their own operation, which its reader does not flatten.
- **A list, kept by hand, of which reader reads through which operation.** It is a second statement of what the readers do, and it is wrong the first time a reader changes: a sum that is a factor of a stuck product is an atom of the product, which a list written from the linear view's name gets wrong.
- **A sorted spelling at the fold**, a commutative operation's operands held in one order so that two spellings are one term: a product's order is a hash and a sum keeps what was written, `Bool` has no normal form the decisions accept, and the fold laws would have to hold under every order a later fold leaves.
- **The elaborator joining the two levels** where a classing meets two instances: an answer the solver can change is not kept, the kernel has no solver, and where a call is commuted or repeated the levels close apart, so the kernel refuses what the elaborator accepted.
- **Parking the pair while a level metavariable stands in it.** It accepts only the rows whose sides close as one term.
- **A vector for a recursive member**, so that two instances apart in a level it does not read are a weak equation: the universe decision rejects it, a member reproducing its levels under every unfolding.
- **One handle table threaded through every reader.** Every reader, the fold laws and the peels among them, would take a table it has no handles to keep in, and the partition already is the table: what holds a reader to the identity is the generated grid.
