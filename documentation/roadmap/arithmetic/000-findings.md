# Arithmetic: findings

- **Uncertain: `Nat/to_int` commutes with no division, so `Int`'s quotient and remainder of a widened natural stay apart from the natural's own.** At `Int`, `/` and `%` are Euclid's and `/sys/Int/rem` answers a natural ([`curios-num`'s README](../../../curios-num/README.md#int-divides-as-euclid-does)), so `Nat/to_int(m) / +3` is `Nat/to_int(m / 3)` and `Int/rem(Nat/to_int(m), +3)` is `m % 3` at every `m`; conversion holds both apart, `Eq()(Nat/to_int(m) % +3, Nat/to_int(m % 3))` refused at `1d6bc272e`. A fold cannot state either: the reduct is a `Nat` division, which carries a proof its divisor is nonzero that a reducer may not invent ([A law is decided where it neither respells nor invents](../../design/arithmetic/a-law-is-decided-where-it-neither-respells-nor-invents.md)), and `Int`'s proof states `d != +0` where `Nat`'s states `0 < |d|`. The route is the peel, which equates two stuck values without building either, as it does two reads of one position. No `/std` proof needs it; the nearest is `/std/Int/shl/unique`, whose odd mantissa is stated on `Int/abs(m) % 2` where `m % 2` would read better, a parity the procedure cannot carry across the magnitude without it. What is not known: whether a consumer appears, and whether the peel's pairing reads a symbolic divisor's magnitude or only a literal's. Settled by the first proof that needs one spelling to be the other, then a peel rule with its value grid, or by dropping this entry. Size: unknown.
