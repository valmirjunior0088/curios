# Arithmetic: findings

- **Uncertain: a fold law's fixture passes by the order two atoms' hashes fall in.** `every_open_fold_law_preserves_the_value_at_every_closed_instantiation` (`curios-core/src/reduce/intrinsic/laws_tests.rs`) holds `to_int(abs(to_int(x) - to_int(x - y)))` to its stated reduct, `to_int(x) - to_int(x - y)`, by the equality of the two folds. `read` (`curios-core/src/reduce/intrinsic/magnitude.rs`) orders a combination's monomials by their factors' structural hashes and takes the first for its lead, and `widened_magnitude` hands back what is left in that order, where the reduct's own fold keeps the order it is written in: the two are one spelling where `to_int(x)` ranks first and two where `to_int(x - y)` does. A term's structural hash is taken over its variant's discriminant (`curios-core/src/term/scalars.rs`), so with `Subterm`'s variants after its sorts each numbered one lower the case fails, `(-1) * to_int(x - y) + to_int(x)` against `to_int(x) + (-1) * to_int(x - y)`, two terms that convert. Not known: whether the law promises the spelling of what it leaves or its value, and so whether the fold is to hand back one order whatever the ranks or the fixture is to compare two sums by their monomials. Fix: once that is known. Check: the case passes with two of `Subterm`'s variants exchanged. Size: unknown.
- **Uncertain: a direction in which the checkers could part on a guard over local definitions does not reproduce.** The kernel records a guard over local definitions with them substituted, and the elaborator records the spelling that names them, with the kernel's beside it as an alias no settlement reads, so under `let n = Byte/to_nat(c); match Nat/in_range(n, 0, 0x7F)` the kernel's reduct would answer `Byte/to_nat(c) <= 0x7F` where the elaborator's does not. Two programs written to meet it are accepted by both checkers at `9d121c13f`: in the `true` arm, `let _: Holds(Byte/to_nat(c) <= 0x7F) = True/qed()`, and over `Nat/in_range(n, 1, 0x7F)`, `let _: Holds(1 <= Byte/to_nat(c)) = Nat/le/of_in_range(Byte/to_nat(c), 1, 0x7F, True/qed()).low`. So is the opposite direction's program, `Nat/le/of_in_range(m, 240, 244, True/qed())` under `let n = m + 0; match Nat/in_range(n, 240, 244)`. Not known: whether a third spelling parts them, or whether only the procedure that proves a bound meets it (`curios`'s `tests::bounds` states the shape with `False/refuted()`). Investigate before any fix: read what each checker records for the guard and which spellings each probes.
