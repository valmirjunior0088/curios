# A shift and a mask are a division by a power of two, and a remainder is a natural

**Refined.** The logic reads a right shift, a mask and a remainder on both carriers through one statement: a right shift by `k` and a mask of `k` low bits are the quotient and the remainder by `2ᵏ`, and a division has one convention, the Euclidean one, whose remainder is a natural. The next step is stage 1, a delivery.

## What is missing

**A right shift is read by no proof.** Conversion holds of `Nat/shr` its zero laws, a count's literal floor taken in two steps and `shr(x, y) <= x`, and ties it to no quotient, since a fold may not build a division node, whose proof that the divisor is nonzero it would invent ([A law is decided where it neither respells nor invents](../../design/arithmetic/a-law-is-decided-where-it-neither-respells-nor-invents.md)); [the elaborator's procedure](../../design/arithmetic/a-bound-that-follows-from-the-facts-in-scope-is-proved-by-the-elaborator.md) reads it as a free atom. At `0476b6d8b`, `proved()` refuses `4 * Nat/shr(x, 2) <= x` and `Nat/shr(x, 4) < 16` from `x < 256`, where it proves `4 * (x / 4) <= x` and `x / 16 < 16`, and conversion holds `Nat/shr(x * 4, 2)` apart from `x` where it holds `(x * 4) / 4` one with it. A mask, `Nat/and(x, 15)`, is as unread beside the remainder `x % 16` it is.

**Its consumers work around it.** `/std/Map/digits` proves a byte its two nibbles — `x == 16 * Nat/shr(x, 4) + Nat/and(x, 15)`, each below sixteen — by evaluating that at all 256 bytes (`table`, through `Nat/all_below`), which a byte's range allows and nothing past it does, and its `higher`, `lower` and `joined` read the table's facts; a program holding that table checks in 449 ms [369, 468] against 240 ms [232, 254] holding one case, timed at `0476b6d8b`, seven interleaved readings of `curios wonder -` over each, median and span. `/std/Char/to_utf8` builds each byte with `/` and `%` by a power of two "under the bound it is proved to satisfy, and none is masked". [The binary64 conversion proofs](304-binary64-conversion-proofs.md) state what `/std/Flt/exact`'s `round_in` keeps, `Nat/shr(m, s)`, and drops, `m - Nat/shl(kept, s)`.

**`Int`'s right shift and remainder are unprovable, not only undecided.** `Int`'s `/` and `%` truncate toward zero and `Int/shr` floors, so `Int`'s shift is no division of its own carrier and its remainder takes the dividend's sign. Conversion holds of `%` Euclid's identity and an exact multiple, and of `Int/shr` its zero laws and count split; neither `Nat/to_int(m) % +3` nor `Int/shr(Nat/to_int(m), 2)` reduces through the widening, nor `(+0 - i) % +3` through the negation, so a lemma by `Int/view` meets a stuck term in each arm, and `i % 3 < +3`, the law grid's refused candidate, has no proof at all. `/std` floors where it divides an `Int`: `/std/time` and `/std/Io` divide through `Int/DivMod/of`, a certified floor written over `Int/view` and `Nat`'s division, at eight sites, and `/std/Int/shl/unique` states its odd mantissa on `Int/abs(m) % 2` for want of `m % 2`; the readers that truncate are `/std/time/Duration`'s `to_secs` and `to_millis`, whose docs say they round toward zero, and `/std/tests/big_num`'s test of the sign a quotient and a remainder take.

**A division by a power of two is emitted as a division.** `curios-emit` lowers `Nat/div` and `Nat/rem` to `i32.div_u` and `i32.rem_u` on the fast path whatever the divisor (`code_emitter.rs`), and the continuation IR keeps a literal one (`Nat/div(x, 4)` at `cps-optm`), where `Nat/shr` and `Nat/and` are one instruction each. `/std` writes a literal power of two as a divisor at about 120 sites, `/std/Nat/hash`'s `n / 256` and `n % 256` for each byte of a key, `/std/Map/digits`'s `p / 4` and `p % 4` at each digit read and `/std/Char/to_utf8`'s among them. Cranelift turns `udiv` and `urem` by a constant power of two into a shift and a mask (`cranelift-codegen` 0.137, `opts/arithmetic.isle`), which no guarantee rests on ([`curios-emit`'s README](../../../curios-emit/README.md)).

## Decided

- **A right shift and a mask are the two halves of a division by a power of two, stated once.** `curios-algebra` names a divisor that is two to a count; `curios-core` reads `shr(x, k)` as the quotient of `x` by `2ᵏ`, a literal mask `and(x, 2ᵏ - 1)` as its remainder, and a nest of shifts and power-of-two divisions as one count; and every reader of a division takes a shift or a mask through that view and through no rule of its own:
  - **the folds** decide once when a quotient by a literal folds — the Euclidean split, then the floor law — and rebuild what is left in the spelling it was read in, a shift with its count, a mask with its width, a division with its proof;
  - **Euclid's recombination** pairs a remainder in either spelling with a quotient's multiple in either, so `pow(2, k) * shr(x, k) + x % pow(2, k)` and `16 * shr(x, 4) + and(x, 15)` are `x`, at every count a substitution puts for `k` — `k + 1`, whose shift folds to a nest and whose power to a product, `k + j` and `2 * k` among them;
  - **the bounds** — `Operation::upper_bound` and `dominators` — read a shift as the quotient it is, where `ShiftRight` restates `Quotient`'s arm today;
  - **the procedure** reads a shift's bounds as a quotient's, `pow(2, k) * shr(x, k) <= x < pow(2, k) * shr(x, k) + pow(2, k)`, the remainder by the power written with `True/qed()` for the nonzero divisor conversion decides, and lifts a mask out of the facts as it lifts a remainder.
- **Every reader takes a spelling at once.** A rule that reads a shift or a mask as a division lands in conversion and in the procedure together: where conversion recombines what the procedure reads apart, a fact relating a value to its parts reduces to a tautology and the procedure loses the tie it proved from.
- **The emitter reads the same view backwards.** A division and a remainder by a literal power of two are emitted as a shift and a mask, so a program never picks a spelling for its speed against the one a proof reads.
- **`Int` divides Euclidean, and its remainder is a natural.** `i / d` is the quotient whose remainder lies in `[0, |d|)`, and `i % d` is the widening of that natural remainder, built so by its `/sys` row from the moment it enters Core, as `gt` and `ge` are built as their mirrors: its bound is `Nat`'s, read through the widening by the comparison's preimages. `Int/shr(i, k)` is then the quotient `i / pow(2, k)` at every `i`, a floor being Euclid's quotient by a positive divisor, so the view reads `Int`'s shift and mask as `Nat`'s; the floor law and the Euclidean split hold at `Int` and are its families, where the law decision holds them controls; `Nat/to_int` commutes with `/`, `%` and `shr`; and `Int/DivMod` is `/` and `%` themselves. Lean changed `Int`'s `/` and `%` to this convention after starting from C's, and SMT-LIB, Dafny, Agda — whose `%` answers a `ℕ` below `∣ d ∣` — and Why3's `EuclideanDivision` state it, as Boute (*The Euclidean definition of the functions div and mod*, TOPLAS 1992) and Leijen (*Division and Modulus for Computer Scientists*, 2001) argue it.
- **A shift is the quotient as a lemma.** `Nat/DivMod/unique(x, pow(2, k), shr(x, k), x % pow(2, k), Eq/refl(), qed)` proves `shr(x, k) = x / pow(2, k)` once the recombination lands, so every quotient lemma reaches a shift through one `Eq/subst`; it is written where a consumer first asks for it.

**Rejected.**

- **A peel holding `shr(x, k)` and `x / pow(2, k)` one stuck value.** At `k + 1` the shift folds to the nest `shr(shr(x, k), 1)` and the divisor to the product `2 * pow(2, k)`, so a peel closed under substitution must read both as one count, and then holds `(x / 4) / 2` one with `x / 8` through the shift between them: a law of nested divisions no fold of the division states, entering by a shift's back door.
- **The bounds in the comparison.** Conversion decides no quotient's scaled bound — `3 * (x / 3) <= x` is the procedure's — so a comparison rule for a shift alone would decide of it what it does not of the quotient it is.
- **A shift's and a mask's folds beside the division's.** A prototype at `0476b6d8b` wrote them as second rules beside the division's: it held every row, and is two statements of when a quotient folds, which the view makes one.
- **The quotient written in `/std` and a shift emitted, the shift left unread.** Nothing trusted changes, and `Nat/shr` stays a spelling the logic never reads, so a reader is told to write a division where a proof follows; the emitter's half is kept.
- **`Int`'s truncation kept, with a law for each half.** `Int/abs(i % d)` is `Int/abs(i) % Int/abs(d)` at a literal divisor and the remainder's sign is the dividend's, and `Int/shr` reaches `Nat/shr` through the sign view in two folds: `Int`'s shift and division stay two conventions, every `Int` lemma about either goes through `Int/view`, and `/std` keeps a second floor of its own in `Int/DivMod`.
- **A shift through a division node**, for the law decision's reasons, and **a total division**, `x / 0 = 0` as Lean's is: a division here carries its divisor's precondition (`/std/ops/Div`'s `Ok`), which a total one would drop for every carrier to buy a law for one.
