# Boolean agreement is transitive and closed under substitution

[Conversion is one relation in both checkers](../../design/soundness/conversion-is-one-relation-in-both-checkers.md) holds conversion to transitivity and congruence, [The carriers' algebra stays in conversion](../../design/arithmetic/the-carriers-algebra-stays-in-conversion.md) to symmetry, transitivity and closure under substitution with the theory in it, and [A term is one where conversion says so](../../design/arithmetic/a-term-is-one-where-conversion-says-so.md) to atoms known as conversion knows them. The conversion chain's Boolean rung, which decides two `Bool` terms equal where they agree at every assignment of their atoms, breaks all three: it decides a pair only while the pair holds at most eight atoms, and it knows an atom, and an atom's negation, by its spelling where conversion knows a comparison by its linear view.

Its next step is delivery, in the stages below, each independent of the others.

## What is wrong

Probed at `0fc2fc1bd` with the release compiler. Each refusal is the elaborator's; the kernel reads a pair through the same rung (`curios-analysis`'s `convert_intrinsics` and `connectives_agree`).

**A cap on the pair.** `Formula::new_atom` (`curios-algebra/src/boolean.rs`) declines a ninth atom of the pair, `decide_bool` (`curios-core/src/reduce/intrinsic/truth.rs`) answers undecided, and the elaborator's report says the two were `not compared` (`curios-elab/src/error/display.rs`, through `past_bool_cap`). The cap bounds the pair and not either side:

- **Transitivity.** With `A` the conjunction of `a0 || a1 || a2 || a3 || a4` and the negation of each of its five atoms, and `B` the same over `a5` to `a9`, `Eq/refl()` closes `Eq()(A, false)` and `Eq()(false, B)` and is refused at `Eq()(A, B)`, whose pair holds ten atoms (`curios`'s `tests::laws::written`, `a_mismatch_nothing_compared_says_so`).
- **Substitution.** Absorption, `Eq()(b || (b && c), b)`, closes by `Eq/refl()`. At `c := a1 && a2 && a3 && a4 && a5 && a6 && a7 && a8` it closes as a call of that lemma and is refused by `Eq/refl()` (the row `Bool, at the table's cap`).

A cap at any value breaks both: two contradictions over more than half of it each convert with `false` and not with each other. A cap on each side instead keeps transitivity and still breaks substitution, since an instance carries a side past any size its law had.

**Atoms known by spelling.** The rung's `Table::atom` knows a leaf by `same_atom` and a comparison's negation by the spelling `dual_comparison` writes, and the chain's classification holds a node only to the nodes of its own operation (`curios-analysis`'s `bucket`). So `x + 1 <= y` and `x < y`, one proposition by the linear view's alignment (`curios-core`'s `linear`, clause 5), are two atoms to the rung, and `y < x + 1` is no negation of `x < y`. Each link below closes by `Eq/refl()`, and the claim is refused:

- **Congruence.** `y < x + 1` converts with `y <= x`, and `(x < y) && (y < x + 1)` does not with `(x < y) && (y <= x)`: the second side's complement law (`normalize_bool`) makes it `false`, and the first is read over three independent atoms.
- **Transitivity.** `(x < y) || ((x + 1 <= y) && c)` converts with `(x < y) || ((x < y) && c)`, which converts with `x < y`, and the ends are refused. So are, against `false`: `(x < y) && (y < x + 1)` at `Nat` and at `Int`, `(i == j) && (j != i)` at `Int`, `(n != 0) && Bool/not(0 < n)`, `(m < n) && (Nat/to_int(n) <= Nat/to_int(m))` and `Bool/xor(x < y, x + 1 <= y)`; against `true`: `(x < y) || (y < x + 1)`, `(x == y) || (y != x)` and `(n == 0) || (0 < n)`; and `(x + 1 <= y) && c && d` against `d && c && (x < y)`, where `(x + 1 <= y) && c` against `c && (x < y)` closes through the commutative pairing's residual.

`tests::laws::audit` meets neither: its substitution rows put one atom for one, `b := x < y`; its chains join laws of a few atoms; and its row at two spellings writes a truth value's variable `x0 == x1` on one side and `x1 == x0` on the other, one relation at one polarity.

**Laws decided and not declared.** The rung decides every identity of the connectives, and `curios-algebra`'s `TABLE` declares for `Bool` neither `xor`'s associativity, complement and dual, nor `==`'s associativity and nested cancellation, nor `!=`'s associativity, nested cancellation and unit on the left, nor `&&` distributing over `xor` and over `!=`, nor `||` over `==`. Each closes by `Eq/refl()`, and no generated row, reversal, chain or substitution holds it, where `curios-algebra`'s README has the table declare, for each operation at each carrier, exactly the families conversion decides.

**An uncharged read.** `Table::atom` compares each new leaf with every atom met, `same_atom` erasing both sides' universes at each comparison: quadratic in the atoms and charged nothing, harmless under the cap and not past it.

## What is known

- **Some bound stands, and only the budget keeps the laws.** Deciding two formulas equivalent is co-NP-complete. A limit on a pair breaks both laws and a limit on each side breaks substitution. The audit counts no refusal by a spent budget (the fourth point of [Conversion is one relation in both checkers](../../design/soundness/conversion-is-one-relation-in-both-checkers.md)), and [the soundness board](../../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md) states acceptance relative to a deterministic step budget and upward closed in it.
- **Exhaustion propagates; a comparison never declines.** A comparison that declined at a limit scaled to the budget would be the site `Probe` (`curios-core/src/reduce.rs`) forbids, one that "absorbed exhaustion instead would answer a question it could not afford", and a declined comparison sends the elaborator to another rule, a congruence that may solve a metavariable, so acceptance could turn to refusal as the budget grows.
- **Identity at a polarity is what deciding over theory atoms needs.** Linear decision diagrams label their nodes with linear constraints and ask of the theory a negation and a normalization `N` with `N(c) = N(¬c)`, a constraint and its negation one label read at two polarities; over the integers `¬(t ≤ k)` is `-t ≤ -k - 1` (Chaki, Gurfinkel and Strichman, [*Decision Diagrams for Linear Arithmetic*](https://ofers.dds.technion.ac.il/publications/fmcad09.pdf), FMCAD 2009, §II-B). The view's alignment is that normalization at one polarity, `<` read as the `<=` of its successor, an equality oriented by its first coefficient. Implication between two constraints, which the same diagrams read to reduce a path, relates two atoms and identifies none: it is [Boolean and bitwise terms meet in a normal form](307-boolean-and-bitwise-terms-meet-in-a-normal-form.md)'s.
- **A reduced ordered decision diagram decides what the table decides.** It is canonical under a fixed order, so two formulas agree exactly where their roots are one node, and applying a connective to diagrams of sizes `m` and `n` takes at most `m · n` steps (Bryant, *Graph-Based Algorithms for Boolean Function Manipulation*, 1986); some functions are exponential in every order, and finding the best order is NP-complete (Bollig and Wegener, 1996). Ordering inputs by a depth-first walk from the outputs is the classic static heuristic and serves tree-like circuits well ([Rice and Kulhari's survey](https://code.garrettmills.dev/Archives/papers-we-love_papers-we-love/raw/commit/d29da5eab4090fefd918e19130e6aad87a766c41/binary_decision_trees/bdd_ordering_heuristics_survey.pdf), after Fujita, Fujisawa and Kawato and Malik et al., 1988); a formula a program writes is such a tree, sharing aside.
- **What each procedure costs, counted at `0fc2fc1bd`.** A model reads both sides into one postorder node list over first-met atoms as `truth.rs` reads them, prices the table as `Formula::evaluation_size` does, and builds one diagram per pair in first-met order, counting apply steps as computed-table misses and nodes as unique-table insertions; its scripts are not checked in.

  | Pair | Atoms | Table | Diagram steps |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Transitivity, five and five (above) | 10 | 39,936 | 102 |
  | Substitution, `c` over eight (above) | 9 | 9,216 | 73 |
  | Transitivity, ten and ten | 20 | 82,837,504 | 302 |
  | Transitivity, a hundred and a hundred | 200 | about 10⁶² | 21,002 |
  | Absorption, `c` over 256 | 257 | about 10⁸⁰ | 33,925 |
  | De Morgan, distribution, a reassociated parity, 256 atoms | 256 | about 10⁸⁰ | 34,692 to 66,821 |

  Over 4,000 random pairs of one to eight atoms and up to 24 nodes a side, the diagram's steps and nodes exceed the table's price in 20, each at one or two atoms and by at most a quarter, 30 units against 24; at eight atoms the diagram costs at most a twentieth of the table. The default budget is 30,000,000 units.
- **What moves a diagram's cost is its order.** Read off the pair as classification reads one, `this` first, a term's operands in the order it holds them, each atom placed where a walk first meets it, the order reads no identity's age and no rank, so no declaration order moves it and [arithmetic 101](101-an-atom-is-ranked-from-its-childrens-ranks.md)'s change to the rank moves nothing here. Operand order and the side a pair is posed from do move it: two sides each a disjunction of twenty pairs `xᵢ && yᵢ`, one of them meeting every `xᵢ` first, cost 7,340,573 steps read from that side and 1,182 from the other (counted, the model above), at one verdict. Every other cost of conversion, classification's among them, follows the walk the same way.
- **Deterministic budgets elsewhere are charged as the work happens.** Lean's heartbeats count small allocations ([`Lean.Util.Heartbeats`](https://lean-lang.org/doc/api/Lean/Util/Heartbeats.html)); F*'s Z3 rlimits count solver operations and are deterministic, and still "renaming an unrelated variable" moves an outcome ([F* wiki](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/FStarLang/FStar/wiki/rlimits:-Machine-Independent-Resource-Limits-for-Deterministic-Execution)), the dependence no Curios verdict may have; MiniSat bounds a search by conflicts and propagations, and CaDiCaL and Kissat schedule by "ticks", an estimate of cache lines touched ([CaDiCaL 3.0](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.SAT.2026.40)). None prices an adaptive search before it starts: only enumeration has a price known before the work, and charging before allocating is kept step by step, as a reducer charges a transition ([A reduction step costs what it builds](../../design/soundness/a-reduction-step-costs-what-it-builds.md)).
- **The answer stays equality or nothing.** A disagreement over independent atoms is no counterexample (`curios-algebra`'s `boolean` module).

## The design

- **A comparison atom is known by its linear view, at a polarity.** The `Bool` carrier's identity is stated once, in `curios-core`'s `atoms` beside the other carriers': a leaf is known by its spelling up to universe instances, and a `Nat` or `Int` comparison by its view aligned (`LinearForm::aligned`) at one of two polarities, the comparison's or its negation's, `L <= 0` against `-L + 1 <= 0` and `L == 0` against `L != 0`, the one leading with a positive coefficient read positive. So `x < y`, `x + 1 <= y` and `Nat/to_int(x) < Nat/to_int(y)` are one atom, and `y <= x`, `y < x + 1` and the reduct of `Bool/not(x < y)` that atom negated. `same_atom` holds two comparisons one where their aligned views agree, a table hands a comparison one handle per aligned view (`Atoms::handle`), so a proposition and its negation are two, and the rung takes one handle per polarized view with the polarity beside it (`Atoms::signed`). Every reader of `Bool` atoms takes it through them, as the decision requires of a carrier's identity: the rung, the leaf-set peel, the commutative pairing, and the complement and idempotence laws in `normalize_bool` and in the fold (`bool_laws`); `dual_comparison` still writes a comparison's negation, which they read up to its view. One view reader keys a pair's atoms, so their handles compare, and the rung keys its atoms in a map, one read a leaf. Which polarity leads follows which monomial leads, an order of ranks; a variable read negated throughout decides and costs what it does read positive, so neither follows the rank.
- **A reduced ordered decision diagram decides every pair.** `curios-algebra`'s `boolean` module builds, for one comparison, each side's diagram over one unique table, in the order the pair's atoms were met, and the two agree where their roots are one node. It reads the formula the rung reads, a graph each node of which is read once, so a web that is a tree of millions and a graph of dozens costs the graph (`curios`'s `tests::unfolding`). Nothing is written back and nothing outlives the comparison. The truth table and `BOOL_ATOM_CAP` leave production; the table stays in the module's tests as the diagram's oracle, the two held to one verdict over generated pairs.
- **Priced step by step in the reduction budget, declining nothing.** The diagram is a machine that reports each step's charge before it takes it, a unit an apply step and the logical size of a node or a memo entry before it is stored, and Core spends each through `Reducer::spend`, as it spent `evaluation_size`; the algebra holds no budget and names no term. The machine keeps its own stack: `curios-algebra` reaches no `recurse`, and a diagram's depth is its pair's atom count. A pair its budget cannot finish exhausts the declaration's budget and is refused as every exhaustion is, and a larger budget decides it. The price is a function of the pair as read, in units no target moves, and a `Category` row names it, so an exhaustion says it was spent deciding two `Bool` terms.
- **No comparison is declined, so none is reported as one.** `past_bool_cap`, `Declined::PastCap` and its `not compared` line retire with the cap; the line for operands nothing pairs stays.
- **The law table declares what the rung decides.** Every family that holds of a `Bool` connective at every value is declared, and a test holds the table to it, but for what declared families give: a commutative operation's dual with its operands swapped, and an idempotent operation's distribution over itself.

**Rejected.**

- **A cap, raised or per side**, for the reasons under *What is wrong*.
- **Declining at a limit scaled to the budget**, for the reason under *What is known*.
- **The table without a cap, priced before its first assignment.** Its price is the only one no order moves and the only one known before the work; but at the default budget it stops near twenty atoms, transitivity at ten atoms a side pricing 82,837,504 units, and it charges a pair whose sides differ in full, so `p(a + b) || C` against `p(b + a) || C` over a conjunction `C` of 24 atoms, accepted today by the commutative pairing once the table declines, would exhaust its budget instead.
- **A satisfiability search over the two sides' difference.** Trusted, a solver is a larger part of the base both checkers trust than a diagram is; checked, its certificate needs the evidence transport [Conversion decides a linear formula by a checked certificate](306-conversion-decides-a-linear-formula-by-a-checked-certificate.md) reserves, for formulas a diagram decides in thousands of steps. Its cost follows its order both ways, a refutation's included.
- **Stålmarck's saturation, priced by its depth.** At a fixed depth its work is polynomial in the formula's length and follows no order (Sheeran and Stålmarck, *A Tutorial on Stålmarck's Proof Procedure for Propositional Logic*, FMCAD 1998), but a pair that does not agree is concluded only at full depth, which is enumeration again.
- **A normal form in the fold**, a diagram written back or algebraic normal form. It needs one order for every term, which is the rank and so the order binders are declared in, and it respells what a guard was keyed on; [A law is decided where it neither respells nor invents](../../design/arithmetic/a-law-is-decided-where-it-neither-respells-nor-invents.md)'s rejection stands, and its claim that a diagram probe-side is the table under the same cap is corrected.
- **An order read off the rank, the best order, or one reordered as the diagram grows.** The first follows declaration order, the second is NP-complete, and sifting is trusted code whose cost no step states.
- **Classing comparisons across operations**, holding `x + 1 <= y` to `x < y` in classification's buckets. It reaches no negation, and the carrier's identity reaches every reader of a `Bool` atom.
- **The table kept below some size.** Two procedures in the trusted base for a saving the count above does not show.

## Not part of it

A `Bool` elimination whose scrutinee the rung decides is a literal stays stuck, so `match b && Bool/not(b) | true => 1 | false => 2 end` meets no arm: [A Boolean elimination reads its scrutinee as conversion does](102-a-boolean-elimination-reads-its-scrutinee-as-conversion-does.md).

## Stages

1. **The carrier's identity.** `LinearForm::polarized` in `curios-algebra`'s `view`; `same_atom`, `Atoms::handle` and `Atoms::signed` stating the identity in `curios-core`'s `atoms`, read through `linear`; the rung keyed in a map. **Check**: every claim under *Atoms known by spelling* closes by `Eq/refl()` through both checkers, as rows of `tests::laws::written`'s `Bool` carrier beside their controls, `x < y && c` against `y <= x && c` still refused; the audit gains every `Bool` law with each variable a comparison spelled one way on a side and another on the other, across the alignment's clauses, `<` against the `<=` of its successor, an equality in either orientation, a sum of naturals against zero, widened naturals, and each negated occurrence a spelling of the dual, at several orders of its binders.
2. **The diagram.** The machine in `curios-algebra`, spent by `truth.rs`; the table moved to the module's tests; the cap, `evaluation_size`, `past_bool_cap` and `Declined::PastCap` retired; the `Category` row. **Check**: both examples under *A cap on the pair* close by `Eq/refl()` through both checkers, the `written` row and the claim in `a_mismatch_nothing_compared_says_so` restated as held; the audit gains each `Bool` law with a variable put to a conjunction of nine and of thirty-two fresh atoms, and two contradictions of five and of sixteen atoms a side chained through `false`; the diagram is held to the table over generated pairs of up to eight atoms; `tests::runaways` states absorption over 256 atoms decided inside a stated budget, and the pairs of twenty read from the side that meets every `xᵢ` first refused at one stated budget and decided at a larger.
3. **The families.** A test in `curios-algebra`'s `law` that evaluates every family of a `Bool` connective at every value and holds `TABLE` to declare exactly those that hold, less what declared families give; the declarations it asks for. **Check**: the generated grid and the audit hold their rows, and a row that proposes no solution for a metavariable is stated so in the audit, with its reason.

Each stage compares `/std`'s image digest before and after, at one path, and names what moved. The figures it may move are each declaration's consumption where a `Bool` pair is compared, the prelude's floor in `DEFAULT_STEP_BUDGET`'s documentation, and `runaway_measurements`.

It retires once the decisions it moves state the design: [A law is decided where it neither respells nor invents](../../design/arithmetic/a-law-is-decided-where-it-neither-respells-nor-invents.md) (agreement "up to eight atoms", the rejected diagram), [A term is one where conversion says so](../../design/arithmetic/a-term-is-one-where-conversion-says-so.md) (the `Bool` carrier's identity, the mismatch line, "the truth table holding two formulas one over two sets of atoms"), [The carriers' algebra stays in conversion](../../design/arithmetic/the-carriers-algebra-stays-in-conversion.md) ("the truth table", `evaluation_size`), the `boolean` module's and `truth.rs`'s documentation, and the mention in [arithmetic 311](311-conversion-decides-parity-and-map-fusion.md).
