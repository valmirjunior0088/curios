# A wait holds nothing

**Adopted by the user on 2026-11-22 and refined the same day; its next step is a ready delivery.** At every wait, everything a branch has taken is either answered already or held by an owner that outlives the branch — a handle's queue, a box, a channel — so a branch cancelled where it waits loses nothing and tears nothing. Every move between owners happens in one step that cannot wait, and a step that cannot go ahead waits before it takes anything. [A wait is a suspension, and only the host waits](../../design/standard-library/a-wait-is-a-suspension-and-only-the-host-waits.md) states the rule for a take, a read, a join and a sleep ("an attempt takes nothing unless it answers") and for what outlives a wait ("kept in a `/std/Box`"); this specification makes it hold of every operation of `/std`, so cancellation stays structural, `select` stays a driver that steps its branches, and `timeout` stays a bound on any wait.

## What is wrong today

- **A write waits holding the rest of its message.** `write_waiting` (`curios-prelude-build/std/internal/Handle.crs`) sends what the host accepts and, after a short write, waits with the rest, as `handle_write`'s row allows (`curios-abi/src/host/ops.rs`: "a handle may take only a prefix, and the caller resends the tail"). A `select` or `timeout` that drops it there leaves the peer holding part of a message, and the next write follows the cut bytes as though nothing was cut: at `2efe3b57a`, a 1 MiB write to standard output bounded by a 10 ms `timeout`, then a write of `END`, reaches a reader that waits a second as the first 65,536 bytes and then `END`. In `/std`, `http/Server` writes `100 Continue` inside the request's timeout and a `408` after it, and `Tui/Session/draw` keeps a frame as shown before a write a `select` can cut.
- **`Io/read_all` waits holding what it has read** (`curios-prelude-build/std/Io.crs`), so a cancel drops bytes taken from a stream its caller still holds: at `2efe3b57a`, a `read_all` of a child's output bounded by a `timeout` that passes between its `abc` and its `def`, then run again on the same pipe, answers `def`. `tcp/Socket/call` reads its caller's socket through it. `Io/Reader/rest` is the same read with what it has read kept in the reader's box: the same program through it answers `abcdef`.
- **`copy` hands a chunk it has read to a write that can wait before the chunk is sent**, which the first item settles: once a write takes its whole message before it waits, the chunk is never held in `copy`'s hands.

## What is decided

- **The outbound queue is the host's.** The host already keeps a TLS stream's records and hands them on by `handle_flush`; a queue a program kept in a box would need a box beside every handle, and `/std/Proc/stdout` is a constant no box hangs off. It costs no row: `handle_write` takes the whole message or fails, with no count to report, and `handle_flush` hands on what any handle's queue holds, answering `WouldBlock` until it is empty, as it does today of a TLS stream alone.
- **A write never waits before it takes.** The row takes the whole message whatever the queue holds, and the write then waits until the queue is empty. Cancelled in that wait, its message stays queued whole, and the next write, flush or finish on the handle hands it on first; close discards it, as close already discards what a serial port has not sent. The queue holds at most one message for each write cancelled while it drained, and a reservation the host cannot make answers the write a failure; a bound that made a write wait before it takes would leave `copy` holding a chunk at that wait.
- **A failure while a queue drains** is answered by the row that meets it, and the queue is dropped: the write's own wait, or, where a cancelled write left the queue, the next write, flush or finish on the handle.
- **A finish hands the queue on first**, answering `WouldBlock` until it is empty, as a TLS stream's closure alert already waits behind its records.
- **`Io/read_all` is withdrawn**: `Io/Reader/rest` is the read to a stream's end that a cancel leaves whole.
- **`Box/held` stays**, an observation that holds until the branch waits, as a channel's length does.

## What is known

- **A step between two waits is atomic already.** A program's branches run one at a time, so a step that cannot wait is a transaction with no log to keep, and `/std/internal/until`, the loop every wait on the program's own state goes through, runs an attempt that answers or declines naming what it waits on.
- **Commit follows from the rule rather than from a protocol.** A move never spans a wait, so a branch `select` drops has made whole moves or none, Concurrent ML's guarantee at the grain of a move: a branch cancelled between two writes has sent the first whole and none of the second, an action unfinished and no effect torn, as an event's post-commit `wrap` leaves one. Committing a choice at a branch's first effect was weighed on 2026-11-22 and set aside: it turns `select` from the first to finish into the first to act and leaves `timeout` unable to bound a begun write, which the decision's rejection of socket timeouts keeps.
- **The decision's other choices stand on the rule**, and two of its sentences change with it: "every write is one attempt that reports how much it accepted" becomes a write taken whole, since no caller reads the count but the resend loop this retires and a failure's prefix was never reported; and `select`'s statement of what an operation loses where it is cancelled becomes a guarantee that `/std`'s lose nothing. Readiness stays the rule for reads: a queued message is the host's to hand on, not a read in flight to cancel or keep.
- **A snapshot is a program's own.** A value read out of a box and written back after a wait loses what another branch changed meanwhile, through `Box/held` or through a `Box/change` that answers what it holds — at `2efe3b57a`, two branches that each bump a box from a snapshot taken before a `yield` leave it at 1 either way — as the decision's rejection of a box set by value states, and as Haskell's STM loses it between two `atomically` blocks ([Harris, Marlow, Peyton Jones and Herlihy, Composable Memory Transactions](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/composable-memory-transactions/)). A public attempt over several boxes and channels, with `Box/held` withdrawn, was weighed on 2026-11-22 and set aside: withdrawing `held` removes a spelling and not the loss, and an attempt a program writes is the offer whose contract the decision's rejection of a public offer form finds held by prose alone, which nothing in the tree consumes.
- **Peers.** Node.js's `write` takes every chunk into the stream's buffer, its return only advisory ([nodejs/node@238466bcf0](https://github.com/nodejs/node/commit/238466bcf0)); Erlang hands a message whole to its port's queue and holds the sender before it while the port is busy ([port signals](https://erlang.org/doc/apps/erts/portsignals)), and closes a socket whose send timed out, its stream cut ([`send_timeout_close`](https://erlang.org/pipermail/erlang-questions/2013-February/072355.html)); tokio's `write_all` leaves a buffer partly written where `select!` drops it, and `write_all_buf` advances the caller's buffer, an owner outside the choice ([`AsyncWriteExt`](https://docs.rs/tokio/latest/tokio/io/trait.AsyncWriteExt.html)); [Trio](https://trio.readthedocs.io/en/stable/reference-io.html#trio.abc.SendStream.send_all) declares a stream cut mid-`send_all` broken, the state this rule keeps from arising; Eio's `Buf_write.with_flow` copies to its flow from a fiber of its own ([`Buf_write`](https://ocaml.org/p/eio/1.1/doc/Eio/Buf_write/index.html)). Curios takes the whole-message queue of the first two, its write waiting only after it has taken, and drains the queue in the next row on the handle rather than in work the host starts on its own; marking a cut write's half broken and shielding a region from cancellation were weighed on 2026-11-22 and set aside.

## Stages

**1. A write takes its whole message.** `handle_write`'s contract and reply, the native host's queue for every handle it writes, the standard streams and TLS among them, the scripted host, the test runner's relay and the browser harness; `/std`'s write as one take and one wait, its flush, its finish and `Serial`'s drain. Check: under the native host, a `timeout` around a 1 MiB write to a child that reads after a pause answers `none`, and the child reads the whole message and then the next write's; a `copy` bounded the same way, then run again, moves every byte once; a drain that fails is answered by the next write.

**2. `read_all` withdrawn.** Its callers in `/std`, in the tests and in `documentation/language.md` read through `Io/Reader/rest`, or, where the stream is theirs alone, as `File/read_all` and `Proc/run` hold theirs, through one private loop. Check: a `Reader/rest` bounded by a `timeout` between two arrivals, then run again, answers both.

## Verification and retirement

Each stage's check, then `cargo xtask workspace lint` and the suite. Once both land, the decision states the rule for every operation of `/std` and the write row's queue, `select`'s cancel-safety paragraph in `Io.crs` becomes a guarantee, `Box/held` is documented as the observation it is, and this file is deleted.
