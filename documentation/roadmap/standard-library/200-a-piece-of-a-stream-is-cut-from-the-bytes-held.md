# A piece of a stream is cut from the bytes held

Not refined yet. `/std` reads a line off a stream one byte at a time, a host call each, because a stream is a handle that holds nothing and a line is offered as a function of the bare stream: `/std/read()` takes about five seconds a megabyte. `/std/http` and `/std/Tui/Session` read the other way, each cutting what it wants from bytes it holds and handing the rest on. This spec makes that the one way a piece is read off any stream, and removes the reads that take a byte at a time. Its shape is settled below; it waits on which reads `/std` names and under what names, and on its stages. [A program reads text and may fail](201-a-program-reads-text-and-may-fail.md) takes its first step on what this leaves.

## What is missing

**Three reads take a byte at a time.** `Io/read_line` (`curios-std/src/Io.crs`), `Async/read_line` and `Async/read_until` (`curios-std/src/Async.crs`) each ask `read(s, 1)`, append the byte to what they hold, `x[..acc, ..b]`, and ask again until the byte is their delimiter. Each doc gives the reason, `Io/read_line`'s as "It reads a byte at a time, so it consumes nothing past the newline". A stream has nowhere to keep more: `Io/Input` is a struct over a `Handle`, `Async/Read(M, S)` is `read(s, n)` answering a chunk of one byte to `n`, and a function of the bare stream that took a chunk would drop what the chunk holds past the delimiter. The two of `Async` are written over any `Read(M, S)`, which nine witnesses satisfy: `Io/Input`, `Handle` and `File` at `Io` and at `Async`, and `Serial`, `Command/Pipe` and `tcp/Socket` at `Async`. So the rule is every stream's, a socket's and a pipe's with standard input's. They are the three reads of one byte in `curios-std/src/`.

**A byte is a host call.** For standard input each read is a `poll` and a `read` of the process's descriptor (`handle_read`, `curios-runtime/src/os_host.rs`). `/std/read()` is `Io/read_line(Io/stdin)` (`curios-std/src/lib.crs`), and it is how the 24 programs of `programs/`, each once, and nine files of `curios`'s tests read their input. A program that reads two lines through `/std/read()` and prints their lengths, compiled with `curios compile`, takes 0.40 to 0.41 s over a first line of 100,000 letters and 5.69 to 10.02 s over one of 1,000,000, the second line being `second`. A program of thirty lines over the same input holds its bytes: it cuts a line at the first newline of what it holds (`Bytes/index_of`, `Bytes/slice`, `Bytes/drop`, the bound decided by `Nat/lt/try`), and where there is none asks `Io/read(Io/stdin, 65536)`, cuts the chunk that arrives, appends what precedes the newline to what it held and answers the bytes after it beside the line. It takes under 0.01 s and 0.02 to 0.08 s over those two inputs, 0.26 to 0.41 s over a first line of 8,000,000 letters, and 0.11 s, one reading, over the 1,000,000 fed through a pipe in writes of 1,024 bytes; its second line is read from the bytes held past the first. Timed on top of `d211e3af7` through the release compiler, three readings each on a machine in use, so what they show is the ratio.

**Two readers of `/std` hold their bytes already.** `/std/http` reads a message [through pieces that hold no stream](../../../curios-std/README.md#an-http-message-is-read-through-one-framing-core-and-made-as-one-a-wire-can-carry): a piece is a function of the bytes held that answers the piece and the bytes after it, a refusal, or that more is wanted (`Step`, `curios-std/src/http/read.crs`), and `fetch` (`curios-std/src/http/stream.crs`) asks it again each time a socket yields more, 65,536 bytes a read. `Tui/Session` (`curios-std/src/Tui/Session.crs`) keeps the bytes a read left undecoded in its `rest` and answers the session beside its events. Each is written for its own stream, `fetch` over a `Socket` in `Async` and the session over `Io/stdin`. No reader of `/std` cuts a line from the bytes held off any stream.

**What reading no further was for.** A read that takes nothing past its delimiter leaves the descriptor where the line ends, and the program that needs that shares its input: `Command/new` hands a child every stream by `Stdio/inherit()` (`curios-std/src/Command.crs`), and a `Tui/Session` reads `Io/stdin` itself. That is read off the code: no test holds a child to what its parent left unread. What the tests hold is a sequence of reads off one stream, `lines_read_across_the_chunks_a_stream_arrives_in` (`curios/src/tests/host/stream_tests.rs`) and a serial port's reply line (`curios/src/tests/host/serial_tests.rs`), which a reader that hands on the bytes it held answers alike.

**A line read under a deadline loses what it read.** `Async/timeout` spawns its body and cancels it where the deadline passes first, and `first`'s doc (`curios-std/src/Async.crs`) states what that costs: "A task abandoned part-way loses whatever it had taken and not yet answered with". A line read holds the bytes it has taken inside its body. A program that reads a line by `Async/timeout(Duration/of_millis(100), Async/read_line(@Async, Io/stdin))` and then a line with no deadline, fed `ab` through a pipe and `cd` with a newline 0.4 s later, answers no line within the deadline and `cd` after it: the `ab` is gone, with nothing said. A second program holds its bytes outside the body and puts the deadline on one `Async/Read/read(Io/stdin, 65536)` at a time: it answers no line, holding two bytes, and `abcd` after. Probed on top of `d211e3af7` through the release compiler, three runs each.

## Prior art

- **Reading no further is a shell's rule.** POSIX asks it of `sh` and of nothing else: where the shell reads its commands from standard input and runs one that reads it too, "the shell shall ensure that the standard input file pointer points directly after the command it has read", and it does not read ahead of what the command is to read ([`sh`, STDIN](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9699919799/utilities/sh.html)).
- **Rust's standard input is one buffer a process.** "Each handle is a shared reference to a global buffer of input data to this process" ([`std::io::Stdin`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/io/struct.Stdin.html)), and a line is read through it.
- **Go's bare reader has no line, and the reader that has one holds bytes.** `bufio` "wraps an io.Reader or io.Writer object", and of its `Scanner`, "the reader may have advanced arbitrarily far past the last token" ([`bufio`](https://pkg.go.dev/bufio)).
- **Rust's buffered reader is two operations and one holder.** `BufRead` asks `fn fill_buf(&mut self) -> Result<&[u8], Error>` and `fn consume(&mut self, amount: usize)`, the bytes that have arrived and how many of them were used, and `read_line` is provided over the two; `&mut self` holds a reader to one user at a time ([`std::io::BufRead`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/io/trait.BufRead.html)).
- **A state carried by a monad does not pass through control.** Haskell's `unliftio` gives no instance for "Transformers with some monadic state (e.g., `StateT`, `WriterT`)": "Trying to unlift actions in stateful monads leads to unpredictable behavior", its example that "the `finally` operation will discard mutated state coming from the cleanup action" ([`unliftio`](https://github.com/fpco/unliftio)).

## Decisions

- **No read takes a byte at a time.** `Io/read_line`, `Async/read_line` and `Async/read_until` are removed, and no line read that reads no further is kept under another name. A program that must leave its descriptor where a line ends asks `read(s, 1)` itself.
- **`/std/read()` is removed.** It takes no argument, so it hands nothing on from one call to the next. The 24 programs of `programs/` and the nine test files read through the reader over `Io/stdin`.
- **The bytes held are handed on.** A reader is a value, the stream with the bytes held, and it has two operations, as `BufRead` has: a cut, a function of the bytes held that runs nothing, and an arrival, one `read` of the stream appended to them, which is its one effect. A read of a piece is the loop over the two that `fetch` is, answering the piece with the reader after it. The bytes held are outside the body a deadline abandons only where the caller holds them, which is the second program above.
- **A piece is a `Step`, and one loop asks it.** `http/read`'s `Step` is the general type, its refusal a parameter: `more(St, Bytes)`, where to resume and the bytes kept, so no byte is looked at twice, `took(A, Bytes)`, the piece and the bytes after it, or `refused(R)`. `fetch` is the general loop, over any `Read(M, S)`, and `/std/http` keeps its pieces as they are written and loses a loop of its own. Its decision's reason against two decoders is the reason against two loops, and its rejection of a message parsed again from its start at each arrival is what the state is for: a piece that is a function of the bytes alone has nowhere to keep how far it searched. A refusal is no more HTTP's than the state is, a line past a bound being one. Written as a program, one `take` over `Step(St, R, A)` and `Async/Read(M, S)`, with a line as a piece whose state is how far it searched and which refuses past a limit, counts 1,000,000 lines of seven letters in 0.36 s at `Io` and 0.52 s in a fiber, where a loop written for the one stream and the one piece takes 0.25 s: medians of five readings each, interleaved, 0.36 to 0.37 s, 0.51 to 0.52 s and 0.24 to 0.34 s. Over one line of 1,000,000 letters fed through a pipe in writes of 1,024 bytes it takes 0.03 s, and 0.16 s with the same piece searching from the start at each arrival, three readings each, a gap that grows with the square of the line. Timed on top of `d211e3af7` on a machine in use. The type is named at sixteen places, eight in `http/read.crs`, one in `http/stream.crs` and seven in `curios-std/src/tests/http/cuts.crs`, and `fetch` is called at three. Weighed: `/std/http`'s decision rejects "one driver generic over the monad it reads in, a socket in `Async` and a list of segments in `State`, where the read of a list fails in its type and never in fact". This loop is generic over the monad of a stream whose read fails in fact, a file's and standard input's at `Io` and a socket's at `Async`, as `Async/read_all` is; a message held entire keeps `whole`, which asks its pieces of no stream.
- **The loop drops nothing.** It answers the piece, a refusal, that the stream ended or that it failed, and beside each the reader as it then stands, with where the piece stood for the last two. A failure is not always a stream's last word, `Io/Error` having `would_block`, "waiting for it is the caller's to do", so a loop that kept the bytes held from its caller at a failure would lose them at a second try. The three reads differ today: `Io/read_line` reads a failure as the end and keeps what it read as the line, `Async/read_line` answers the failure and drops what it read, and `fetch`'s `Stop` hands back the bytes held where the peer closed.
- **The end of a stream is its owner's to read, and no piece's.** `/std/http`'s decision has each owner decide "what a stream that ended means" and rejects a primitive that marks the end of its input. Each read `/std` names is an owner and states its rule once: a line where the stream ends is what is held, and none where nothing is, as both line reads answer today; a stated count the stream ends short of is answered as short, with what arrived, as the client's `incomplete` is; and bytes up to a delimiter say whether the delimiter was met, which `Async/read_until` does not. The program above reads `ab`, a newline and `cd` as two lines, and no input as none, at `Io` and in a fiber.
- **A read `/std` names is a `Try`.** It is declared `Try(M, Io/Error, A)`, as [a fallible operation](../../design/standard-library/a-fallible-operation-returns-try-and-bang-lifts-along-declared-edges.md) is, so `!` carries its failure and the reader after it is not answered: a program that wants what was held at a failure asks the loop.
- **A piece states its bound.** It refuses past it, as `head` and `length` do under `http/Limits`.

What the tree's rules already fix:

- **The bytes held are a value.** [Only a fiber waits](../../design/standard-library/only-a-fiber-waits.md) rejects "a general rewritable cell beside the write-once one", so no stream keeps what it read in a cell: a read answers what it held and its caller hands it on, as `fetch` and `Tui/Session` do.
- **What a deadline may abandon holds no bytes.** `first`'s doc gives the way to choose without paying for an abandoned task: "a selection over `Offer`s takes nothing until it keeps it". So the bytes held stand outside any body `timeout` or `race` cancels, and what a deadline is put on is one arrival: one `read` of the stream, which has taken nothing while it is parked.
- **No host row.** The host keeps no buffer for standard input, and `handle_read` says why: "a request smaller than what arrived would leave the remainder in a buffer `handle_poll` cannot see, and a fiber waiting on fd 0 would stall with input already inside the process". A piece is cut by the program from what `read` answers, which the program above does with nothing `/std` lacks.
- **`/std/http`'s pieces stand.** Its decision is its crate's and none of it is restated: what is general is the loop that asks a piece again as a stream yields, which `fetch` is at a socket.

## Refinement

- **How the bytes are handed on under one name.** `let held = f(held)` is refused, `held` mentions itself and states no type, a binding that names itself being a recursion, and so is `let (found, held) = line(held)!;`, a recursive binding being a plain name. A pattern rebinds the name, in a `match` arm or in `let got = line(held)!; let (found, held) = got;`, and a loop hands the next reader to its own call. Written with a new name at each step, an older reader stays in scope, and read again after a later one took an arrival it answers bytes out of their order: nothing in the language holds a value to one use, as `&mut` does. What most programs want, every line of a stream in turn, can be a loop of `/std`'s in which no reader shows; whether `/std` offers it as a fold, and what it is named, is not settled.
- **Whether an arrival is an `Offer`.** A stream's read is an `Async` action today, and `timeout` spawns it. As an offer it would be chosen beside a timer by `select`, which takes nothing until it keeps it.
- **Which reads `/std` names.** What a program asks for is a line, with the carriage return `Async/read_line` drops before a newline, the bytes before a delimiter, a stated count, and what is left. Which of them `/std` names, under what names and in which module, is not settled.
- **What a line is bounded at.** A line with no newline grows what is held without bound, as `acc` does in the three reads today. The bound the line `/std` names refuses past, and whether a program states it, is not settled.
- **What a search and an append cost.** `Bytes/index_of` is a fold over every byte of the run with a closure a byte, and walks on past its first hit (`find_index`, `curios-std/src/Bytes.crs`); the programs that count lines search by a recursion that stops at the newline. Each appends an arrival to what it holds, `x[..kept, ..bytes]`. Not read: what appending to a run costs as the run grows, and whether a piece's search wants a primitive of its own. A chunk's size is stated three ways today: `drain` asks 4,096, `fetch` 65,536 and `Tui/Session` 1,024.
- **Stages, once those are read**: the reader and its pieces over any `Read(M, S)`, held to the mock host's chunks at every cut of an input, as `/std/http`'s tests hold a message; `/std/read()` removed, with `programs/` and the tests that read through it moved to the reader, and `programs/README.md` and `xbench/README.md` naming what moved; the three reads removed with their callers, the two tests above rewritten over the reader; then the decision written in `curios-std/README.md` beside HTTP's, with `fetch` and `Tui/Session` read against it. Check at the last: no read of one byte is left in `curios-std/src/`, and a line of 1,000,000 letters is read in the time `Str/of_bytes` takes over it, timed on a quiet machine.

## Rejected

- **A reading region**, a monad over the stream's own whose state is the bytes held, so a line is read with `!` and no bytes are named: a struct over `(Bytes) -> M({A, Bytes})` with its `Monad` witness and a `Monad/Lift` edge from `M`, written at `Io` and at `Async`. A program declares it, and `!` lifts an action of the base along the edge. Counting 1,000,000 lines of seven letters it takes 0.29 s where the handed-on form takes 0.23 s: timed on top of `d211e3af7`, the median of five readings each with the two interleaved, 0.28 to 0.29 s and 0.22 to 0.23 s, on a machine in use; a loop of `/std/read()` takes 1.89 s over 100,000 of the lines, one reading. But `/std`'s control takes an action of a fixed monad, `Async/timeout`, `spawn`, `race` and `using` an `Async` and `File/with` and `Tty/with_raw` a `Try`, and a region handed to one is refused: `no witness of Monad/Lift(Reading, Async) found`, a lift going upward alone ([A fallible operation returns `Try`, and `!` lifts along declared edges](../../design/standard-library/a-fallible-operation-returns-try-and-bang-lifts-along-declared-edges.md)). Run down to its base first, the region's state is the body's, to be lost where the body is abandoned. Each operation of control would want a twin that knows the reader.
- **A buffer behind the handle**, the host holding the bytes outside every fiber. It is what Rust's `Stdin` is, and a deadline loses nothing of it. It adds rows that answer a line, each written for the native host and the JavaScript host (`.claude/rules/host.md`) and for the mock the tests read through; the host never waits on a peer, a write being one attempt and a read answering what has arrived ([Only a fiber waits](../../design/standard-library/only-a-fiber-waits.md)), so the loop that gathers a line is `/std`'s; and a cut the host makes is judged by no kernel and held to no test at every cut of its input, as `http/read`'s are. A program could not take the bytes held either, to hand a stream on with what was read past a piece.
- **A chain of write-once cells**, one an arrival, so that a reader read twice answers alike. [Only a fiber waits](../../design/standard-library/only-a-fiber-waits.md) gives the reason against a queue of them, that it "retains consumed chains through old ends": here through any older reader a program still holds.
