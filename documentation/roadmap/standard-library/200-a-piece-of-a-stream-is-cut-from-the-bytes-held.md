# A piece of a stream is cut from the bytes held

Working specification for how `/std` reads a piece off a stream. `/std` reads a line one byte at a time, a host call each, because a stream is a handle that holds nothing and a line is offered as a function of the bare stream: `/std/read()` takes about five seconds a megabyte. `/std/http` and `/std/Tui/Session` read the other way, each cutting what it wants from bytes it holds and handing the rest on. This spec gives a stream a reader, `/std/Io/Reader`, which holds beside the stream the bytes read past a piece, makes it the one way a piece is read off any stream, and removes the reads that take a byte at a time. [A program reads text and may fail](201-a-program-reads-text-and-may-fail.md) takes its first step on what this leaves.

## What is missing

**Three reads take a byte at a time.** `Io/read_line` (`curios-std/src/Io.crs`), `Async/read_line` and `Async/read_until` (`curios-std/src/Async.crs`) each ask `read(s, 1)`, append the byte to what they hold, `x[..acc, ..b]`, and ask again until the byte is their delimiter. Each doc gives the reason, `Io/read_line`'s as "It reads a byte at a time, so it consumes nothing past the newline". A stream has nowhere to keep more: `Io/Input` is a struct over a `Handle`, `Async/Read(M, S)` is `read(s, n)` answering a chunk of one byte to `n`, and a function of the bare stream that took a chunk would drop what the chunk holds past the delimiter. The two of `Async` are written over any `Read(M, S)`, which nine witnesses satisfy: `Io/Input`, `Handle` and `File` at `Io` and at `Async`, and `Serial`, `Command/Pipe` and `tcp/Socket` at `Async`. So the rule is every stream's, a socket's and a pipe's with standard input's. They are the three reads of one byte in `curios-std/src/`.

**A byte is a host call.** For standard input each read is a `poll` and a `read` of the process's descriptor (`handle_read`, `curios-runtime/src/os_host.rs`). `/std/read()` is `Io/read_line(Io/stdin)` (`curios-std/src/lib.crs`), and it is how the 24 programs of `programs/`, each once, and nine files of `curios`'s tests read their input. A program that reads two lines through `/std/read()` and prints their lengths, compiled with `curios compile`, takes 0.40 to 0.41 s over a first line of 100,000 letters and 5.69 to 10.02 s over one of 1,000,000, the second line being `second`. A program of thirty lines over the same input holds its bytes: it cuts a line at the first newline of what it holds (`Bytes/index_of`, `Bytes/slice`, `Bytes/drop`, the bound decided by `Nat/lt/try`), and where there is none asks `Io/read(Io/stdin, 65536)`, cuts the chunk that arrives, appends what precedes the newline to what it held and answers the bytes after it beside the line. It takes under 0.01 s and 0.02 to 0.08 s over those two inputs, 0.26 to 0.41 s over a first line of 8,000,000 letters, and 0.11 s, one reading, over the 1,000,000 fed through a pipe in writes of 1,024 bytes; its second line is read from the bytes held past the first. Timed on top of `d211e3af7` through the release compiler, three readings each on a machine in use, so what they show is the ratio.

**Two readers of `/std` hold their bytes already.** `/std/http` reads a message [through pieces that hold no stream](../../../curios-std/README.md#an-http-message-is-read-through-one-framing-core-and-made-as-one-a-wire-can-carry): a piece is a function of the bytes held that answers the piece and the bytes after it, a refusal, or that more is wanted (`Step`, `curios-std/src/http/read.crs`), and `fetch` (`curios-std/src/http/stream.crs`) asks it again each time a socket yields more, 65,536 bytes a read. `Tui/Session` (`curios-std/src/Tui/Session.crs`) keeps the bytes a read left undecoded in its `rest` and answers the session beside its events. Each is written for its own stream, `fetch` over a `Socket` in `Async` and the session over `Io/stdin`. No reader of `/std` cuts a line from the bytes held off any stream.

**What reading no further was for.** A read that takes nothing past its delimiter leaves the descriptor where the line ends, and the program that needs that shares its input: `Command/new` hands a child every stream by `Stdio/inherit()` (`curios-std/src/Command.crs`), and a `Tui/Session` reads `Io/stdin` itself. That is read off the code: no test holds a child to what its parent left unread. What the tests hold is a sequence of reads off one stream, `lines_read_across_the_chunks_a_stream_arrives_in` (`curios/src/tests/host/stream_tests.rs`) and a serial port's reply line (`curios/src/tests/host/serial_tests.rs`), which a reader that holds its bytes answers alike.

**A line read under a deadline loses what it read.** `Async/timeout` spawns its body and cancels it where the deadline passes first, and `first`'s doc (`curios-std/src/Async.crs`) states what that costs: "A task abandoned part-way loses whatever it had taken and not yet answered with". A line read holds the bytes it has taken inside its body. A program that reads a line by `Async/timeout(Duration/of_millis(100), Async/read_line(@Async, Io/stdin))` and then a line with no deadline, fed `ab` through a pipe and `cd` with a newline 0.4 s later, answers no line within the deadline and `cd` after it: the `ab` is gone, with nothing said. A second program holds its bytes outside the body and puts the deadline on one `Async/Read/read(Io/stdin, 65536)` at a time: it answers no line, holding two bytes, and `abcd` after. Probed on top of `d211e3af7` through the release compiler, three runs each.

## Prior art

- **Reading no further is a shell's rule.** POSIX asks it of `sh` and of nothing else: where the shell reads its commands from standard input and runs one that reads it too, "the shell shall ensure that the standard input file pointer points directly after the command it has read", and it does not read ahead of what the command is to read ([`sh`, STDIN](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9699919799/utilities/sh.html)).
- **Rust's standard input is one buffer a process.** "Each handle is a shared reference to a global buffer of input data to this process" ([`std::io::Stdin`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/io/struct.Stdin.html)), and a line is read through it.
- **Go's bare reader has no line, and the reader that has one holds bytes.** `bufio` "wraps an io.Reader or io.Writer object", and of its `Scanner`, "the reader may have advanced arbitrarily far past the last token" ([`bufio`](https://pkg.go.dev/bufio)).
- **Rust's buffered reader is two operations and one holder.** `BufRead` asks `fn fill_buf(&mut self) -> Result<&[u8], Error>` and `fn consume(&mut self, amount: usize)`, the bytes that have arrived and how many of them were used, and `read_line` is provided over the two; `&mut self` holds a reader to one user at a time ([`std::io::BufRead`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/io/trait.BufRead.html)).
- **A state carried by a monad does not pass through control.** Haskell's `unliftio` gives no instance for "Transformers with some monadic state (e.g., `StateT`, `WriterT`)": "Trying to unlift actions in stateful monads leads to unpredictable behavior", its example that "the `finally` operation will discard mutated state coming from the cleanup action" ([`unliftio`](https://github.com/fpco/unliftio)).
- **A read that keeps its bytes in its own body loses them to a deadline, and one that keeps them in the reader does not.** Tokio says of `read_line`, "This method is not cancel safe. If the method is used as a branch in `tokio::select!` and another branch completes first, then some data may have been partially read, and this data is lost", of `fill_buf` that "it is guaranteed that no data was read", and gives the stream `lines` returns "a cancellation safe `next_line` method" ([`AsyncBufReadExt`](https://docs.rs/tokio/latest/tokio/io/trait.AsyncBufReadExt.html)).
- **A runtime with no linear types keeps the bytes behind an identity.** GHC's `Handle` is `FileHandle FilePath !(MVar Handle__)`, its bytes in `haByteBuffer :: !(IORef (Buffer Word8))` ([`GHC.IO.Handle.Types`](https://hackage.haskell.org/package/base-4.22.0.0/docs/GHC-IO-Handle-Types.html)). Lean 4's `IO.FS.Stream` has `getLine : IO String` and answers no stream ([Files, file handles and streams](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/IO/Files___-File-Handles___-and-Streams/)). Eio's `of_flow ~max_size flow` "is a buffered reader backed by `flow`" ([`Eio.Buf_read`](https://ocaml-multicore.github.io/eio/eio/Eio/Buf_read/index.html)). Of Zig's reader, "the buffer is in the interface, not the implementation" ([0.15.1 release notes](https://ziglang.org/download/0.15.1/release-notes.html)). Erlang's I/O server "is a process that handles the requests", and a `get_until` callback's continuation "is sent as the state in later calls to the function when more characters are available" ([The Erlang I/O protocol](https://www.erlang.org/doc/apps/stdlib/io_protocol.html)).
- **A reader handed on as a value is held to one use by its type.** linear-base's `hGetLine :: Handle %1 -> RIO (Ur Text, Handle)` takes its handle once and answers the next ([`System.IO.Resource.Linear`](https://hackage.haskell.org/package/linear-base-0.8.1/docs/System-IO-Resource-Linear.html)).
- **A piece is a function of the bytes held.** Go's `type SplitFunc func(data []byte, atEOF bool) (advance int, token []byte, err error)` is asked again as its buffer fills, and a scan stops at "a token too large to fit in the Scanner.Buffer" ([`bufio`](https://pkg.go.dev/bufio)).
- **A reader states a bound, and a refusal keeps the bytes.** Eio's `max_size` is "the maximum size to which the buffer may grow". Where asyncio's `readuntil` passes its limit, 64 KiB by default, "a `LimitOverrunError` exception is raised, and the data is left in the internal buffer and can be read again" ([Streams](https://docs.python.org/3/library/asyncio-stream.html)). Rust's `read_until` states no bound and warns that "it is possible for an attacker to continuously send bytes without ever sending the delimiter or EOF".

## Decisions

Each settled on 2026-10-13.

- **No read takes a byte at a time.** `Io/read_line`, `Async/read_line` and `Async/read_until` are removed, and no line read that reads no further is kept under another name. A program that must leave its descriptor where a line ends asks `read(s, 1)` itself.
- **`/std/read()` is removed.** It takes no argument, so it names no reader, and a reader of the process's own would be [a value every program runs at its start](../tools/203-a-value-every-program-runs-at-its-start-is-reported.md). The 24 programs of `programs/` and the nine test files read through a reader over `Io/stdin`.
- **A stream's reader holds its bytes.** `Io/Reader(S)` is a stream with the bytes read ahead of what was asked of it, kept in the guest heap in one slot beside the stream: a channel of capacity one, as `/std/Async/Signal` keeps its flag in. A read answers its piece and no reader, the reader it was asked of being the reader after it. The loop takes the bytes from the slot, asks its piece, and puts back what is left before every read of the stream, with no park between a take and a put; a fiber is cancelled only where the scheduler dequeues it and an `Io` step never yields, so what a deadline abandons has put its bytes back. A stream has an identity already, its descriptor's position, and the bytes read ahead are part of that position: held as a value beside it, the two can disagree. Probed at `aaeb42b28` through the release compiler, three runs each and alike. A program that reads a line under `Async/timeout(Duration/of_millis(100), …)` and then a line with no deadline, fed `ab` through a pipe and `cd` with a newline 0.4 s later, answers no line and then `abcd` where the bytes stand in the slot, and no line and then `cd` where the loop hands them on as a value. A program that hands them on and reads the reader it began with twice, fed `ab` and `cd` each with a newline and `ef` with a newline 0.3 s later, answers `ab` and then `ef`. The slot costs a take and a put a read: counting 1,000,000 lines of seven letters in reads of 65,536 bytes takes 0.355 s through it and 0.306 s handed on, medians of five readings each, interleaved, 0.348 to 0.362 s and 0.300 to 0.319 s, timed at `aaeb42b28` on a machine in use.
- **A piece is a `Step`, and one loop asks it.** `http/read`'s `Step` is the general type, `Io/Reader/Step(St, R, A)`, its refusal a parameter: `more(St, Bytes)`, where to resume and the bytes kept, so no byte is looked at twice, `took(A, Bytes)`, the piece and the bytes after it, or `refused(R)`. The loop is `Io/Reader/take`, over any monad `Io` embeds in and any stream read in it, `use Monad/Lift(Io, M)` and `use Async/Read(M, S)`, and `/std/http` keeps its pieces as they are written and loses a loop of its own. Its decision's reason against two decoders is the reason against two loops, and its rejection of a message parsed again from its start at each arrival is what the state is for: a piece that is a function of the bytes alone has nowhere to keep how far it searched. Over one line of 1,000,000 letters fed through a pipe in writes of 1,024 bytes a loop that resumes its search takes 0.03 s, and 0.16 s with the same piece searching from the start at each arrival, three readings each, timed on top of `d211e3af7` on a machine in use: a gap that grows with the square of the line. A refusal is no more HTTP's than the state is, a line past a bound being one. One loop written over `M` reads a line at `Io` and the next in a fiber through one reader, probed at `aaeb42b28`; in a region whose monad is a variable an `Io` step is embedded by `Monad/lift`, written. The type is named at sixteen places, eight in `http/read.crs`, one in `http/stream.crs` and seven in `curios-std/src/tests/http/cuts.crs`, and `fetch` is called at three. Weighed: `/std/http`'s decision rejects "one driver generic over the monad it reads in, a socket in `Async` and a list of segments in `State`, where the read of a list fails in its type and never in fact". This loop is generic over the monad of a stream whose read fails in fact, a file's and standard input's at `Io` and a socket's at `Async`, as `Async/read_all` is; a message held entire keeps `whole`, which asks its pieces of no stream.
- **The loop drops nothing.** It answers the piece, a refusal, that the stream ended or that it failed (`Io/Reader/Stop`), with where the piece stood for the last two, and the bytes stay in the reader each time, where `held` reads them. A failure is not always a stream's last word, `Io/Error` having `would_block`, "waiting for it is the caller's to do", so a second try finds what the first left. The three reads differ today: `Io/read_line` reads a failure as the end and keeps what it read as the line, `Async/read_line` answers the failure and drops what it read, and `fetch`'s `Stop` hands back the bytes held where the peer closed.
- **The end of a stream is its owner's to read, and no piece's.** `/std/http`'s decision has each owner decide "what a stream that ended means" and rejects a primitive that marks the end of its input. Each read `/std` names is an owner and states its rule once: a line where the stream ends is what is held, and none where nothing is, as both line reads answer today; a stated count the stream ends short of is answered as short, with what arrived, as the client's `incomplete` is; and bytes up to a delimiter say whether the delimiter was met, which `Async/read_until` does not.
- **A read `/std` names is a `Try`.** It is declared `Try(M, Io/Error, A)`, as [a fallible operation](../../design/standard-library/a-fallible-operation-returns-try-and-bang-lifts-along-declared-edges.md) is, so `!` carries its failure. A program that wants what was held at a failure reads `held`.
- **A reader states the bound its reads refuse past.** `Io/Reader/new(stream)` takes 1 MiB and `Io/Reader/within(stream, limit)` another, stated once a reader, as Eio's and asyncio's are. A named read refuses where the bytes held pass the bound with no piece cut, and leaves them held, as asyncio does. Go's and asyncio's 64 KiB refuses a line of minified JSON, and a line of 1,000,000 letters arriving 1,024 bytes at a time is read in 0.05 s, by the measurement of [A read after an append does not flatten the run](../compilation/215-a-read-after-an-append-does-not-flatten-the-run.md). A piece asked through `take` states its own, as `head` and `length` do under `http/Limits`.
- **Past its bound is a case of `Io/Error`.** A named read raises `Io/Error/too_long()`, so it stays a `Try(M, Io/Error, A)` and is sequenced beside `File/open` in one region. An error of the reader's own would want `Try/map_error` at each such region, `Try`'s edges keeping one error type, and a case of the answer is matched by every caller.
- **The names.** The module is `/std/Io/Reader`, housing `Reader(S)`. `new` and `within` make one, spelled as `Cell/new`, `Channel/new` and `Signal/new` are. `take(reader, piece, state)` is the loop and `held(reader)` reads the bytes held without taking them. The named reads are `line`, a line without its line ending, a newline or a carriage return and a newline; `until(reader, delimiter)`, the bytes before a delimiter; `exactly(reader, n)`, a stated count; and `rest`, what is left. `exactly` and `rest` have no caller in the tree: a program that holds a reader reads what is left through it, where `Async/read_all` of the bare stream would miss the bytes held.
- **`/std/http` and `Tui/Session` read through it.** `fetch` and its `Stop` give way to `take` and `Io/Reader/Stop`, the server and the client hold a reader a connection, and a session holds one where it held `rest`, so `Session/read` answers its events and no session.
- **A reader two fibers share hands each whole pieces.** A count of the pieces cut rides in the slot beside the bytes, and a search resumed after another fiber's cut starts over from the piece's first state; a stream that ends or fails after another reader changed the bytes has its piece asked of them once more before the end is answered. The slot is empty until a reader's first read, which is the one state that holds nothing and has cut nothing.
- **An arrival is no `Offer`.** A read under `timeout` loses nothing, so nothing in the tree waits on one. The bytes standing outside the fiber, a piece offered beside a timer or a channel can be written later, as an Erlang socket in `{packet, line}` mode under `{active, once}` sends its process one line as one message ([`inet`](https://www.erlang.org/doc/apps/kernel/inet.html)).
- **What a search and an append cost is the rope's.** The first read of an appended run copies all of it, so asking a piece again at each arrival copies what is held each time, which [A read after an append does not flatten the run](../compilation/215-a-read-after-an-append-does-not-flatten-the-run.md) takes up. A bound caps it here.

What the tree's rules already fix:

- **The slot is a channel, and no cell of a new kind.** [Only a fiber waits](../../design/standard-library/only-a-fiber-waits.md) rejects "a general rewritable cell beside the write-once one, two mutation vocabularies", and has fibers coordinate through "write-once cells and positive-capacity channels in the guest heap, whose operations return `Io` descriptions". A channel of one slot is the second of those.
- **What a deadline abandons holds no bytes.** `first`'s doc gives what an abandoned task costs: it "loses whatever it had taken and not yet answered with". A read through a reader has put back whatever it took before it parks.
- **No host row.** The host keeps no buffer for standard input, and `handle_read` says why: "a request smaller than what arrived would leave the remainder in a buffer `handle_poll` cannot see, and a fiber waiting on fd 0 would stall with input already inside the process". A piece is cut by the program from what `read` answers.
- **`/std/http`'s pieces stand.** Its decision is its crate's and none of it is restated: what is general is the loop that asks a piece again as a stream yields.

## Stages

1. **The reader, its loop and its reads.** `curios-std/src/Io/Reader.crs`, declared in `Io.crs` as `Io/Error` is: `Reader(S)`, `new`, `within`, `Step`, `Stop`, `take`, `held`, `line`, `until`, `exactly` and `rest`, and `Io/Error` gains `too_long()`. Nothing is removed. Check: in `curios-std/src/tests/`, each named read's piece answers alike at every cut of its input into two segments, wants more at every stop short of whole and refuses past a bound, as `tests/http/cuts.crs` holds a message; under the mock host, `curios/src/tests/host/stream_tests.rs`, the four reads answer alike over every cut of one input into `stdin_chunks`, a line under a deadline that passes loses nothing to the read after it, a refusal past the bound leaves `held` as it was, two fibers on one reader each get whole lines, and a program's own stream is read at `Io`.
2. **Standard input is read through a reader.** `/std/read()` is removed, and `programs/` and the tests that read through it open with `Io/Reader/new(Io/stdin)!` and `Try/run(Io/Reader/line(input))!`; `documentation/usage.md`'s sentence about a program's own standard input is reworded. What a program of `programs/` weighs and takes in the bench's next reading may have moved, and no document holds a figure of either. Check: every program of `programs/` compiles and answers as it did over one input, and `curios`'s tests pass.
3. **No read takes a byte at a time.** The three reads are removed with their callers, the tests that held them rewritten over the reader. Check: no read of one byte is left in `curios-std/src/`.
4. **An HTTP message is read through a reader.** `http/stream.crs`, `http/read.crs`'s `Step`, `http/Server.crs` and `http/Client.crs`. Check: `tests/http/` pass with the pieces changed in their type's name alone, and the server's and the client's tests in `curios` pass as they did.
5. **A terminal's input is read through a reader.** `Tui/Session.crs` and `Tui.crs`. Check: `curios/src/tests/tui.rs`, its session handing on a frame and a size and no input.
6. **The decision is written** in `curios-std/README.md` beside HTTP's. Check at the last, through the release compiler: the deadline probe of this spec answers `abcd` through `/std`'s reader, and the timing above is retaken against it.

## Verification

`cargo xtask workspace format` and `cargo xtask workspace lint` after each stage, with `curios-std`'s tests and the stage's own by name; the second stage moves programs many tests compile, so it takes `curios`'s tests whole. At the end, the whole workspace's tests once. A figure a measurement test's comment holds is named as one the change may have moved, and is not retaken.

## Rejected

- **The bytes held handed on as a value**, the reader a stream with its bytes and each read answering the reader after it, as `fetch` and `Tui/Session` do. Two wrong programs are well typed under it, both probed above: a deadline put on a read loses the arrival its abandoned body took, so `timeout`, `race` and `first` would each want a twin that knows the reader; and a reader read again after a later one took an arrival answers bytes out of their order, nothing in the language holding a value to one use. A binding that names itself being a recursion, `let held = f(held)` is refused, so each step also takes a new name.
- **A reading region**, a monad over the stream's own whose state is the bytes held, so a line is read with `!` and no bytes are named: a struct over `(Bytes) -> M({A, Bytes})` with its `Monad` witness and a `Monad/Lift` edge from `M`, written at `Io` and at `Async`. `/std`'s control takes an action of a fixed monad, `Async/timeout`, `spawn`, `race` and `using` an `Async` and `File/with` and `Tty/with_raw` a `Try`, and a region handed to one is refused: `no witness of Monad/Lift(Reading, Async) found`, a lift going upward alone ([A fallible operation returns `Try`, and `!` lifts along declared edges](../../design/standard-library/a-fallible-operation-returns-try-and-bang-lifts-along-declared-edges.md)). Run down to its base first, the region's state is the body's, to be lost where the body is abandoned.
- **A buffer behind the handle**, the host holding the bytes outside every fiber. It is what Rust's `Stdin` is. It adds rows that answer a line, each written for the native host and the JavaScript host (`.claude/rules/host.md`) and for the mock the tests read through; the host never waits on a peer, a write being one attempt and a read answering what has arrived ([Only a fiber waits](../../design/standard-library/only-a-fiber-waits.md)), so the loop that gathers a line is `/std`'s; and a cut the host makes is judged by no kernel and held to no test at every cut of its input, as `http/read`'s are.
- **A chain of write-once cells**, one an arrival, so that a reader read twice answers alike. [Only a fiber waits](../../design/standard-library/only-a-fiber-waits.md) gives the reason against a queue of them, that it "retains consumed chains through old ends": here through any older reader a program still holds.
- **A rewritable cell of the reader's own**, a `/sys` carrier that always holds a value. It is the second mutation vocabulary [Only a fiber waits](../../design/standard-library/only-a-fiber-waits.md) rejects, and the channel of one slot does what it would.
- **A lock held across a piece**, so that a second fiber waits its turn and no search starts over. The fiber that holds it and is cancelled has to release it, which `Async/using` does in a fiber and nothing does at `Io`, and a fiber waiting for a line would hold every other reader of its stream.
- **A fiber that reads the stream and pushes its pieces onto a channel**, as Erlang's I/O server is a process. It reads in `Async` alone, and a program wanting one line would pay for a scheduler ([Only a fiber waits](../../design/standard-library/only-a-fiber-waits.md)).

## Completion and retirement

The decision and what it rejected go to `curios-std/README.md`, beside [An HTTP message is read through one framing core](../../../curios-std/README.md#an-http-message-is-read-through-one-framing-core-and-made-as-one-a-wire-can-carry); the reader's doc comments state each read's rule for the end and its bound; the tests hold the behaviour. The line in `documentation/roadmap.md` becomes a checked summary, [A program reads text and may fail](201-a-program-reads-text-and-may-fail.md) is restated for a reader, and this file is deleted.
