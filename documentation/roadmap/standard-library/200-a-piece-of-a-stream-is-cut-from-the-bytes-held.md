# A piece of a stream is cut from the bytes held

Not refined yet. `/std` reads a line off a stream one byte at a time, a host call each, because a stream is a handle that holds nothing and a line is offered as a function of the bare stream: `/std/read()` takes about five seconds a megabyte. `/std/http` and `/std/Tui/Session` read the other way, each cutting what it wants from bytes it holds and handing the rest on. This spec makes that the one way a piece is read off any stream, and removes the reads that take a byte at a time. It waits on two questions: how a program carries the bytes held, and what `/std/read()` becomes. [A program reads text and may fail](201-a-program-reads-text-and-may-fail.md) takes its first step on what this leaves.

## What is missing

**Three reads take a byte at a time.** `Io/read_line` (`curios-std/src/Io.crs`), `Async/read_line` and `Async/read_until` (`curios-std/src/Async.crs`) each ask `read(s, 1)`, append the byte to what they hold, `x[..acc, ..b]`, and ask again until the byte is their delimiter. Each doc gives the reason, `Io/read_line`'s as "It reads a byte at a time, so it consumes nothing past the newline". A stream has nowhere to keep more: `Io/Input` is a struct over a `Handle`, `Async/Read(M, S)` is `read(s, n)` answering a chunk of one byte to `n`, and a function of the bare stream that took a chunk would drop what the chunk holds past the delimiter. The two of `Async` are written over any `Read(M, S)`, which nine witnesses satisfy: `Io/Input`, `Handle` and `File` at `Io` and at `Async`, and `Serial`, `Command/Pipe` and `tcp/Socket` at `Async`. So the rule is every stream's, a socket's and a pipe's with standard input's. They are the three reads of one byte in `curios-std/src/`.

**A byte is a host call.** For standard input each read is a `poll` and a `read` of the process's descriptor (`handle_read`, `curios-runtime/src/os_host.rs`). `/std/read()` is `Io/read_line(Io/stdin)` (`curios-std/src/lib.crs`), and it is how the 24 programs of `programs/`, each once, and nine files of `curios`'s tests read their input. A program that reads two lines through `/std/read()` and prints their lengths, compiled with `curios compile`, takes 0.40 to 0.41 s over a first line of 100,000 letters and 5.69 to 10.02 s over one of 1,000,000, the second line being `second`. A program of thirty lines over the same input holds its bytes: it cuts a line at the first newline of what it holds (`Bytes/index_of`, `Bytes/slice`, `Bytes/drop`, the bound decided by `Nat/lt/try`), and where there is none asks `Io/read(Io/stdin, 65536)`, cuts the chunk that arrives, appends what precedes the newline to what it held and answers the bytes after it beside the line. It takes under 0.01 s and 0.02 to 0.08 s over those two inputs, 0.26 to 0.41 s over a first line of 8,000,000 letters, and 0.11 s, one reading, over the 1,000,000 fed through a pipe in writes of 1,024 bytes; its second line is read from the bytes held past the first. Timed on top of `d211e3af7` through the release compiler, three readings each on a machine in use, so what they show is the ratio.

**Two readers of `/std` hold their bytes already.** `/std/http` reads a message [through pieces that hold no stream](../../../curios-std/README.md#an-http-message-is-read-through-one-framing-core-and-made-as-one-a-wire-can-carry): a piece is a function of the bytes held that answers the piece and the bytes after it, a refusal, or that more is wanted (`Step`, `curios-std/src/http/read.crs`), and `fetch` (`curios-std/src/http/stream.crs`) asks it again each time a socket yields more, 65,536 bytes a read. `Tui/Session` (`curios-std/src/Tui/Session.crs`) keeps the bytes a read left undecoded in its `rest` and answers the session beside its events. Each is written for its own stream, `fetch` over a `Socket` in `Async` and the session over `Io/stdin`. No reader of `/std` cuts a line from the bytes held off any stream.

**What reading no further was for.** A read that takes nothing past its delimiter leaves the descriptor where the line ends, and the program that needs that shares its input: `Command/new` hands a child every stream by `Stdio/inherit()` (`curios-std/src/Command.crs`), and a `Tui/Session` reads `Io/stdin` itself. That is read off the code: no test holds a child to what its parent left unread. What the tests hold is a sequence of reads off one stream, `lines_read_across_the_chunks_a_stream_arrives_in` (`curios/src/tests/host/stream_tests.rs`) and a serial port's reply line (`curios/src/tests/host/serial_tests.rs`), which a reader that hands on the bytes it held answers alike.

## Prior art

- **Reading no further is a shell's rule.** POSIX asks it of `sh` and of nothing else: where the shell reads its commands from standard input and runs one that reads it too, "the shell shall ensure that the standard input file pointer points directly after the command it has read", and it does not read ahead of what the command is to read ([`sh`, STDIN](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9699919799/utilities/sh.html)).
- **Rust's standard input is one buffer a process.** "Each handle is a shared reference to a global buffer of input data to this process" ([`std::io::Stdin`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/io/struct.Stdin.html)), and a line is read through it.
- **Go's bare reader has no line, and the reader that has one holds bytes.** `bufio` "wraps an io.Reader or io.Writer object", and of its `Scanner`, "the reader may have advanced arbitrarily far past the last token" ([`bufio`](https://pkg.go.dev/bufio)).

## Decisions

- **No read takes a byte at a time.** `Io/read_line`, `Async/read_line` and `Async/read_until` are removed, and no line read that reads no further is kept under another name. A program that must leave its descriptor where a line ends asks `read(s, 1)` itself.

What the tree's rules already fix:

- **The bytes held are a value.** [Only a fiber waits](../../design/standard-library/only-a-fiber-waits.md) rejects "a general rewritable cell beside the write-once one", so no stream keeps what it read in a cell: a read answers what it held and its caller hands it on, as `fetch` and `Tui/Session` do.
- **No host row.** The host keeps no buffer for standard input, and `handle_read` says why: "a request smaller than what arrived would leave the remainder in a buffer `handle_poll` cannot see, and a fiber waiting on fd 0 would stall with input already inside the process". A piece is cut by the program from what `read` answers, which the program above does with nothing `/std` lacks.
- **`/std/http`'s pieces stand.** Its decision is its crate's and none of it is restated: what is general is the loop that asks a piece again as a stream yields, which `fetch` is at a socket.

## Refinement

- **How a program carries the bytes held.** Two shapes. Handed on: a read takes the bytes held and answers the piece with the bytes after it, as `fetch` does and as the program above does, so nothing is hidden and each read of a sequence names the bytes the one before left. A reading region: a monad over the stream's own whose state is the bytes held, nominal as `.claude/rules/curios.md` asks a monad-shaped type to be, so a line is read with `!`, no bytes are named, and the region's end answers what was still held. Not probed: whether an `Io` or an `Async` action is lifted into such a region along a declared edge, as one is into a `Try` (`curios-std/src/Try.crs`), and what a step of it costs a compiled program beside the handed-on form.
- **What `/std/read()` becomes.** It takes no argument, so it hands nothing on from one call to the next. Three shapes: it is removed, and a program reads its lines through the reader over `Io/stdin`; it answers the whole of standard input, which each program of `programs/`, reading one line once, could take as it is; or the name stays for one line of a reader the program holds, with an argument. Each moves the 24 programs of `programs/` and the tests that read through it, and [201](201-a-program-reads-text-and-may-fail.md)'s first step states its type. Not read: which of the tests' programs read more than one line.
- **What a piece is, off any stream.** `http/read`'s `Step` answers `more(S, Bytes)`, `took(A, Bytes)` or `refused(Refusal)`: where to resume a search, so no byte is looked at twice, and a refusal that is HTTP's. What a program asks for is a line, with the carriage return `Async/read_line` drops before a newline, the bytes before a delimiter, a stated count, and what is left. Whether `Step` becomes the general type with its refusal a parameter, or the general reader takes a piece as a function and `/std/http` keeps its own, is not settled.
- **What the end of a stream and a failure answer.** The three reads differ today: `Io/read_line` reads a failure as the end and keeps what it read as the line, `Async/read_line` answers the failure and drops what it read, and `fetch`'s `Stop` hands back the bytes held where the peer closed. The general reader has one answer, and what was held when a stream ended or failed is the program's.
- **What bounds the bytes held.** `http/Limits` bounds a head and a body. A line with no newline grows what is held without bound, as `acc` does in the three reads today.
- **What a search and an append cost.** The program above searches by `Bytes/index_of`, a fold over every byte of the run with a closure a byte (`find_index`, `curios-std/src/Bytes.crs`), and appends each arrival to what it holds, `x[..kept, ..bytes]`. Not read: what appending to a run costs as the run grows, and whether a piece's search wants a primitive of its own. A chunk's size is stated three ways today: `drain` asks 4,096, `fetch` 65,536 and `Tui/Session` 1,024.
- **Stages, once those are read**: the reader and its pieces over any `Read(M, S)`, held to the mock host's chunks at every cut of an input, as `/std/http`'s tests hold a message; `/std/read()` as its question settles, with `programs/` and the tests that read through it moved, and `programs/README.md` and `xbench/README.md` naming what moved; the three reads removed with their callers, the two tests above rewritten over the reader; then the decision written in `curios-std/README.md` beside HTTP's, with `fetch` and `Tui/Session` read against it. Check at the last: no read of one byte is left in `curios-std/src/`, and a line of 1,000,000 letters is read in the time `Str/of_bytes` takes over it, timed on a quiet machine.
