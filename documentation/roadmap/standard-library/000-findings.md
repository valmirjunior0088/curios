# Standard library: findings

- **What the merge sort's neighbours cost is not measured.** The machine they landed on was shared, so no figure is stated for any of four changes: `List/sort_by` as a merge sort (`curios-std/src/List/sort_by.crs`), where the sort it replaced stopped at five thousand elements; `Bytes/ord` and `Bits/ord` as walks over both runs (`curios-std/src/Bytes/ord.crs`, `Bits.crs`), which `Str`'s comparisons go through, against the index walks they replaced; a program's start under a stack of one gibibyte (`curios-runtime/src/engine.rs`); and the prelude's build with the sort's proofs in it. The sort itself is read. Timed at `68b4a6d20` on a quiet machine, the wall time of `run -` through the release compiler as the median and span of three readings, the sort and its control interleaved, each program appending by a spread (`[..acc, x]`) and the third order's first term 42 itself: at 100,000 elements the control and the sort take 0.17 s [0.17–0.17] and 0.36 s [0.36–0.37] ascending, 0.17 s [0.17–0.17] and 0.35 s [0.35–0.35] descending, and 0.44 s [0.34–0.44] and 0.77 s [0.68–0.77] over the congruential sequence; at 1,000,000, 0.36 s [0.35–0.37] and 2.33 s [2.30–2.34], 0.36 s [0.35–0.36] and 2.29 s [2.17–2.31], and 4.80 s [4.70–4.81] and 7.98 s [7.85–8.02]. So a million elements sort in about two seconds in either order and in about three over the sequence, whose control is its own arithmetic past an immediate. Fix: take the other three on a quiet machine. The sort's protocol: a program builds `n` naturals into a list by appending, in one of three orders — ascending from zero, descending to zero, and the sequence `s ↦ (1103515245 · s + 12345) mod 2³¹` from 42 with each term reduced modulo 1,000,003 — sorts it and prints its length and first element, beside a control that prints the same of the unsorted list; each runs through `cargo run --release --package curios -- run -` at `n` of 100,000 and 1,000,000, the sort and its control interleaved over three readings, reported as a median with its span. The others are read against their parents: the comparison's against the commit before the walks changed, the start's by `hello_world`'s wall time and resident set, the build's by `curios-prelude-archive`'s build profile. Check: the figures, and a decision that rests on one stating it. Size: quick.
- **A step over text costs a type several times a step over bytes, and half of it is the position past built again.** **Uncertain, and warrants investigation:** what of that the erased program is spared. Over 32 letters at `85da54b97`, `Parse/text/take_while` costs each checker about 48 900 units where `Parse/bytes/take_while` costs about 9 900, and `Parse/text/literal` about 74 900 against 18 400, where a reader that takes no step costs about 8 500 (`parse_primitive_measurements`, `curios/src/tests/reduction.rs`, counted); a `Str` literal's validity, a fold the closed machine runs over the same bytes, costs 69 units a character (`str_literal_cost_measurements`, beside it). A text walk decodes a character, builds the position past it and descends on a proof of accessibility a step (`Str/span` and `Str/follows` over `Str/At/forward`). Counted at `7d28c0209`, the units the elaborator spends on a claim, proved by `Eq/refl()`, that a walk over a literal of `n` letters counts `n`, read at 32 and at 64 letters and divided by the 32 between them, each the `budget::consumed` samples of the claim's declaration in the stream a profiling build files for `wonder diagnostics`: 1,192 a character for `Str/span(s, At/start(s), (_c) => true).past.offset`, 1,097 for a count by `At/forward` over `At/try_next`, 960 for one over `At/skip` behind `at.offset < Bytes/len(s.bytes)`, 356 for `Nat/descend` counting up to `n` over the measure `n - k`, and 120 for the structural `plain(k + 1) = 1 + plain(k)`. So decoding is about 140 of a character's units and the span's own answer about 95; the descent, its comparison with it, is at most 356; and about 600 is `skip` building the position past, the rest cut off (`Str/after`), its first character's width read (`hop`) and a position of the whole text built from the offset (`of_after`). Not known: how much of that 600 the erased program is spared, a position's bound and a cut's validity being proofs it does not hold, and whether a walk could hand on the rest it has cut. Fix: once that is known. Check: `parse_primitive_measurements`, read again. Size: unknown.
