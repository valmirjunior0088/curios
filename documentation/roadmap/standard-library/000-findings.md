# Standard library: findings

- **What the merge sort's neighbours cost is not measured.** No figure is stated for two costs: `Bytes/ord` and `Bits/ord` as walks over both runs (`curios-std/src/Bytes/ord.crs`, `Bits.crs`), which `Str`'s comparisons go through, and the prelude's build with the sort's proofs in it. The sort itself is read. Timed at `68b4a6d20` on a quiet machine, the wall time of `run -` through the release compiler as the median and span of three readings, the sort and its control interleaved, each program appending by a spread (`[..acc, x]`) and the third order's first term 42 itself: at 100,000 elements the control and the sort take 0.17 s [0.17–0.17] and 0.36 s [0.36–0.37] ascending, 0.17 s [0.17–0.17] and 0.35 s [0.35–0.35] descending, and 0.44 s [0.34–0.44] and 0.77 s [0.68–0.77] over the congruential sequence; at 1,000,000, 0.36 s [0.35–0.37] and 2.33 s [2.30–2.34], 0.36 s [0.35–0.36] and 2.29 s [2.17–2.31], and 4.80 s [4.70–4.81] and 7.98 s [7.85–8.02]. So a million elements sort in about two seconds in either order and in about three over the sequence, whose control is its own arithmetic past an immediate. A program's start under a stack of one gibibyte (`curios-runtime/src/engine.rs`) is read too: timed on top of `33f341867`, `programs/hello_world.crs` compiled by `curios compile` and run as its executable takes 5.00 ms [4.60–5.54] from spawn to exit, the median and span of twenty runs after a first, and holds 7,852 KiB resident at its peak [7,608–7,984] over seven. The sort's protocol: a program builds `n` naturals into a list by appending, in one of three orders — ascending from zero, descending to zero, and the sequence `s ↦ (1103515245 · s + 12345) mod 2³¹` from 42 with each term reduced modulo 1,000,003 — sorts it and prints its length and first element, beside a control that prints the same of the unsorted list; each runs through `cargo run --release --package curios -- run -` at `n` of 100,000 and 1,000,000, the sort and its control interleaved over three readings, reported as a median with its span. - **A step over text costs a type several times a step over bytes, half of it the position past built again, and the erased step keeps the cut.** Over 32 letters at `85da54b97`, `Parse/text/take_while` costs each checker about 48 900 units where `Parse/bytes/take_while` costs about 9 900, and `Parse/text/literal` about 74 900 against 18 400, where a reader that takes no step costs about 8 500 (`parse_primitive_measurements`, `curios/src/tests/reduction.rs`, counted); a `Str` literal's validity, a fold the closed machine runs over the same bytes, costs 69 units a character (`str_literal_cost_measurements`, beside it). A text walk decodes a character, builds the position past it and descends on a proof of accessibility a step (`Str/span` and `Str/follows` over `Str/At/forward`). Counted at `7d28c0209`, the units the elaborator spends on a claim, proved by `Eq/refl()`, that a walk over a literal of `n` letters counts `n`, read at 32 and at 64 letters and divided by the 32 between them, each the `budget::consumed` samples of the claim's declaration in the stream a profiling build files for `wonder diagnostics`: 1,192 a character for `Str/span(s, At/start(s), (_c) => true).past.offset`, 1,097 for a count by `At/forward` over `At/try_next`, 960 for one over `At/skip` behind `at.offset < Bytes/len(s.bytes)`, 356 for `Nat/descend` counting up to `n` over the measure `n - k`, and 120 for the structural `plain(k + 1) = 1 + plain(k)`. So decoding is about 140 of a character's units and the span's own answer about 95; the descent, its comparison with it, is at most 356; and about 600 is `skip` building the position past, the rest cut off (`Str/after`), its first character's width read (`hop`) and a position of the whole text built from the offset (`of_after`). What the erased program is spared is the proofs and not the cut. `Str/At/next` prints at `cont-optm`, on top of `ddf5bbf85`, as `Bytes/slice(s, at, Bytes/len(s) - at)`, the rest cut from the whole text again, a `Bytes/get` of its first byte, `Bytes/rest` of it past that byte, the decoding of what follows, and the position past as a sum of offsets: a position is its offset and holds no bound, and each step makes two cuts it hands to no step after it. Fix direction: a position holds the rest it was cut to, so a step cuts once from what the step before left, in the type and in the program alike; that is a shape of `At`, and the user's. Check: `parse_primitive_measurements`, read again, and the emitted step's cuts. Size: medium.
- **`Str/span` rebuilds its answer around its recursion, a frame and a pair a character, and the compiled walk grows faster than its text.** `span` (`curios-std/src/Str.crs`) answers `{past, reach}` and writes its step as `let rest = again(n.past, n.progress); (past = rest.past, reach = True/proved())`. A record written in place keeps a slot for its proof once erased, so the call is no tail call (`documentation/roadmap/compilation/206-what-a-compiled-program-still-pays-at-run-time.md`): at `cont-optm` the step calls `again` and resumes at `let a = rest.0; let p = (a, 0); return p`. `meets` beside it hands its proof down with the position, under a comment that says why: the recursion is a tail call and a character passed keeps no frame. `span` is the one walk of `/std` written the other way, and `Parse/text/take_while` its one caller there. Over `Str/repeat("a", n)`, in programs compiled with `curios compile` that read `n` from standard input, the span to the text's end takes 0.18 s at 500,000 letters, 0.55 s at 1,000,000, 1.95 s at 2,000,000, 9.34 s at 4,000,000 and 394 s at 8,000,000, where `Parse/bytes/take_while` over the same bytes takes 0.03 s, 0.04 s, 0.08 s and 0.14 s to 4,000,000 and a count by `Str/fold` as much; `Parse/text/take_while` takes 0.21 s, 0.67 s, 2.46 s and 12.83 s to 4,000,000, and over a line of 8,000,000 letters read from standard input it traps, `call stack exhausted`. One reading each on top of `ddf5bbf85`, on a machine in use: the growth is what they show, and none is a reading to keep. Not read: where the growth past a square comes from, a collection meeting a stack as deep as the walk being the likely part. Fix: `span` takes `reach` down with the position, as `meets` does, and answers what its last step holds. Check: the span over 8,000,000 letters answers in about eight times its time at 1,000,000, timed on a quiet machine, and `Parse/text/take_while` answers there; `parse_primitive_measurements` read again, a claim over `span` unfolding another recursion; `curios-std`'s tests. Size: small.
- **`Io/read_line` asks the host for one byte at a time, so `/std/read()` takes about five seconds a megabyte.** `read_line` (`curios-std/src/Io.crs`) reads by `read(i, 1)` and appends the byte to what it holds, `x[..acc, ..b]`, until the byte is a newline: a call through `Async/Read/read` and an append for every byte of the line, where `read` answers up to the count it is asked for. `/std/read()` is `Io/read_line(Io/stdin)` (`curios-std/src/lib.crs`), the way the corpus's programs read their input. A program that reads one line and prints its length, compiled with `curios compile`, takes 0.39 s over a line of 100,000 letters and 4.83 s over one of 1,000,000, and with `Str/of_bytes` over what it read 0.41 s and 5.11 s; one reading each on top of `ddf5bbf85`, on a machine in use. Fix direction: a line is read a chunk at a time and what a chunk holds past the newline is kept for the next read, which needs an `Input` that holds what it has read and not yet handed on; that is a shape of `Input` and of what `Io/read` answers beside it, and the user's. Check: the line of 1,000,000 letters read in the time `Str/of_bytes` takes over it, timed on a quiet machine. Size: medium.
