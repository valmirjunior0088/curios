- **A key past three bytes costs a map's insert more than twice a short key's.** `Hash/hash` of a `Nat` of `2^20` or more, of an `Int` as large and of a `Str` past three bytes is a `Bytes` on the heap, built an append a byte, where a shorter one rides the `i31` ([A field is declared at the carrier its shape names](../../design/compilation/a-field-is-declared-at-the-carrier-its-shape-names.md)); a branch reads its digit through `$bytes/read`, and a leaf's key is encoded again where it is compared. Timed at `5bccb9ebd` (landed as `231acaf5d`), each workload a program in the shape of `spines` built by the release compiler, seven interleaved rounds, the slope between 200,000 and 1,000,000 inserts, median and span over the rounds: `spines`' keys 226.6 ns [221.7, 231.8] an insert, the same keys times a thousand and seven 548.8 [544.8, 563.3], and `"key/"` followed by the number 772.8 [769.6, 787.5]. The routes, none timed: a key read through a prepared form `Key` supplies, a digit reader with a law to `Hash/hash`, which changes a public concept; the emitter keeping a recursion's unchanged `Bytes` argument resolved, as a loop keeps the run it last resolved; or `Hash/hash` building one flat run. Settled by timing each over the map, text keys beside the numbers, and taking the one that costs the fewest declarations.
- **Uncertain: a union, a filter, an intersection and a difference of maps insert entry by entry, where one walk of both tries would build them.** Each is a `Map/fold` over one map's entries inserting into the map it builds (`curios-prelude-build/std/Map.crs`): `union(a, b)` inserts each of `b`'s entries into `a`, each a walk from `a`'s root that, where a twig is empty or a leaf holds another key, encodes a key it meets to learn where the two part; `filter(m, pred)` builds from the empty map what is already a trie of the shape it builds; and `intersection(a, b)` and `difference(a, b)` are filters of `a` asking `has(b, k)` at each entry, a walk of `b` from its root an entry. `rekey` keeps inserting, its trie's shape being another dictionary's. Okasaki and Gill's merge of two big-endian Patricia trees descends both at once in time linear in the two sizes (*Fast Mergeable Integer Maps*, 1998; Haskell's [`Data.IntMap`](https://hackage.haskell.org/package/containers/docs/Data-IntMap.html)); a branch here holds the position it forks at and no prefix, so a merge reads a twig's prefix off its first key's encoding, as `Node/insert` reads where a key parts from a branch, and two branches at one position merge twig by twig. The laws are stated (`Map/try_get/of_union`, `of_filter`, `of_intersection`, `of_difference`), and extensionality (`Map/try_get/injective`) makes any two implementations that meet them one map. Not yet known: whether a program spends its time there — `Map/union`'s one consumer in `/std` is `Set/union`, and no workload of `xbench` or program of `programs/` calls either. Settled by a program a union's insertions dominate, then a merge with its soundness built in its own recursion, as `Node/insert` builds its outcome's, and a filter that empties a branch's twigs without inserting, the four laws proved of them and the program timed against the fold; or by dropping this entry. Size: unknown.
- **Uncertain: a child process takes its program, arguments and environment as text, so names the system holds as bytes cannot be handed on.** `Proc` (`curios-prelude-build/std/Proc.crs`) holds `program: Str`, `args: List(Str)` and `env: List({Str, Str})`, while `Path` is bytes, `fs` lists names as `Path`s, and `Proc/args` and `Proc/env` answer `Bytes`: a file name that is not UTF-8 can be listed and opened but not passed to a child. `Cli/main` likewise refuses a command line holding an argument that is not UTF-8, so a program declared with `Cli` cannot be given such a path at all. Rust's `Command` takes `OsStr` arguments and clap reads a path option as an `OsString`; Python passes bytes arguments and carries undecodable names through `str` as surrogates (PEP 383); Go's strings are bytes. Open: whether `Proc` takes `Bytes`, with `Str` and `Path` converting into it, and `Cli` reads a path-valued option from the bytes as they arrived, or whether text stays the rule and the limit is documented. Settled by listing who builds a `Proc` and what each passes, and what a path-valued `Cli/Value` would read.
- **Uncertain: no codec of `/std` proves that what it writes it reads back.** `Json`, `Toml`, `Html`, `http/Url`, `time/rfc3339` and `time/iso8601`, and `Flt`'s decimal and hex text each say in prose that `decode` reads back what `encode` writes, and each holds it by examples in its `tests` module; none states it as a theorem, though each is a total function over certified text and the claim is a closed equation, `decode(encode(v)) = success(v)`. `time/Date`'s `of_days` and `to_days` are inverse by prose alone too. EverParse proves in F* that each parser it generates inverts its serializer and that every message has one encoding, for the TLS, Bitcoin and CBOR formats (USENIX Security 2019; PulseParse and EverCDDL, 2025), and Narcissus derives such pairs in Coq. Open: what a proof through `Parse`'s combinators costs, whether a round-trip law for each combinator (parser and printer proved inverse a piece at a time, Narcissus's shape) makes each codec's theorem a composition, or whether each codec is proved by hand. Settled by proving one small codec, `time/rfc3339`'s date, through the combinators and measuring what it took.
- **Uncertain: `List` and `Str` state almost none of their laws.** `/std/List`'s functions (`reverse`, `filter`, `take`, `drop`, `zip`, `map`, `fold`, `range`, `replicate`) carry no theorem, and `/std/Str` states one law among its 43 public functions (`eq_of_bytes`). `Map`, by contrast, proves what lookup answers after each of its operations, about where Lean's `Std.HashMap` and `TreeMap` stand, and `Set/has` and `List/sort_by` prove theirs. Lean's core and Batteries, Agda's `Data.List.Properties`, Rocq's `Lists`, Dafny's `Std.Collections` and Verus's `seq_lib` each hold hundreds of list lemmas: the length, membership and composition laws a proof about a program's lists reaches for. Conversion already decides concatenation's laws, so those need no lemma. Open: which laws carry weight here. Settled by surveying the proofs in `/std` and its tests for list and text facts they derive by hand, and stating those.
- **Uncertain: `Tui/View`'s size index is met by `View/fit` in every widget, so it constrains little a program writes.** `View(w, h)` carries its size in its type, so that two views join only where their sizes agree and a screen is the terminal's size by construction (`curios-prelude-build/std/Tui/View.crs`). But a `Pane` is handed its size only as the program runs, so every layout and widget in `/std` (`Pane/rows`, `cols`, `paragraph` and `window`, `border`, `Field`, `Listing`) ends in `View/fit`, which crops or pads at run time, and its folds carry a dependent pair `{h: Nat, view: View(w, h)}` to keep the index while they stack. What the index still refuses is a hand-written `beside` or `above` of two views whose sizes differ, which `fit` would otherwise pad. ratatui renders into a `Rect` and clips; Brick sizes a widget by a policy, `Fixed` or `Greedy`, and crops; neither puts a size in a type. Open: whether the index earns its folds and `Point` proofs, or whether an unindexed view, fitted once where the loop draws the frame (`Frame(w, h)` kept for the diff `render` takes), gives the same screens with the same guarantee. Settled by writing one widget and one test program each way and comparing what each refuses and what each costs to write.
- **Uncertain: `Cli/Parsed` walks a list by hand where `/std/List` names the walk.** `option_slots`, `required_slots`, `optional_slots` and `rest_slots` are maps, `joined` and `appended` are a concatenation and its values, and `Has` beside `Lookup`, `HasIn` beside `NamedIn`, and `HasCommand` beside `Named` each pair a membership proposition with a lookup that walks the same list again (`curios-prelude-build/std/Cli/Parsed.crs`). Each is what `List/map`, a list spread or `List/any` states, if those reduce in a type as far as a declared command needs. Also, `presented` is a type-valued function under a lowercase name, where `Carrier`, `Lookup` and `Slots` are capitalized. Open: whether `Parsed`'s index reduces through `List/map`, a spread and `List/any` at a closed declaration. Settled by rewriting `Parsed` over them and compiling `Cli`'s tests.
- **Uncertain: no decision states whether a `/std` concept carries its laws.** `Eql`, `Ord`, `Hash` and `Monad` carry none (`ops.crs`: a witness "owes no proof that its answer agrees"; `Ord.crs`: "`Ord` itself carries no law"), while `Map/Key` carries `injective` and `monotone`; a theorem that needs a law takes it as a hypothesis, `Ord/Flips(ord)` for the sort, each comparator proving its own beside it (`Nat/ord/flips`). Since `Flt` holds no `Eql`, every `Eql` witness in `/std` decides `Eq`, as `eq_of_eql` proves carrier by carrier, so the law could be the concept's own field and derive with the witness, the roster row [A witness body may be written by the compiler](../../design/surface/a-witness-body-may-be-written-by-the-compiler.md) leaves for law fields. Lean splits each class from its laws (`BEq` and `LawfulBEq`, `Ord` and `TransCmp`); Agda's structures bundle theirs; Haskell states them in prose. Open: whether `Eql` and `Ord` carry their laws as fields, and what a hand-written witness then owes. Settled by listing the generic functions and theorems over each concept and what each needs of it.
- **Of `/std`'s formats, only JSON's numbers and text's grapheme breaks are run against their standard's conformance suite.** `Json/tests` runs JSONTestSuite's number cases and `curios-prelude/tests/std.rs` holds `/std/Str`'s clusters to `GraphemeBreakTest`; the rest of JSONTestSuite and `toml-test` are run by no test. Read at `ae5d868b6`: `Json/decode` answers all 318 of JSONTestSuite's cases as the suite asks (the 95 to read read, the 188 to refuse refused, the 35 left to a parser 11 read and 24 refused), and `Toml/decode` refuses all 509 invalid TOML 1.0 documents of `toml-test` and reads 206 of its 208 valid ones (the entry on dotted tables). Fix: both corpora pinned by version and SHA-256, as `curios-unicode` pins `GraphemeBreakTest.txt`, and a test a suite holding each case to its verdict and a valid TOML document's value to the tagged JSON beside it. The Web Platform Tests' URL cases and html5lib's stay out: `http/Url` follows RFC 3986 where they test WHATWG's standard, and `Html/decode` reads what `encode` writes. Size: two commits, after the entry on dotted tables.
- **`/std/Str` has no Unicode normalization, so two spellings of one text are two texts.** `é` written as U+00E9 and as `e` followed by U+0301 differ under `==`, as `Map` keys and in `List/sort`, though Unicode calls them canonically equivalent (UAX #15); text a user types, a file name macOS hands back decomposed and text from the web meet as unequal. Python's `unicodedata.normalize`, Java's `java.text.Normalizer` and ICU ship it; Swift's `String` compares by canonical equivalence; Rust and Go leave it to `unicode-normalization` and `golang.org/x/text`. `UnicodeData.txt`, which `curios-unicode` already pins, holds 2,081 canonical decompositions, 3,901 compatibility ones and 1,002 nonzero combining classes, and its `full` mapping already builds a scalar-to-sequence table, a case's. Fix (adopted by the user on 2026-11-22): NFC, NFD, NFKC and NFKD as `Str` functions, from those decompositions and combining classes and the composition exclusions, pinned beside them; `==` stays a comparison of scalars, as in every peer but Swift, a program asking for a form where it means one. Check: `NormalizationTest.txt` pinned and held, as `GraphemeBreakTest.txt` is. Size: one commit, two at most.
- **`/std` makes no temporary file or directory.** A program that stages a download, writes a file to rename over another, or runs a child in a scratch directory names one itself, under `Proc/env("TMPDIR")` or a fixed path, and races any other program choosing the same name. Python's `tempfile`, Go's `os.CreateTemp` and `os.MkdirTemp`, Java's `Files.createTempFile` and Deno's `Deno.makeTempFile` open a fresh name exclusively in the system's temporary directory and retry on a collision; Rust leaves it to the `tempfile` crate. It rests on `File/Mode/create`, which opens a path only where nothing is. Open: where the system's temporary directory is read (an environment variable or a host row), and whether the file is removed with the scope it is opened into. Settled by what a scope-owned temporary needs of `Io/Scope`. Adopted by the user on 2026-11-22: a fix of `/std`'s, its open choices asked with it.
- **`/std` has no priority queue.** A scheduler, a shortest-path search or a merge of sorted streams takes the least element next; `Map/least` answers that only where keys are unique, so equal priorities need a key made distinct by hand. Rust's `BinaryHeap`, Python's `heapq`, Java's `PriorityQueue` and Go's `container/heap` each ship one. Open: whether a heap earns a subject beside `Map`, or a multiset over `Map` (a count per key) answers the same question with what `/std` already proves. Settled by writing a shortest-path search each way. Adopted by the user on 2026-11-22: a fix of `/std`'s, its open choices asked with it.
- **`/std` mints no UUID.** A record's identifier, an idempotency key or a file name meant to be unique is drawn by hand from `rand/bytes`, with the version and variant bits RFC 9562 sets left to each program. Java's `java.util.UUID`, Python's `uuid` and Deno's `@std/uuid` mint and parse them; Go and Rust leave them to packages. Open: version 4 (random) alone or version 7 (time-ordered, sorting as created) too, and whether a UUID is its own type or sixteen `Bytes`. Settled by RFC 9562's examples and what a program keys a `Map` by. Adopted by the user on 2026-11-22: a fix of `/std`'s, its open choices asked with it.
- **`/std/fs` walks no directory tree.** `fs/list` answers one directory, so finding every file under a path is a recursion each program writes, and each decides alone whether to follow a symbolic link and what a listing error midway does. Go's `filepath.WalkDir`, Python's `os.walk`, Java's `Files.walk` and Deno's `@std/fs` `walk` ship one; Rust leaves it to `walkdir`. Open: whether links are followed, the order entries arrive in, and whether an error ends the walk or is reported beside the entries. Settled by those three choices against the peers'. Adopted by the user on 2026-11-22: a fix of `/std`'s, its open choices asked with it.
- **Uncertain: `http` neither parses nor keeps cookies.** A server that keeps a session reads `Cookie` and writes `Set-Cookie`, and a client that logs in sends back what it was set; `/std/http` treats both as opaque fields, so each program parses RFC 6265's grammar and its attributes (`Expires`, `Max-Age`, `Path`, `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite`) by hand. Go's `net/http` (`Cookie`, `SetCookie`, `cookiejar`), Python's `http.cookies` and `http.cookiejar` and Java's `HttpCookie` and `CookieManager` ship them. Open: parsing and rendering alone, or a client's jar too, which then decides which cookies a redirect hands on, where `http/Client`'s `redirected` hands on the request's own only within its origin. Settled by RFC 6265's grammar and the jar's rules against the peers'.
- **Uncertain: every write is two host calls.** Since `3c9262483`, `write_waiting` (`curios-prelude-build/std/internal/Handle.crs`) calls `handle_write`, which takes the whole message and hands on what the handle takes now, and then `handle_flush` to wait while anything stays held, because the write's reply says nothing of what it left: a write the handle took whole crosses the boundary twice. Open: whether that matters to a program that writes in a loop. Settled by a print loop's host calls and time at `3c9262483~1` and after; where it does, `handle_write` answers whether anything stays held, a status rather than a count, and the flush follows only then.
- **`Toml/decode` refuses a header beneath a table dotted keys made.** TOML 1.0 lets a header add a sub-table or an array of tables below a table dotted keys defined, and forbids only redefining that table: its own example, `[fruit]`, `apple.color = "red"`, then `[fruit.apple.texture]`, is marked "you can add sub-tables", and `toml-test`'s `valid/spec-1.0.0/table-9.toml` and `valid/table/array-within-dotted.toml` hold it. `Toml/build.crs` refuses every header that reaches through such a table, "cannot extend a table created by dotted keys" (at `3ea89cfbf`). Fix: a header naming a table or an array of tables below one dotted keys made is read, and one naming that table itself stays refused, "cannot redefine a table created by dotted keys"; the rule's doc (`Toml/Error.crs`) restated. Check: both documents read to `toml-test`'s values; the spec's two `# INVALID` headers refused. Size: one commit.
