# Standard library: findings

- **Uncertain: a key whose encoding passes three bytes is read through its rope at every fork, so a map over large numbers pays about twice an insert.** `Hash/hash` of a `Nat` of `2^20` or more, of an `Int` as large and of a `Str` past three bytes is a `Bytes` on the heap, where a shorter one rides the `i31` ([A field is declared at the carrier its shape names](../../design/compilation/a-field-is-declared-at-the-carrier-its-shape-names.md)); `/std/Map/bits/bit` reads it through `$bytes/read` at every fork, and a leaf's key is encoded again where it is compared. Timed at `8ed961f9c` (child CPU time of compiled executables, the slope from 200,000 to 1,000,000 steps, the median of seven interleaved rounds' slopes with their span): an insert of `programs/spines/spines.crs` keyed `y * 1000 + 7` costs 736.9 ns [729.9, 747.6] where its own keys, below `2^17`, cost 388.5 [385.6, 397.4], and a mirror of the same trie with no proofs, whose bits are read off the number and its rank and which builds no encoding, costs 358.5 [350.1, 366.8] at the large keys. Not yet known: which fix the cost wants — a key read through a prepared form `Key` supplies, a digit reader with a law to `Hash/hash`, which changes a public concept and so is a specification; the emitter keeping a recursion's unchanged `Bytes` argument resolved, as [a read in a loop keeps the run it last resolved](../../../curios-emit/README.md#a-read-in-a-loop-keeps-the-run-it-last-resolved); or `Hash/hash` building one flat run — and how much of it a `Str` key pays; settled by timing each on the mirror, `Str` keys beside the numbers, over the node of [A map forks sixteen ways](100-a-map-forks-sixteen-ways.md).
