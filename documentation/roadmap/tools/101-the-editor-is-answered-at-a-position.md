# The editor is answered at a position

Adopted, to be taken soon (the user, 2026-11-18): a place in a file is answered as every typed language's hover answers one — the narrowest thing written there and the range it covers, its type where the compilation established it, its head and prose, and where it is declared — in one record per place, which the editor's hover and `curios wonder --at` render alike, each over the check's fold. Today a written name answers where it is declared, what its declaration states of it and what its file declares, at the command line and in an editor ([A place in a file is answered from the declaration that writes it](../../design/tools/a-place-in-a-file-is-answered-from-the-declaration-that-writes-it.md)), off the lowering alone: no answer holds a type, the editor's hover renders a declaration's head and prose and no range where `--at` renders its path, kind, members, witnesses and place as well, and a place no name stands at answers nothing. What is left is what no lowered term holds at its own place: a segment of a path, a name on a `use` line, a name a node holds rather than a term, a binder, and the type of a term. Each name a stage answers is classed as every answered name is, by what it resolved to ([Curios is coloured from one class set](../../design/tools/curios-is-coloured-from-one-class-set.md)).

Its first delivery has no prerequisite in another specification. `--at` takes no view: [A declaration is asked through one selector](100-a-declaration-is-asked-through-one-selector.md) gives views to `--name` alone, and keeps `--at` to the record a place holds. The next step is stage 1, a delivery: the one record and its two renderers over the check's fold, the types it already holds — a name's declaration's — in it. Then a bounded design and measurement of the settled list and binder records for stage 2: what is retained, its context, its cost and what survives a refused declaration. Local-variable navigation and term-type hover precede broader name coverage and path completion.

## What this builds on

- **A name is read off the lowered declaration.** `Text::mentions` (`curios-module/src/into_core/mention.rs`) reads the names a declaration's text writes off its lowered items, and `mention/tests.rs` holds it over a program that writes a name in every position and marks each one that has no answer, so a position that gains one fails there until its mark is taken out.
- **One engine and one record serve both transports.** `written` (`curios-wonder/src/named.rs`) lowers a subject over the fold and no further, and hands back each text of a file with its names; `Named::at` is the one rule for the name at an offset. `wonder --at` and `wonder --declarations` print the records, and the server answers a definition, a hover and a document's symbols from the ones its last check left (`Answers`, `Standing`, `curios-wonder/src/server.rs`).
- **A declaration's head and prose are read off its own text** (`heading`, `curios-module/src/into_core/document.rs`), through the one reading of what an item states of its definitions (`stated`), which the documentation pages are built by too.
- **A unit keeps what each module declares and exposes, and every binding a `use` brought into scope** (`PreparedText`'s `table`, `public` and `imports`, `curios-module/src/into_core.rs`). An import holds the spelling it came in under and no place (`curios_core::Import`), and a `use` item is the text of no declaration: `anchored` gives it no anchor. A `use` line, and the name a `mod` line gives, is a text of its module's namespace (`curios_core::Anchored::Namespace`) only where a lint stands on it, sited for that lint and kept while one does (`Context::line` and `Context::keep_lines`, `curios-module/src/into_core/context.rs`).
- **The elaborator settles a type for every term it finishes.** Each rebuilt term is recorded with its type as it settles, the list of a finished item is zonked once, the erasure obligations read it, and it is dropped (`Context::record_checked`, `settle_checked`, `split_checked`, `curios-elab/src/context.rs`). A written goal reports its scope, its expected type and what fits.
- **A type a report shows is settled where its reader stands** (`written`, `curios-elab/src/written.rs`): a witness resolution restores becomes an open slot and a method its call, under the binders and arms in scope, before the refusal leaves the elaborator ([A diagnostic spells what its reader can write](../../design/tools/a-diagnostic-spells-what-its-reader-can-write.md)). A type shown at a place is such a term, so it is settled with its declaration's frames at hand: from the settled list where they are kept, or by elaborating the declaration again where it is asked.
- **A parse is not the protocol thread's to pay for a document**, whose cost grows with the file: a hover parses the one declaration it is asked of.
- **An item the parser cannot read costs that item** (`Module::parse`, `curios-surface/src/module.rs`), so a file being typed keeps the declarations around the one being written.

## Prior art

- **Three compilers record at elaboration what an editor asks, by place.** Lean: "The InfoTree is a structure that is generated during elaboration and used by the language server to look up information about objects at particular points", a term's entry holding its local context, its expected type and its expression ([`InfoTree/Types.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/master/src/Lean/Elab/InfoTree/Types.lean)). Idris 2: a "Mapping from annotation to the name defined with that annotation, and its type" ([`Core/Metadata.idr`](https://github.com/idris-lang/Idris2/blob/main/src/Core/Metadata.idr)). GHC: "HIE files are a new type of file that can be emitted by GHC via the `-fwrite-ide-info` flag", since otherwise "all this information is not easily accessible to you after GHC is finished" ([HIE files](https://www.haskell.org/ghc/blog/20190626-HIEFiles.html)). Lean's terms carry no place, so its tree is where a place meets a term and nothing is held twice; GHC's file is derived from the tree its checker built.
- **A hover is first a type, and its terminal form is the same answer.** OCaml's Merlin answers `type-enclosing` at a position with the type of the narrowest expression there and of each enclosing one, each with its range, in an editor and as `ocamlmerlin single type-enclosing -position <L>:<C>` ([Merlin tutorial](https://core.ac.uk/download/162968996.pdf)); GHCi answers `:type-at`, `:loc-at` and `:uses` of a span, and its `+c` keeps what they read between loads, a failed one among them ([GHC #10874](https://gitlab.haskell.org/ghc/ghc/issues/10874)); rust-analyzer's hover states a type's memory layout beside its signature and documentation ([`MemoryLayoutHoverConfig`](https://rust-lang.github.io/rust-analyzer/ide/hover/struct.MemoryLayoutHoverConfig.html)); clangd's `--check-locations` runs its hover at every token of a file from the terminal ([`Check.cpp`](https://llvm.googlesource.com/llvm-project/clang-tools-extra/+/refs/heads/main/clangd/tool/Check.cpp)); and Lean keeps the info trees of an elaboration that failed so the editor stays answered ([`InfoTree/Types`](https://lean-lang.org/doc/api/Lean/Elab/InfoTree/Types.html)).
- **Idris edits through the same questions**: the type of a hole, a case split on a pattern variable, the clauses a definition lacks ([Idris 2, Interactive Editing](https://idris2.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorial/interactive.html)). The written goal is Curios's form of the first.
- **A rename is resolution read backwards.** rust-analyzer renames an item with every use from its own resolution, and its structural search and replace matches a pattern's paths "if they resolve to the same item, even if they're written differently" ([`ide_ssr`](https://rust-lang.github.io/rust-analyzer/ide_ssr/index.html)); [Comby](https://comby.dev/docs/basic-usage) matches text within balanced delimiters and [Semgrep](https://semgrep.dev/docs/writing-rules/glossary) binds metavariables over a parsed pattern, neither by what a name resolves to.
- **An editor lays what the compiler knows of a name over its grammar.** VS Code applies LSP semantic tokens "on top of the highlighting from grammars" ([semantic highlight guide](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/language-extensions/semantic-highlight-guide)); Zed lays them over tree-sitter in its `combined` mode, off by default ([semantic tokens](https://zed.dev/docs/semantic-tokens)); Lean's server makes them from syntax and from the info trees elaboration left ([`SemanticHighlighting`](https://lean-lang.org/doc/api/Lean/Server/FileWorker/SemanticHighlighting.html)), the record its other answers read.
- **Proof writers work by reacting to what the assistant shows.** Across thirty users of Rocq and Lean observed at their own work, proof writers iterate on a proof by reacting to the feedback the assistant gives (Shi, Torczon, Goldstein, Pierce and Head, [QED in Context](https://2025.splashcon.org/details/OOPSLA/15/QED-in-Context-An-Observation-Study-of-Proof-Assistant-Users), OOPSLA 2025).

## Decisions

Settled 2026-11-18, with the user:

- **One record per place, made by one function, rendered twice.** What a place is answered with is one record the engine makes (`curios-wonder/src/named.rs`): the narrowest thing written there — a name, and as the stages below record them a binder, a term, a name no term holds — the range it covers, and what the compilation established of it. The server's hover renders the whole record as markup with its range, and `wonder --at` renders it as a report block; neither holds anything of its own. What the two share is that function, never a process: `--at` is a question, which compiles the subject once, answers and exits, as every `wonder` query does, with no server behind it and nothing kept between questions but what the store keeps.
- **The check's fold, the lowering always and elaboration where it established more.** Each answers off what one check of the subject left (`Fold::reading`): the server's, which its session runs as a document changes, and `--at`'s own, run once for the question asked: the lowering answers a place whatever the check concludes, and a declaration the check elaborated answers what its elaboration established, a refused declaration leaving its neighbours' answers standing, as Lean's kept info trees and GHCi's kept load do. This revisits [the decision](../../design/tools/a-place-in-a-file-is-answered-from-the-declaration-that-writes-it.md)'s "the lowering's answer, and no checker's": its reason, that a file being edited seldom elaborates, holds of a file and not of each declaration in it, which the compiler judges one at a time.
- **A name answers its declaration's type and totality** wherever its declaration elaborated: always for a unit in scope, compiled and certified before the subject, and for the subject's own where the check elaborated it. Probed at `bc3f3817c` over a file one of whose declarations is refused: `--at` on `List/map` answers `/sys/List/map`, `public definition`, and nothing else, where `--name /std/List/map` over the same file answers its type and `total`.
- **A name answers both paths**, the one written and the one declared, as `--name` answers "declared at": `List/map` written answers `/std/List/map`, declared at `/sys/List/map`.
- **A place no name stands at answers the narrowest thing there**, a literal among them, rather than nothing: a refusal's place is most often a term, and a reader pasting it is answered with what stands there, its type once stage 2 records terms.

Settled 2026-10-13.

- **What the elaborator knows is the list it already settles, kept.** A type at a place is read from the settled terms of a declaration, kept with it where they are dropped today: the record the three compilers above write, made once, by the elaboration that already makes it. Not verified: whether every written node has an entry; what the list weighs, in a session and stored with a declaration a recompile keeps; what a refused item's entries hold, which are truncated today; and where an expected type would be recorded, the list holding the settled one alone.
- **A name no term holds is recorded where it is resolved.** The lowering resolves a bare literal's head and a structure pattern's into the node they open, and the elaborator resolves a pattern's constructor, a field's label, a projection's and a method reached through a witness: none is a term at its own place, so each is recorded by its extent where it is resolved, in one record beside the settled list. Not a second copy, since no term holds them. Chosen over a record in the lowering alone, whose home and shape would have been set before the elaborator's were designed.
- **A stage's answers reach both transports as the names' do**: a record of the engine, a `wonder` query, and the server's request from what the last check left.

## What has no answer yet

- **A segment of a path.** A path is one name with one place, so a cursor anywhere in `Io/Reader/line` is answered with `line`'s declaration, and no segment with the module it names.
- **A name on a `use` line.** The line is a text only where a lint stands on it, and its bindings are held under no place.
- **A name no term holds at its own place**: the bare head of a structure literal and of a structure pattern, a pattern's constructor, a field's label where a literal writes it, a projection's label, and a method reached through a witness.
- **A binder.** A local variable answers nothing, at its use or where it is bound.
- **The type of a term.**
- **The place of a declaration no file holds**: the library a compiler was built with, under an installed release. Its head and prose are answered.
- **Every place a declaration is written.** A place answers the declaration written there, and no query answers the places a declaration is written at, within a package or across the units that import it.

## What an answer costs, not measured

- **A hover parses one declaration on the protocol thread**, which the declaration's size bounds and nothing else does.

## Stages

1. **One record, two renderers, the check's fold.** The record a place is answered with holds the narrowest thing written there, the range it covers, both paths of a name, and a name's declaration's type and totality where its declaration elaborated; the server's hover renders all of it with its range, and `wonder --at` the same as a report block, over the check's fold, which `--at` runs where today it lowers alone; a place no name stands at answers what is written there. `documentation/usage.md`'s `--at` row and [the decision](../../design/tools/a-place-in-a-file-is-answered-from-the-declaration-that-writes-it.md) restated. Check: one fixture answered through both transports alike; `List/map` and a dependency's name in a refused file answering their type and totality; the subject's own declaration that elaborated answering its type, and the refused one its lowering's answer; a literal at a refusal's place answered as one. One commit.
2. **A binder and the settled type of a term.** Keep the settled list with its declaration and record binder declarations and references by place, so a local variable answers where it is bound and a term answers its settled type in the context that checked it; the binder places are the one record stage 3's note reads. Settle the first decision's coverage, memory, failed-item and expected-type questions before retaining the records; a settled type is not presented as an expected type. Measure record count, retained bytes and request cost over library files and an edited program, including a refused declaration. Check: the same query through `wonder` and the server, a local at its declaration and use, and a term under binders and arms; edit one declaration and ask about another, and ensure a changed or unchecked term receives no stale type. This delivery waits on neither path completion nor the six names of stage 4.
3. **A mismatch names the binder that hides a module's name.** Against `(@n: Nat, x: Nat) -> Nat`, `(@Nat, x) => let y: Nat = x; y` is refused as `type mismatch`, `inferred: /std/Nat`, `expected: Type`, its caret under the `let`'s annotation, probed at `dca35f725`: after `@` a lambda reads a binder ([Plicity is part of function identity](../../design/theory/plicity-is-part-of-function-identity.md)), so `@Nat` binds a variable that hides `/std/Nat`, and nothing says so; the `unused-binder` lint names the binder only where the body never mentions it. The report spells its sides past the binder since `20db06a92`, so `inferred: /std/Nat` pastes back as the type it names, and nothing yet says why `Nat` there is no type. Where a mismatch stands at a reference that resolves to a local hiding a module binding of its name, the report holds the binder as a note, a place with a line saying whose the name is there and what it hides ([A diagnostic spells what its reader can write](../../design/tools/a-diagnostic-spells-what-its-reader-can-write.md)); no lint is added. The note reads the binder's place from stage 2's record, and what it hides from the lowering: `Lowerer::resolve_name` (`curios-module/src/into_core/lowerer.rs`) answers from the binders in scope and asks the module's scope nothing where one of them matches, so where a binder wins a name it asks the module's scope as well and files what the binder hides, which the mismatch reads by the binder's ordinal (`Scope::written`, `curios-core/src/scope.rs`). That lookup joins the lowering's record, so a name a module gains lowers again the declarations that bind it, and this delivery waits on the lowering-record census, a [compilation finding](../compilation/000-findings.md). Check: a fixture over the program above, its note placed at `@Nat`.
4. **A name no term holds at its own place.** Record the bare head of a structure literal and pattern, a pattern's constructor, a field's label, a projection's label and a method reached through a witness where each is resolved, beside the settled list. Not refined: the shape each of the six names is recorded as. Check: each position loses its unanswered mark in `mention/tests.rs` and agrees through both transports.
5. **The members of the module a path names, and a `use` line's names.** A segment of a path answered as its module; every `use` line sited as a text of its module's namespace, as a lint sites the one it stands on, so its names are placed as a declaration's are; and the names a module exposes, read from what the unit's text stage holds of each module, offered where a path is being typed, as the tools findings' `--name <PATH> --exports` lists them at the command line. Not refined: what a path being typed parses to, an item the parser cannot read costing that item, and what a module is answered with where no file is its own. Check: a path segment, an import and completion of an unfinished path in the CLI query and a scripted server session.
6. **Every place a declaration is written, and a rename.** Not refined: a query answering each place a declaration is written across a package and every unit that imports it, read off the names each unit's check keeps (`Named`), with the names on `use` lines stage 5 places and the binders stage 2 records; and a rename answered from it, the edit handed back as text the formatter lays out. A declaration's name is told from another of the same spelling by what it resolves to, as `wonder --at` already tells `/std/Bool/not` from `/std/Bytes/not` a place at a time. Settled first: what `curios-wonder`'s README states the engine is once it writes a file as well as a store. A structural search and replace over a pattern is left out, no consumer asking for one. Check: a `/std` declaration renamed with its importers, a local renamed at its declaration and uses, and one of two declarations sharing a name renamed alone, through both transports.

## Verification

`cargo xtask workspace lint` and `cargo xtask format` after each stage, with the stage's tests by name, and the whole workspace's tests once where both have landed. `mention/tests.rs`'s program loses the mark of each name a stage answers. Through a release build: `wonder --at` over a file of the library and a program of `programs/`, and a scripted session with the server — a document opened, a path's segment asked of, a pattern's constructor, a term's type, one declaration edited and another asked of, the document's semantic tokens — each answer shown.

## Rejected

- **The lowering alone as a place's source**, the decision this specification revisits: a file one declaration of which is refused answers no type of any, where the compiler judged the rest.
- **A hover of the editor's own beside `--at`'s**: two renderings of two subsets, which drift; the record is one and each transport renders it.

- **A type asked of the certifier again.** The elaborator settled it, and a second derivation is a second answer that may be spelled another way.
- **A record of names by place for every name.** A written name's term holds its place and what it resolved to; the record is for the names no term holds.
- **A record in the lowering alone, made before the elaborator's is designed**: two records of one kind, each with a home of its own.

## Completion and retirement

Each landed stage is restated here as what is left; `documentation/usage.md` lists the queries, `curios-wonder`'s rustdoc their contracts, and [the decision](../../design/tools/a-place-in-a-file-is-answered-from-the-declaration-that-writes-it.md) takes each stage's design with what it rejects. Once all six have landed and no reference to this specification remains, this file is deleted. A landed stage leaves only the remaining stages here; the first delivery does not wait for the others to expose its queries.
