# A report's term elaborates back to the term shown

[A diagnostic spells what its reader can write](../../design/tools/a-diagnostic-spells-what-its-reader-can-write.md) decides that a term a report shows is spelled so that, written where the reader stands, it elaborates back to the term shown, and that a `use` argument is left out exactly when witness resolution would put it back. Every other part of a printed term is held by the stage that can answer for it: a name by `spell`, which stands beside the resolver it reverses and is held to it; a form by the surface tree; an implicit argument by being written. A witness is the one part the grammar gives no spelling, so a report must leave it out exactly where resolution restores it and decline the term elsewhere. That is decided today by a model of resolution in the text stage, and the model is wrong in both directions.

## What is wrong

**Whether resolution restores a witness is decided without resolution.** `restorable` (`curios-text/src/from_core.rs`) runs a transcription of resolution's three rules (`resolved`) over a model of conversion that compares two applications by the heads they are written with (`meets`, `differ`, `rigid_head`), reads a name through the definition the lexicon holds for it (`Spelling::written_head`, `curios-core/src/print.rs`), and reduces nothing. Resolution compares a candidate with its goal by conversion (`commit_match`, `probe_match`, `curios-elab/src/resolve.rs`), under the definitions as they elaborated and the refinements of the arms the reader stands in. The two part, here over `struct Box(A: Type, use Show(A)): pub Type { value: A }`, whose dictionary conversion compares:

- **What resolution restores is declined.** A binder at an argument that computes: on `(@A: Type, use Show(Same(A)), x: Box(Nat))` over `Same(A) = A`, the report writes the table's witness and shows the term as the compiler holds it (`a_binder_at_an_argument_that_computes_keeps_the_table_witness_written`, `curios-pipeline/src/tests/paste_back_tests.rs`). A binder at the goal's former and other arguments, `use Show(Option(A))` against a goal at `Option(Nat)`, where nothing computes, is declined the same way.
- **What resolution would not restore is left out.** Inside `match e | refl() =>` over `e: Eq()(A, Nat)`, under `use Show(A)`, a goal on `x: Box(Nat)` reports `Box(Nat)` with no line saying otherwise, and `Box(Nat)` written there is a type mismatch: the arm refines `A` to `Nat` as a frame's refinement (`solve_indices`), the model reads `A` as a binder apart from `Nat`, and resolution takes the binder. Under `use MyShow(Nat)` over `MyShow(A) = Show(A)`, a goal on `u: (x: Box(Nat)) -> Nat` reports `(x: Box(Nat)) -> Nat`, refused where it is written: the model passes over a binder whose type the lexicon does not read as a concept, and resolution reduces the type and takes the binder.
- **A binder of the reader's is named.** A goal under `use MyShow(Nat)` on `Box { value = 0 }` reports `Box(Nat, use x)`: a binder the walk opens declines a term that refers to it, and the reader's own, which `within_reader` places, never do.

**The paste-back tests cannot see a wrong omission.** Their witness programs are written over `Wrap(A: Type, use Show(A)) -> Type = {A}` and its `Monad` twin, aliases whose bodies ignore the witness, so `Wrap(Nat)` converts with `Wrap(Nat, use w)` for every `w`: a report that leaves out a witness resolution would not restore still pastes. No `/std` signature or body meets any of the forms above: both sweeps pass, and the body sweep declines two `let`s, each for a long string.

**A name the reader cannot reach is printed as source.** `Spelling::symbol` falls back to a name's full path where no spelling reaches it from the reader, with no line saying so, and `from_core` prints it: eleven `let`s of `/std` hold such a name in their elaborated terms (`UNREACHED`, `curios-pipeline/src/standard/tests.rs`). The decision keeps the full path, where whole-or-none, and its own sentence that a print that is half source is read as source, decline it.

**The model is what keeps two tables in the lexicon.** `Lexicon::declarations` exists for `written_head`, and the witness tables a report spells against (`WitnessSpelling`: each concept's record and edges, each witness's declared type) for `resolved` and the method spelling beside it, which re-derives from a witness's declared type the concept application the elaborator knew. The own unit's definitions are read as lowered, since on an error path no elaborated module reaches the report: `refold_recs` (`curios-elab/src/denoise.rs`) lives in the elaborator for that reason, and its module documentation keeps the witness out of the elaborator on the premise that only the printer's walk sees the binders in scope at each node. The elaborator sees them too, and alone sees what resolution does with them.

## What this builds on

- **The elaborator already shapes what a report shows, standing where the reader stands.** `report_goals` (`curios-elab/src/zonk.rs`) zonks, settles qualities, erases levels, refolds groups and contracts redexes for every term of a goal report, after elaboration, from the goal's birth record, whose frames hold its binders, definitions, witness binders and the arms' refinements (`HeldFrames`). A mismatch's two sides are shaped where it is raised (`resolved_for_display`, `curios-elab/src/typing.rs`). An error records the witness binders in scope where it arises (`Error::in_scope`), the reader position a report is spelled for.
- **A suggestion is settled in the elaborator, in the shape its author writes.** A fit is built with its hidden slots left out of the Core term itself, and a hole-free constructor fit is put to `check` standing on the goal's frames (`verifies`, `curios-elab/src/suggest.rs`): "the paste-and-recheck promise is a machine guarantee". An application fit is not checked again, since the conversion that found it is definitive.
- **Core has the vocabulary for a left-out witness.** An open witness slot is a metavariable of origin `Witness`, naming the callee whose `use` binder it fills (`WitnessOrigin`, `curios-core/src/term/shape.rs`), and the translation already leaves one out. An operator elaborates to the method it dispatches to, projected off the dictionary resolution found and applied to the operands (`elaborate_infix`, `curios-elab/src/elaborate/binding.rs`), and its lowered form before that is `Transient::Infix`.
- **Frames are held, not copied.** `HeldFrames` is one shared list, held by every metavariable from its birth (`Context::hold_frames`), and a judgment stands on held frames with `Context::standing_on`. Erasure opens a finished arm under its refinements (`refine_arm`, `curios-elab/src/into_ersd/eliminate.rs`).
- **Keying reads no definition through its arguments**: `satisfy Show(Same(P))` is refused, its signature not spelling its key. A reading of names that substitutes would change which witnesses are declared, so a report cannot share one with keying.
- **No checker's reduction reaches a report.** The pipeline renders a refusal over the lowered module (`curios-pipeline/src/compile.rs`), the elaborated one dropped, and `curios-core` shares no weak-head reducer of open terms ([A reader of finished terms holds no elaboration state](../compilation/303-a-reader-of-finished-terms-holds-no-elaboration-state.md)).

## Prior art

- **A printer that predicts what elaboration restores gives no guarantee.** Rocq decides by a static classification: "By default, the basic pretty-printing rules display implicit arguments that are not detected as strict implicit arguments", strict being one that "whatever the other arguments of the function are, it is still inferable" ([Implicit arguments](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/master/refman/language/extensions/implicit-arguments.html)). Idris 2's resugaring is "entirely for readability therefore there is NO GUARANTEE that the result will type check" ([`Idris/Resugar.idr`](https://github.com/idris-lang/Idris2/blob/main/src/Idris/Resugar.idr)). Agda printed a hole's solution without its instance argument, "not reconstructible after printing", and its maintainer: "In general pretty-printing is not always very good at guessing which arguments can be inferred or not" ([agda#6203](https://github.com/agda/agda/issues/6203)), adding that "it's a good idea to reconstruct these arguments then for interactive commands"; the fix, "Add {-# NOT_PROJECTION_LIKE #-}, and reduce projections in elaborate_give" ([agda#6204](https://github.com/agda/agda/pull/6204)), works on the elaborator's side of the command.
- **Lean asks the elaborator at display time, and keeps elaboration's state for it.** A message holds `MessageDataContext` — `env`, `mctx`, `lctx`, `opts` — and "`withContext ctx d` specifies the pretty printing context `(env, mctx, lctx, opts)` for the nested expressions in `d`" ([`Lean/Message.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/master/src/Lean/Message.lean)). Its top-down analyzer "aims to determine the minimal annotations necessary to ensure that the delaborated expression can be re-elaborated correctly. Currently, the top-down analyzer is neither sound nor complete"; it is off by default, and its instance check — `trySynthInstance`, then `isDefEq` with the argument — is off because "`MessageData` does not include the `LocalInstances`, so this will be very over-aggressive in inserting instances" ([`TopDownAnalyze.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/f47dfe9e7fe203caa1977b2bf41b7c0f30eb2d32/src/Lean/PrettyPrinter/Delaborator/TopDownAnalyze.lean), Selsam, 2021).
- **What is guaranteed is checked by the checker.** GHC's hole fits: "By leveraging the type checker, these fits are selected from identifiers in scope such that if the hole is substituted with a valid hole fit, the resulting expression is guaranteed to type check" (Gissurarson, [Suggesting valid hole fits for typed-holes](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/507467), Haskell 2018). rust-analyzer keeps two displays apart: one for diagnostics, where "There's no reason for this to fail", and one "for inserting them in source files. The generated code should compile", which returns an error where it cannot (`DisplayKind`, `display_source_code`, [`hir-ide/src/display.rs`](https://github.com/rust-lang/rust-analyzer/blob/ff1dc899758fccbbf22f73e3fcea95cf6066827a/crates/hir-ide/src/display.rs)). Curios's decision makes every report the second kind.

## Decisions

Settled 2026-10-16.

1. **What a report leaves out for elaboration to fill is settled by the procedure that fills it, standing where the reader stands, before the refusal leaves the elaborator.** A pass in `curios-elab` walks each term a report shows, entering its binders and an arm's refinements as elaboration does, and puts each witness to resolution: where resolution, run there, answers with a dictionary that converts with the witness, the witness becomes the open slot elaboration would mint for it; every other witness stays. Each is a fact only the stage that owns it can invert: resolution's answer is the elaborator's, as the paths are the text stage's.
2. **A method projected off a dictionary resolution restores is written as the program calls it**, by the same pass: as its operator where the field has one and every proof the operator form drops is proved again there by the procedure that proves a bound from the facts in scope, and otherwise as its wrapper's call over the concept's parameters and an open slot.
3. **The translation reads the settled term and decides nothing about resolution.** It leaves out an open slot and declines a witness still standing, a global one or a binder having no spelling, the reader's own binders among them. Its model, the method spelling beside it, and the lexicon tables only they read go.
4. **An error holds its reader's frames until it is settled**, where it is final and the context still stands: a goal where `report_goals` shapes it, a refusal where its declaration refuses it, before the declaration is undone. The pass leaves the attempt as it found it: nothing it solves stays, nothing parks, nothing is recorded as needed. Once settled, an error holds the witness binders it was raised under and no frame, so no elaboration state outlives the refusal. An error discarded in a sandbox is never settled.
5. **A name the reader cannot reach declines the term**, as any form no program writes does. Core's own printer keeps the full path.
6. **A report made where no elaborator stands** — an erasure refusal — settles nothing, and declines where a witness is shown.

## Stages

1. **Settle witnesses in the elaborator.** The pass, the frames an error holds, the two places it runs, and an entry the tests that print finished terms settle through (`translation_tests`, the `/std` sweep). The paste-back tests' witness programs are written over a structure declared under a premise and a `Monad` twin, so a wrong omission fails. Check: the computing-argument and one-former programs read back, and so does the answer under an alias binder (`Box(Nat)`); a bound proof the procedure cannot find is written with the call that carries it; both sweeps unchanged.
2. **The translation decides nothing about resolution.** `restorable` is an open slot; the model, the method spelling, `Spelling::written_head`, `Lexicon::declarations`, the witness tables and the reader's binder types go, and a reference to a witness binder still standing declines, the reader's own included. Check: the arm-refined and alias-binder programs decline under the line saying so, and so does a reference to a reader's binder; both sweeps unchanged.
3. **An unreachable name declines.** The reader's printer asks for a spelling the reader reaches and declines where there is none; the sweep's `UNREACHED` joins `DECLINED`. A program cannot name `/sys`, so an intrinsic `/std` re-exports no spelling for is read from a root reader over the intrinsic table, and each one found is filed as a finding. Check: a goal whose answer names another module's private declaration declines; the sweep's lists hold.
4. **Decision and retirement.** The decision states who settles a witness, that an unreachable name declines and what a lexicon holds; its rejected list gains the model beside the printer, the elaboration context kept for display, and a reduction handed to the printer. [The editor is answered at a position](300-the-editor-is-answered-at-a-position.md) notes what a type shown at a place is settled by. Check: the docs build; nothing cites this specification.

## Open questions

- **An intrinsic's operator form drops its proof.** `NatDiv`, `NatRem`, `IntDiv` and `IntRem` are written as their operators (`intrinsic_form`, `curios-core/src/print.rs`), the proof they hold left out, which a mismatch's reduced sides show. No program is known where a paste fails for it: one whose proof is a lemma from `0 < b * b`, which the procedure cannot find again, still reads back, unification taking the proof from the term the pasted type is checked against. Where neither does, the proof is written as the call of the `/sys` declaration, whose path the table states for no operator row today.
- **The editor's type of a term** ([The editor is answered at a position](300-the-editor-is-answered-at-a-position.md)) is a report read after elaboration: it is settled by elaborating its declaration again where it is asked, or carries what settling needs from the check that recorded it.

## Verification and retirement

The paste-back tests hold each form above; both sweeps hold the library, every public declaration reading back and every `let`'s printed type and body proved its `let`. Once every stage lands, the decision states the settling, nothing cites this specification, and it is deleted.
