# Every lint is read off what the stages recorded of each written site

**The benefit.** Two mistakes a program can state today pass without a word: an action bound and never run — `let _ = print("…");` runs and prints nothing, and a test whose body writes `let _ = Test/assert(false);` passes — and a `_` binding `{}`, which lets a result that stops being `{}` pass silently. Each is a fact about a site the program wrote, a binder or a `_`, whose halves two stages know: the lowering knows what reads the site and whether its `let` runs anything, elaboration knows what the site binds and whether a proof it wrote reads it. Recording each half where it is known, under one key the declaration's own text determines, and deciding every lint in one owner from the records, makes each later lint one rule over records the stages keep, and a lint the same however its unit was reached. The project has decided to adopt it.

The next step is stage 4, a delivery. It waits on nothing.

## What is missing

- **A `let` that runs nothing and that nothing reads is reported nowhere.** Probed at `326a94186`: `let _ = print("never printed\n"); print("ran\n")` prints `ran`, and `wonder` reports nothing; a test whose body is `let _ = Test/assert(false); Test/pure(())` passes. The same `let` with a name, `let x = print(…);` or `let x = 3;`, is `unused-binder`, whose way out, "write `_` in its name's place", leads to the silent form. No term outside `Io` performs an effect ([Effects are descriptions, and the carrier has no eliminator](../../design/theory/effects-are-descriptions-and-the-carrier-has-no-eliminator.md)), so such a `let` does nothing whatever its type, unless a proof the elaborator wrote reads it: `/std/Bytes/to_nat`'s `let _ = below(t);` is the fact `proved()` needs, and without it `proved()` is refused (probed). Elaboration credits that read for a named binder; a `_` has no identity a credit can name.
- **A `_` has no key, and one in a match has no binder.** The lowering numbers each written binder among its declaration's written binders (`Free::local_written`), the scope closing over it keeps the number (`curios_core::Label`), elaboration opens a local with it (`Context::fresh_for`, `curios-elab/src/context/scope.rs`) and credits name a binder by its declaration and number (`LintedBinder`). A `_` is minted with none (`bindable`, `curios-module/src/into_core/lowerer.rs`), and in a match pattern it lowers to `Pattern::Wildcard`, no binder at all (`match_compile.rs`). An extent is no key: a `let` block is one Core term with one extent (`elaborate_let`), and a lambda's binders hold none.
- **The compiler teaches what a lint of `{}` would report.** A program closing on `print("a\n")!;` is refused with "sequence an action that runs before the last, as `let _ = …!;`" (`curios-surface/src/parse.rs`), and a `print` yields `{}`.

## What this builds on

- **The written-binder number** is a function of the declaration's own text, ignored by identity as a hint is, and stored with every scope, so a recompile and a restored unit give a site the number they gave it.
- **The trace** is one record per declaration, shared by a recompile that keeps the declaration, stored in the unit's baseline half, and the candidate a store finds the declaration's elaboration by (`curios-elab/src/traces.rs`). What elaboration concluded of a declaration beyond its items is the record's from where the declaration elaborates (`Concluded`) — what each definition was classified as (`Classified`) and each written binder a proof it wrote read — and a unit reads a definition's by the record that concludes of it; `Unit::new` decides a unit's lints with the credits its records hold and, for the program's final term, which no record holds, those the elaboration that ran made.
- **The owner and the lowering's zeros.** `curios-lint` decides every lint, `unused-dependency` among them, from the zeros the lowering records (`curios_module::Zero`) — per declaration and per module body in its baseline half (`BaselineHalf::zeros`), with the reachability of the unit's declarations revised over a baseline (`Reachability::revised`) — less the binders credited, and words, places and orders them. `Unit::new` asks it, so a unit's lints are decided once, where it is made, and stored with it (`Written::Program`); the pipeline asks it for a unit refused and for an entry put whole.
- **`()` is accepted wherever `_` binds `{}`**: a `let`, a part of one, a lambda's and a definition's parameter, annotated or not, and a payload and a tuple's component in a match pattern (probed at `326a94186`).

## What the peers do

**Where a lint is decided.** In each phase: GHC, OCaml, Agda and Gleam warn as they check, and GHC moved `warnDiscardedDoBindings` from its desugarer to its typechecker, whose desugarer runs twice over `where` clauses and reports their warnings twice ([#23569](https://gitlab.haskell.org/ghc/ghc/-/issues/23569)). After resolution, in one owner: Kotlin K2 reports diagnostics only in its checkers phase over resolved FIR ([`fir-basics.md`](https://pigweed.googlesource.com/third_party/github/JetBrains/kotlin/+/49b2ac1b100c5042a0e98582713938062efa6a31/docs/fir/fir-basics.md)), and Scala 3's `CheckUnused` is a phase over the typer's trees ([#12857](https://github.com/scala/scala3/pull/12857)). rustc registers every lint in one place and detects each where its facts are: its resolver buffers `unused_imports` ([`check_unused.rs`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/stable/nightly-rustc/src/rustc_resolve/check_unused.rs.html)), liveness over MIR decides `unused_variables` ([`liveness.rs`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/nightly/nightly-rustc/src/rustc_mir_transform/liveness.rs.html)), and a late pass over typed HIR decides `unused_must_use` ([`unused.rs`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/stable/nightly-rustc/src/rustc_lint/unused.rs.html)). From a record a phase writes: Lean's linters read the info trees elaboration left ([unused variables](https://lean-lang.org/doc/api/Lean/Linter/UnusedVariables.html)), Scalafix reads SemanticDB, and Go's analyzers export facts that travel with a package ([`analysis.go`](https://go.googlesource.com/tools/+/HEAD/go/analysis/analysis.go)). Kept with the result: rustc stores the diagnostics a query emitted beside its cached result and emits them again when the query is reused ([`QuerySideEffects`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/stable/nightly-rustc/rustc_query_system/query/struct.QuerySideEffects.html)).

**How a discarded effect is found.** By purity: Flix rejects "a pure expression whose result is discarded" ("It has no side-effect(s) and its result is discarded"), decided by its effect system, polymorphism included, which it argues a `must_use` cannot match ([redundancies](https://doc.flix.dev/redundancy.html), [the case for errors](https://blog.flix.dev/blog/redundancies-as-compile-time-errors/)); Roc refuses a statement whose result is not `{}`, "IGNORED RESULT", "assign it to `_`" ([report](https://roc-lang.github.io/zulip-export/stream/463736-bugs/topic/Lame.20error.20message.20-.20.22Something.20is.20off.22.html)). By type at a statement: Zig ("value of type … ignored", [`_ =`](https://ziggit.dev/t/error-value-of-type-bool-ignored/2895)), Swift (a warning by default, `_ =`, [`@discardableResult`](https://sarunw.com/posts/what-is-discardableresult)), Lean and PureScript, whose `do` element must be `Unit` (`let _ ←`, [`Discard`](https://pursuit.purescript.org/packages/purescript-prelude/6.0.2/docs/Control.Bind)), GHC's `-Wunused-do-bind` and `-Wwrong-do-bind` ([GHC-81995](https://errors.haskell.org/messages/GHC-81995/), [GHC-08838](https://errors.haskell.org/messages/GHC-08838/)), C#'s [CS4014](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/csharp/language-reference/compiler-messages/cs4014) for a task not awaited, and two that are off by default — Scala's `-Wnonunit-statement` ([E176](https://scala-lang.org/api/3.x/docs/error-codes/E176.html)), which Cats Effect recommends for a dropped `IO`, and Kotlin's [return value checker](https://kotlinlang.org/docs/unused-return-value-checker.html). By a list of types: Rust's [`#[must_use]`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/attribute.must_use.html), whose lint reads statements and believes `let _ =`, Clippy's `let_underscore_future` and `let_underscore_must_use` ([`let_underscore.rs`](https://rust.googlesource.com/rust-clippy/+/refs/heads/beta/clippy_lints/src/let_underscore.rs)), typescript-eslint's [`no-floating-promises`](https://typescript-eslint.io/rules/no-floating-promises), Effect's `floatingEffect` ([language service](https://github.com/Effect-TS/language-service)), and typelevel-scalafix's `UnusedIO`, which misses an `IO` wrapped in `OptionT` or `EitherT` ([README](https://git.rossabaker.com/typelevel/typelevel-scalafix/raw/commit/2efbf6aeb0eca6fe37a3424a345ed426c0e7ea00/README.md)). An explicit `_` is believed everywhere, except where what it discards cannot have been meant: OCaml warns on `let _ = f 42` for an `f` of two parameters ([warning 5](https://discuss.ocaml.org/t/unwarned-error-between-ignored-partial-application-and-unused-var/14168)), Clippy on `let _ = future`, Flix on any pure discard. And `{}` is asked to be named: Clippy's [`ignored_unit_patterns`](https://rust.googlesource.com/rust/+/28c53f2eef1c8b9ffe2f2fd7e9fac89f646ea0e2/clippy_lints/src/ignored_unit_patterns.rs).

**What is taken.** Purity over a list of types (Flix): a `let` that runs nothing and that nothing reads is dead exactly, whatever its type, which reaches every description — an `Io`, a `Try`, a `Test`, a `State`, a `Parse` — with no list to keep, and the type only words the way out. A `_` believed where something ran, as everywhere — `/std/tcp`'s `let _ = Try/run(Io/flush(h))!;` drops a result on purpose — and `()` asked for where the result is `{}` (Clippy). One owner reading records the stages keep (K2, Lean, rustc's buffer, Go's facts), and the decided result kept with what it was decided of (rustc's side effects). **What is set aside.** A list of types or an annotation (`must_use`, `@MustUseReturnValues`, a shape of `Io`), which misses what it does not name and asks a declaration to opt in; a result discarded after `!` reported (Flix's `@MustUse`, Kotlin's checker), which reports `/std`'s deliberate drops and so needs a suppression the decision forbids; detection in every phase (GHC); a second type check for lints, "generally as slow as type checking that same project" ([typed linting](https://typescript-eslint.io/troubleshooting/typed-linting/performance)); and flags, levels and suppression comments (Scala, Kotlin, Effect).

## Settled

- **One owner, `curios-lint`.** It holds `Lint`, its kinds, wording, order and reports, and decides every lint from the records, `unused-dependency` among them. It sits over `curios-module` and `curios-elab`, which do not depend on each other, and under `curios-unit`, `curios-pipeline` and `curios-wonder`.
- **A stage records facts, never lints.** The lowering records, per declaration, each written site — its place, its spelling or `_`, its kind (a `let`'s whole pattern, a part of one, a parameter, a match pattern's binder or `_`), its extent, whether a written name reads it, and for a `let` whether its value runs an action, a `!` hoisted out of it — and whether a written goal stands in the declaration; per module body, each `use` selector and whether anything resolved through it; per unit, the declarations and private modules its roots do not reach and the mounts its references were written under. It keeps them where it keeps its lints now, per body and per declaration in its baseline half, and its incremental reachability is unchanged. Elaboration records, per declaration, each site a proof it wrote read and each site's class.
- **A site is keyed by its declaration and its place among the declaration's written sites**, every written binder and every written `_` among them, in a `let`, a lambda, a definition's parameters and a match pattern — the key credits use now. `Pattern::Wildcard` carries a written `_`'s place as a scope carries a binder's, ignored by identity. An extent places a report and keys nothing. A binding the lowering makes has no place and no record.
- **Elaboration's record is its trace's**, made when the declaration elaborates, not when a store files it, and so shared by a recompile, stored, archived with the prelude's units and adopted with a store's candidate as the trace is. What each definition was classified as is an entry of it (`Concluded`), and a credit is another: the unit's stored `credited`, `credit_reused`, `credited_in` and `Made::credited` go. A unit refused and a program's final term, which no trace holds, are read with what the elaboration that ran credited.
- **Lints are decided once, where a unit is made.** `Unit::new` asks the owner, from the lowering's record and the traces, and the unit stores the lints for its readers; the pipeline asks it for a unit refused and for a program's final term. A unit compiled now, restored, compiled over a baseline or read from the prelude's archive reports the same lints, and reading them reads neither the records nor the baseline half, which a restore reads only when a compile over the unit asks. A unit read from Core text holds no lowering and reports none. The owner never runs under `lint` alone. Rejected: deciding on each read, which reads a restored unit's baseline half to report it; and a `lint` that elaborates every unit again.
- **A class is decided or absent.** It is `{}` or an action, read off the site's type reduced at the declaration's close under an allowance of its own, separate from the declaration's budget, rolling back what it solved and crediting nothing; what it reads of other declarations, a struct's field among them, joins the declaration's trace, so a record a recompile keeps is still true of what it read. A type left a metavariable, a classification the allowance ran out on and a declaration refused record none. The allowance is a constant beside `DEFAULT_BUDGET`, the least power of ten above the most a site of `/std`, `programs/` and `language.md`'s examples spends, `counted` and held by a test. An action is what a `!` would run where the site stands: a value whose type reduces to `Io` (`curios_core::Intrinsic::IoType`), to a struct of one field holding an action, or to an application of the region's monad — the type a `!` reads its region off, which `elaborate_let` holds as the block's expected type where the block is checked.
- **A class words a finding and decides one only for `{}`.** `unused-let` is decided without a class, so a class missing makes its message plainer and loses no finding; `unit-wildcard` is the class.
- **Reported as today.** `wonder` reports a lint, the server publishes it as a warning, `run`, `compile` and `test` never mention it, and only `lint` turns it into an exit code ([`lint`](../../usage.md#lint)).
- **What the store holds changes, and nothing a program is judged or runs as.** The stored bytes that change are the unit's lints, held by the unit and no longer by its lowering, the lowering's baseline half holding facts where it holds worded lints, each trace holding elaboration's record and no `Made::credited`, and the stored items of a declaration that writes a `_`, whose scopes and wildcards hold its place; the store's `SCHEMA` is raised with each. Every verdict, every certification and every erased and emitted program stay what they are, since a place is ignored by identity and by every stage past elaboration.
- **Lint clean is held where each corpus is compiled:** `/std`'s library by the lints its archive holds (`every_authored_source_is_lint_clean`), its test modules in the compile `curios-prelude/tests/std.rs` makes of them over the archive, `programs/` by `every_program_lints_clean`, and `language.md`'s examples by `curios/src/tests/reference.rs`.

## The lints

- **`unused-let`: a `let` that runs nothing and that nothing reads.** A `let` whose value runs no action and none of whose binders a written name or a proof reads — `let _ = e;`, `let x = e;`, `let (a, b) = e;` — does nothing, and is deleted with nothing lost, a refusal at run time at most, which [Totality of the erased program](../../design/soundness/totality-of-the-erased-program.md) permits losing. It is reported once, at the `let`, and its binders are not `unused-binder`'s. Holding an action, it says "`x` is an action this program never runs", or "this `_`", and "run it with `!`, or delete it"; otherwise "nothing reads this `let`" and "delete it". Rejected: deciding it by the value's type, by `Io`'s shape, which misses a `Test`, a `State` and a `Parse`, or by a `Monad` witness, which reports data, where the value's purity decides it exactly; and `unrun-action` as a kind of its own, two kinds for one fact.
- **`unit-wildcard`: a `_` that binds `{}`.** Written `()`, the pattern checks the value is `{}`, so a result that stops being one is refused where `_` would discard whatever it became. A site is a written `_` of `{}` in a `let` that runs an action, a part of a `let`'s pattern, a parameter, or a match pattern's payload or component; a final arm's `| _ =>` takes what the arms before it left and is none, and a `let` that is `unused-let` is reported as that. It says "this `_` binds `{}`" and "write `()`, which refuses a value that stops being `{}`".
- **`unused-binder` reads the class too:** a binder nothing reads that binds `{}` is told to write `()` in its name's place, so following one lint never lands on another; and the refusal of a program closing on `action!;` names `let () = …!;` for an action that yields `{}` beside `let _ = …!;` for one whose result is dropped.

**Considered and left out.** A result discarded after `!`, which Flix's `@MustUse`, Rust's `must_use` and Kotlin's checker report: `/std` drops a `Try`'s outcome on purpose, and a lint that reports a deliberate drop needs a way to say so. An arm the elaborator proves is never taken, by reduction or by the facts in scope: the probe at `9688a9e33` found 177 in the library, and the ones read are arms exhaustiveness makes a program write and a refuted proof discharges — `Option/get`'s `| none() => match ok end` — so it becomes a lint only where the language lets such an arm go unwritten, a question for the language. A written proof argument entailment would have found, and an explicit implicit argument inference would have chosen, report a deletion that may document intent, and wait for a consumer.

## What is known

- **Probed at `326a94186`**, on standard input against the worktree's build: the program above prints `ran` and `wonder` reports nothing; `let x = print(…);` and `let x = 3;` each report `unused-binder` with "write `_`", and `let _ = 3;` reports nothing; a test of `let _ = Test/assert(false);` passes beside one of `let () = Test/assert(false)!;` that fails; `let _ = Bytes/to_nat/below(t); proved()` checks and `proved()` alone is refused; `()` checks in each site `unit-wildcard` names; a program closing on `print("a\n")!;` is refused with the advice to write `let _ = …!;`.
- **Read off the sources, not counted by the compiler:** `/std` and `programs/` hold three `let _ =` with no `!` on their line — two whose `!` closes a later line, and `Bytes/to_nat`'s `below`, which a proof reads — so `unused-let` reports nothing there once a `_` can be credited. `programs/` writes `let _ = …!;` twenty-eight times; `/std` and `programs/` hold 235 lines with a `(_) =>` and 72 with a `some(_)`, `success(_)` or `ok(_)`, of whose sites those binding `{}` are `unit-wildcard`'s, a count stage 4's record gives.
- **Counted at `9688a9e33`** in a scratch copy with temporary `note!`s at each binding with no name in `elaborate_let`, written or made by the lowering: in the library, 140 of `{}` and 23 of `Io(…)`; in `/std`'s test modules, fifteen and four; in `programs/` and the decoders, twenty-nine of `{}`. Every `Io` one read is a binding the lowering made.
- **A question can cost what a declaration has:** the same probe's `entail` of `False`, asked under the declaration's budget, spent it and refused two declarations of `Str/tests/decomposition.crs` for `budget spent`, which is why a class has an allowance of its own.

## Stages

Each stage lands what it records with the lint that first reads it.

4. **`unused-let`.** A written `_` in a `let`'s pattern, a lambda's parameters and a definition's takes a place, so a credit reaches it; the lowering records each `let` that runs no action and none of whose binders a written name reads (a let zero, with the binders its pattern writes), and the owner reports one none of whose binders a proof read either as `unused-let`, worded "delete it", its binders none of `unused-binder`'s. Check: `a_wildcard_binder_is_never_reported` restated to the slots it is about; `let _ = print(…)`, `let x = print(…)`, `let _ = Test/assert(false)` in a test, `let x = 3` and `let (a, b) = p` with neither read reported, each once; `let _ = e!;`, a `_` a proof reads (the shape of `Bytes/to_nat`'s `below`), a parameter and a part of a pattern not; the four corpora lint clean; the store's `SCHEMA` raised.
5. **Classes.** Elaboration records each placed site's class under the allowance, measured and named, in its declaration's record; `unused-let` words one holding an action "run it with `!`, or delete it", and `unused-binder` tells a binder of `{}` to write `()`. Check: `curios-elab` tests that a site of `{}`, of `Io(T)`, of `Try(Io, E, T)`, of `Test({})` in a `Test` region and of anything else records its class, that a metavariable left open and a refused declaration record none, and that the declaration's budget is left as it was found; the allowance's test classifying every site of the four corpora within it; each wording.
6. **`unit-wildcard`.** A match pattern's `_` takes its place on `Pattern::Wildcard` and the matrix compiler records its column's class; the owner reports a `_` of `{}` as `unit-wildcard`; the refusal of a program closing on `action!;` names `let () = …!;`; the corpus's `let _ = …!;` of a `{}` is rewritten to `let () = …!;`, with every other site the four corpora hold. Check: a `_` of `{}` in a `let` that runs an action, a part of a pattern, a lambda's and a definition's parameter, a payload and a match tuple's component reported; one of anything else and a final arm's `| _ =>` not; the four corpora lint clean.

Each stage's contract lands in its owner as it lands: [A lint is an exact finding read off the compilation](../../design/tools/a-lint-is-an-exact-finding-read-off-the-compilation.md) restates where a lint is decided and what it may read, its **Rejected** paragraph's "walking elaborated terms instead of the lowering" restated as re-deriving a fact a stage decided, and a list of types added to what is rejected; `usage.md`'s `lint` table gains the kinds and `unused-binder`'s new reach; `curios-lint`'s README states its mission and `curios-module`'s gives up the lints.

## Retirement

Once the stages have landed, the decision, `usage.md` and the crates' READMEs state the owner, the records and the lints, and nothing references this specification, it is deleted. A later lint is a rule in `curios-lint` and, where the records lack its fact, a fact the stage that knows it records, filed as its own work when a consumer argues for it.
