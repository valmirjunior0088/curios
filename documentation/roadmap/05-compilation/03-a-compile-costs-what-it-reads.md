# A compile costs what it reads

Working specification for making what a compilation does follow what its declarations read, at every stage it runs: an edit redoes, stage by stage, the declarations that read what it changed and no other, and a compile pays for the declarations of its scope it reads, not for the scope. The first is the minimality of a build, with early cutoff, at the grain of a declaration; the second is the same rule read from the other side, a scope being an input like any other.

It rests on [A declaration is a function of what it reads](../../design/compilation/a-declaration-is-a-function-of-what-it-reads.md): a record of reads decides nothing while anything but reads reaches a declaration. [Item tasks](04-item-tasks.md) needs this, for the cells its workers write and the records that say which of them may run together.

## What this builds on

- **The `Cache` seam and the stored-unit format** (`curios-pipeline/src/compile.rs`, `curios-unit`): a product-supplied policy the fold consults, and a serialized unit that already crosses processes.
- **A stored unit is a baseline** ([A stored unit is a baseline for an item-level recompile](../../design/compilation/a-stored-unit-is-a-baseline-for-an-item-level-recompile.md)): a unit compiled over one is stored as the bytes it is compiled whole, and the closure it re-elaborates is `invalidated`'s (`curios-pipeline/src/recompile.rs`).
- **A declaration is the unit of work** ([A declaration is a function of what it reads](../../design/compilation/a-declaration-is-a-function-of-what-it-reads.md)): lowered in identities of its own, elaborated on demand in a state of its own, deciding its vectors, totality and erasure obligations as it is published, judged by the kernel alone, and stored in one order.
- **The accessors that record a need** (`Context::assumption`, `definition_body`, `var_reduct`, `induct_decl`, `struct_decl`, `concept`, `witness_unfinished`, `curios-elab/src/context.rs`): every read of another declaration's name, body or registry entry passes one of them, and a read of one not yet finished is recorded there.
- **The certifier records what it read.** `curios_core::Reads` holds, per definition, the items whose type, universe scheme or registry entry a judgment read and the bodies it asked for, and `curios-pipeline`'s `the_prelude_reads_along_the_graph_its_definitions_reach` holds the reads inside the elaborated graph. Every unit files them, and no stage reads them.
- **A witness is known by the key its signature is spelled at** before anything elaborates (`curios-elab/src/concept/spelled.rs`), and registration refuses one whose elaborated signature keys anywhere else.
- **An erased form is its item's, and a program links what it reaches**, decisions 7 and 8 ([`curios-unit`'s README](../../../curios-unit/README.md#an-erased-form-is-its-items), `curios-elab/src/into_ersd/erased.rs`, `link.rs`, `curios_ersd::Module::link`): each item erases into a fragment on a budget restored for it, a unit compiled over a baseline erases the items it elaborates again (`erase_unit_over`), and the optimizer's prune decides what a linked program keeps (`prune_unreachable`, `curios-ersd/src/optimize/prune.rs`).
- **A file is parsed once for each text a process holds of it** (`parsed`, `curios-text/src/root_source.rs`), and the lowering reads its predecessors as layers, copying none (`Scoped`, `curios-text/src/into_core/scoped.rs`).

## The gap

What a question about `/std` costs, compiled over the archived unit, in allocations, with `hello_world` beside it. Counted at `bf21a9065`, as *Verification* states; a count moves in its fifth digit from one run to the next.

| What a compile does | What it follows today | Nothing edited | `/std/Bool/not`'s body respelled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parses | every file of the unit, once for each process | 61.78 M | 61.78 M |
| Lowers | the unit | 4.41 M | 4.41 M |
| Elaborates | what reaches the edit | 0.24 M | 65.86 M |
| Judges | what reaches the edit | 0.02 M | 22.91 M |
| Stores | the unit | 0.59 M | 0.73 M |

**Lowering is the unit's.** A unit compiled over a baseline lowers every module again, 4.41 M allocations, where one file changed and no interface did; and a process that has not parsed a file parses it, 61.78 M for the library's 136 files, whatever the store holds of the unit.

**An edit redoes everything that reaches it.** Respelling `/std/Bool/not`'s body elaborates and judges 1,109 items again, 88.77 M allocations between the two checkers. An edit to one item of `/std` reaches 39.2 items on average through the reverse closure `invalidated` takes, 5 at the median and 1,449 for `/std/ops/Cmp`, where the kernel's recorded body reads name 5.8 with the item itself, 1 at the median. The closure is over names an item reaches, whatever it read of them, and transitive, since nothing says where a re-elaborated item came out the same. The averages counted at `87189c97d` over the restored prelude.

**Nothing records what the elaborator read.** The kernel's reads are its own judgment's. What elaboration read and left no name for — a body unfolded while a candidate was tried, a key at which no witness stands, whether a proof was offered the unit's lemmas — is in no term, and elaboration adds 3,018 edges to the 6,488 the lowered forms of `/std`'s declarations name, 1,881 of them to a witness, so the lowered graph is not the record either. Counted at `0283c7242`. One question a recompile answers from its baseline instead: whether the prover's vocabulary reads a declaration is read off the baseline's elaborated graph (`vocabulary_reach`, `curios-elab/src/elaborate/module.rs`), so an edit that changes what the vocabulary reads leaves a reused declaration elaborated under the other answer.

**A reused verdict rests on a closure the certifier does not check.** The kernel passes a reused declaration over where its terms are the mounted ones (`recheck_over`, `Judged::of`), and which declarations are reused is the pipeline's closure: one cut short files the baseline's verdict beside a declaration it no longer describes, the finding in [`01-soundness`](../01-soundness/00-findings.md).

**A witness is named by its place.** `WitnessId` is the declaring module and an ordinal, so a witness inserted ahead of another renames every witness after it in its module, and every declaration that resolves through one of them reads another name.

**Every compile brings in its whole scope.** Elaboration defines every definition of every predecessor and registers each witness again by reducing its signature to a key the unit that declared it had in hand (`Established::replay_definitions`); `Globals::of` copies each registry and maps each definition, and the kernel copies the result again (`Kernel::seed`); erasure projects every predecessor's Core and defines it a third time (`seed`, `curios-elab/src/into_ersd/lower.rs`); and before any of it a process validates and restores both of the prelude's images whole. Compiling `programs/hello_world.crs` makes 0.13 M allocations elaborating and 0.06 M in the kernel, after it restores the prelude.

**A unit is addressed by the units before it.** A slot names every unit placed ahead of it in the fold and its record vouches for what each contained, where `curios-package` has the dependency graph and flattens it into that line (`curios_package::order`). Over a package depending on `alpha` and on `beta`, neither naming the other, an edit to `alpha` compiles `/beta` again, whole, and an edit to `beta` leaves `/alpha` reused.

**A unit is stored whole.** Filing a unit serializes all of it, 0.59 M allocations for `/std`, and a hit decodes all of it.

**A void attempt is forgotten.** An attempt that read a declaration not yet elaborated is made again, and the next compile of the same unit finds the same order again, attempt by attempt: a whole compile of the prelude voids 41, counted at `bf21a9065`.

## Prior art

- **The goal has a definition.** Mokhov, Mitchell and Peyton Jones: "A build system is minimal if it executes tasks at most once per build and only if they transitively depend on inputs that changed since the previous build" ([Build Systems à la Carte](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf), definition 2.1), and under early cutoff, "When it executes a task and the result is unchanged from the previous build, it is unnecessary to execute the dependent tasks" (section 2.3). The tree today is the paper's Make for what an edit dirties: a static over-approximation, read before anything runs.
- **A trace of what was read decides what is rebuilt.** A verifying trace is "to record the values/hashes of dependencies used last time, and if something has changed, the key is dirty and must be rebuilt" (section 4.2.2). A restarting scheduler, which the elaborator's is, "is also not minimal in the sense that a task may start, do some meaningful work, and then abort", and "To reduce the number of aborts (often to zero) Excel records the discovered task order in its calc chain" (section 4.1.2).
- **Reads are revalidated in the order they were made.** rustc's try-mark-green: "it is important that we visit each node in `reads(Q)` in same order as they occurred in the original compilation", since a later read may exist only because of what an earlier one answered ([Incremental compilation](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/queries/incremental-compilation.html)). A small query over a large one is a firewall: "even if the result of the monolithic query changes (which it is very likely to do) the small projections can still mostly be marked as green" ([Incremental compilation in detail](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/queries/incremental-compilation-in-detail.html)).
- **The peers cut off the same way.** Skyframe: "If a node is invalidated, but upon rebuild, it is discovered that its new value is the same as its old value, the nodes that were invalidated due to a change in this node are 'resurrected'" ([Skyframe](https://bazel.build/reference/skyframe)). salsa backdates: "even though the inputs changed, the output didn't" ([The red-green algorithm](https://salsa-rs.github.io/salsa/reference/algorithm.html)). rust-analyzer names the price, "fine-grained tracking of dependencies takes time and memory" ([Three Architectures for a Responsive IDE](https://rust-analyzer.github.io/blog/2020/07/20/three-architectures-for-responsive-ide.html)).
- **What is known not to have changed is not revalidated.** salsa's durability: "If a tracked function depends (transitively) only on high durability inputs, and you change a low durability input, then we can very easily determine that the tracked function result is still valid, avoiding the need to traverse the input edges one by one" ([The red-green algorithm](https://salsa-rs.github.io/salsa/reference/algorithm.html)). A read of another unit is such an input here: the unit's address vouches for it.
- **Every stage is keyed, not one.** Sixty's build library "keeps track of what dependencies a task used when it was executed (much like Shake) in a table, i.e. what keys it fetched and what the values were", over a key for each parsed module, each resolved module and each definition's type ([Query-based compiler architectures](https://ollef.github.io/blog/posts/query-based-compilers.html)).
- **A declaration is the grain, in a proof assistant too.** iCoq "tracks fine-grained dependencies between Coq definitions, propositions, and proofs, and only checks those proofs affected by changes between two revisions" ([Çelik, Palmskog and Gligoric](https://users.ece.utexas.edu/~gligoric/papers/abstracts/CelikETAL17iCoq.txt), ASE 2017). Leino and Wüstholz cache a verifier's results per procedure, using "the program's call graph and control-flow graph to focus the verification effort on just the parts of the program that were affected by the user's most recent modifications" ([Fine-grained Caching of Verification Results](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/fine-grained-caching-verification-results/), CAV 2015). Lean's own incremental elaboration reuses a prefix: "If all the syntax inspected up to a certain point is unchanged, we can assume that the old state can be reused" ([`Lean.Language.Lean`](https://lean-lang.org/doc/api/Lean/Language/Lean.html)).
- **The peers split a signature from a body by declaring the body opaque.** Rocq's `.vos` is "similar to `foo.vo` except that all opaque proofs are skipped in the compilation process" ([Compiled interfaces](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/V9.0.0/refman/practical-tools/coq-commands.html)), and under Lean's module system "Changes to files that affect only non-exported information (e.g. proofs) will not trigger rebuilds outside of these files" ([The Module System](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/4.24.0/The-Module-System/)). No body is opaque here, so which bodies a declaration unfolded is recorded, never declared.
- **A lookup is a read whatever it finds.** Kotlin's incremental compiler keeps "for each file … the ABI it produces, and the names (not full declarations) that were looked up during its compilation" ([The Dark Secrets of Fast Compilation for Kotlin](https://blog.jetbrains.com/kotlin/2020/09/the-dark-secrets-of-fast-compilation-for-kotlin/)). GHC "identifies orphan modules, and visits the interface file of every orphan module below the module being compiled" for the instance no name leads to ([Separate compilation](https://ghc.gitlab.haskell.org/ghc/doc/users_guide/separate_compilation.html)). Curios's orphan rule and its keys spelled before elaboration are what let a goal's read be one key, found or absent.
- **A scope is read as it is asked for.** rustc's metadata is "A list of lazily-decoded values, with the added capability of random access" ([`rustc_metadata::rmeta`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/nightly/nightly-rustc/src/rustc_metadata/rmeta/mod.rs.html)). GHC finds an instance by names alone: "we use the is_cls_nm :: Name and is_tcs :: [RoughMatchTc] fields to perform a "rough match", *without* poking inside the DFunId" ([`GHC.Core.InstEnv`](https://hackage-content.haskell.org/package/ghc-9.10.3/docs/src/GHC.Core.InstEnv.html)).
- **Code is collected from roots.** rustc's collector: "Lazy strategy means that items will only be instantiated when actually used. The goal is to produce the least amount of machine code possible", while "Eager strategy is meant to be used in conjunction with incremental compilation where a stable set of mono items is more important than a minimal one" ([`rustc_monomorphize::collector`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/nightly/nightly-rustc/rustc_monomorphize/collector/index.html)). Erased by declaration and linked by program, a unit's set is stable and a program's is minimal.

## Decisions

1. **A declaration has a cell for each stage, written once.** Its lowered form; what it elaborates to, with the registry entries, vectors and totality it publishes and, where it is a witness, its key; the certifier's verdict on it; and its erased form. One task writes each, and reads another declaration through its cells alone.
2. **Every read goes through one environment, and is recorded.** A read is a request for one cell — a signature read apart from a body unfolded — or a question no cell holds: whether a witness stands at a key, a key found apart from a key absent, and whether the unit's lemmas were on offer to the declaration's proofs. The elaborator records its reads in the order it made them.
3. **A record is its reader's own, stored with its unit.** The elaborator's for what the declaration elaborated to, the certifier's for its verdict, as `Certification` holds it, and erasure's for the erased form.
4. **A cell is reused where what it was made from stands.** Its own input is unchanged — the text and the interfaces it resolved against for a lowered form, the lowered form for an elaboration, the elaborated declaration for a verdict and for an erased form — and every read its record holds finds a cell equal to the one it read, each reader comparing what it reads of a cell. Reads are revalidated in the order they were made, each one's declaration settled first, and a cell made again and found equal dirties nothing.
5. **The certifier decides which of its verdicts stand.** It is handed the baseline whole and adopts a verdict where the declaration's terms are the baseline's and every cell its own filed reads name is unchanged, a declaration's verdict among them. No closure is handed in, and [Cached verdicts](../../design/soundness/cached-verdicts.md)' per-item argument is restated over the certifier's record in the change that makes it so.
6. **Whether a proof was offered the unit's lemmas is one bit of its declaration's record.** The walk elaborates what the vocabulary reads before it offers anything, whole or over a baseline, so a declaration settled on the other side of that line from its record is elaborated again, and no graph is consulted.
7. **Erasure is a declaration's, addressed by name.** A declaration erases into a form of its own, in identities of its own: another declaration's value or function is an import by name, and so is the row of a structure or an inductive, which the declaration's own form registers; a constant or an erased binding is read where it is used. A unit holds its declarations' erased forms, and no arena.
8. **A program links what it reaches.** Its entry's imports, transitively, and every declaration in scope whose top-level statement computes at initialization, anything but a group of functions or a bare construction over atoms. The optimizer's prune then keeps what is reached or observable, so what a program runs at initialization does not follow what it links.
9. **A module whose text and interfaces stand is not lowered again.** Its lowered declarations, registry entries, mints and lints are the baseline's, and what the lowering decides of a unit as a whole is decided from its modules' parts.
10. **A witness is named by its module, its concept and the key it is spelled at.** Where it sits among its module's witnesses is no part of it, and a unit's witness table is its witnesses' names.
11. **A unit's scope is a view.** `Established`, `Globals` and erasure's scope read through to the units, and nothing of a scope is copied, replayed or keyed again.
12. **A unit is addressed by what it depends on.** Its slot, its record and the units it is compiled against name its dependencies' closure, and no unit it could not have read.
13. **Invalidation is within a unit.** A baseline is offered only after the same dependencies, so a read of another unit stands on the unit's address and is not revalidated.

## Open questions

- **What a store write costs.** A unit is serialized whole each time it is filed, hash-consed in one table, where one declaration changed. Whether a slot can be written by its parts without giving up one node for one structure is not known.
- **Whether a process restores an image one declaration at a time.** Validating and restoring the prelude is paid before a program's first declaration, whatever the program reads, and an erased form for each item costs more to restore than one arena did. A lazy restoration keeps one pool of shared nodes per image and meets [item tasks](04-item-tasks.md)' restoration once per process.
- **Whether a process that has not parsed a file may skip it.** A file that stands is not lowered again; whether it is parsed depends on what the lowering's unit-wide passes need of a surface tree. Where they need it, a cold question's parse is the parser's cost, the finding in [`03-surface`](../03-surface/00-findings.md).
- **Whether a declaration that computes at initialization is linked before it is known to be observable.** A program links every one in its scope with what it reaches, and the optimizer's prune then drops the ones whose evaluation nothing observes. Deciding it before the link needs a declaration's divergence beside the effects its interface states, and a function is marked total only where the linked program meets the certifier's records.
- **Invalidation across units.** A unit compiled against an edited dependency is compiled whole. Reusing its declarations needs each read of another unit to carry what it read, since no baseline holds a dependency's earlier cells.

## The components

| Component | Treatment | Stage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lowering | Per module where its text and the unit's interfaces stand; unit-wide passes over the modules' parts | Lowering |
| Witness names | Module, concept and spelled key; the key's types in `curios-core` | Witnesses |
| Reads | Recorded where the environment is asked, per cell and per key, in order, hits as misses, and stored with the unit | Records |
| Invalidation | By recorded reads, revalidated in order, stopping where a cell comes out equal; `invalidated`'s reverse closure and `vocabulary_reach` are deleted | Records |
| Verdicts | Adopted by the certifier from the baseline by its own reads | Records |
| Scope | `Established`, `Globals` and erasure's scope are views | Views |
| Addresses | A slot, a record and a unit's predecessors are its dependencies' | Addresses |

## Stages

Each lands alone, on its own check, and says what it expects to move; a move outside that is a finding.

1. **Lowering.** Decision 9. Expected to move: a recompile's lowering.
2. **Witnesses.** Decision 10. Expected to move: a stored unit's bytes, by its witnesses' names and the order they give, and every report that names a witness.
3. **Records.** Decisions 1 to 6. Expected to move: a stored unit's bytes, by its records; what elaboration costs, by the recording; and which declarations a recompile redoes.
4. **Views.** Decision 11. Expected to move: what a compile costs before its first declaration.
5. **Addresses.** Decisions 12 and 13. Expected to move: every slot's address, and which units an edit to a dependency compiles.

## Verification

- **The cost rows.** `cargo xtask profile programs/hello_world.crs --profile release` builds the compiler and files the program's rows. For the library, a copy of `curios-std/src` with no `.curios` beside it, and `target/release/curios --profile <stream> wonder diagnostics <copy>/Nat.crs`, folded by `curios profile <stream>`: as it is; with `pub let _census_probe(a: Nat) -> Nat = a;` appended to `Nat.crs`; and with `xor(b, true)` respelled `xor(true, b)` in `Bool.crs`, asked of `Bool.crs`. The rows are `parse` over its modules, `into_core_unit` less it, `elaborate_and_zonk_unit_over`, `recheck_module`, `erase_unit`, `verdicts::put`, `restore_archives`, and for the program `compile_entrypoint`, `elaborate_and_zonk`, `recheck`, `erase_unit`, `link_program` and `lower_from_ersd`, each by its allocations. Counted. Their durations are a second measurement, timed, the median of repeated readings on a quiet machine, and no stage quotes one it has not taken so.
- **Lowering.** A unit lowered over a baseline is the unit lowered whole, its lints and reports with it, held by `incremental_tests` over an edited body, an added declaration and a changed interface.
- **Witnesses.** A unit and the same witnesses written in another order store the same module, and a moved witness's dependents are reused.
- **The record is complete.** An ignored measurement beside `std_replay_census`: each declaration of `/std` elaborated against the declarations its record names and no other gives the declaration the library holds. Counted.
- **Invalidation.** A recompile over a baseline redoes exactly the declarations whose recorded reads changed, held by fixtures over a signature change, a body change, a witness added at a key a goal found empty, and a declaration moved into what the prover's vocabulary reaches; a module handed to the certifier with a changed dependency is judged again where it read it; a unit compiled over a baseline is still the bytes it is compiled whole; and `std_recompile_closure_census` is retaken for its hub.
- **Views.** `budget_tests` holds a program to the budget it takes whatever its scope holds, and the program's rows are retaken.
- **Addresses.** Over a package depending on two packages that do not name each other, an edit to either leaves the other reused, held by a test.
- **The graph's figures.** The nodes are `/std`'s declarations, an item with the items generated from it. An edge is a name `Module::reaches` gives for an item of one, declared by another: lowered, over the module `into_core_unit` gives for the library's tree, and elaborated, over the restored prelude's. Counted.
- **The closure's figures.** The nodes are the restored prelude's items and an edge is a name `Module::reaches`; the reverse closure is `invalidated`'s; body readers are the items whose `Certification::reads` hold the name among their bodies. Counted.
- **The void attempts.** The `demand::void` samples of the profile the prelude's elaboration files, `curios-prelude-archive/.artifacts/profile.tsv`, one for each attempt at a declaration that read another before it had finished. Counted.

## Design decisions this overturns or corrects

- [A stored unit is a baseline for an item-level recompile](../../design/compilation/a-stored-unit-is-a-baseline-for-an-item-level-recompile.md): invalidation by a reverse closure. Records.
- [Cached verdicts](../../design/soundness/cached-verdicts.md): the per-item argument restated over the certifier's record, and the address's ordered predecessors. Records, Addresses.
- [An independent kernel re-checks what the elaborator accepts](../../design/soundness/an-independent-kernel-re-checks-what-the-elaborator-accepts.md): which declarations a walk passes over. Records.
- [Reused payloads](../../design/soundness/reused-payloads.md): the ordered slots of a payload's predecessors. Addresses.
- [A declaration is a function of what it reads](../../design/compilation/a-declaration-is-a-function-of-what-it-reads.md): a witness named by its place, and what a recompile asks for with the vocabulary. Witnesses, Records.
- [A module is a compilation unit, and the prelude is an environment](../../design/compilation/a-module-is-a-compilation-unit-and-the-prelude-is-an-environment.md): a compilation as units folded over one order. Addresses.
- [`curios-unit`'s README](../../../curios-unit/README.md): *Predecessors are borrowed, per stage*, with `Predecessors`' own documentation. Views.

## Rejected

- **salsa.** At 0.28 with no 1.0, it removed its experimental parallel feature in 0.25.0; its database model would own the term representation, and its invalidation would sit beside the store's trust argument rather than under it. The graph here has thousands of nodes and obligations — budgets, deterministic identities, charged caches — that a small component states directly.
- **The certifier's reads as the elaborator's record.** They are what its judgment of the finished terms read, and a key found absent, a candidate tried and dropped and a lemma offered and not applied are in no finished term.
- **The names an elaborated declaration reaches as the record.** It is what a unit's items are ordered by, and it holds no read that left no name.
- **A closure the pipeline hands the certifier**, narrowed by the elaborator's record: the trusted base would rest on a record the certifier did not make.
- **A digest of each cell in a record.** Within a unit the baseline holds the cell itself, and a term's comparison ends at a pointer or a cached hash.
- **A stored order of declarations**, Excel's calc chain. A reused declaration's reads are settled before it is examined, in the order its record gives, so only a declaration made again can meet one unfinished, and a whole compile has no baseline to learn an order from.
- **An erased arena for each unit, relocated where units meet.** Two arenas both count from zero, so every identity a unit hands on would be rewritten by every unit that reads it, where a name is the same in all of them.
- **Linking every declaration in scope and pruning**: a program's first analysis walks the library to drop nearly all of it.
- **Evaluating a top-level value only where it is read.** It is the language's meaning that moves, a declaration whose initialization is observable running or not by what a program names.
- **A witness named by its concept and key alone.** Two units declaring one witness would hold one name, and what refuses them is the elaborator's coherence check, which runs after names are stored by mount.
- **Erasure per declaration left to [item tasks](04-item-tasks.md).** An arena every unit's erasure resumes over orders the units and is what a recompile pays most for, and neither waits on a second worker.

## Completion and retirement

Done when every stage's cell for a declaration is written once and reused where what it read stands, a recompile lowers, elaborates, judges and erases the declarations whose reads changed and no other, a program links what it reaches, and a unit's scope and address name what it reads. The rule, its rationale and what is rejected go to a design decision under `documentation/design/compilation/`; the environment's and the linker's contracts are in the owning crates' READMEs and rustdoc, and `.claude/rules/`' area routing names them.
