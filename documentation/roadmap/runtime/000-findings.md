# Runtime: findings

- **Uncertain: a live set just under a doubling of the heap costs a program four times its time natively.** The rope of `a_loop_started_on_a_literal_rebuilds_the_links_it_takes_apart` (`curios/src/tests/codegen/rebuild.rs`, `ROPE` under `ROPE_FLIPPED_TWICE`), compiled at `348a17f9d` and run natively over n from 250 000 to 4 000 000, takes 120 to 170 ns a link except at n = 500 000, 1 000 000, 2 000 000 and 4 000 000, where it takes 610 to 630 ns a link: 307, 616, 1 255 and 2 520 ms, three timed readings each within 2%, rising from about 900 000 and 1 750 000 and falling past each. Under V8 the same module grows smoothly, 84 to 920 ms over the same points. The rope is live whole once built and the cliffs sit at doublings, so the reading is the copying collector, which the engine grows only where an allocation does not fit after a collection (`curios-native/README.md`, *The heap is sized ahead of its churn*), recopying a live set that nearly fills the heap at each allocation it cannot fit. Not known: whether collections are what a cliff costs, how many bytes a link takes, whether the cliff is the engine's growth rule or the thirty-two mebibytes it starts from, and which other programs meet one. Settled by: the collections each n makes, counted from the engine's `Begin GC` records through `curios_native::test_support::collected` as `rope_alternation_collections` counts them, over the same grid and at a finer step around each cliff, beside the times. Size unknown until then.
