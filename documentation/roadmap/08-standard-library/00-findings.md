# Standard library: findings

- **A terminal session reads a failed read as silence.** `Tui/Session`'s `read` answers a failed read as the end of input (its `error(_)` arm), and ends a burst of input on `_ => settle()`, which covers a timeout, the end of standard input and a failed read alike; `Tui/run`'s ticker retries a failed size query forever (`failure(_) => ticker(session)`, `Tui.crs`). A program whose terminal has failed keeps running, without input and without a reason. Fix: `Session/read` answers a `Try` and raises a failed read from either arm, and `Tui/run`'s reader and ticker hand a failure to its loop, which ends the run with its `Io/Error`. The end of input stops the reader and the program runs on, as [bubbletea's](https://github.com/charmbracelet/bubbletea/blob/v1.3.4/tty.go) `readLoop` does, which passes every other read error to the program's error channel; `Tui/run`'s documentation, silent on both, states it. A failure is no `Event`, which would widen a type every program matches. Check: over the mock host, a failed read and a failed size query each end `Tui/run` with their error, through the `Lying` host, which `tests::host`'s `test_support` keeps to itself and `tests::tui` then shares; after the end of input a program still quits through its own directive; `a_session_carries_incomplete_input_into_its_next_read` (`curios/src/tests/tui.rs`) is unchanged. Size: medium.
- **`Flt`'s `div_mod` answers `(0, r)` for a zero divisor** (`Flt.crs`). Its documentation says every caller's divisor is positive and the arm discharges `/`'s precondition by the match, which is a made-up value on a branch no one proves unreachable. Fix: `div_mod` takes `@positive: Holds(0 < s)` and loses the arm, and its three callers discharge it — `generate` by carrying the fact down from `render`'s `setup` and `scale_up`, `of_decimal_in` over `d0`, which is `1` or a power of ten, and its shift, and `decimal_digits` over a product of `Nat/shl(1, _)` and `Nat/pow(10, _)`. No lemma is missing for it: `0 < Nat/pow(10, k)` holds by conversion, a power of a base at least one being at least one, and `0 < d` gives `0 < Nat/shl(d, k)`, a shift reading as its value times such a power and a product of two positives being the elaborator's to prove. Check: `/std` builds without the arm, and the rounding tests pass. Size: medium.
- **Two arms no input reaches answer a value, and a counted walk has no category** (`Tui/Layout.crs`, `http/Server.crs`). [`curios-std`'s README](../../../curios-std/README.md#std-invents-no-value-where-a-proof-belongs-and-text-is-addressed-by-position) keeps a default only where it is the specified answer. The calls of `unwrap_or` have their verdict: the eight left, in seven modules, each answer what a standard or the operation's own documented contract specifies, or no default at all — the zero a run of digits accumulates from (`Tui/input.crs`) and the length of a run that is all whitespace (`http/octets.crs`). The arms were swept by shape — an absence or a dropped failure answered with a value, a default arm, an arm for the empty sequence, a refused guard, a counted walk — and beyond `div_mod` above and the two entries below, two answer on a branch nothing reaches: `Tui/Layout`'s `share_head` and `share_tail` answer `0` and `[]` past the end of a list `distribute` builds one share a constraint long, and `http/Server`'s `dated` answers the reply without its `Date` where the date it has just spelled does not read as a field. None was found that an input reaches. A clamp spelled as arithmetic, a subtraction at `Nat` or a `Nat/min`, is outside the sweep. One kind of default has no category in the README's list: a count a walk provably never exhausts, its reason stated, as `List/balanced` and `Flt`'s `scaling_fuel`, `digit_fuel` and `hex_fuel` state theirs. Proving those away is not the fix: the fuels' bounds are argued where they are declared. Fix: the README's list gains that kind, and each of the two has its unreachability proved and its arm gone. **Uncertain, and warrants investigation:** what carries each proof — the shares' length, the octets of a date. Check: `/std` builds without the two arms. Size: medium.
- **`http/octets` cuts at offsets nothing bounds** (`http/octets.crs`, `http/read.crs`, `http/syntax.crs`, `http/Field.crs`). `front`, `past` and `between` take a bare offset and answer the whole run, or none of it, where the offset falls outside, which their documentation states and 26 calls lean on. At most of them the offset is one a search has just answered or a guard has just decided — `read/length` cuts at `n` under `n <= Bytes/len(held)`, `read/head` and `syntax/lines` at what `line_end` and `blank_line` found, `octets/split` and `Field`'s `pieces` at a fold's own count — so the clamp's arm is one no input reaches, answering a value where a proof belongs. A few are reached on purpose: `syntax/status_line` cuts a line shorter than its fixed columns and refuses what is left. Fix: a cut under a guard is `Bytes/slice` and `Bytes/drop`, whose bounds the guard discharges, as a probe of `read/length`'s shape shows; `line_end` and `blank_line` answer their index with the proof that it lies within the bytes, as `Tui/input`'s `find` does; a reader that refuses a short run decides its length first; and the three go once nothing calls them. **Uncertain, and warrants investigation:** how the two folds carry the bound of the count they keep. Check: `/std` builds without the three, and the `http` test units pass. Size: medium.
- **A code point a guard has bounded becomes a `Char` only through an `Option`** (`Char.crs`, `Tui/input.crs`). `Char/of_nat` and `Char/of_ascii` answer an `Option` and `Char`'s representation is private, so a caller that has already decided its code is a scalar value must still answer a `none` no input reaches: `Tui/input`'s `control` answers the Escape key there, twice, under `n <= 0x1A` and under a control byte past it, and its `escape` answers no key under `Nat/in_range(second, 0x20, 0x7E)`. `List/get`, `Bytes/get` and `Flt/to_nat` each stand under a proof beside their `try_` form; `Char` has the checked one alone. Fix: `Char/of_code(code: Nat, @valid: Char/Valid(code))`, discharged below the surrogates through `Valid/below_surrogates` as `Char/to_ascii_lower` discharges its own, and the three arms gone. **Uncertain, and warrants investigation:** whether `scalar`'s arm for a text with no first character goes the same way, the text being one `Str/of_bytes` has just accepted at one to four bytes. Check: `/std` builds without the arms, and `curios-std/src/tests/tui/input.crs` passes. Size: quick.
- **`Str`'s `pending` states its range once per count** (`Str.crs`). **Uncertain, and warrants investigation:** whether its uses still close by reduction over `Nat/pow`. It spells the range at literal weights for one, two and three continuation bytes, where `Nat/pow(0x40, r)` could state it once. Its documentation defends the literal weights, and the nine uses that reduce it in proofs are what a rewrite puts at risk. A probe answers for the two that take it apart: with `pending` stated once over `Nat/pow(0x40, r)` behind a guard `r <= 2`, `concluded` and `advanced` check as they are written, since each splits on the count before it reads the range and a power at a literal exponent reduces. The other seven, in `Str.crs` and `Str/Valid.crs`, are unprobed, and a count that stays symbolic would not reduce, `Nat/pow` being a recursion by squaring. Fix: state it over `Nat/pow` only if every use still closes by reduction; otherwise leave it. Check: `/std` builds. Size: quick.
- **`Hash/digest` bounds its lanes at `2³¹`, one bit past what a `Nat` holds unboxed** (`Hash.crs`). **Uncertain, and warrants investigation:** whether the bound was meant as the unboxed one, and what the boxed steps cost, which nothing has measured. Its documentation chooses each lane's prime and base so that every intermediate stays below `2^31`, without saying what that buys, while a `Nat` is an `i31ref` only below `2³⁰` ([Nat and Int are an i31 until they outgrow it](../../design/arithmetic/nat-and-int-are-an-i31-until-they-outgrow-it.md)). Each lane's product passes it: an accumulator at the top of its range, times its base, plus a byte and one, reaches `1 098 905 808`, `1 149 235 716` and `1 166 011 486` over the three lanes, against `2³⁰ = 1 073 741 824`. By that arithmetic alone, a step whose accumulator lies in the top 2.3%, 6.6% and 7.9% of its lane's range builds a `$big` that the `%` behind it takes back. Neither `digest` nor `Hash/of` has a caller in `/std`, the corpus or `programs/`, so no program pays it today. Fix: if the bound is the unboxed one, a base at or below `127` keeps every intermediate below `2³⁰` under the same primes, and the documentation names the bound for what it is; a digest is documented as unstable across compiler versions, so its bytes may change. Otherwise the sentence says what `2^31` is for. Check: `/std` builds, and a corpus fixture pins a digest's bytes, which none does today. Size: quick.
- **Three handle wrappers are built by anyone holding a `Handle`.** `Command/Pipe/of_handle`, `Command/Child/of_handle` and `tcp/Socket/of_handle` are public because each type is declared in a module of its own and built by its parent or a sibling — `Command/spawn` builds a `Child` and its pipes, `tcp/Listener` an accepted `Socket` — and a private representation is visible only beneath the module that declares it. So a program wraps a file's or a terminal's handle as a `Socket` or a `Pipe` and reads it through the wrapper's `Async` witnesses, where the type says the handle came from a connection or a spawn. **Uncertain, and warrants investigation:** whether the answer is the library's or the language's — each type declared in the module whose subtree holds every builder, as `/std/Async/Probe` is, or a visibility that reaches the parent's subtree, which the surface does not have.
- **What the merge sort and its neighbours cost is not measured.** The machine they landed on was shared, so no figure is stated for any of four changes: `List/sort_by` as a merge sort (`curios-std/src/List/sort_by.crs`), where the sort it replaced stopped at five thousand elements; `Bytes/ord` and `Bits/ord` as walks over both runs (`curios-std/src/Bytes/ord.crs`, `Bits.crs`), which `Str`'s comparisons go through, against the index walks they replaced; a program's start under a stack of one gibibyte (`curios-runtime/src/engine.rs`); and the prelude's build with the sort's proofs in it. Fix: take them on a quiet machine. The sort's protocol: a program builds `n` naturals into a list by appending, in one of three orders — ascending from zero, descending to zero, and the sequence `s ↦ (1103515245 · s + 12345) mod 2³¹` from 42 with each term reduced modulo 1,000,003 — sorts it and prints its length and first element, beside a control that prints the same of the unsorted list; each runs through `cargo run --release --package curios -- run -` at `n` of 100,000 and 1,000,000, the sort and its control interleaved over three readings, reported as a median with its span. The others are read against their parents: the comparison's against the commit before the walks changed, the start's by `hello_world`'s wall time and resident set, the build's by `curios-prelude-archive`'s build profile. Check: the figures, and a decision that rests on one stating it. Size: quick.
- **`Str/repeat` builds a text of `n` copies out of `n` pieces** (`Str.crs`). It is `List/balanced` over `n` singles, each the text itself, so a repetition is a tree of `n` leaves and `n - 1` concatenations where the two halves of every level are the same text. One reading each, at `a490a9092`, of a program compiled with `curios compile` that builds the text and searches it for a character it does not hold: sixteen million copies of one character peak at 2 052 MiB resident, and the same text built by twenty-four concatenations of a text with itself at 52 MiB. `Bytes` and `Bits` have no repetition to share one with. Fix: build each level once and concatenate it with itself, the odd copy beside it, on the fuel `List/balanced` descends on. Check: rows in `curios-std/src/tests/strings/str.crs`, which holds none for `repeat` today: zero, one and an odd count of copies, and a count the tree of leaves does not fit a test in. Size: quick.
- **A reading of the monotonic clock has two types, and the scheduler keeps the one that is a length.** `/std/time/Instant` is the reading, opaque and good for measuring alone, and `/std/time/Duration/now` hands the same reading out as a `Duration` since an origin nobody names. `Async`'s scheduler keeps a sleeper's deadline as `due: Duration` and reads the clock through `Duration/now()` at six sites (`curios-std/src/Async.crs`), since an `Instant` offers no way to add a `Duration` to it: a deadline, which is a moment, is typed as a length, and `Duration/before(now, s.due)` orders two moments with the comparison of lengths. Fix: `Instant/add(Instant, Duration)`, the scheduler holding `Instant`, and `Duration/now` removed, `Instant/now` reading the clock itself. Check: nothing in `/std` calls `Duration/now`, and `curios`'s `tests::scheduler` passes. Size: quick.
- **A step over text costs a type several times a step over bytes.** **Uncertain, and warrants investigation:** what the units are spent on. Over 32 letters at `85da54b97`, `Parse/text/take_while` costs each checker about 48 900 units where `Parse/bytes/take_while` costs about 9 900, and `Parse/text/literal` about 74 900 against 18 400, where a reader that takes no step costs about 8 500 (`parse_primitive_measurements`, `curios/src/tests/reduction.rs`, counted); a `Str` literal's validity, a fold the closed machine runs over the same bytes, costs 69 units a character (`str_literal_cost_measurements`, beside it). A text walk decodes a character, builds the position past it and descends on a proof of accessibility a step (`Str/span` and `Str/follows` over `Str/At/forward`), and nothing measured says which of the three the units are, or whether any is work the erased program does not do. Fix: once that is known. Check: `parse_primitive_measurements`, read again. Size: unknown.
