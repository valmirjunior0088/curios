# Standard library: findings

- **A terminal session reads a failed read as silence.** `Tui/Session`'s `read` answers a failed read as the end of input (its `error(_)` arm), and ends a burst of input on `_ => settle()`, which covers a timeout, the end of standard input and a failed read alike; `Tui/run`'s ticker retries a failed size query forever (`failure(_) => ticker(session)`, `Tui.crs`). A program whose terminal has failed keeps running, without input and without a reason. Fix: `Session/read` answers a `Try` and raises a failed read from either arm, and `Tui/run`'s reader and ticker hand a failure to its loop, which ends the run with its `Io/Error`. The end of input stops the reader and the program runs on, as [bubbletea's](https://github.com/charmbracelet/bubbletea/blob/v1.3.4/tty.go) `readLoop` does, which passes every other read error to the program's error channel; `Tui/run`'s documentation, silent on both, states it. A failure is no `Event`, which would widen a type every program matches. Check: over the mock host, a failed read and a failed size query each end `Tui/run` with their error, through the `Lying` host, which `tests::host`'s `test_support` keeps to itself and `tests::tui` then shares; after the end of input a program still quits through its own directive; `a_session_carries_incomplete_input_into_its_next_read` (`curios/src/tests/tui.rs`) is unchanged. Size: medium.
- **`Flt`'s `div_mod` answers `(0, r)` for a zero divisor** (`Flt.crs`). Its documentation says every caller's divisor is positive and the arm discharges `/`'s precondition by the match, which is a made-up value on a branch no one proves unreachable. Fix: `div_mod` takes `@positive: Nat/Lt(0, s)` and loses the arm, and its three callers discharge it — `generate` by carrying the fact down from `render`'s `setup` and `scale_up`, `of_decimal_in` over `d0`, which is `1` or a power of ten, and its shift, and `decimal_digits` over a product of `Nat/shl(1, _)` and `Nat/pow(10, _)`. That needs two lemmas `/std` lacks and the elaborator does not prove, as probes show: `0 < Nat/pow(10, k)`, and `0 < d` giving `0 < Nat/shl(d, k)`, which it refuses even at `d = 1`; a product of two positives it does prove, so the lemmas are all the callers need. The first is an induction in `/std/Nat` either way; the second is one too until [`pow` is a declared operation](../04-arithmetic/02-declared-operations.md), which reads a shift as a product by a power and leaves it to the first. Check: `/std` builds without the arm, and the rounding tests pass. Size: medium.
- **No inventory says which defaults are specified.** [`curios-prelude-archive`'s README](../../../curios-prelude-archive/README.md#std-invents-no-value-where-a-proof-belongs-and-text-is-addressed-by-position) keeps a default only where it is the specified answer, which needs a verdict for every value the library answers where its input gave none — 16 calls of `unwrap_or` in 10 modules beside its two definitions, and the arms answering a literal in place of a missing value — and none has been taken. Two kinds of default have no category in the README's list: a total projection only a proof reads — `Bytes/head` and `Map/byte_of`, whose value on empty bytes nothing observes — and a count a walk provably never exhausts, its reason stated, as `List/balanced` and `Flt`'s `scaling_fuel` state theirs. Proving those away is not the fix: the projections are total because congruence needs one function of the whole value, and the fuels' bounds are argued where they are declared. Fix: the README's list gains the two kinds, and every other site gets its verdict where it stands — its unreachability proved and the default gone, the standard or contract that specifies it cited in its documentation, or the spec that changes it named: a text walk under fuel is [text read as text](02-text-read-as-text.md)'s, and HTTP's defaults are [HTTP framing](01-http-framing.md)'s. Check: a verdict per site, where it stands. Size: medium.
- **`Str`'s `pending` states its range once per count** (`Str.crs`). **Uncertain, and warrants investigation:** whether its uses still close by reduction over `Nat/pow`. It spells the range at literal weights for one, two and three continuation bytes, where `Nat/pow(0x40, r)` could state it once. Its documentation defends the literal weights, and the nine uses that reduce it in proofs are what a rewrite puts at risk. A probe answers for the two that take it apart: with `pending` stated once over `Nat/pow(0x40, r)` behind a guard `r <= 2`, `concluded` and `advanced` check as they are written, since each splits on the count before it reads the range and a power at a literal exponent reduces. The other seven, in `Str.crs` and `Str/Valid.crs`, are unprobed, and a count that stays symbolic would not reduce, `Nat/pow` being a recursion by squaring. Fix: state it over `Nat/pow` only if every use still closes by reduction; otherwise leave it. Check: `/std` builds. Size: quick.
- **`Hash/digest` bounds its lanes at `2³¹`, one bit past what a `Nat` holds unboxed** (`Hash.crs`). **Uncertain, and warrants investigation:** whether the bound was meant as the unboxed one, and what the boxed steps cost, which nothing has measured. Its documentation chooses each lane's prime and base so that every intermediate stays below `2^31`, without saying what that buys, while a `Nat` is an `i31ref` only below `2³⁰` ([Nat and Int are an i31 until they outgrow it](../../design/arithmetic/nat-and-int-are-an-i31-until-they-outgrow-it.md)). Each lane's product passes it: an accumulator at the top of its range, times its base, plus a byte and one, reaches `1 098 905 808`, `1 149 235 716` and `1 166 011 486` over the three lanes, against `2³⁰ = 1 073 741 824`. By that arithmetic alone, a step whose accumulator lies in the top 2.3%, 6.6% and 7.9% of its lane's range builds a `$big` that the `%` behind it takes back. Neither `digest` nor `Hash/of` has a caller in `/std`, the corpus or `programs/`, so no program pays it today. Fix: if the bound is the unboxed one, a base at or below `127` keeps every intermediate below `2³⁰` under the same primes, and the documentation names the bound for what it is; a digest is documented as unstable across compiler versions, so its bytes may change. Otherwise the sentence says what `2^31` is for. Check: `/std` builds, and a corpus fixture pins a digest's bytes, which none does today. Size: quick.
- **`/std/Map` conflates keys of two types whose encodings coincide** (`Map.crs`). `Key(K)`'s law is `injective(a: K, b: K, same: Eq()(Hash/hash(a), Hash/hash(b))) -> Eq()(a, b)`, quantified over one type, so nothing relates the encodings of two; `Hash(Str)`'s `Str/to_bytes` and `Hash(Bytes)`'s identity therefore agree on `"a"` and `x[97]`. Because `Map(V)` carries no key type, a value filed under the `Str` key reads back under the `Bytes` one: `Map/get(@Bytes, Map/insert(@Str, Map/empty(), "a", 7), x[97])` answers `some(7)`. No law is broken and no `False` follows — the law never claimed cross-type injectivity — and no consumer is affected, since `Set(K)` is consistent in its `K` and `Toml` keys everything by `Bytes`. What is missing is the statement: the module documents the neighbouring hazard, that one type under two dictionaries gives two members, and is silent on this one. Fix: [a map keyed by its key type](04-a-map-keyed-by-its-key-type.md) makes the read a type error; until it lands, `Key`'s own documentation says that its law holds within a type and that a map over encodings admits any type encoding alike. Check: the probe above answers `none()` once the key type is in the type, and refuses to elaborate once `get` is keyed. Size: quick for the sentence, medium for the fix.
