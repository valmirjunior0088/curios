# Conversion is one relation in both checkers

Working specification for holding conversion to its laws at every rule it has, with each checker asked by itself. A conversion verdict is a function of the two terms and their type: it follows neither the checker that asked, nor the path that checker took to the question, nor the position the pair sits in. The two checkers never ask the same goals of one program, so what makes a program that elaborates certify is that the relation each decides is closed under its laws, and that it is the same relation. The audit states the laws as rows, derives each row's verdict from the equation it was built from, and reads each checker's answer apart from the other's.

It is independent of every other spec; [Checked evidence and trusted reasoning](03-checked-evidence.md) names it in its verification. The audit stands, and what remains is what it found: the solver's re-validation, the elaborator's eta brought to the order the kernel fires it in, and the record of the decision.

## What this builds on

- **Two checkers, one shared layer.** Each runs its own conversion over the representation, the carriers' algebra and the chain both run when two intrinsics meet ([An independent kernel re-checks what the elaborator accepts](../../design/soundness/an-independent-kernel-re-checks-what-the-elaborator-accepts.md)).
- **The law grid.** `curios`'s `tests::laws` states every family `curios-algebra`'s law table declares, at every carrier declaring it (`generated`), and by hand the laws no family states, the controls and the refused candidates (`written`). A held row is an `Eq/refl()` proof compiled through both checkers (`closes`); every row is also a written goal read back through the `? ≈ Eq/refl()` candidate line (`misplaced`).
- **The audit of the theory.** `tests::laws::audit` puts every declared law reversed, chained through a shared side and substituted at compound terms, and holds constructors free modulo the theory: the conditions [The carriers' algebra stays in conversion](../../design/arithmetic/the-carriers-algebra-stays-in-conversion.md) takes from Coq Modulo Theory.
- **One fixture states a path.** `written`'s `a_step_taken_through_an_annotation_is_one_the_kernel_takes_in_one` holds that a step the elaborator takes through a `let`'s annotation is one the kernel takes comparing the first type with the last ([An atom is one where conversion says so](../../design/arithmetic/an-atom-is-one-where-conversion-says-so.md)).
- **Two doors read one checker.** `curios-pipeline`'s `typecheck_with_prelude` lowers and elaborates a program and stops short of the kernel, handing back the program it built. `recheck_with_prelude` puts a program to the kernel alone with the prelude in scope, which is how `xboard` puts a module built by hand.
- **The audit of conversion's own rules.** `tests::laws::structural` states a seed for each rule no law table states — beta, delta, zeta, iota at each elimination, eta at a function, a record, a struct and a unit, irrelevance — a held equation against a neutral, and the near miss beside it. `contexts` states every seed under each child a term former holds, and a lint reads back where each hole landed against a `match` over Core's formers with no wildcard. `audit` states every seed reversed, chained through a shared side and at a compound term. A row states no side: `asked_alone` reads the elaborator by the proof and the kernel by a claim put to it alone, and `parted` lists every row a checker puts on the other side than its seed derives, held equal to what the audits find.
- **Where each checker fires eta.** The kernel is type-directed first: at a function type it applies both sides to fresh binders, at a Σ it projects both, and at a type with no field — the empty Σ, a nominal struct that declares none — it accepts, each ahead of every structural rule (`compare`, `curios-cert/src/kernel/convert.rs`). Where no type directs it, a literal against a neutral fires the rule by its shape (`function_eta`, `tuple_eta`, `struct_eta`), and `whnf` contracts `(x) => f(x)` to `f`. The elaborator is structure-first: it decides a goal at a type with no field by the type, beside irrelevance (`field_less`, `curios-elab/src/convert.rs`), fires eta by a literal against any term (`eta_expand_func`, `eta_expand_tuple`, `eta_expand_struct`), and by a function or a record type only between two sides no structural rule claims (`eta_expand_neutral`). Both refuse a literal's eta at a type former that is not the literal's own, by one classifier (`Subterm::is_type_former`, `curios-core`). A child the kernel has no type for is compared at `Type` ([Eta and untyped child positions](../../design/soundness/conversion/eta-and-untyped-child-positions.md)).

## The gap

**The checkers walk one program by different goals.** The elaborator takes an annotation as a step where the kernel compares the first type with the last; it compares a part where it is written where the kernel compares the whole it sits in; it checks a definition before the kernel unfolds it. Equal verdicts on equal goals do not make the two agree on a program. Each law of the relation absorbs one of those differences, and each place the checkers part is a law failing in one of them:

| Law | The difference it absorbs | Where it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transitivity | A step through an annotation against the first type compared with the last | The elaborator, between two terms of one stuck shape at a record of units or a function into a unit; the truth table's cap in both, by design ([What conversion still decides by spelling or by cap](../04-arithmetic/01-decided-by-spelling-or-cap.md)) |
| Congruence | A part compared where it is written against the whole compared where it sits | Both checkers, at two neutrals of a unit under a child compared at `Type` |
| Stability under respelling | A term compared before a definition is unfolded against after | A case equation's key, which [What conversion still decides by spelling or by cap](../04-arithmetic/01-decided-by-spelling-or-cap.md) holds |
| One set of rules | — | Π and Σ eta by the goal's type, which the elaborator fires only between two sides no structural rule claims ([the findings](00-findings.md)); eta by a literal, which the elaborator takes against any term and the kernel against a neutral |

**The elaborator's eta is not type-directed first.** At a record of units, `match b: (_) => {{}, {}} | true => r | false => r end` against the same match over `s` is accepted by the kernel, which projects both sides by the goal's type and decides each projection at `{}`, and refused by the elaborator, which compares two stuck matches arm against arm at `Type`. Each match converts with `((), ())` in the elaborator, so its conversion is not transitive there. The audit lists the rows: two neutrals at a record of units and at a function into a unit, in every arm of a stuck elimination and under a definition's argument. No program reaches the kernel with one, since the elaborator refuses first.

**Congruence stops where only a type directs a rule.** Two neutrals at a unit convert at their own type in both checkers, and are refused by both under a child neither compares at its type, a tuple literal's component in a stuck elimination's arm. Eta by a literal holds there in both.

**An implicit is refused against a lambda while its own type is unsolved.** `let t(g: (Nat) -> Nat) -> Eq()((x: Nat) => g(0), (x: Nat) => g(0)) = Eq/refl();` is refused, `inferred: ?` against `expected: (x) => g(0)`, and compiles once either `Eq` or `Eq/refl` states `@((Nat) -> Nat)`. The claim's two lambdas park against the claim's type `?B`, and `Eq/refl()` leaves `?A` against `?B` and its `z : ?A` against each lambda's placeholder, every one parked. The item's drain settles the first lambda, which pins `?B`; the sweep that does so holds every parked goal, so the wake for `?B` meets an empty store and is lost (`settle_synthesizable`, `curios-elab/src/typing.rs`; `wake_parked`, `context/solutions.rs`); and the next round tries `z` against the lambda with `?A` still unsolved, one retry short of `?A` against `?B`. `solve` (`curios-elab/src/convert.rs`) re-validates a candidate by checking it against its metavariable's frozen type under an oracle, where parking is suppressed: `elaborate_func_check` (`curios-elab/src/elaborate/binding.rs`) raises `not_a_function_type` where it would have parked, and re-validation reads that as a rejected candidate. The verdict follows the order the retries ran in.

## Prior art

- **Lennon-Bertrand** ([*What does it take to certify a conversion checker?*, FSCD 2025](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSCD.2025.27)). "In Rocq and Lean, conversion does not maintain any type information, and Agda's conversion, while primarily type-directed, similarly uses term-directed η-expansion of functions." An untyped checker replaces the type-directed rule by term-directed ones, with "no η-rule when the two sides are neutrals"; a definitional unit type and strict propositions "completely wreck completeness of neutral comparison" and need the type. It names three ways out: Agda's type-directed conversion, Rocq requiring a record to have a relevant field and so forbidding the definitional unit type, and Lean inferring a neutral's type again when the untyped comparison fails, with a criterion for unit-like types.
- **Abel and Coquand** ([*Untyped algorithmic equality for Martin-Löf's logical framework with surjective pairs*, Fundamenta Informaticae 77(4), 2007](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/175708)). An untyped βη-equality test, eta for functions and for pairs fired by a side's shape, is complete for the judgmental equality on well-typed terms.
- **Lean 4.33.1** ([release notes](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/releases/v4.33.1/)). The kernel's `is_def_eq` is not transitive, and a cache that closed it transitively made "a query's result depend on the order of earlier queries", from which a crafted input derives `False`; the fix makes `is_def_eq` "again a function of its two arguments".
- **Lean issue 2258** ([*DefEq transitivity failures for unit-like eta*](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/issues/2258)). `p` against the literal and the literal against `q` hold, and `p` against `q` does not: the failure above, open there.
- **The Lean Kernel Arena** ([arena.lean-lang.org](https://arena.lean-lang.org/)). Several kernels put to one battery of proofs to accept and proofs to reject, each answering accept, reject or decline, a declined test counting as neither.
- **Rocq's `SProp`** ([reference manual](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/v9.3/refman/addendum/sprop.html)). Each binder caches whether its type is a strict proposition, so conversion decides irrelevance with no type, and a mark elaboration left wrong makes a conversion fail.

## Decisions

Each with its reason, so a stage meets none of them as a fork. The audit is built to the first six, the kernel's eta by a literal to the seventh, unit eta to the twelfth and the refusal of a literal's eta at another type to the thirteenth. Curios promises no stability yet, so each fix takes the form that states its rule once, whatever it moves.

1. **The audit extends `tests::laws`, and a row is a program.** Both sides of a row elaborate at one type before either checker compares them, which is the invariant every conversion in a compilation stands on, so no row asks what no program can.
2. **A row's verdict is derived from its seed.** A seed is one equation a rule decides, or a near miss beside it. A held seed reversed, chained, substituted or placed under a context is held; a near miss under a context that keeps its hole is refused. No cell states a verdict by hand, and neither checker is the other's oracle.
3. **Each checker is asked by itself.** The elaborator's answer to a carrier's row is the candidate line, and to a seed's row the proof itself, elaborated and stopped short of the kernel, which costs less than a candidate search and names the refusal. The kernel's is a claim put to it alone: a row is stated as one call that pairs the claim with a proof that its left side equals itself, which elaborates whatever the row's side, and the claim `recheck_with_prelude` judges is that call's second half at its first, under the statement's binders. One call hands both halves one type and one left side, so no level separates them, and every term in the claim is one the elaborator built.
4. **A refusal by a spent budget is not a refusal.** A near miss is refused as a mismatch, and a row a checker exhausts on is its own class.
5. **A row the checkers part on is listed where the audit runs**, in one table naming its finding, held equal to what the run finds: a row that starts parting fails, and so does one that stops while it is still listed. The finding's text is [the findings](00-findings.md)'; a row driven to `/std/Bool/False` is a ticket in `xboard/src/board/conversion.rs`.
6. **The positions are held by a lint.** Where each context's hole landed is read back from the term the elaborator built, and the formers are matched with no wildcard, so a former added to Core states its contexts before the audit compiles. The lint holds formers and kinds of elimination; which child of a former a context targets is the context's to state.
7. **The fixes land with the audit.** The kernel fires Π and Σ eta by the literal against a neutral, the set `struct_eta` takes, under the invariant `struct_eta` states. Unit eta is decision 12's, a literal's eta at another type decision 13's, and the elaborator's order decisions 14 and 15.
8. **What needs a type the position does not carry stays a stated row.** Unit eta and irrelevance between two neutrals under a child compared at `Type`, and two neutrals at a nominal struct compared by their projections, are refused by both checkers, under an open roadmap line.
9. **A seed's row states its sides' type**, `Eq(@T)(left, right)`, so that neither reader leaves `Eq/refl()`'s implicit a type to infer and a row asks for a comparison, never for a solution.
10. **Re-validation has three answers.** A candidate fits its metavariable's type, does not, or cannot be judged yet, its check having met a type that still waits on a metavariable, where a check outside the oracle parks. `solve` commits on the first, fails on the second and postpones on the third, so a verdict does not follow the order parked work is retried in. The sites that park mark the refusal they raise under the oracle as undecided, as a mismatch already carries what declined it; the oracle's other readers, a goal candidate's fit (`suggest.rs`) and an entailment (`entailment.rs`), keep reading it as yes or no. Across `/std` re-validation rejects 34 candidates at `30e166df7`, counted from the notes `curios-prelude-archive`'s build profile records, every one a struct literal of the wrong field count and none undecided, so the change moves nothing there.
11. **Out of the audit.** A case equation's key is [What conversion still decides by spelling or by cap](../04-arithmetic/01-decided-by-spelling-or-cap.md)'s. A level is solved by the elaborator and judged by the kernel, which is not one question, so a row keeps levels out of what it asks: no seed is a type, since a `Type` written on each side settles a level of its own where the elaborator is not asked to compare them, and a hole that is a type is filled by a seed under a family over the seed's type. Recurrence stays with `kernel::convert::recursion_tests` unless a source program reaches it.
12. **Unit eta is the goal type's, at a type with no field, in both checkers.** The empty Σ and a nominal struct that declares no field decide their goal ahead of every structural rule, which is the zero-field case of eta by the goal's type and the place that rule sits. The kernel reaches a record of units and a function into a unit by composing it with the eta it fires by type; the elaborator reaches them between two neutrals, whose projections it compares at their fields' types. A struct that has fields is left out: neither checker compares two neutrals at one by their projections.
13. **A literal's eta is refused at a type former that is not the literal's own, in both checkers.** Conversion is asked about two terms of one type and a literal's eta stands on it, so where the goal states another type the invariant is broken at that goal, and a literal with no field would convert with anything. One classifier in `curios-core` says what a former is, and both read it. A sort, an unsolved metavariable and a neutral type say nothing of the literal's type and keep the expansion: a sort is what a child nothing types is compared at.
14. **The elaborator fires Π and Σ eta by the goal's type ahead of structure**, as the kernel does, for a pair with no flexible head. It makes the two one relation at a function and a record type by construction, where the elaborator reaches the rule only between two sides no structural rule claims. It adds a step to every rigid goal at such a type, so what elaborating `/std` costs is taken before and after by the protocol `curios-prelude-archive`'s `stored_prelude_measurements` states.
15. **A literal's eta is taken against every weak-head normal form that is not an introduction form, one set in both checkers.** Eta holds for every inhabitant of the type, and what licenses it is that the two sides have one type, which decision 13 checks wherever the goal states one. Where it states none the restriction is a proxy, and what a term's shape can tell is the terms whose type shows in their head: a lambda, a tuple or struct literal, a constructor, a literal value, a type former, a sort. A stuck elimination says no more of its type than a variable, so the kernel's set — a variable, a projection, a stuck application — refuses true equations the wider one does not, and refuses nothing the wider one accepts unsoundly. One classifier in `curios-core`, a `match` over `Subterm` with no wildcard, says which a former is; an intrinsic is read off its signature, its type showing where what it produces is a sort or an intrinsic carrier, so that a stuck operation at an element's type — a list's element, a fold's result — is the neutral it is. The kernel widens to it; the elaborator, which takes the rule against any term, narrows to it once decision 14 has made the rule at a typed goal the type's.

## Open questions

- Whether a source program reaches the recurrence rule, so that it has a seed.- Whether two neutrals at a struct that has fields are compared by their projections. Neither checker does so at the fields' types, and the rule fired by the goal's type does not terminate on a struct that reaches itself under a function — at `struct T { force: () -> T }` each round projects, applies, and meets the same goal one layer down — so it is the field-less rule alone, or it needs the recurrence rule to close it.
- Whether unit eta and irrelevance under a child compared at `Type` are recovered from the type a neutral synthesizes, as Lean infers one again. `synth_neutral` is a lookup in both checkers. The rule would have to stay terminal: Σ eta fired at a synthesized type projects `p` against `q` to `p.0` against `q.0`, whose heads ask `p` against `q` again, and a goal that recurs is assumed.

## Stages

Each fix empties its rows of the table of parted rows and deletes its finding. Its check is its own tests in the crate it changes, mutation-checked; the programs it accepts as tests in `curios/src/tests/board/`; `cargo xboard`; and `/std` certifying.

1. **Re-validation has three answers**, decision 10. Check: the program above compiles with neither type stated, and its near miss is refused; fixtures in `curios-elab`'s `convert::solve_tests` hold a lambda candidate postponed while its metavariable's type is unsolved and committed once it is, the same for a lambda inside a tuple, and a candidate at a type that contradicts it refused outright; the other two readers of the oracle answer as they did; `/std` elaborates to what it did.
2. **Eta by the goal's type in the elaborator**, decision 14. Check: the rows the table of parted rows lists under [its finding](00-findings.md), deleted; two stuck eliminations at a record of units beside `any_two_terms_converge_at_a_type_with_no_field`; the cost of elaborating `/std`, before and after.
3. **One set of terms a literal's eta is taken against**, decision 15. Check: `let t(c: Bool, s: Record, r: Record) -> Eq(@(Record))(Record { a = m.a, b = m.b }, m) = Eq/refl();`, with `m` the stuck `match c: (_) => Record | true => s | false => r end`, compiles, and so do a tuple literal, a lambda and a struct literal against a stuck elimination in a stuck elimination's arm, and a tuple literal there against a definition that unfolds to one, each refused by the kernel alone before ([the finding](00-findings.md)); each near miss is refused; a literal against an introduction form of another shape is refused at a sort in both checkers, under one test name; seeds whose neutral is a stuck elimination keep their side under every context; the kernel's widening is mutation-checked both ways.
4. **Landing.** The decision record, the entries and the roadmap.

## Verification

- Every program this specification names as refused runs, and every near miss beside it is still refused, by both checkers.
- The table of parted rows is empty.
- `cargo xboard` reports the conversion part as it stood, and `/std` certifies.
- A fix may move what certifying a unit costs; `curios-prelude-archive`'s `stored_prelude_measurements` states the protocol that retakes it.

## Rejected

- **Replaying a corpus's goals in the other checker.** A program that compiles holds true equations, and certification re-decides them already; a near miss is what an admitting flaw needs, and no accepted program holds one. A replay also needs a tap in one checker and the transport of a scope the other is built never to be told — a metavariable, a refinement layer, a parked goal.
- **A grid of terms built by hand in `curios-elab`'s tests.** Each cell would state its verdict by hand, nothing would keep a cell well typed, and it reaches no `/std` type.
- **The other checker as the oracle.** Two checkers wrong alike agree, and a pair of verdicts says which of them is wrong only against a statement of the rule.
- **A law of history**, a verdict asked again after other questions. The kernel keeps no cache of conversions: its history is built per comparison and left on return, and its memos are cleared at each declaration (`recheck_module_verdicts`), with `kernel_memo_parity` holding the memos themselves.
- **A closure in the elaborator**, deciding ahead of structure any goal whose type has one inhabitant by eta alone: a record of units or proofs, a function into one. It closes the rows decision 8 leaves by a second statement of what the kernel derives from two rules composed, which only the rows would hold equal to it, and which eta by the goal's type in the elaborator replaces.
- **No unit eta**, Rocq's way. A literal against a neutral without two neutrals against each other is itself not transitive, so the rule goes whole or stays whole, and going refuses programs that compile.
- **Settling a candidate in re-validation**, inferring a lambda candidate's type and pinning its metavariable's type to it. It commits the lambda's written shape at the first candidate, where the settle tiers hold a lambda to the drain so that a later pin can still deliver a hidden binder.
- **Postponing by the candidate's shape**, a lambda, a tuple or a list literal against a type stuck at its root. It repeats the list of forms the parking sites already are, and misses the same candidate one level down.
- **Repairing the lost wake alone.** It orders the retries so that this program compiles, and leaves the verdict following the order.
- **A relevance mark on a binder**, so that irrelevance is decided with no type: [An atom is one where conversion says so](../../design/arithmetic/an-atom-is-one-where-conversion-says-so.md) rejects it as a second statement of what the type says.
