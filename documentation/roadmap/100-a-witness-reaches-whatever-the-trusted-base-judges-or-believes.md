# A witness reaches whatever the trusted base judges or believes

[The soundness board](../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md) states that a witness reaches the claim through whatever the trusted base judges or believes, and that every proof of `False` found is a ticket on the board. Neither holds: the board puts a source program, or a module built by hand in Rust, each over the archived prelude (`Proof`, `xboard/src/support/ticket.rs`), so a proof of `False` that passes through an object one walk filed and a later walk read has no form to be put in. This specification resettles the board along what it answers for rather than adding a form beside the two it has: a ticket is data, a witness is a history of walks the compiler's own path runs, a module is written as Core text, the tests that guard a rule sit together beside it, and a hunt needs no worktree.

**Ready.** Every decision below was settled on 2026-11-03. The stages land in order.

## What is wrong today

- **A proof of `False` through a believed object has no form.** `Baseline::holds` (`curios-cert/src/baseline.rs`) compared a `rec` item by its group, its member count and each member's name and proof mark, and not by `RecItem::universe_context`, the context its members' levels positionally mean, which `globals::held` publishes to every reader of a member. So a walk over a baseline adopted a group under constraints no walk assumed, filed it, and a later walk reading that unit admitted Hurkens' paradox: the carrier of [a group met as a bare projection](../../xboard/src/formation/a_group_met_as_a_bare_projection_is_checked_under_the_enclosing_declarations_constraints_and_read_under_the_readers.rs) as a one-member `rec`, accepted under `u + 1 <= w, v <= w, v + 1 <= x, w + 1 <= x`, re-offered with an empty constraint list and read at `carrier@{0, 1, 0, 2}`. It was fixed at `45e8eeb67`, by comparing each item form through a whole destructuring, and is on no ticket: its witness needed a unit one walk filed and a later one read, so it closed at a locally declared empty proposition and `cargo xboard report` never saw it. The route of 2026-11-02 — a unit a walk accepts, mounted with an empty record, so that `Totals::seeded` classified it from its own terms — was put through a third form, `Proof::Mounted` (`6ff9ad99c`), which one ticket constructed and which was removed once that ticket retired (`19e469e02`).
- **A construction a representation change made unspellable was retired.** Four tickets were retired rather than fixed when a representation change left the construction they spelled in Rust unspellable (`c0a2f60a0`, `d5c4715c2`, noted on `xboard/src/formation.rs` and `xboard/src/totality.rs`).
- **Tickets are code that only Rust reads.** The paper's harness reads them with regular expressions (`capture/xboard.crs` in `curios-papers`). The board is the exception to the workspace's flat namespace (`.claude/rules/rust.md`), and `every_ticket_is_filed_under_its_title` (`xboard/src/tests.rs`) holds a file's name to the title it restates. `Status::Open` carries the workspace's one `dead_code` allowance, since no ticket constructs it while every flaw is fixed.
- **The tests that guard the rules that can admit a term are scattered, and assert text.** `curios/src/tests/board/` holds 213 of them under the board's name, while its own doc sends positivity, the totality obligations and witness coherence to `tests::positivity`, `tests::soundness` and `tests::concepts`. A refusal there asserts a fragment of the printed diagnostic — 139 do, through `rejected_by` — so a program refused for another reason, whose message happens to hold the fragment, passes. The kernel's own tests build their modules from Rust constructors and rot with the representation as the board's witnesses did.
- **A hunt assumes worktrees.** `.claude/commands/hunt-findings.md`'s **Worktrees**, `.claude/commands/hunt-soundness.md`'s probing and filing, `.claude/agents/hunter.md`'s **What you touch**, and the hunter's three scripts — `guard.sh`, which admits cargo only inside a worktree, `write-guard.sh` and `worktree.sh` — all rest on one, and a hunter builds in it, so its `target/` grows as the main checkout's does.

## What this builds on

- **The claim and its reach.** A defect is the board's where it can move a verdict or an object; the kernel judges every module put to it whoever built it, so a module built by hand is a witness exactly as a program is; and a walk believes the objects in scope on the ground the second boundary states, so a proof of `False` through an object one walk filed and a later walk read is the board's ([The soundness board](../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md), `36e802e10`).
- **The kernel decides what stands.** A walk over a baseline "decides for itself, declaration by declaration, which of that walk's verdicts stand", and "no caller says which declarations are kept" (`curios-cert/src/baseline.rs`): sharing with a baseline is a shortcut the stages before the kernel provide, and `Baseline::holds` compares terms whatever is shared. The one word the kernel takes from a caller is `Globals::standing`, which the compiler's own fold answers.
- **The compiler's recompile path.** `compile_unit_over` lowers a unit whole, keeps what stands, and elaborates, judges and erases the rest over a baseline (`curios-pipeline/src/recompile.rs`); `compile_units` folds a compilation's units over a `Cache` (`curios-pipeline/src/compile.rs`), which `Verdicts` implements over the store as a compilation sees it (`curios-verdict`). `recheck_with_prelude` (`curios-pipeline/src/test_support.rs`) is the seam a module witness enters today.
- **A record that does not cover its unit has a rule.** `Globals::partial` reads a classification only where the record covers its unit, and a unit mounted with any other record, an empty one among them, is classified from its own terms by `Totals::seeded`.
- **The written form has its own crate.** `curios-surface` holds the surface tree, its parser, its printer, its formatter, `from_core` and `spell`, and the printer of Core text with the naming policy every term is spelled by, above `curios-core`; `curios-module` holds loading, resolution, the lints, the registry and the lowering. `curios-core` keeps the `Lexicon` a unit is spelled by and `Notation`, the hook through which a kernel refusal is written by the printer its reporter hands in (`Spelling::for_refusal`), so `curios-cert` reaches no printer.
- **A module is written as Core text and read back.** Core text — Core in the program's grammar, with nothing left to an elaborator — is `curios-surface`'s, printer and reader ([its decision](../../curios-surface/README.md#core-text-is-core-in-the-programs-grammar-and-reads-back-as-the-term-it-writes)): a dump and a kernel's refusal are written in it, each declaration in its form, `to_core` makes a module and an entry of a `.crsc` over the scope it is handed (`FormScope`), and `curios-prelude`'s suite holds the two to every unit of the prelude.
- **A report's term already reaches the surface tree.** `from_core` (`curios-surface/src/from_core.rs`) translates a Core term to the surface tree for a reader, printed by the printer that formats source. Core text's default spelling writes every global as its absolute path and every binder by its hint, renamed where it would capture or has none.
- **The surface spells every argument.** `@value` supplies an implicit parameter and `use value` a witness ([`documentation/syntax.md`](../syntax.md)); `#` is no part of the language.
- **Data the workspace already reads.** `toml` is a row of the root manifest, taken by `curios-package` for its manifests and by `xbench` for its readings, and `/std/Toml` decodes a document, dates and arrays of tables among it.

## Prior art

- **A compiler's IR as text, read back by a reader the compiler does not need.** Cranelift's `cranelift-reader` reads `.clif` files, "needed for testing Cranelift, but is not essential for a JIT compiler" ([docs](https://docs.rs/cranelift-reader)), and its filetests are text ([`cranelift_filetests`](https://docs.wasmtime.dev/api/cranelift_filetests/index.html)); LLVM's tests are textual IR and WebAssembly's are its text format.
- **A text form nothing holds rots.** GHC's External Core was removed from GHC in 2014, nobody having maintained it for years and the format "pretty bitrotten" ([#5630](https://mail.haskell.org/pipermail/ghc-tickets/2014-August/015221.html)). The round-trip over `/std` is what holds this one to the compiler.
- **The explicit form of a surface.** Lean writes a constant's universe instance as `.{u, v}` after its name ([Universes](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/4.28.0/The-Type-System/Universes/)) and prints implicit arguments and levels on request, as Rocq's `Set Printing All` and `Set Printing Universes` do: the surface, with what it elides spelled out.
- **Kernel tests as data.** The Lean Kernel Arena's tests are a record of an expected outcome and an export, built from a module, a file or a generator, or written by hand ([lean-kernel-arena](https://github.com/datokrat/lean-kernel-arena)).
- **A witness that is a history.** rustc's incremental tests compile revisions into one directory, "reusing the incremental results from previous steps" ([compiletest](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/tests/compiletest.html)); Rust 1.52.1 turned incremental compilation off by default after a fingerprint check exposed reuse that had silently miscompiled ([announcement](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2021/05/10/Rust-1.52.1/)).
- **A stated attacker power, granted to tests.** V8's sandbox assumes an attacker who "can arbitrarily and concurrently modify any memory inside the sandbox address space", and a build flag exposes that power to JavaScript, so a bypass is shown as a program ([README](https://chromium.googlesource.com/v8/v8.git/+/refs/heads/main/src/sandbox/README.md)). Withholding is this board's stated power over a believed record.
- **A flaw kept open as a test.** rustc's `known-bug` is "a sentinel that will fail if the bug is incidentally fixed" ([UI tests](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/tests/ui.html)), as an open ticket is.

## Decisions

Settled 2026-11-03.

### The board

- **A ticket is data beside the code.** `xboard/tickets/<part>/<ticket>/` holds a `metadata.toml` and its witnesses' texts, a program as `.crs` and Core text as `.crsc`; the directory's name is its title's, which the loader holds, so no test restates it. The `metadata.toml` states the title, the day the flaw was found as a TOML date, the status as `open` or `fixed`, and each witness: what it puts, its steps, and what refuses it. A part's question is its directory's `metadata.toml`. TOML holds the board's tickets and the bench's readings, and nothing else in the workspace.
- **`support` is undone.** `xboard/src/support/` was nested when the parts came to the crate's root (`bc29b6b03`); with the records out of `src/`, its modules are the crate's own, and the board stops being the exception to the flat namespace, as `xbench`'s did with its readings.
- **A record's words are held to the code.** A witness names the checker that refuses it, the error by its variant, and where a conversion's refusal stands, as `refused!` does today. Each checker's crate states its errors' names in one exhaustive match under `test-support`, so a variant added or removed does not compile until it is named, and a name no variant has fails the board's run. The status's two words are the loader's, so `Status::Open` is constructed and its allowance goes; a day that does not exist fails the run where it used to fail the build.
- **`xboard` embeds its tickets.** A build script reads `xboard/tickets/` and includes every file, so a binary carries the board of the commit it was built at and `report` reads no path. `probe` reads its candidate where it runs: a `.crs`, a `.crsc`, a ticket's directory, or standard input.
- **A witness is a history.** Each step walks one unit, named by its mount: a program step is compiled as `curios` compiles one, and a Core-text step enters the same path with the module read from its text in place of its lowering and elaboration. The steps share one store, in a temporary directory; a unit's later step is compiled over its earlier one as a recompile is over its baseline, and each unit's latest step is in scope of every step after it. The last step closes with the term put at `/std/Bool/False`. Today's two forms are histories of one step over the prelude. The board says nothing of what stands: the kernel decides what it adopts, and the compiler's own path answers `Globals::standing`.
- **A believed record is attacked by withholding, never by forging.** A step may mount a unit an earlier step filed with part or all of its record withheld, written into the history's store so that the compiler mounts it as a build would. Every verdict the mounted record still states is one a walk concluded, so whatever `False` comes of it comes of a check the kernel makes of the record; adding or altering a verdict stays outside the claim, as the second boundary states. The board's own example holds the power while no ticket uses it.
- **Each form is put by the lowest crate that can put it.** A module read from Core text is put to the kernel by `curios-cert`, over the environment it is handed, the empty one included; a program, or a module over the prelude, by `curios-pipeline`; a history by `curios-verdict`, where the fold meets the store. Each exports its put under `test-support`, the board's put becomes a choice among them, and each crate's tests put what they put as the board does.

### Core text

- **A text's mounts are the unit's and no text's.** The reader is handed the scope a unit's lowering is — by a history's step in its `metadata.toml`, and by `probe --mount` for a lone `.crsc` — as the round-trip hands it the units before the one it printed. A unit's witnesses and tests are the items declared as each, as their forms say (`curios_core::Definitions::witnesses`, `tests`).

### Nothing retires

- **A construction made unspellable is refused by name.** Its Core text still parses, and the reader refuses it because no type of the trusted base can hold it — a refusal a witness expects like any other, by the reader's error; a text that no longer parses is rot, and fails the run. So no witness is deleted, which `.claude/commands/hunt-soundness.md` already asks of a fix. The four tickets retired under the old form are not written again, and their parts' retirement notes go when the parts become data: git keeps them, and the kernel tests they name keep the lessons.

### The tests that guard a rule

- **An attack that held is a test in the lowest crate whose suite can do what it asks.** A module put as Core text is a test of `curios-cert`: a held attack is a refusal, not a proof of `False`, so it needs no prelude and declares what it uses. A program is a test of `curios`, since a control runs its program and checks what the erased program prints, which only `curios` reaches. A history is a test of `curios-verdict`. An attack below a checker's entry is a Rust test of the crate whose function it calls, as it is today. The rule is stated once, in [The soundness board](../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md), replacing "an attack that found nothing is a test of the crate it attacked", and the board's README and the hunt command cite it.
- **Its candidate is text, and its expectation a pattern.** A test's program or Core text is text in the test; its refusal is a pattern over the checker's error, by the variant and where it stands, and a term the test pins is written as Core text and compared with the Core-text print of the error's term, which the round-trip makes one-to-one. No test asserts a fragment of a diagnostic.
- **A rule's tests are one cluster.** Its kernel tests sit beside it in its own `_tests.rs`, as `curios-cert/src/kernel/infer/eliminate/fold_tests.rs` sits beside the fold's rule; its program tests sit in one module of `curios`, `tests::rules`, a file for each rule, each refusal beside its control; and each file names its counterpart and the rule's ticket where it has one. `curios/src/tests/board/` becomes `tests::rules`, and `tests::positivity`, the totality obligations of `tests::soundness` and the coherence tests of `tests::concepts` join it, by rule.

### Hunts

- **A hunt reads a snapshot and probes frozen binaries.** At the hunt's commit the gatherer makes `.claude/snapshots/<commit>/`, ignored as `.claude/worktrees` is: `tree/` holds `git archive <commit>` unpacked, and `bin/` the `curios` and `xboard` binaries built once in the main checkout at that commit. Every hunter of the run shares it, reads the code its binaries were built from while the main checkout moves on, and builds nothing.
- **A hunter writes nothing.** Every unit carries its text: a candidate on `xboard probe`'s standard input, a module as Core text, a held attack as the test it would land as. The gatherer lands and runs the tests, and the mutation checks their comments claim, in the main checkout, in the sweep it already makes. `guard.sh` admits the snapshot's two binaries by path with the subcommands it admits today, and the readers; `write-guard.sh` and `worktree.sh` go, and the hunter takes neither `Write` nor `Edit`. `.claude/worktrees` stays, for a worktree the user asks for.

### The Hurkens route is verification, not a ticket

- A ticket enters the board open ([`xboard`'s README](../../xboard/README.md)), and this route's fix landed before the board could put it, so the route is this specification's verification and files no ticket.

## Stages

Each stage lands under `cargo xtask workspace format` and `cargo xtask workspace lint` and the checks it names; the whole gate runs once at the end. Every reference to a path or a name a stage moves is updated in that stage.

### 3. The board's records

- `xboard/tickets/<part>/` with its `metadata.toml`, and each ticket's directory, `metadata.toml` and witness files; the loader and the words it holds, each checker's names stated once under `test-support`; the tickets embedded at build; `probe` reading a ticket's directory; `xboard/src/support/` undone into `xboard/src/`; `every_ticket_is_filed_under_its_title` and the allowance on `Status::Open` gone; the parts' retirement notes dropped.
- `xboard/README.md` restated: a ticket is data; a witness is put by the layer that can put it; a probe reads a file; the rationale that a ticket's fields are Rust values rewritten. `.claude/rules/rust.md` loses the board's exception to the flat namespace; `.claude/rules/documentation.md`'s row, [The soundness board](../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md) and every comment citing a ticket's file name `xboard/tickets/<part>/`.
- Check: `cargo xboard report` gives every witness the verdict it gave before the stage; the board's own tests, a whole ticket read from its files among them.

### 4. Histories and withholding

- First, what a unit compiled from Core text holds where `Unit::new` takes the lowering of its text: what reads a unit's lowering after its walk decides between an absent lowering and a stage of its own, and the stage stops for a decision where neither is clean.
- `curios-pipeline` gains, under `test-support`, the seam where a Core-text step enters the fold in place of its lowering and elaboration; `curios-verdict` the put of a history over a store in a temporary directory, a step's withheld record written into it; a witness's steps in its `metadata.toml`; `probe` reading a history's directory. The board's own tests hold an example of a history and one of a withheld record.
- `xboard/README.md` states the history and withholding; [The soundness board](../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md)'s second boundary names withholding as the power a witness has over a believed record.
- Check: the board's examples; the verification below.

### 5. The tests that guard a rule

- The rule for an attack that held goes into [The soundness board](../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md). `curios/src/tests/board/` becomes `curios/src/tests/rules/`, and the positivity tests, the totality obligations of `tests::soundness` and the coherence tests of `tests::concepts` join it, a file for each rule, each refusal beside its control; each `rejected_by` becomes a pattern over the checker's error, a term it pins written as Core text; each file names its counterpart in `curios-cert` and the rule's ticket; the references to `tests/board` in `curios-cert`, `curios-analysis` and the documents are updated.
- Check: every test moved runs under its name or the name the move gives it, listed before and after; `curios`'s suite.

### 6. Hunts without worktrees

- `.claude/commands/hunt-findings.md`'s **Worktrees** becomes the snapshot: made at the hunt's commit, removed at the close. `.claude/commands/hunt-soundness.md` probes through the snapshot's binaries, writes a module built by hand as Core text on `probe`'s standard input, and sends a held attack as the test it would land as. `.claude/agents/hunter.md`'s **What you touch** becomes the snapshot, its tools lose `Write` and `Edit`; `guard.sh` admits the snapshot's binaries and no cargo; `write-guard.sh` and `worktree.sh` are removed; `.gitignore` ignores `/.claude/snapshots`.
- Check: `guard.sh` put to a stated list of commands, each admitted or refused as the list says.

## Verification

The gate, `cargo xtask gate`. The board gives every witness the verdict it gave before stages 2 and 3. The round-trip holds over `/std`. The Hurkens route, written as a history of three steps — the carrier under its constraints, the carrier without them, and the paradox read through it — is admitted in a scratch copy with `45e8eeb67`'s comparison reverted and refused at the tree's commit. The route of 2026-11-02, written as a library compiled, its record withheld and the library read, runs as the board's example of withholding. Every test stage 5 moves still runs.

Its contracts are owned by `xboard/README.md`; `curios-surface/README.md` for the written form, Core text and its reader; `curios-module/README.md` for what it holds; the READMEs of `curios-cert`, `curios-pipeline` and `curios-verdict` for the puts; [The soundness board](../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md) for the rule a held attack follows and the power a witness has over a believed record; `.claude/rules/` for the layout; and the hunt commands and the hunter's definition for the snapshot.
