# A record is its fields wherever an equation is read

**A record is its fields wherever an equation is read: its consumer is `/std`'s convoys and helpers under *What it simplifies*, which work as they stand, and its stages could be taken as they are written; it waits on a clear view of what it does to the rest of the theory.** Its stages touch the unifier both checkers share and conversion in each, the area every proof of `False` met so far came through, so it is taken once its consequences there are understood, the hunt round its *Verification* names among what shows them. [Conversion is one relation in both checkers](../../design/soundness/conversion-is-one-relation-in-both-checkers.md) holds conversion transitive, and at a nominal struct it is not once arms fix its fields. A struct and a tuple have eta, and a pattern on either is flattened into its fields' projections ([Patterns are compiled where their types are known](../../design/surface/patterns-are-compiled-where-their-types-are-known.md)), so a field read off a variable is the only name a field has. The rule that a record is its fields is stated at three neighbours: an arm on a field read refines it, conversion projects both sides at a tuple type, and the unifier takes a tuple literal apart. It is missing where an index equation names a field, where two struct literals meet, and where two struct neutrals are compared. Stated at all three, a proof that two records are one is a `refl()` match for each field and `Eq/refl()`, as it is for an inductive's payloads, and an equation over a field is used as one over a variable is.

## What is wrong today

Each is probed at `249545b42`, over `struct P { x: Nat, y: Nat }`.

- **An index equation over a field read specializes nothing.** The unifier's solution rule takes a variable (`Unifier::flexible`, `curios-analysis/src/invert.rs`), and a projection falls to `decompose`'s last arm and stands aside. Matching `refl()` on a proof of `Eq()(a.x, b.x)` is admitted and teaches the arm nothing, whether or not `a` and `b` were taken apart first, a struct or tuple pattern binding projections: after one such match for each field, `Eq/refl()` is refused at `Eq()(a, b)`, for a struct and for a tuple alike. Under `h: Eq()(p.x, 3)` matched to `refl()`, `Holds(p.x == 3)` is not proved by `True/qed()`. Under `h: Eq()(a.T, Nat)` over `struct Any { T: Type, v: T }`, `a.v + 1` is refused, `a.v` standing at `a.T`.
- **A struct literal is not taken apart.** `decompose` has an arm for two variants and one for two tuples and none for two struct literals, though the comment over the variant arm argues the structure's case. From a proof of `Eq()(P { x = u, y = v }, P { x = 1, y = 2 })` matched to `refl()`, `Eq()(u, 1)` is refused; the same over `(u, v)` and `(1, 2)` is accepted.
- **Conversion is not transitive at a nominal struct once arms fix its fields.** Under four arms that match `a.x`, `a.y`, `b.x` and `b.y` at `true`, over `struct P { x: Bool, y: Bool }`, `Eq/refl()` is accepted at `Eq()(a, P { x = true, y = true })` and at `Eq()(P { x = true, y = true }, b)` and refused at `Eq()(a, b)`; over `{Bool, Bool}` it is accepted at all three. Both checkers compare two struct neutrals by their heads: "two neutrals have none to open" (`forced`, `curios-cert/src/certifier/convert.rs`), since "projecting both poses their heads again, which only two equal heads pass" (`eta_expand_neutral`, `curios-elab/src/convert.rs`), and the decision rejects "two neutrals at a struct compared by their projections" for that reason. That holds while no equation over a field is in force, and an arm on a field read puts one in force.

## What it simplifies

- **`Str/eq_of_bytes` and `Uuid/eq_of_bytes`.** Each carries its equation over the proof field by `Eq/subst` under a written motive, a convoy. Each becomes a `refl()` match on the equation and `Eq/refl()`.
- **The proof of a law at a struct and at a tuple.** `Eql`'s `eq` is written at each as a congruence over field variables in a helper, taking an equation for each field, applied at the projections, the form both checkers accept today, eta closing it: the derivation's (`congruent`, `curios-elab/src/derive.rs`), and one in each written witness of `Char`, `Map`, `Path`, `Set`, `Str/At` and the eight tuples of a field or more (`Tuple.crs`). `Ord`'s `eq` reads the same congruence at each, the derivation's, `Char/eq_of_code`, `Str/At`'s `eq_of_offset` and the tuples' `same1` to `same8`, and `OffsetDateTime`'s writes one more. `Vec`'s witness reads the convoy beside it, `Vec/eq_of_list`, as `Str`'s and `Uuid`'s read `eq_of_bytes`, and `Map/try_get`'s `of_root` is another. Each helper and each convoy becomes a `refl()` match on its field's equation and `Eq/refl()`.
- **A hypothesis that equates a field.** `h: Eq()(p.x, 3)` is used by `let refl() = h;`, where today it takes `Eq/subst` with a motive written by hand.
- **A field equation that cannot hold.** `match h end` over `h: Eq()(p.l, [1, ..p.l])`, a list field met against a longer list holding it, has no arm, as it has none over `Eq()(l, [1, ..l])`; today it is refused for its missing arm, where `Eq()(p.x, p.x + 1)` is closed by the contradiction the facts in scope prove where the match stands, which a list's structure gives the facts nothing of.

## Goal

- **The unifier solves a field read.** A place is a flexible variable, or a projection with a positional slot whose head, read through the solutions, is a place. A place that is a field read is solved to the other side of its equation where the two sides, read as the arm's typing reads them, are not one term; where the other side is no metavariable and does not reach the place's root, by the variable rule's occurs check closed also through the field equations found; where the value's sort is below the field's, if the field's type is a sort; and where no flexible variable stands on either side, so every equation solved today is solved as it was. Where both sides are field reads the scrutinee's is refined to the case's. A field read met against a value that holds it as a proper part makes the case impossible. A root solved after one of its fields turns that field's equation into one between the solution's field and the value.
- **The unifier takes a struct literal apart.** Two literals of one structure become an equation for each field, and a struct literal is a layer of the cycle rule, as a tuple is of both.
- **Conversion projects both sides at a struct that does not reach itself.** Eta by the goal's type is taken at a function type, at a Σ and at a nominal struct that does not reach itself, whatever the two sides' shapes, a proof field unread. A struct that reaches itself keeps a literal's eta and two neutrals by their heads.
- **A field equation is applied as an arm on that field read applies its own.** The unifier hands back the variables it solved and the field equations it found. The certifier records each by `Certifier::refine` in the arm's bracket, and the elaborator by `refine_head`, each only under a spelling `records_case_equation` admits (`curios-analysis/src/case_equation.rs`), judged on the certifier's spelling in both, as an arm's own equation is: one recorded by one checker and not the other reads the arm two ways. Nothing is bound and no identity is minted, so a reader of the finished term reads the arm as it does today.

## Settled

- **One rule, where Agda has the same two halves.** Agda replaces a record pattern by projections (`Agda.TypeChecking.RecordPatterns`) and gives its unifier `EtaExpandVar` and `EtaExpandEquation` for what that leaves ([Cockx and Devriese](https://doi.org/10.1017/S095679681800014X), the source [An arm is checked in a context specialized by index inversion](../../design/theory/an-arm-is-checked-in-a-context-specialized-by-index-inversion.md) cites for its rules). Curios has the flattening and not the rules. Rocq's primitive projections carry eta in conversion and in unification; Lean, whose structures are inductives of one constructor, splits one into variables and derives `p.x = q.x → p.y = q.y → p = q` by `@[ext]`.
- **An elimination assumes the equation, and no hypothesis enters conversion.** `h: Eq()(p.x, 3)` moves nothing until a `refl()` match eliminates it, the line [A bound that follows from the facts in scope is proved by the elaborator](../../design/arithmetic/a-bound-that-follows-from-the-facts-in-scope-is-proved-by-the-elaborator.md) draws in rejecting hypotheses in conversion. Within that arm the two sides are one value, by definitional K and the totality of proofs, which the arm rule assumes already of every equation it solves; the restriction to a field read off a variable is what keeps the reading decidable, the key a rigid neutral, the equation oriented, the occurs check ending it.
- **The size order does not read a field equation.** "The size order reads what reduction took apart and nothing conversion concluded" (`curios-analysis/src/totality/grade.rs`), and a solved variable's binding is marked for that (`Value::solved`, `curios-cert/src/certifier/scope.rs`). A field equation an inversion found carries the same mark, and the graded door (`Driver::force_graded`) reads with the equations withheld from the first marked one inward, the view `ByValue::withholding` gives a settlement, which sets the memos aside. The elaborator needs no mark: its size order runs over finished terms, where no arm's frame stands.
- **A value need not be a constructor.** The certifier's `whnf` and the elaborator's `Frames::refine_scrutinee` continue from an equation's value on the argument that it is a constructor, a normal form. A field equation's value is any term the occurs check admitted, and that check is what the argument becomes: no value reaches its own key through what is in force.
- **A field at a sort is held to its universe**, as `fits_its_variable` holds a variable: solved to a type of a larger sort, the field would put that type wherever its own stands.
- **Two fields of one root are not solved against each other.** The occurs check refuses a value that names the root, and the equation stands aside as it does today.
- **A struct that reaches itself keeps today's conversion.** Projected, two neutrals at `struct S { next: S }` pose their goal again at `next` without end, which is why Agda infers no eta for a recursive record. `curios_analysis::struct_reaches_itself`, the judgment the one-inhabitant reading ends by, says which structs those are, and it sees every way back: it reads struct declarations alone, and a struct and a definition that name each other are refused as a declaration cycle (probed at `aa7866355`), while a path through an inductive ends where conversion compares two neutrals at the inductive by their heads.
- **A struct literal's injectivity needs no sort test.** A struct's sort after its `:` is `Type` or `Prop`, never one left to a caller, and every field of a `Prop` struct is a proposition (`informative field`, probed at `aa7866355`), so taking two literals of a proof record apart yields equations between proofs, which deletion removes: the reason the tuple arm needs none. The variant arm's test reads a family's declared sort because a family's may hold data at `Prop`.
