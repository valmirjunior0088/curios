# Theory: findings

- **Conversion sets apart two members of one group met again inside their own unfolding, though their arguments have moved, so it is not transitive across a group.** For two calls of different members of a recursive group, `curios-elab`'s conversion takes one symmetric delta step and holds the pair of heads, and a pair of the same two heads met again inside that unfolding is apart, "since unfolded again it poses itself again" (`curios-elab/src/convert.rs`), whatever its arguments now are. With `first(t)` and `first_in(l)` one group, `first_in` answering `first(h)` for a head that holds anything and `first(T/node(k))` answering `first_in(k)`, `first_in([T/node(k), ..r])` and `first(T/node(k))` are refused as one, closed too at `k = []`, where both reduce to `0`, while each converts with `first_in(k)`; declared apart rather than as a group, the two convert. The rule stops a pair that would unfold forever, and it keys the pair by its heads alone. In `/std/Map/internal/Node` before `first_in` was declared on its own, a lemma's branch arm met such a pair under its case equations, the elaborator admitted it and the certifier refused it (expected `first_in([branch(at, twigs), ..r])`, found `first(branch(at, twigs))`), so the two checkers also decide it apart. Not yet known: whether this rule is what refuses the closed pair too, whose report shows the two sides only as their reducts, `Eq(@Nat)(0, 0)` against itself at two universe metavariables; what a pair is held by, which the user settled on 2026-11-18 as the step's progress: the same two heads met again are apart only where no unfolding between their two meetings took a match apart at a constructor, as Rocq unfolds a fixpoint only where its decreasing argument starts with one; an unfolding that leaves its match stuck poses the first meeting's question one binder further in, which is all [Conversion recurrence](../../design/soundness/conversion-recurrence.md) argues apart, and one that fires a match computes. A pair held by its heads with their arguments was weighed and set aside: an open recursion's arguments move every round, so nothing recurs whole and the budget alone would refuse, which the decision rejects; and how the certifier decides each pair, which `cargo xboard probe` on the pair written as Core text settles. Check: the open and the closed pair above admitted by both checkers, and `curios`'s `tests::runaways` unchanged. Size: unknown.
- **Uncertain: conversion's reading of two projections of one group forces every member's body, so a group whose body computes runs that computation at each such comparison.** `held_pairs` reads the positions two projections apart in levels alone differ at through `group_held` (`curios-analysis/src/variance.rs`), the least fixpoint of the members' unfoldings, each body opened at fresh binders and walked as `held_levels` walks, forcing each closed part it reads. A derived `Spell` witness of a recursive family is such a group, and its body renders through `Spell/call`, `Fmt/render` over a format string, which forcing runs on what an arm binds: in a trial of 2026-11-18 that read a member's vector through `group_held`, `tests::derive::spell::an_indexed_family_spells_each_constructor` and three like it ran past twenty seconds and a gigabyte where they elaborate in under one, and in one that forced the member's application, past two minutes and five gigabytes. A member's vector is read with nothing reduced instead (`group_levels`, `curios-elab/src/universes/variance.rs`), so the vector does not meet it. Not yet known: whether a program compares two instances of such a group apart in levels alone, where the elaborator's identification or the certifier's `rec_instances` would pay it — a probe of a derived witness of a universe-polymorphic family used at two levels in one signature settles it; and whether `group_held` itself could read as written what it now forces, which the two checkers share. Size: unknown.
