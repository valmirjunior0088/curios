- **Conversion sets apart two members of one group met again inside their own unfolding, though the unfolding computes there, so it is not transitive across a group.** For two calls of different members of a recursive group, both checkers take one symmetric delta step and hold the pair of heads, and a pair of the same two heads met again inside that unfolding is apart, "since unfolded again it poses itself again" (`Pairs::hold`, `curios-elab/src/convert.rs`; `Pairs::unfold`, `curios-cert/src/certifier/convert.rs`), whatever its arguments now are. With `first(t)` and `first_in(l)` one group, `first_in` answering `first(h)` for a head that holds anything and `first(T/node(k))` answering `first_in(k)`, `first_in([T/node(k), ..r])` and `first(T/node(k))` are refused as one by the elaborator and, written as Core text, by the certifier (expected `first(node(kids))`, found `first_in(…)`), and so are the closed `first_in([T/node([])])` and `first(T/node([]))`, while each side converts with what the two share — `first_in(k)`, or `0` — and declared apart rather than as a group the two convert. Re-shown on 2026-11-19 at `5b3028ed8`. The closed pair's report misleads, showing the two sides as their reducts, `Eq.{Type; ?u1}(@Nat)(0, 0)` against `Eq.{Type; ?u0}(@Nat)(0, 0)`; a closed pair is apart only by this rule, so the report goes with it. [Conversion recurrence](../../design/soundness/conversion-recurrence.md) argues the rule by "a second unfolding poses what the first posed one binder further in, so it decides nothing the first did not", which fails where the pair met again takes a match apart at a constructor: there the second unfolding computes, as the first did, and here `first(T/node(k))` holds one. The rule the user settled on 2026-11-19: the same two heads met again are apart only where neither call's unfolding takes its match apart at a constructor, both re-sticking, read off the pair met again and not counted between the meetings, which a memo answering a reduction would leave unmoved; Rocq unfolds a fixpoint only where its decreasing argument starts with a constructor, and the elaborator already tells a forcing that makes no progress (`force_folded_call`). A non-structural recursion that keeps rebuilding a constructor is the budget's, as a partial one is. Fix: the condition in both checkers' pair rule, and the decision's third rule and its rationale restated by it. Check: the open and the closed pair above admitted by both checkers, a pair met again that re-sticks on both sides still apart, a differential case in both suites, and `curios`'s `tests::runaways` unchanged. - **Reading two instances of a recursive group apart in levels climbs every other group a walk meets again in every round and every nested walk, so one comparison of a derived witness's instances costs minutes.** `group_held` (`curios-analysis/src/variance.rs`), by which `held_pairs` reads two projections of one group — the elaborator wherever two instances meet, their levels unsolved metavariables included (`identify_universe_levels`, `curios-elab/src/convert/neutral.rs`), and the certifier at `rec_instances` — climbs the group's least fixpoint a round at a time, each round a `Walk` of its own whose table of the other groups it meets (`Walk::groups`) starts empty, and a member of another group met in a walk is read by that group's own fixpoint (`Walk::group_marks`), climbed afresh inside it. Re-shown on 2026-11-19 at `5b3028ed8`: a derived `Spell` witness of a recursive `Tree(A)` compared through a definition that spells as it does — `shown(t) = Spell/spell(t)` and `Eq()(shown(t), Spell/spell(t))` by `Eq/refl()`, fifteen lines — checks in 742 s and 9.5 GB, where the witness compared with itself checks in 0.2 s; under gdb the one `identify_universe_levels` that asks it calls `group_held` once, and the walks under that call call it more than 19,800 times more, each from `Walk::call`. A group's held levels are a function of the group, but for the group whose fixpoint is being climbed, which a walk reads by its estimate; groups are the strongly connected parts of what names what, so those a reading meets form a DAG and no other's answer waits on the estimate in hand.
