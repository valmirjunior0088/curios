# Theory: findings

- **Conversion sets apart two members of one group met again inside their own unfolding, though their arguments have moved, so it is not transitive across a group.** For two calls of different members of a recursive group, `curios-elab`'s conversion takes one symmetric delta step and holds the pair of heads, and a pair of the same two heads met again inside that unfolding is apart, "since unfolded again it poses itself again" (`curios-elab/src/convert.rs`), whatever its arguments now are. With `first(t)` and `first_in(l)` one group, `first_in` answering `first(h)` for a head that holds anything and `first(T/node(k))` answering `first_in(k)`, `first_in([T/node(k), ..r])` and `first(T/node(k))` are refused as one, closed too at `k = []`, where both reduce to `0`, while each converts with `first_in(k)`; declared apart rather than as a group, the two convert. The rule stops a pair that would unfold forever, and it keys the pair by its heads alone. In `/std/Map/internal/Node` before `first_in` was declared on its own, a lemma's branch arm met such a pair under its case equations, the elaborator admitted it and the certifier refused it (expected `first_in([branch(at, twigs), ..r])`, found `first(branch(at, twigs))`), so the two checkers also decide it apart.
