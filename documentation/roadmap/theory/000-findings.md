# Theory: findings

- **Two instances of a recursive member that answers no type are left apart by conversion and both held by settlement, so a declaration naming the member at two levels keeps both.** Conversion compares two projections of one group at the positions its unfolding holds (`held_pairs`, [A nominal type's levels compare by variance](../../design/soundness/a-nominal-types-levels-compare-by-variance.md)), so `all.{v}` and `all.{w}` are one with nothing committed where `/std/List/all/cons` meets them in its body. A member's own vector is read off its projection as a `let`'s is off its body (`member_vectors`, `curios-elab/src/elaborate/module.rs`): as written, for a value that answers no type, so `all`'s projection, which mentions every level of its group, files none. Settlement then holds both levels where `cons`'s signature names `all`, and `cons` declares three universe parameters where it declared two. Read by `curios-prelude`'s `universe_parameter_census` at `3064332fb` beside `7df96cef5`: thirteen declarations gain seventeen levels, ten of them in `/std/List` (`all/cons`, `ordered/cons`, `ordered/tail`, the `sort_by` lemmas) and three in that commit's `/std/Map/internal/Node` (`map_first`, `wedge_agree`, `wedge_held`, the last two gone with its node), while 138 declarations lose 564; at `f380b3eeb`, `/std/List/all/cons` still declares three. Fix: a member's vector read through its group's unfolding whether or not it answers a type, as conversion reads its projections, so `all` files `*` and the levels settle at their least values; a `let` that answers no type keeps the written reading, its lambdas being compared at their type. Check: `/std/List/all/cons` declaring no more universe parameters than at `7df96cef5`, a `curios` settlement test of a recursive `Bool`-valued member named at two levels, and the universe-parameter census read again. Size: one commit.
- **Conversion sets apart two members of one group met again inside their own unfolding, though their arguments have moved, so it is not transitive across a group.** For two calls of different members of a recursive group, `curios-elab`'s conversion takes one symmetric delta step and holds the pair of heads, and a pair of the same two heads met again inside that unfolding is apart, "since unfolded again it poses itself again" (`curios-elab/src/convert.rs`), whatever its arguments now are. With `first(t)` and `first_in(l)` one group, `first_in` answering `first(h)` for a head that holds anything and `first(T/node(k))` answering `first_in(k)`, `first_in([T/node(k), ..r])` and `first(T/node(k))` are refused as one, closed too at `k = []`, where both reduce to `0`, while each converts with `first_in(k)`; declared apart rather than as a group, the two convert. The rule stops a pair that would unfold forever, and it keys the pair by its heads alone. In `/std/Map/internal/Node` before `first_in` was declared on its own, a lemma's branch arm met such a pair under its case equations, the elaborator admitted it and the certifier refused it (expected `first_in([branch(at, twigs), ..r])`, found `first(branch(at, twigs))`), so the two checkers also decide it apart. Not yet known: whether this rule is what refuses the closed pair too, whose report shows the two sides only as their reducts, `Eq(@Nat)(0, 0)` against itself at two universe metavariables; what a pair should be held by, its heads with their arguments, so a pair is apart only where it recurs whole and the conversion budget bounds one whose arguments grow, or the step's progress, an unfolding that took a match apart; and how the certifier decides each pair, which `cargo xboard probe` on the pair written as Core text settles. Check: the open and the closed pair above admitted by both checkers, and `curios`'s `tests::runaways` unchanged. Size: unknown.
