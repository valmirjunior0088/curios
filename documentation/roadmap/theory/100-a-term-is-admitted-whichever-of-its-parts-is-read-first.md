# A term is admitted whichever of its parts is read first

Working specification for who decides a problem the elaborator has parked and that has an answer of its own: a numeral's default, a proposition kept a proposition, a tuple's plain product, a lambda's annotated type. It is held to [Subsumption is a relation, not a traversal order](../../design/theory/subsumption-is-a-relation-not-a-traversal-order.md), which has a metavariable met at `Prop` wait while the term that minted it reads its parts, so that the order of a call's arguments, of a list's elements, or of an argument and the expectation decides nothing, and to [A literal is realized by its expected type](../../design/surface/a-literal-is-realized-by-its-expected-type.md). The code keeps that rule for one kind of problem, a sort met first, and for no other.

## What this builds on

- **The wait exists, for a sort.** A term that mints a metavariable awaits it until it has read its parts and met its expectation (`Context::await_bounds`, `Context::settle_bounds`, `curios-elab/src/typing.rs`): a call its implicits and the holes its head's spine leaves open (`elaborate_apply`), a list literal its element type (`elaborate_intrinsic`), a structure literal its parameters (`elaborate_struct`), a match its elided motive (`eliminate`). `Prop ≤ ?m` met meanwhile parks (`Convert::waits_at_prop`, `curios-elab/src/convert.rs`), and is retried where the term settles, when it is the equation the same problem is where nothing awaits.
- **A parked problem is an obligation, never a raw substitution** ([`curios-elab`'s README](../../../curios-elab/README.md#postponement-is-a-parked-obligation-never-a-raw-substitution)): a placeholder stands in the tree, only its obligation fills it, and whatever runs the obligation holds it out of the store.
- **Conversion solves no metavariable to another.** Two unsolved metavariables met against each other park as a pair (`curios-elab/src/convert.rs`, "flex–flex does no intersection"; [Flex–flex problems with distinct heads](202-flex-flex-intersection.md)), and a candidate that holds an unsolved metavariable commits where that one's context is contained in the solved one's. So what holds an inner term's metavariable to an enclosing term's is a solution or a parked conversion.
- **Peers.** Lean runs one staged pass over the problems a region of elaboration postponed — resumed, then resumed with postponement off, then given their defaults, then reported (`withSynthesize` and `synthesizeSyntheticMVars`, [`Lean/Elab/SyntheticMVars.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/master/src/Lean/Elab/SyntheticMVars.lean)). GHC gives each unification variable the level of the implication that may solve it, and promotes the variables of an equality it floats out of one to the level it floats to ([Note \[Promoting unification variables\]](https://ghc-compiler-notes.readthedocs.io/en/latest/notes/compiler/typecheck/TcSimplify.hs.html)). rustc checks a call's closure arguments in a second pass over its arguments and says of it "This isn't really the right way to do this" ([`check_argument_types`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/nightly/nightly-rustc/src/rustc_hir_typeck/fn_ctxt/checks.rs.html)), and binds an integer variable nothing constrained when the function's constraints are all known ([RFC 212](https://rust-lang.github.io/rfcs/0212-restore-int-fallback.html)).

## The gap

The programs below are over `let id(@A: Type, a: A) -> A = a;`, `let g(@A: Type, x: A, pin: A) -> Nat = 0;`, `let go(@A: Type, x: Option(A), pin: Option(A)) -> Nat = 0;`, `let b: Byte = 7;`, `let i: Int = -2;`, `let c: Bool = true;`, `let ob: Option(Byte) = Option/some(b);` and `struct Pair(T: Type): pub Type { a: T, z: T }`, each read at `b2c975654`.

**A parked problem that has an answer of its own does not say who takes it.** A `ParkedProblem` (`curios-elab/src/context/solutions.rs`) holds its work, its frame and the metavariables whose solution would wake it. Who answers it where nothing wakes it is held outside it, four ways, each recognizing its problems by their shape: `settle_bounds` takes the conversions parked at below that watch what a term awaited (`Solutions::take_bounds_on`); a call keeps the arguments it parked in a vector of its own and settles them in a force tier, after its bounds and in the order of its slots (`elaborate_apply_awaiting`, `curios-elab/src/elaborate/apply.rs`); an intrinsic's operand is settled where it is read (`elaborate_demand`); and every other parked tuple or lambda waits for its item's drain, which takes the whole store and settles each in the order it was parked (`Context::settle_synthesizable`). A parked projection and a parked match (`ParkedProjection`, `ParkedMatch`) exist for what that last wait strands: a match's tuple arms, typed only once the item ends.

**A numeral commits to its default where it is read.** `elaborate_num_lit` (`curios-elab/src/elaborate/binding.rs`) takes the shape default at an unsolved metavariable, for the reason that waiting would strand what reads the type next, and reads a type stuck on one as a type no numeral has. So `let r: Option(Int) = Option/some(5);` is refused, `Option(Nat)` against `Option(Int)`, as is `let x: Byte = id(5);`; and each of `g(5, b)`, `g(5, -3)`, `g(1, c)`, `[5, b]`, `Pair { a = 5, z = b }`, `Eq()(5, b)` and `match c | true => i | false => 5 end` is refused where the same parts in the other order compile. `go(Option/some(5), ob)` is refused in either order. Over `concept Sized(A: Type): pub Type { Carrier: Type, zero: Carrier }` and `f1(@A: Type, use Sized(A), x: Sized/Carrier(@A), pin: A)`, `f1(5, true)` reports `Nat` against `(?).0`. An operator's operands are the one place a numeral is read last: `elaborate_infix` checks the operand that is no literal first and gives the two a default they share (`infix_default_type`), so `5 == b` and `5 + -3` compile.

**Two literals parked on one type are settled in written order.** The force tier synthesizes the first tuple or list whose expectation never gained structure and checks the next against what that pinned, after the call has stopped awaiting. `g((5, 1), (b, b))` is refused and `g((b, b), (5, 1))` compiles. `g([Holds(true)], [Nat])` and `g((Holds(true), c), (Nat, c))` are each refused as `Type` against `Prop` where the other order compiles: the rule the decision states for a sort, broken where the sort stands inside a literal the call parked. The drain does the same to what it settles: `[(5, 1), (b, b)]`, `Pair { a = (5, 1), z = (b, b) }` and `match c | true => (b, b) | false => (5, 1) end` are each refused where the other order compiles. Through a call of its own a literal is settled where that call ends, whatever the enclosing call has still to read or has parked unread: `g(id((5, 1)), (b, b))` and `g((b, b), id((5, 1)))` are both refused.

**A lambda waits for its item's end, or is forced before a sibling could type it.** `let z = id((x: Nat) => x);` leaves `z` at an unsolved type until the drain, so `z(3)` on the next line is refused as `applied a non-function`; and the force tier checks `(y) => y` at a domain nothing gave it before it reads the annotated lambda beside it, so `g((y) => y, (x: Nat) => x)` is refused where `g((x: Nat) => x, (y) => y)` compiles.

## Decisions

**A metavariable is awaited at a depth, and a term that settles hands outward what is still held to an enclosing term's.** The depth is how many awaiting terms enclose the one that awaits it. Where a term has read its parts, a metavariable it awaited that is still unsolved, and that a solution or a parked conversion holds to one awaited further out, is awaited from then on by that enclosing term; the rest are the term's to settle. The relation is read once, where the term settles, with every problem in the store, and kept, so whether something still awaits a metavariable is a lookup. GHC's floating does the same to an equality an implication could not solve.

**A term settles in three steps.** A tuple or a list literal still parked on a metavariable the term settles gives the metavariable its own shape with its parts left open — a non-dependent product of one open type a field, labelled as the literal is, or `List` of one open type — and is checked at it, the open parts awaited by the settling term; so a second literal parked on the same metavariable is checked against the first's shape and each fills what the other left open. Then the choices are taken: a `Prop` nothing raised, a numeral nothing pinned. Then a lambda still parked is checked, at its annotated domains where it has them. The item is the outermost such term, and its drain settles what no term awaited.

**A numeral waits as a proposition does.** Checked at a type stuck on an unsolved metavariable that some term awaits, it parks as a checking problem, is realized where a solution wakes it, and takes its shape default where the term that awaits its type settles. Where no term awaits the type the default is taken at once, as today, and the comparison with a type stuck on a metavariable parks. Numerals a term settles together take a default each of them realizes at: a signed one is decided first, whose default is `Int`, and a character last, so `Char` is the default only where every one is a character. An operator awaits its operand type as any term does, and its two phases and `infix_default_type` go; `'a' == 'b'` is then compared at `Char`, as [the reference](../../syntax.md#character-and-string-literals) has a character nothing pins realize.

**A problem is not handed its answer.** Retried where nothing awaits it, it takes it, as a `Prop` bound does today. What the store asks of a parked problem is the step at which a settling term answers it, stated once on `ParkedWork` in place of a shape matched by each consumer.

## Open questions

**How the open parts of a shape take their universe levels.** Tried at `b2c975654` and not landed, with each open part a hole at a fresh classifier level and only at a call's own arguments: `g((5, 1), (b, b))`, `g((Holds(true), c), (Nat, c))`, `g(id((5, 1)), (b, b))`, `g((b, b), id((5, 1)))` and `g((5, 1), (-3, b))` compile, but `/std`'s elaborated module grows from 7,996,116 bytes to 7,997,262, differing first where `/std/Async/attempt` is stored, whose `Option/some((i, a))` is such an argument, and `g([Nat], [Holds(true)])` is accepted by the elaborator and refused by the kernel, `expected Type, found Type.{1}`. Counted, as *Verification* states. A product synthesized from its fields takes their levels; one shaped ahead of them must come to the same term.

**Whether a lambda bound through a call takes its type where the call settles.** `z(3)` then compiles. Today the wait lets a later use of `z` hand the lambda a function type with hidden binders, which a lambda committed to its written shape can no longer take.

**Whether a parked projection and a parked match are still reached** once a match settles its own arms.

**Whether a metavariable no term awaits is awaited once a parked conversion holds it to one a term does**: a lambda's unannotated domain, held to a call's implicit by its body.

## Stages

Each lands alone, on its own check.

1. **Awaited by depth, and a numeral that waits.** `Solutions` keeps each awaited metavariable's depth and hands outward where a term settles; `elaborate_num_lit` parks; `settle_bounds` takes the numerals with the bounds; the operator awaits its operand type. Tried at `b2c975654` and not landed, without the operator and with the relation read by a scan of the store at every question: every numeral program above runs in either order, and of `/std`'s image only what its declarations asked as they elaborated moves, 1,738,513 bytes to 1,738,498, counted as *Verification* states. Check: the numeral programs as fixtures in `curios/src/tests/inference/numeral_tests.rs`, and the image compared.
2. **A call settles its literals by shape, ahead of its choices.** Needs the first open question answered. Check: the call programs above in either order, and the image compared.
3. **Every term that awaits settles as a call does**: a match, a list literal, a structure literal, an operator, an intrinsic's operand, and the item. Check: the list, structure and match programs above, and a literal nested in one, `g(((5, 1), c), ((b, b), c))`.
4. **A lambda is settled by the term that awaits its type.** Needs the second open question answered. Check: the two lambda programs.
5. **What the missing owner made necessary goes.** The force tier's synthesis and `SettleTier`, the drain's sweep of the whole store, the list of structures `blocked_on_metavar` parks whole, and the parked projection and match where nothing reaches them. Check: each counted at zero over `/std` and the test suite before it is removed.

## Verification

- Every program under *The gap* is a fixture in both orders, run and not only elaborated, so that the kernel has judged what the elaborator accepted.
- **The image compared.** `curios-prelude-archive`'s build writes `/std`'s image as `std.rkyv` under its `OUT_DIR`, and `curios-prelude`'s writes `certification.rkyv` and `reads.rkyv` under its own. The image is read with `curios-unit`'s `segments`, `halves` and `apart_parts`, which give the unit and the eight parts it stores apart from itself: its lowered items, its erased forms, its elaborated module, where its declarations lie, its import scopes, and what the lowering, the elaborator and erasure each asked. A release build of the commit before a stage and one of the stage are compared part by part, and the certifier's two files whole. A stage that changes only the order work is retried in moves what the elaborator asked and nothing else; any other part that moves is named with the declaration it first differs at, and is the stage's to explain. Counted.
- `cargo xtask clippy` and `cargo xtask test` at every stage.
- Each stage names the figures it may have moved: the budget every declaration of `/std` consumes, which a problem that parks and wakes spends otherwise.

## Rejected

- **The relation read by a scan of the store at every question**, as the first stage was tried: it answers rightly, costs a pass over the parked problems wherever a numeral or a bound asks, and misses a pair while that pair is out of the store being retried.
- **A term that hands outward whenever it was checked at a type still open.** It waits where nothing can arrive, and does not wait where something can: a term inferred, whose metavariable a binder's type holds to an enclosing call's.
- **Every choice held to its item's end**, as Rust holds an integer's to the end of its function: what reads the type next — a match, a projection, a call of `z` — meets none, which is what the parked projection and the parked match are there to survive.
- **A numeral as a fourth form the call walk parks.** A numeral under a nested call, in a list, a structure literal or a match arm still commits where it is read.
- **Literals whose type involves no choice settled first**, rustc's two passes: `g((5, 1), (-3, b))` holds two that must share one.

## Non-goals

Solving one metavariable to another ([Flex–flex problems with distinct heads](202-flex-flex-intersection.md)); which metavariables a parked problem watches; the order a call resolves its `use` slots in; the spine approximation. Each has an entry of its own.

## Completion and retirement

- Every program under *The gap* compiles in either order of its parts, and a numeral nothing pins is its default where the next line reads it.
- One function settles a term, and no consumer of the store recognizes a problem by its shape.

Before this specification is deleted, `curios-elab`'s README states the settling of a parked problem in its decision on postponement, the two decisions it is held to state the rule for a numeral and for a literal with their rejected alternatives, [the reference](../../syntax.md#numeric-literals) says where a numeral is unconstrained, the finding on which dictionary a call uses no longer cites the numeral's eagerness, the roadmap entry is a checked summary, and no reference to this filename remains.
