# A list built under a cons is built by a loop

Working specification for building by a loop a recursion whose answer is its own call's answer combined under a monoid: a list built under a cons, a run of bytes or a text built around its tail, a comparison of two lists joined by `&&`, as `count(t) + 1` already is. The monoid rebase is restated as tail recursion modulo a monoid context — the combines closing a leaf read as a word with one hole, a placement rule where the monoid does not commute — and reads which operations are monoids from Ersd's oracle, whose table joins the free monoids' concatenations and the lawful scalar operations the rebase leaves out today.

## What this builds on

- **The rebase** (`curios-ersd/src/optimize/rebase.rs`) splits a recursion whose leaf blocks end `[…, a = f(args), b = ⊕(a, k)]` into a wrapper seeding the identity and a worker threading an accumulator. Its six rows are `NatAdd`, `NatMul`, `NatOr`, `IntAdd`, `IntMul` and `IntOr`, the combine is accepted with the recursion on either side because every row commutes, and the addend must be defined before the call. It runs once per linked program (`optimize_verified`), so no prelude image moves with it.
- **A `List`, a `Bytes` and a `Bits` are ropes** (`curios-emit/src/types.rs`): a concatenation or an append is one node, and the first whole read forces the rope by an iterative walk that descends left and pushes each right child on a worklist (`curios-emit/src/rope_emitter.rs`).
- **The laws are the language's own.** `Nat`, `Bin` and `List` are Core's free monoids (`curios-core/src/free_monoid.rs`), and conversion decides a concatenation's associativity and its two identities ([The carriers' algebra stays in conversion](../../design/arithmetic/the-carriers-algebra-stays-in-conversion.md)).
- **Ersd has a semantic oracle.** `semantics.rs` states each operation's behavior and folds it over constants, by matches over the closed alphabets with no fallback arm, so a new operation cannot be misclassified silently. Its behavior decides whether an unused binding may be deleted, never whether a statement may move: "a guard keeps its proof by position, and nothing here moves an operation above one".
- **A self tail call is a backedge** (`curios-cont`'s `contify`), and any other tail call a `return_call`.
- **A module's stack holds the depth** ([`curios-runtime`'s README](../../../curios-runtime/README.md#a-module-runs-on-a-stack-of-its-own)), so a recursion left as written answers at any depth a program meets, and pays a frame and a return an element.

## The gap

**A builder written `[h, ..f(t)]` takes a frame an element.** At `34f5e7600`, `take`, `range`'s `go` and `Bytes/of_nat` erase to `…, b = List/build(h), r = f(t), c = List/concat(b, r)`, and at the continuation rung each keeps a call that resumes into the concatenation. The rebase registers no concatenation: its comment holds that an append is heterogeneous, so the carriers do not line up, and that a row which does not commute needs a placement rule. Read off `/std`'s source at `34f5e7600`, and not each confirmed to survive erasure, eleven list builders put the recursion on the right of the cons — `List`'s `take`, `take_while`, `zip` and `range`'s `go`, `sort_by`'s `push`, `Cli`'s `option_slots`, `required_slots`, `optional_slots` and `joined`, `internal/Handle`'s `each_ready`, `Tui/input`'s `flush` — and two byte builders do, `Bytes/of_nat` and `Hash/nat`'s `first`. A text built by `Str/concat(x, f(t))` reaches the same concatenation, since `/std/Str/concat` erases to a one-statement forwarder onto `Bytes/concat`.

**A comparison joined by `&&` takes one too.** `&&` erases to `/sys/Bool/and`, a one-statement forwarder onto `BoolAnd`, so `List/eql`'s `x == y && eql(xs, ys)` keeps a frame an element. The registry's reason for no `And` row, that "boolean and bitwise `and` share an erased operator with different identities", does not hold: `BoolAnd`, `NatAnd` and `IntAnd` are three operations, and were before the rebase was written.

**A context of two combines is declined.** The envelope takes one combine as a block's last statement, so `[h, ..f(t), h]`, erasing to `append(□, h)` and then `concat(build(h), □)`, keeps its frames, though its context is one word of one monoid.

**The rebase's account of itself is stale.** Its `//!` calls it "a correctness transform, not an optimization — without it the deferred-context corpus overflows the native stack". Since a module runs on a stack of its own, the stack holds that depth, as `curios`'s `tests::runtime::loop_tests::arena_deferred_context_recursion_is_stack_safe_at_depth` says: what a rebase removes is a frame and a return an element, and the stack the depth touches.

## Prior art

- **Koka derives the accumulator from the monoid's laws** ([Leijen and Lorenzen, POPL 2023](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2023/07/trmc-popl23.pdf), §4.4–4.5; extended in [JFP 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/tail-recursion-modulo-context-an-equational-approach-extended-version/)). A left-biased context `□ | v ⊙ A` folds to one accumulator applied as `k ⊙ e`; a right-biased one `A ⊙ v` composes the other way and applies as `e ⊙ k`, from which they derive "the text book accumulator version of reverse" over `++`; and a monoid that may not commute takes "a product context where we accumulate the left- and right context separately", composed `(l1, r1) • (l2, r2) = (l1 ⊙ l2, r2 ⊙ r1)` and applied `l ⊙ e ⊙ r`. Their liberal constructor context lets expressions stand before the hole and only values after it, "the semantics of Koka which has a strict left-to-right evaluation order".
- **OCaml passes a destination** ([Tail Modulo Constructor](https://ocaml.org/manual/5.3/tail_mod_cons.html)): opt-in by `[@tail_mod_cons]`, a constructor allocated with a hole that the callee fills, and "the constructor argument that is transformed into tail-position will always be evaluated last". Where two arguments recurse, "the compiler will not make an implicit choice, but ask the user to provide an explicit disambiguation".
- **LLVM's accumulator rewrite asks commutativity** ([`TailRecursionElimination.cpp`](https://llvm.org/doxygen/TailRecursionElimination_8cpp_source.html)): it "transforms functions that are prevented from being tail recursive by an associative and commutative expression to use an accumulator variable", the licence the rebase's rows have today.
- **Lean proves a twin equal instead** ([`Init.Data.List.Impl`](https://lean-lang.org/doc/api/Init/Data/List/Impl.html)): `take`, `takeWhile` and `zipWith` each have a hand-written version threading an `Array`, and a `@[csimp]` theorem such as `@take = @takeTR` has the compiler run the twin.
- **What it costs where measured.** Mercury's last call modulo constructor slowed its own compiler by 0.3% in one grade and about 2.1% in another ([users list, April 2016](https://lists.mercurylang.org/archives/users/2016-April/008141.html)); Erlang finds the order of a body and a tail recursion generally unpredictable, except that "a tail-recursive function that does not need to reverse the list at the end is faster than a body-recursive function" ([The Seven Myths of Erlang Performance](https://www.erlang.org/docs/22/efficiency_guide/myths)) — a rope's accumulator needs no reverse.

## Decisions

1. **The rebase is tail recursion modulo a monoid context.** The combines closing a leaf after its recursive call are read as one word `l ⊙ □ ⊙ r` of one monoid. The worker threads the sides its leaves use: a monoid that commutes folds both into one accumulator, as today; one that does not takes `acc ⊙ l` for a recursion on the right, `r ⊙ acc` for one on the left, and both accumulators where both sides occur. A base arm answers `l ⊙ v ⊙ r`, an identity in place of `v` dropped.
2. **A sequence's product is its concatenation, and an append is a word of it.** `append(s, x)` is `s ++ [x]` and `build(x…)` a word of generators, so the carriers line up: the accumulator has the sequence's type, and an element enters it as a one-element word. The sequence monoids are `ListConcat`, and `BinConcat` at each grain.
3. **Which operation is a monoid is the oracle's to state.** `semantics.rs` states an operation's identity and whether it commutes, by a match over the closed alphabets with no fallback arm, and the rebase reads it there rather than from a private table whose fallback arm leaves an operation out silently. The monoids: `BoolAnd` (`true`), `BoolOr`, `BoolXor` and `BoolNeq` (`false`), `BoolEql` (`true`), `NatAdd`, `NatOr`, `NatXor` and `NatMax` (`0`), `NatMul` (`1`), `IntAdd`, `IntOr` and `IntXor` (`0`), `IntMul` (`1`), `IntAnd` (`-1`, on the infinite two's complement `curios-num` computes), all commuting, and the sequence concatenations, which do not. `NatAnd`, `NatMin`, `IntMin` and `IntMax` have no identity on an unbounded carrier, and no float operation is associative. A law test holds every one the oracle names against its own folds.
4. **Only the combines move.** The recursive call and the chain of combines after it close the block, so every other operand is computed before the call, Koka's liberal context; the rewrite moves the combines across the call and nothing else.
5. **A forwarder reaches a sequence monoid** as it reaches a scalar one, so `Str/concat` resolves to `BinConcat` at the byte grain.
6. **Totality does not gate a monoid**, as it gates none of the numeric ones: a builder with no end becomes a loop whose accumulator grows on the heap, as a tail call that appends already does.
7. **The counted checks gate the landing; the timing is a reading.** Read off the code, an element of a builder goes from a frame and three allocations to three allocations. The accumulator leans left, as the ropes `List/fold` builders make already do (`filter`, `filter_map`, `partition`, `concat_map`), and its first forcing then grows a worklist to one entry an element where a right-leaning rope keeps about one.

## Open questions

- **What stops a builder with no end once it is a loop.** The engine sets the heap's initial size and states no maximum (`curios-runtime/src/engine.rs`), and what Wasmtime does when the heap cannot grow is not read. Settled by running `[n, ..forever(n + 1)]` once the sequence monoids land, under a time and a memory cap: a trap is stated in the runtime's decision, and a process killed rather than trapped is a finding of the runtime's.

## Stages

1. **The oracle's monoids.** Decisions 3 and 4 over the scalar monoids: the oracle's table and its law test, the rebase reading it, a chain of combines folded into one accumulator, the rebase's `//!` corrected. Expected to move: `List/eql` and every recursion joined by `&&`, `||` or another monoid the rebase leaves out today, each named.
2. **The sequence monoids.** Decisions 1, 2 and 5. Expected to move: every builder *The gap* names, `curios`'s `tests::runtime::loop_tests::a_recursion_with_no_end_stops_on_the_stack_it_was_given`, whose `forever` is a builder under a cons and takes a shape no monoid closes, and `a_recursion_as_deep_as_its_data_runs`, whose list builders stop being a recursion the stack holds.

## Verification

- **Nothing else moves.** `curios wonder stage cont-optm` of every program of `programs/` prints the same text before and after stage 1, but for the programs it names, each holding a new worker. Counted.
- **The laws.** `curios-ersd`'s semantics tests fold every operation the oracle names a monoid over sample constants, its identity on both sides, three-way associativity, and commutation where it is claimed. The test's enumeration of the alphabet is held complete by an exhaustive match. Counted.
- **The context forms.** `curios-ersd`'s rebase tests hold one module each: a chain of two combines, a recursion on the right of a concatenation, one on the left of an append, one in the middle, an identity dropped at a base, and an addend computed after the call, a hole read twice and a hole used as an element, each declined.
- **The workers by name.** One program reaching `List/take`, `take_while`, `zip`, `range` and `eql`, `Bytes/of_nat` and a recursion over `Str/concat` finds each one's worker in its `cont_optm`, as `arena_deferred_context_recursion_is_stack_safe_at_depth` finds `count`'s, and answers at a hundred thousand elements. Counted.
- **What a builder costs.** Two release compilers, the commit before stage 1 and stage 2's, each compile one program that builds a million elements by `List/range`, `List/zip`, `List/take` and a recursion over `Str/concat`, and reads each answer's length. Five groups of five runs, the variants interleaved inside each group, one untimed run opening each group; a variant's figure is the median of its group medians and its span their least and greatest. Timed, on a quiet machine.
- `cargo xtask workspace lint`, and `cargo xtask workspace test` once the stages land.

## Design decisions this overturns or corrects

- `curios-ersd`'s `optimize/rebase.rs`, its `//!` and its registry: a cost transform, not a correctness one; commutativity replaced by the placement rule; the `And` and append objections withdrawn. Both stages.
- [`curios-ersd`'s README](../../../curios-ersd/README.md#the-ersd-optimizer-is-thin), *The Ersd optimizer is thin*: the rebase re-bases a frame an element, which the stack no longer stops. The oracle's monoids.
- [`curios-runtime`'s README](../../../curios-runtime/README.md#a-module-runs-on-a-stack-of-its-own), *A module runs on a stack of its own*: a list built under a cons leaves its rationale's recursions the stack holds, and a recursion with no end stops on the stack only where it keeps its frames. The sequence monoids.
- [Nat and Int are an i31 until they outgrow it](../../design/arithmetic/nat-and-int-are-an-i31-until-they-outgrow-it.md): the rebase registers every monoid the oracle names, commuting or not. The sequence monoids.

## Rejected

- **A destination with a hole**, OCaml's passing and Koka's constructor contexts: a rope node states its length where it is built, and that length is the part not yet built, so a hole needs a length patched on the way out or a node shape of its own.
- **A twin proved equal**, Lean's `@[csimp]`: a proof about `zip` follows its recursion (`curios`'s `tests::universes::instance_tests`), so each builder takes a second definition, a proof and a door that swaps a definition where it is erased, reaches `/std` alone, and leaves a program's own builders a frame an element. The transform's licence is the free monoid's laws, which conversion already decides.
- **A move licensed by the effect summary.** The summary decides whether an unused binding may be deleted; a division takes its proof by position, and that proof can be the recursive call's answer, erased, so a statement that reads nothing of the answer may still stand on it. Every builder such a move would reach divides — `Flt`'s `digits_of`, `Hash/nat`'s `rest` and `from`, an addend computed after the call — and each is as deep as a number's digits.
- **Builders rewritten over an induction hypothesis**, whose `FoldSequence` is a backward loop: it fits a builder that takes one list apart, and `take`, `zip` and `range` do not.
- **Commutativity as the licence**, LLVM's: it leaves every sequence out.
- **Any evaluation context as the accumulator**, a continuation or its defunctionalization (Koka §4.1–4.3): it moves the frames to the heap, and the stack already holds them.
- **Mutually recursive groups and a recursion with two calls**: the rebase takes a function bound alone, and `/std`'s two groups (`Cli/check`'s `problems` and `beneath`, `Cli`'s `variables` and `variables_of`) and its one tree recursion (`Map/Node`'s `keys`) descend a command tree and a key's bits, never a list's length.
