# A compile costs what it reads

Working specification for making what a compilation does follow what its declarations read, at every stage it runs: an edit redoes, stage by stage, the declarations that read what it changed and no other, and a compile pays for the declarations of its scope it reads, not for the scope. The first is the minimality of a build, with early cutoff, at the grain of a declaration; the second is the same rule read from the other side, a scope being an input like any other. Parsing, storing and restoring are stages it runs, and a unit's boundary is no limit of the rule.

It rests on [A declaration is a function of what it reads](../../design/compilation/a-declaration-is-a-function-of-what-it-reads.md): a record of reads decides nothing while anything but reads reaches a declaration. [Item tasks](04-item-tasks.md) needs this, for the facets its workers write and the records that say which of them may run together.

## Where it stands

What is in the tree and what is open, each part by where it is written. What is in the tree moves the question that redoes nothing — a program restores neither unit's baseline half, and a question about the library parses the unit's header and no other file, lowers no declaration and elaborates and erases none — and what an edit redoes, which is the declarations that read what it changed, elaborated and erased again and no other. What is open is what a unit still costs to link, which *The gap* counts.

## What this builds on

- **The `Cache` seam and the stored-unit format** (`curios-pipeline/src/compile.rs`, `curios-unit`): a product-supplied policy the fold consults, and a serialized unit that already crosses processes, read in place before anything of it is restored (`curios_archive::access`).
- **A stored unit is a baseline, and each stage keeps what its own record says stands** ([A stored unit is a baseline for an item-level recompile](../../design/compilation/a-stored-unit-is-a-baseline-for-an-item-level-recompile.md)): a unit compiled over one is stored as the bytes it is compiled whole. Every door a declaration reads another through notes its question (`curios-elab/src/context/traced.rs`), a unit stores each declaration's trace and each item's erasure trace in its baseline half, and a unit compiled over a baseline is walked whole, each kept declaration's questions asked again in order (`Demand`, `curios-elab/src/elaborate/module/demand.rs`) and a part of a declaration elaborated again compared as it is stored (`curios-elab/src/same.rs`); erasure keeps an erased form by its own trace (`curios-elab/src/into_ersd/over.rs`), and a build takes a baseline as a question does (`Verdicts`' `Cache::baseline`).
- **A declaration is the unit of work** ([A declaration is a function of what it reads](../../design/compilation/a-declaration-is-a-function-of-what-it-reads.md)): lowered in identities of its own, elaborated on demand in a state of its own, an attempt that read a declaration not yet finished made again after it (`Demand`, `curios-elab/src/elaborate/module/demand.rs`), judged by the kernel alone, and stored in one order.
- **The accessors that record a need** (`Context::assumption`, `definition_body`, `var_reduct`, `induct_decl`, `struct_decl`, `concept`, `witness_unfinished`, `curios-elab/src/context.rs`): every read of another declaration's name, body or registry entry passes one of them, and no memo outlives the declaration that filled it, so no answer reaches a declaration without its read.
- **The certifier records what it read, and decides by it which of its verdicts stand** ([Cached verdicts](../../design/soundness/cached-verdicts.md), `curios_cert::Baseline`). `curios_core::Reads` holds, per definition, the items whose type, universe scheme or registry entry a judgment read and the bodies it asked for, and every unit files them. A walk over a baseline is handed the baseline whole and adopts its verdict on a declaration whose terms are the baseline's and whose filed reads name nothing that moved in the part read; no closure is handed in.
- **A witness is named by its module, its concept and the key its signature is spelled at** (`curios-core/src/witness.rs`), so whether a witness stands at a key is read off a unit's names.
- **An erased form is its item's, and a program links what it reaches** ([`curios-unit`'s README](../../../curios-unit/README.md#an-erased-form-is-its-items), `curios-elab/src/into_ersd/erased.rs`, `link.rs`).
- **Elaboration, erasure and the kernel read a scope where it stands** (`InScope`, `curios-elab/src/context/in_scope.rs`; `Globals`, `curios-cert/src/kernel/globals.rs`): a context asks the units in scope where its own stores hold nothing under a name, and is answered by reference. The kernel's environment is its units, each mounted by the places it keeps and the record filed with it, and a declaration whose verdict a walk adopts is read where the baseline holds it.
- **A stored elaborated module holds no position** ([`curios-unit`'s README](../../../curios-unit/README.md#a-stored-unit-says-what-its-items-mean-not-where-they-were-written-or-how-they-were-come-by)): one node for each structure as it is spelled (`curios_core::Sharing`), which is what lets a recompiled unit be held to the bytes of a whole one.
- **A unit is handed over, never copied** ([`curios-unit`'s README](../../../curios-unit/README.md#a-unit-is-handed-over-never-copied)): its lowered state, its elaborated module, its erased forms and its certification are each held by pointer, a unit compiled over a baseline keeps the baseline's own erased form for every item it does not erase again, and where a unit holds each name (`curios_core::Places`) is made once and kept with it. Elaboration reads the declarations a recompile keeps where the baseline holds them (`Established::keeping`, `curios-elab/src/established.rs`).
- **A term is placed in the declaration that wrote it** ([decision](../../design/compilation/a-term-is-placed-in-the-declaration-that-wrote-it.md)): a position in a Core term is an extent of its declaration and holds no source, a unit's text part says where each declaration lies (`curios_core::Anchors`), a report resolves an extent where it is made, and a parsed module says where each top-level item's own text lies (`Module::spans`, `curios-text/src/module.rs`).
- **A unit's documentation record and the paths a report spells a name by are made where they are asked for** ([A library is documented for its consumers, from the compilation that builds it](../../design/tools/a-library-is-documented-for-its-consumers-from-the-compilation-that-builds-it.md), [A diagnostic spells what its reader can write](../../design/tools/a-diagnostic-spells-what-its-reader-can-write.md)): each from the tables a unit stores for the units lowered after it (`document_unit`, `PreparedText::spellings`, `curios-text/src/into_core.rs`), so no unit holds either and a compilation that asks for neither makes none.
- **A declaration's lowering is kept where its text and the names it resolved stand** ([decision](../../design/compilation/a-lowering-is-kept-where-its-text-and-the-names-it-resolved-stand.md)): a unit lowered over a baseline asks each declaration's trace of lookups again (`curios_core::Trace`, `curios-text/src/into_core/lowered.rs`) and lowers the ones whose text or whose answers moved; the unit-wide passes read facts recorded with each item, and a recompile compares with the baseline only what it lowered again.
- **A file is parsed where a declaration of it is lowered** ([decision](../../design/compilation/a-file-is-parsed-where-a-declaration-of-it-is-lowered.md)): a unit keeps what each of its files declares with the text it was read from (`Scan`, `curios-text/src/into_core/head.rs`), in its baseline half, and a unit lowered over it takes a standing file's heads from there; discovery, the export view and each declaration's scope are decided from heads, and a file's tree is asked for by the first of its declarations that is lowered.
- **A file is read into one source for each text a process holds of it, and parsed at most once** (`sourced`, `tree_of`, `curios-text/src/root_source.rs`), and the lowering reads its predecessors as layers, copying none (`Scoped`, `curios-text/src/into_core/scoped.rs`).

## The gap

What a question about `/std` costs, compiled over the archived unit, in allocations. Counted at `8f1cb4982`, as *Verification* states; a count moves in its fifth digit from one run to the next.

| What a compile does | What it follows today | Nothing edited | `/std/Bool/not`'s body respelled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parses | the unit's header, twice, and whole each file an edit touched or a declaration of which is lowered again | 0.06 M | 0.17 M |
| Lowers | what was edited, after every record and every file's heads of the baseline's half | 0.45 M | 0.45 M |
| Passes from stage to stage | which names the lowering kept, the scope's layers and the finished module's zonk evidence, over every declaration | 0.10 M | 0.05 M |
| Elaborates | what read the edit, found by every declaration's trace, and the module's order where anything was made again | 0.11 M | 50.99 M |
| Judges | what read the edit, found by every declaration's record | 0.05 M | 11.27 M |
| Erases | what was elaborated again or read a binding that moved, found by every item's trace | 0.04 M | 1.60 M |
| Stores | the unit | 0.83 M | 0.76 M |

**A recompile walks the unit it does not redo.** With nothing edited, a unit compiled over a baseline reads every file and compares its text with the one the baseline read, restores the baseline's half whole to keep each declaration's lowering and each file's heads, 0.29 M of the lowering's 0.45 M (`into_core_unit::baseline`), asks again every question of every declaration's trace, 87,802 for the library's 2,223 declarations (`demand::kept`), and of every item's erasure trace, and serializes all of it. Where anything is made again the module is put in its stored order from nothing, 0.36 M whatever was edited (`order_unit_over`, `Module::ordered`), since the order is read off what every item reaches.

**An edit elaborates what read it, each from nothing.** Respelling `/std/Bool/not`'s body elaborates and erases again the 130 declarations that unfolded it and none behind them (`demand::asked`, `erase::asked`), and what they cost is what they cost a whole compile: a declaration is the grain, and one whose trace fails at its last question is elaborated whole.

**A program links what computes, observable or not.** A program links every declaration in its scope that computes at initialization, with what each reaches, for the optimizer's prune to drop the ones nothing observes: `programs/hello_world.crs` links 576 items, 137 of them because they compute.

**A question about a program redoes the program.** The entry is no unit (`Fold::check`, `curios-pipeline/src/standard.rs`): nothing of it is kept or filed, so each question lowers, elaborates, judges and erases every declaration it holds. Asked about `programs/walk_mirror_indexed.crs`, 111 lines, that is 0.22 M allocations elaborating and 0.11 M judging its 14 declarations, whichever was edited.

**A file is parsed whole, and a header twice.** The file an edit touched is parsed whole, and so is a standing file where one of its declarations is lowered again, though its head says where that declaration's text lies: `Bool.crs`, 3,102 bytes, is 0.11 M allocations of the edited question's parse, and a session parses a file again for every text it holds of it; the finding in [`surface`](../surface/00-findings.md), which bounds what a keystroke costs once nothing else follows the file. The rest of both columns is the unit's header, `lib.crs`, parsed twice before any unit is compiled: for the stems its package's root claims (`package_source`, `curios-package/src/layout.rs`), from the disk and outside the parse memo by design, and for whether the unit declares the file asked about (`RootSource::declares_module`).

**A void attempt is forgotten.** An attempt that read a declaration not yet elaborated is made again, and the next compile of the same unit finds the same order again: a whole compile of the prelude voids 45.

## Prior art

- **The goal has a definition.** Mokhov, Mitchell and Peyton Jones: "A build system is minimal if it executes tasks at most once per build and only if they transitively depend on inputs that changed since the previous build" ([Build Systems à la Carte](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/build-systems.pdf), definition 2.1), and under early cutoff, "When it executes a task and the result is unchanged from the previous build, it is unnecessary to execute the dependent tasks" (section 2.3). The tree today is the paper's Make for what an edit dirties: a static over-approximation, read before anything runs.
- **A trace of what was read decides what is rebuilt.** A verifying trace is "to record the values/hashes of dependencies used last time, and if something has changed, the key is dirty and must be rebuilt" (section 4.2.2). A restarting scheduler, which the elaborator's is, "is also not minimal in the sense that a task may start, do some meaningful work, and then abort" (section 4.1.2).
- **Reads are revalidated in the order they were made.** rustc's try-mark-green: "it is important that we visit each node in `reads(Q)` in same order as they occurred in the original compilation" ([Incremental compilation](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/queries/incremental-compilation.html)). A small query over a large one is a firewall: "even if the result of the monolithic query changes (which it is very likely to do) the small projections can still mostly be marked as green" ([Incremental compilation in detail](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/queries/incremental-compilation-in-detail.html)).
- **A result is known by a hash of what it says.** rustc: "Each time a new query result is computed, the query engine will compute a 128 bit hash value of the result", so that marking "can just compare the (already loaded) previous fingerprint to the fingerprint of the new result" ([Incremental compilation in detail](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/queries/incremental-compilation-in-detail.html)). Unison: "Each Unison definition is identified by a hash of its syntax tree", so "we can parse and typecheck definitions once, and then store the results in a cache which is never invalidated" ([The big idea](https://www.unison-lang.org/docs/the-big-idea/)).
- **A file has a summary a body's edit does not move.** rust-analyzer's `ItemTree` "condenses a single `SyntaxTree` into a "summary" data structure, which is stable over modifications to function bodies", under the invariant that "typing inside a function's body never invalidates global derived data" ([Architecture](https://rust-analyzer.github.io/book/contributing/architecture.html)).
- **The peers cut off the same way.** Skyframe: "If a node is invalidated, but upon rebuild, it is discovered that its new value is the same as its old value, the nodes that were invalidated due to a change in this node are 'resurrected'" ([Skyframe](https://bazel.build/reference/skyframe)). salsa backdates: "even though the inputs changed, the output didn't" ([The red-green algorithm](https://salsa-rs.github.io/salsa/reference/algorithm.html)).
- **Every stage is keyed, not one.** Sixty's build library "keeps track of what dependencies a task used when it was executed (much like Shake) in a table, i.e. what keys it fetched and what the values were", over a key for each parsed module, each resolved module and each definition's type ([Query-based compiler architectures](https://ollef.github.io/blog/posts/query-based-compilers.html)).
- **What is known not to have changed is not revalidated.** salsa's durability: "If a tracked function depends (transitively) only on high durability inputs, and you change a low durability input, then we can very easily determine that the tracked function result is still valid, avoiding the need to traverse the input edges one by one" ([The red-green algorithm](https://salsa-rs.github.io/salsa/reference/algorithm.html)). A unit whose own digest stands is such an input here: every read of it stands with it.
- **A read of another unit carries what it read.** GHC's `Usage` records, for a module of the same package, the "Entities we depend on, sorted by occurrence name and fingerprinted", and "if we don't use anything from X it won't appear in our Usage" ([`GHC.Unit.Module.Deps`](https://hackage.haskell.org/package/ghc-9.10.1/docs/src/GHC.Unit.Module.Deps.html)). Go stamps a package with "the action ID, which is a hash of the inputs to the action that produced the packages or binary, and the content ID, which is a hash of the action output", and "we use the content IDs to compute the next action ID" ([`buildid.go`](https://go.dev/src/cmd/go/internal/work/buildid.go)).
- **A declaration is the grain, in a proof assistant too.** iCoq "tracks fine-grained dependencies between Coq definitions, propositions, and proofs, and only checks those proofs affected by changes between two revisions" ([Çelik, Palmskog and Gligoric](https://users.ece.utexas.edu/~gligoric/papers/abstracts/CelikETAL17iCoq.txt), ASE 2017). Leino and Wüstholz cache a verifier's results per procedure ([Fine-grained Caching of Verification Results](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/fine-grained-caching-verification-results/), CAV 2015). Lean's own incremental elaboration reuses a prefix: "If all the syntax inspected up to a certain point is unchanged, we can assume that the old state can be reused" ([`Lean.Language.Lean`](https://lean-lang.org/doc/api/Lean/Language/Lean.html)).
- **The peers split a signature from a body by declaring the body opaque.** Rocq's `.vos` is "similar to `foo.vo` except that all opaque proofs are skipped in the compilation process" ([Compiled interfaces](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/V9.0.0/refman/practical-tools/coq-commands.html)), and under Lean's module system "Changes to files that affect only non-exported information (e.g. proofs) will not trigger rebuilds outside of these files" ([The Module System](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/4.24.0/The-Module-System/)). Here a proof's body is opaque by its type (`curios_core::Definition::proof`), and which other bodies a declaration unfolded is recorded, never declared.
- **A lookup is a read whatever it finds.** Kotlin's incremental compiler keeps "for each file … the ABI it produces, and the names (not full declarations) that were looked up during its compilation" ([The Dark Secrets of Fast Compilation for Kotlin](https://blog.jetbrains.com/kotlin/2020/09/the-dark-secrets-of-fast-compilation-for-kotlin/)), and in Swift "The "depends" sets are tracked by instrumenting lookups in the compiler" ([Dependency analysis](https://github.com/swiftlang/swift/blob/main/docs/DependencyAnalysis.md)). GHC "identifies orphan modules, and visits the interface file of every orphan module below the module being compiled" for the instance no name leads to ([Separate compilation](https://ghc.gitlab.haskell.org/ghc/doc/users_guide/separate_compilation.html)). Curios's orphan rule and its keys spelled before elaboration are what let a goal's read be one key, found or absent.
- **A scope is read as it is asked for.** rustc's metadata is "A list of lazily-decoded values, with the added capability of random access" ([`rustc_metadata::rmeta`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/nightly/nightly-rustc/src/rustc_metadata/rmeta/mod.rs.html)). In Lean's `.olean` files "Lean terms and values are represented just as they are in memory", so a file "can be directly memory-mapped" ([Elaboration and compilation](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Elaboration-and-Compilation/)), and rkyv, which the store is written with, reads an archive with "just a pointer offset and a cast" ([Zero-copy deserialization](https://rkyv.org/zero-copy-deserialization.html)).
- **A stored interface is read by item, and what its items share is written once.** Of rustc's metadata, "Indexing into a `LazyTable<I, T>` does not require decoding entries other than the one being read", and for a type already written "the byte offset within the file being written is encoded instead" ([Serialization in rustc](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/serialization.html)). GHC added such a table for the types of an interface, since "Loading such `.hi` files from disk introduces many duplicates of memory expensive values in `IfaceType`", and over Agda's sources went from "101 MB on disk" to "24 MB on disk" ([`ghc-commits`](https://mail.haskell.org/pipermail/ghc-commits/2024-May/061520.html)). What nothing shares is read where it is asked for: Rocq's opaque proofs "are stored in an optionally loaded segment of .vo files" ([`Opaqueproof`](https://ocaml.org/p/rocq-runtime/9.0.1/rocq-runtime.kernel/Opaqueproof/index.html)).
- **Code is collected from roots, over summaries.** rustc's collector: "Lazy strategy means that items will only be instantiated when actually used" ([`rustc_monomorphize::collector`](https://doc.rust-lang.org/nightly/nightly-rustc/rustc_monomorphize/collector/index.html)). ThinLTO's bitcode "is augmented with a compact summary of the module", and "Fast and efficient whole-program analysis is then performed on the combined summary index" ([ThinLTO](https://clang.llvm.org/docs/ThinLTO.html)).

## Decisions

1. **A declaration has a facet for each stage, and a file has a scan.** A declaration's lowered form; what it elaborates to, with the registry entries, vectors and totality it publishes and, where it is a witness, its key; the certifier's verdict on it; its erased form; and the summary of what evaluating it does. A file's scan is what its items declare, each with where it is written. One task writes each, once, and reads another's through its facets alone.
2. **A facet is held, never copied.** A unit is an index of its declarations' facets and is handed over as that index: what a recompile keeps is the baseline's own facet, and what a session keeps, a store restores and a fold hands on is one unit.
3. **A facet is identified by what it says.** A digest of its content, its positions left out and its binders' names kept, computed once for each node: what "the same facet" means wherever a record is stored, across units and across processes. In one process two facets are compared as terms are, by pointer, then by hash, then by structure.
4. **Every read goes through one environment, and is recorded.** A read is a question — a declaration's signature, apart from its body; a registry entry; whether a witness stands at a key; a name looked up in a module; whether the unit's lemmas were on offer — and the identity of its answer, found apart from absent. A task's trace is its questions in the order it asked them. One type holds a trace and one rule says whether it stands, for every stage.
5. **A facet is reused where what it was made from stands.** Its own input is unchanged — an item's text for a lowered form, the lowered form for an elaboration, the elaborated declaration for a verdict and for an erased form — and every question of its trace is answered as it was, asked again in the order it was made, each one's declaration settled first. A facet made again and found the same dirties nothing. The rule does not ask which unit an answer is in.
6. **A trace is its reader's own, stored with its facet.** The lowering's for a lowered form, the elaborator's for what the declaration elaborated to, the certifier's for its verdict, as `Certification` holds it, and erasure's for the erased form.
7. **The certifier's verdict is a facet, and its reads name facets.** It decides which of its verdicts stand as it does today, from its own record, and by comparing the facets that record names in place of looking names up: a read of another unit carries the digest of what it read, so nothing of an adopted verdict stands on a baseline's address.
8. **Whether a proof was offered the unit's lemmas is one question of its declaration's trace.** The walk elaborates what the vocabulary reads before it offers anything, whole or over a baseline, so a declaration settled on the other side of that line from its trace is elaborated again, and no graph is consulted.
9. **A scope and a baseline are read where they stand, at every stage.** Erasure's view reads a unit's declarations by the places it keeps, as elaboration's scope and the kernel's environment do, and a report spells a name of a unit in scope by that unit's own paths, made where the report is: nothing of a scope or a baseline is copied, replayed, validated or keyed again.
10. **A program links what it reaches and what is observable.** Its entry's imports, transitively, and every declaration in scope whose evaluation at initialization something can observe, with what each reaches. Whether a declaration's top-level statement is observable is decided once, where its unit is certified, since totality is the certifier's: a summary is a facet of a declaration — an exported function's behaviour, and what a callee's resolution reads of an exported value — composed as the optimizer's own summary composes them. The prune then keeps what is reached or observable, as it did, so what a program runs at initialization does not follow what it links.
11. **The program asked for is a unit.** Its declarations have facets as any unit's do, compiled over a baseline as any unit's are, and its final term is treated as one more declaration; a session keeps it and a project files it.
12. **A unit is stored and restored by facet.** A unit's file is an index of its declarations, a blob for each facet that shares nothing with another declaration's — a lowered form, found by the names it declares, an erased form, a verdict, a trace — and one archive of its elaborated declarations, consed in one table, one node still standing for one structure. Reading a declaration restores its blobs and what it reaches of that archive, its signature apart from its body, and no other declaration, and a blob is validated where it is read. Filing a unit copies the blobs of the facets it kept and serializes the ones it made, with the elaborated archive, consed from the nodes its baseline already holds; and a unit compiled over a baseline is still the bytes it is compiled whole. Each name is written once in an image, and each file's text once, which its scan's heads and the places of its declarations both lie in; and whatever of a unit only its own recompile reads is restored by no other reader.
13. **A unit is addressed by who it is.** Its slot names the compiler, its mounts and the prefixes it declared, and no unit before it. What it read of another unit is in its facets' traces, by digest, so a unit after an edited dependency is believed facet by facet.

## Verification

- **The cost rows.** `cargo xtask profile programs/hello_world.crs --profile release` builds the compiler and files the program's rows. ## Design decisions this overturns or corrects

- [A module is a compilation unit, and the prelude is an environment](../../design/compilation/a-module-is-a-compilation-unit-and-the-prelude-is-an-environment.md): the program as something beside the units. Program.
- [A command compiles only as far as its answer needs, and a project keeps what it compiled](../../design/tools/a-command-compiles-only-as-far-as-its-answer-needs-and-a-project-keeps-what-it-compiled.md): what a question keeps of a program. Program.

## Rejected

- **salsa.** At 0.28.5, which describes itself as experimental, with no 1.0; its database model would own the term representation, and its invalidation would sit beside the store's trust argument rather than under it. The graph here has thousands of nodes and obligations — budgets, deterministic identities, charged caches — that a small component states directly.
- **A record and a rule for each stage**, the lowering's by text and interfaces, the elaborator's by its reads, erasure's by a closure: one rule kept in agreement by being read five times.
- **The certifier's reads as the elaborator's record.** They are what its judgment of the finished terms read, and a key found absent, a candidate tried and dropped and a lemma offered and not applied are in no finished term.
- **The names an elaborated declaration reaches as the record.** It is what a unit's items are ordered by, and it holds no read that left no name.
- **A digest compared inside a process.** Where a baseline is in hand it holds the facet itself, and a term's comparison ends at a pointer or a cached hash; a digest is what a record holds.
- **A unit addressed by the closure of what it depends on.** The closure needs an order no fold gives twice, and a unit after an edited dependency is still compiled whole.
- **A stored order of declarations**, Excel's calc chain. A reused declaration's reads are settled before it is examined, in the order its trace gives, so only a declaration made again can meet one unfinished, and a whole compile has no baseline to learn an order from.
- **Linking every declaration in scope and pruning**, or every one that computes: a program's link, its first analysis and its first prune walk what the library reaches, to drop nearly all of it for every program.
- **Evaluating a top-level value only where it is read.** It is the language's meaning that moves, a declaration whose initialization is observable running or not by what a program names; OCaml links a library's module only where it is referenced, "If this flag is not given, unreferenced modules are not linked in" ([`-linkall`](https://ocaml.org/manual/5.3/native.html)), and that is the choice.
- **The entry left a program beside the units.** Its declarations are the ones an author edits most, and nothing of them would be kept.

## Completion and retirement

Done when every stage's facet for a declaration is written once and reused where what it read stands, within a unit and across units; a recompile parses, lowers, elaborates, judges, erases and stores the declarations whose reads changed and no other; a compile restores what it reads of its scope; a program links what it reaches and observes; and the program asked for is compiled as a unit is. The rule, its rationale and what is rejected go to a design decision under `documentation/design/compilation/`; the environment's, the store's and the linker's contracts are in the owning crates' READMEs and rustdoc, and `.claude/rules/`' area routing names them.
