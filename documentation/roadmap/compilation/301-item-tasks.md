# A compilation is a graph of item tasks

**Its next step is stage 1, a bounded investigation nothing holds back.** The cost entries ahead of it under the filing order are uncertain, and an uncertain entry holds no work behind it ([Pending work is owned by its specification](../../design/pending-work-is-owned-by-its-specification.md)). Stage 1 records the serial baseline and models whole-declaration scheduling, and it decides whether the later stages pay on `/std`; stage 2 lands the executor seam and the gate with a job that shares nothing, before any representation changes.

Working specification for multithreaded compilation, the payoff of [a declaration being a function of what it reads](../../design/compilation/a-declaration-is-a-function-of-what-it-reads.md). Once that holds, no artifact carries a minted identity ([a unit carries none another compilation could mint](../../../curios-unit/README.md#a-unit-carries-no-identity-another-compilation-could-mint)) and no verdict depends on history ([no memo outlives its declaration](../../design/soundness/a-reduction-step-costs-what-it-builds.md)), the items of a compilation can run on as many workers as the product supplies, in any order, and the determinism that property gives is what the gate holds them to. The compiler is single-threaded today by omission rather than by decision, and every design statement to the contrary is listed under what this overturns.

## What this builds on

- **What already runs beside the compiler**: [Cranelift's parallel compilation](../../../curios-cranelift/README.md#compilation-runs-across-threads-and-only-where-compilation-exists), its first parallelism, `wonder --server`'s protocol thread, Binaryen behind its process lock (`curios-binaryen/src/lib.rs`), and the prelude images validated once per process (`ARCHIVES`, `curios-prelude/src/restore.rs`).
- **Names already cross threads**: qualifiers and symbols are interned once per process ([`curios-common`'s decision](../../../curios-common/README.md#qualifiers-and-symbols-are-interned-once-per-process)).
- **The instruments already read every thread**: the trace gives each thread a track whose slices nest as one stack, and a slice carries what its own thread allocated ([`curios-profile`'s decision](../../../curios-profile/README.md#each-thread-counts-what-it-takes-while-someone-reads-and-a-program-installs-the-allocator)), so a stream recorded from several workers folds as one from a single worker does.
- **An attempt that reads an unfinished declaration is void already**: the elaborator takes a unit's items on demand, each after the declarations it turns out to read, and an attempt that read one before it finished is void whatever it concluded and is made again (`Needs`, `curios-elab/src/context/needs.rs`). It is the executor's protocol on one worker.
- **A facet for each stage of a declaration, read through one environment** ([A compile costs what it reads](../../design/compilation/a-compile-costs-what-it-reads.md)): the recorded reads say which items may run together, a declaration's erased form is its own, so no arena is two workers', and a stored unit is addressed by who it is, so no order of units is part of what one is.

## Peers

**What worked.**

- **Lean** elaborates theorem bodies in parallel with one another and with the rest of elaboration since 4.19 (lean4#7084, [its release notes](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/4.26.0/releases/v4.19.0/)), behind a synchronization point between a declaration's header and its body; kernel checking and linting run per declaration beside it ([Ullrich, 2026](https://lean-lang.org/presentations/20260424_INRIA/)). The parallel work is the work that is independent by construction, which is what [publishing a signature ahead of its body](302-a-signature-is-published-ahead-of-its-body.md) gives here.
- **rustc** parallelizes type checking per item under one build that selects serial or parallel synchronization at run time (`rustc_data_structures::sync`, `DynSend`/`DynSync`); on one thread it is "typically 0% to 2% slower" than the serial front end ([the 2023 announcement](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2023/11/09/parallel-rustc/)), and its 2025 goal reports 20 to 30% and more off whole compiles on many threads ([the goal](https://goals.rust-lang.org/2025h2/parallel-front-end.html)).
- **Zig** keeps a part of its intern pool per thread beside power-of-two shards (`src/InternPool.zig`), and a compilation's state for each thread apart from what they share (`src/Zcu/PerThread.zig`): the split this draws for the elaborator's context, and the one the interners would take.
- **Kontroli**, a Rust checker for the λΠ-calculus modulo rewriting ([Färber, CPP 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.08766)), reaches up to 6.6× for type checking on eight threads on Isabelle/HOL after subtracting non-checking work. Rocq checks opaque proofs in worker processes (Barras, Tankink and Tassi, ITP 2015); GHC compiles modules in parallel under `-j`.

**What went wrong.**

- **rustc's blocking queries took years to make safe.** A worker that asks a query another runs waits on it, so a cycle between workers is a deadlock, which a separate thread breaks by walking the active queries ([the dev guide](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/parallel-rustc.html)); deadlocks and internal errors kept the front end on nightly from 2023 ([the 2024 goal](https://goals.rust-lang.org/2024h2/parallel-front-end.html)), and the 2026 goal still names cycle errors that differ between the parallel and serial compilers, and no robust test of their consistency ([the 2026 goal](https://rust-lang.github.io/rust-project-goals/2026/parallel-front-end.html)). Here no worker waits, a cycle is read off what the voided items needed, and the differential under injected delays is that test.
- **GHC's `-j` scales to a few cores and stops.** One reporter saw at best 2.5× at `-j8` and another 4×, the time going to contention on tables every module writes, the `NameCache` and the `FastString` table among the suspects, and to the collector's threads; separate processes scaled better ([GHC #9221](https://gitlab.haskell.org/ghc/ghc/issues/9221)). Curios's interners are such tables: each is one `Mutex` over a `HashMap`, taken on every lookup, a hit included (`intern`, `curios-common/src/interner.rs`, for `Qualifier` and `Symbol`).
- **An atomic count is paid by every term.** Kontroli measures a 28.2% single-thread overhead for its atomic term representation on the HOL Light dataset, a workload of the kind Curios's reduction is, building terms at every step; rustc's 0 to 2% is a front end that builds far fewer. Lean's runtime keeps one object layout and a count whose sign says whether the object is shared across threads, so only an object marked shared pays the atomic operation (`lean_mark_mt`, `lean.h`). These are different workloads and measurements, and supply no expected overhead or end-to-end speedup for Curios.

## The gap

**Nothing the compiler holds can cross a thread.** `Term` is an `Rc<Node>` whose caches are `Cell`s and `OnceCell`s (`curios-core/src/term.rs`); the surface trees are `Rc`-shared, and the elaborator's context holds `Rc`s and `RefCell`s throughout. `thread_local!` tables sit under that: the restored prelude (`PRELUDE`, `curios-prelude/src/restore.rs`, restored again by every thread that compiles), the packrat memo (`curios-parse/src/memo.rs`), the comment table (`curios-surface/src/parse.rs`), the parsed-file memo (`curios-module/src/root_source.rs`), the archived pointer an extent read last (`curios-core/src/extent.rs`), and the shared empty set of mentions (`curios-core/src/term/mentions.rs`). The look count (`looks`, `curios-core/src/term.rs`), counted in every build, is per thread as well, and is no obstacle: each of its readers reads it on the thread whose walk it counts.

**One context elaborates every declaration of a unit.** `curios-elab`'s `Context` holds in one value what an attempt at a declaration replaces as it begins (`Context::attempt` and `begin_state`, `curios-elab/src/context.rs`: its binder identities, metavariables and their solutions, and universe levels with their solver) and what every declaration reads once another has published it (`Program`, `curios-elab/src/context/program.rs`: the registry's inductive, struct and concept entries, the witness table, totality, variances and value sorts), written through `&mut self` as each item publishes (`Context::publish`). A worker needs a context of its own over one environment that every worker reads and each declaration writes once.

**The interners take one lock a lookup.** A lookup that finds its entry takes the table's lock as one that adds does, which costs nothing on one thread and is a point every worker meets on many: parsing interns a symbol for each identifier it reads.

**Nothing counts what a void costs.** The executor's waste is the attempts it voids, and on one worker their number and what they allocated are recorded nowhere: the `declaration` span (`curios-elab/src/elaborate/module.rs`) closes over a voided attempt as over a finished one.

## Permanent decisions

**The determinism contract.** A compilation's units, verdicts, diagnostics and emitted bytes are the same for every number of workers: [a declaration is a function of what it reads](../../design/compilation/a-declaration-is-a-function-of-what-it-reads.md), held under a schedule. The schedule is never an input: not to a verdict, not to a minted identity, not to the order any report is written in. That is also what keeps the worker count out of a stored unit's address. The transcript's plain drawing is no report: it is a log of events as they happen, every line naming its subject, so its order is the schedule's and needs to be no other.

**The execution strategy is the product's.** `curios-pipeline` declares an executor seam, as it declares `Cache` (`curios-pipeline/src/compile.rs`): `curios` supplies a work-stealing pool, rayon, which Wasmtime's parallel compilation already links into the native product ([`curios-cranelift`'s decision](../../../curios-cranelift/README.md#compilation-runs-across-threads-and-only-where-compilation-exists)), so the lock gains no package and `curios` one dependency row; `curios-js` and the fixtures supply one worker. One worker is the same code, not a second path.

**No worker waits on another.** A read discovered while running of a facet not yet written voids the attempt, as it does on one worker, and the item is queued again behind the declaration it needs, which is started where no worker holds it. So nothing deadlocks, and a cycle is read off what each voided item needed and reported by its members in source order, which is the same report under any schedule.

**One representation, shareable across threads.** Terms are shared by a count that crosses threads, and their derivations are filled once whichever worker asks first. Its single-threaded cost is measured and reported, never assumed, and no stored byte moves: an archived term restores to the same node either way.

## Open

- **Which count the terms take.** `Arc` is the plain answer and costs every term an atomic operation. Lean's mode bit costs only the terms a second worker reaches and needs a count of the project's own, which is `unsafe` code named before any is written, as `.claude/rules/rust.md` asks. Stage 3's single-worker reading against the bar stage 1 sets decides between them: `Arc` where it is within the bar, the mode bit where it is not.
- **How a scalar cache is filled.** The scalar caches (`ScalarCache`, `curios-core/src/term/scalars.rs`, two `Cell<u64>`s filled by a post-order walk on first demand) are computed at construction, a node restored from an image, which skips its caches, paying for them where it is restored; or kept lazy in atomics, the hash stored before the word whose bit says it is filled, with release and acquire, two workers racing to fill one node writing the same bits, a derivation being a function of its node.
- **How an interner is read.** A lookup that hits reads under a read-mostly lock, through a front cache per worker, or in a shard of its own as Zig's pool does; stage 1's count of lookups per declaration and per file says how much the lock is met, and so whether it is the first contention or none.

## The components

| Component | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Executor | Once-written facets on the product's pool. An item becomes ready when the declarations its lowering names are published; a read discovered while running — a chosen witness, a key, an unfolded body — of a facet not yet written voids the attempt and requeues the item, as the decision above states |
| Parsing and lowering | Per file, then per module in two phases over the possibly cyclic module graph: collect every module's exports, then lower |
| Terms and trees | Shared across threads by the count *Open* settles; `OnceCell`s become `OnceLock`s; the scalar caches as *Open* settles |
| Elaborator's context | A context per worker over one published environment: what an attempt replaces is the worker's own, and the entries a declaration publishes are written once, as it publishes, and read by every worker. The witness table's keys are known before any item elaborates, a witness answering under the key its lowered signature spells, which its elaborated signature must agree with (`Error::WitnessKeyNotSpelled`, `curios-elab/src/error.rs`), and each witness's entry is written as its signature publishes, a read of one not yet written voiding the attempt as any other read does |
| Interners | A lookup that hits takes no lock every worker meets, as *Open* settles |
| Per-thread tables | The prelude restored once per process; the packrat and comment tables become state of the parse that owns them; the parsed-file memo and an extent's last read become the session's; the look count stays per thread |
| Progress and transcript | Each event names its subject, and both drawings hold any number of subjects open ([A command narrates on one transcript and reports in blocks](../../design/tooling/a-command-narrates-on-one-transcript-and-reports-in-blocks.md)); the live drawing streams the oldest open subject, so whether a worker's unit opens in the fold's order, or its events carry the fold's position, is settled with the executor, keeping the scrollback's order the fold's. A running check's count of its declarations (`Walked`, `curios-elab/src/context/underway.rs`, told through `OnWalk` by the demand walk today) is told as each declaration finishes, whichever worker finishes it, and of several in flight the running node names the one that has run longest |

Each declaration's lowering is held to its record and to its answers by a census ([A lowering is kept where its text and the names it resolved stand](../../design/compilation/a-lowering-is-kept-where-its-text-and-the-names-it-resolved-stand.md)), which scheduling from those records rests on. Source identity is settled by [A source is named by its package and read from its file](../../design/compilation/a-source-is-named-by-its-package-and-read-from-its-file.md).

## Stages

Each lands alone, on its own check.

1. **The serial baseline and the graph model.** The investigation's question: on how many workers does whole-declaration scheduling of `/std` keep them busy, and what does a void cost? It first gives the `declaration` span whether the attempt it closes was voided, and the interners a count of their lookups, read back by `curios profile` as every span's fields are; then records, on a default release build, `/std`'s and `programs/`' elapsed time, allocation work, peak memory and serial stage shares, the voided attempts with what they allocated, and the interners' lookups per declaration and per file. It ends at the graph model below with its modeled throughput for one to sixteen workers, and at the bar a single-worker overhead must stay within for stage 3 to proceed, set from those readings; where the model shows the critical path holding the workers idle, the stages after 2 wait on [publishing a signature ahead of its body](302-a-signature-is-published-ahead-of-its-body.md), which this model releases.
2. **The executor seam and the gate, with a job that shares nothing.** The seam and the two executors land with the differential, and the first job they run is `format` over its files, which reads no environment and no term: each file is parsed and formatted on a worker with the per-thread tables as they stand, and the report is written in the files' order. Check: the differential below over `curios format --check` of the tree, and its time with one worker and many.
3. **A term representation that crosses threads.** The count *Open* settles, the scalar caches, the per-thread tables made the owners' state, and the interners. Measured: the prelude build's wall time, the compile time of the corpus in `programs/` and peak memory on a release build, before and after, with one worker, under warm-ups and repeated interleaved baseline and candidate runs, reported as medians and spans with their conditions; the stage proceeds only within stage 1's bar. Check: every stored unit is the bytes it was, and every verdict over `/std` and the corpus is the one before the stage.
4. **The jobs that share the environment, each switched on alone and measured**: the certifier behind elaboration, elaboration by item on a context per worker, parsing and lowering by file and module, erasure by item, and the independent jobs — `curios test`'s library and executables, `lint` over its files, and `wonder`'s analysts.

## Verification

- At every stage, every verdict over `/std` and the corpus in `programs/` is compared with the compiler before the stage; a difference is a finding, never a fixture update.
- **The graph model.** The nodes are `/std`'s declarations, an item with the items generated from it, and an edge is a name `Definitions::reaches` (`curios-core/src/definitions.rs`) gives for an item of one, declared by another, over the restored prelude's module: every name the item's elaboration consumed, a witness it chose among them, so the edges are the ones an executor learns by running and not only those its lowering names. A declaration's weight is the allocations its `declaration` span made on its own thread in the stream of `curios-prelude-build`'s `profile` (`cargo xtask profile prelude`), a build script recording nothing, and a voided attempt's allocations are charged to the item that voided. The protocol records the commit, grouping of generated items, edge extraction, weights, source-order tie breaking and critical path. Modeled throughput on n workers is summed weight divided by the finishing time of a list schedule that hands the lowest-positioned ready item to a free worker, an item ready once every item it reaches has finished: the schedule an executor knowing every edge ahead reaches, which the voids measured beside it bound from below. Counted; no modeled factor is an elapsed-time speedup, since allocations measure no elapsed time, executor overhead, atomic sharing, contention, memory pressure, or the serial optimizers and emitter.
- The differential compiles `/std` and the corpus with one worker and with many, with cold and warm cabinets and under schedules varied by injected delays, and requires identical stored units, diagnostics and emitted bytes. Incremental edits and missing dependencies exercise retries and publication as well as successful full compiles.
- A genuine cycle between two items is reported with the same text under one worker and many.
- Each stage reports the time and peak memory it moved, named by the stage; the executor is compared with both the unchanged serial compiler and the one-worker shareable representation, so representation overhead and scheduling benefit remain separate.

## Design decisions this overturns or corrects

- [`curios-prelude`'s README](../../../curios-prelude/README.md#a-root-is-imaged-as-a-certified-unit): the images are restored once per process.
- `curios-wonder/src/server.rs`: its module documentation's *single-threaded by construction*.
- `curios-core/src/term/mentions.rs`: `Mentions::empty`'s comment justifying a per-thread table by `Rc`.
- `curios-common/src/interner.rs`: `intern`'s one lock a lookup, where a hit is shared by every worker.

## Rejected

- **Scheduling units rather than items.** The time is inside units — `/std` is one, and so is the program asked for — so a unit scheduler buys almost nothing now, and the item graph honours the unit graph without one.
- **Threads sharing nothing, as the end state.** Units crossing as archived bytes serve jobs as coarse as a whole compilation, restore the prelude per worker, and cannot share an environment between items. It is not a stage either: the one environment makes it unnecessary. `format` shares nothing by its nature, reading no environment, which is why it is the seam's first job and no precedent for the rest.
- **Two representations generic over their pointer**, Kontroli's answer: one representation spelled twice, kept in agreement by the type system rather than removed. Lean's mode bit is one representation, which is why it stays open.
- **A worker that blocks on another's item**, as Salsa's runtime does, parking a thread that asks a query another runs and keeping a graph of blocked threads to find a cycle across them ([the cycles chapter](https://docs.rs/crate/salsa/0.26.1/source/book/src/plumbing/cycles.md)), and as rustc's does, breaking a deadlock from a thread of its own: a void attempt and a requeue need neither, and the elaborator voids already.

## Non-goals

Parallelizing the ANF and CPS optimizers and the lowering between them, which [The optimizers read in parallel and write in order](303-the-optimizers-read-in-parallel-and-write-in-order.md) reserves; the emitter beyond Cranelift; caching per item in the cabinet; compiling across processes or machines; sharing cores with an outer build through a job server.

## Completion and retirement

- A compilation is the same bytes for every number of workers, and the handoff holds it.
- The prelude build and a program's compilation schedule ready independent work on the workers the product supplies, with measured end-to-end speedup, single-worker overhead, memory and remaining serial shares recorded. A dependency chain is not promised to occupy every worker.

Before this specification is deleted, its contracts are recorded in the owning crates' READMEs and rustdoc, its decision and its rejected alternatives are a design decision in `documentation/design/compilation/`, `.claude/rules/`' area routing names the executor, no reference to this filename remains.
