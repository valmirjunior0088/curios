# Arithmetic: findings

- **Uncertain: a direction in which the checkers could part on a guard over local definitions does not reproduce.** The kernel records a guard over local definitions with them substituted, and the elaborator records the spelling that names them, with the kernel's beside it as an alias no settlement reads, so under `let n = Byte/to_nat(c); match Nat/in_range(n, 0, 0x7F)` the kernel's reduct would answer `Byte/to_nat(c) <= 0x7F` where the elaborator's does not. Two programs written to meet it are accepted by both checkers at `9d121c13f`: in the `true` arm, `let _: Holds(Byte/to_nat(c) <= 0x7F) = True/qed()`, and over `Nat/in_range(n, 1, 0x7F)`, `let _: Holds(1 <= Byte/to_nat(c)) = Nat/le/of_in_range(Byte/to_nat(c), 1, 0x7F, True/qed()).low`. So is the opposite direction's program, `Nat/le/of_in_range(m, 240, 244, True/qed())` under `let n = m + 0; match Nat/in_range(n, 240, 244)`. Not known: whether a third spelling parts them, or whether only the procedure that proves a bound meets it (`curios`'s `tests::bounds` states the shape with `False/refuted()`). Investigate before any fix: read what each checker records for the guard and which spellings each probes.

- **`peel_int_pair` builds two terms before it asks whether either is its shape** (`curios-core/src/spine.rs`), where `peel_nat_pair` tests the intrinsics first. The peels' agreement is asserted, so a debug build pays the two allocations for every pair the list is asked. Fix: gate on the intrinsics, as `nat_shaped_intrinsic` does. Check: the `spine` tests and the law grid. Size: quick.
- **An operation that commutes by the peel is no atom of what encloses it.** `Eq()(Nat/and(x, y), Nat/and(y, x))` closes by `Eq/refl()`, and each of these is refused: `Nat/and(x, y) - Nat/and(y, x)` against `0`; `Nat/or(x, y) + Nat/xor(z, w)` against `Nat/xor(w, z) + Nat/or(y, x)`, and the same pair under `*` and under `==`; `Nat/and(Nat/or(x, y), Nat/xor(z, w))` against itself with both operands commuted, in either order; `Nat/and(Nat/and(x, y), Nat/and(y, x))` against `Nat/and(x, y)`; `Nat/eql(x, y) && Nat/eql(y, x)` against `Nat/eql(x, y)`; `Int/and(x, y) - Int/and(y, x)` against `+0`; and `Flt/min(a + b, c * d)` against `Flt/min(b + a, d * c)`. With one operand spelled alike on both sides each holds. The walk that hands out atoms (`Walk::spine`, `curios-core/src/reduce/intrinsic/classed.rs`) reads through every operation the law table declares commutative and never hands the operation itself out, while its two operand orders are two spellings only the symmetric peel holds one: so a sum, a product, a difference, an idempotence or a second commutative operation over two of them pairs by an identity they do not have, and the verdict follows the order an operand was written in, which [A term is one where conversion says so](../../design/arithmetic/a-term-is-one-where-conversion-says-so.md) rules out. Every operation declared commutative is in it, the float sum, product, `min`, `max` and fused multiply-add among them. Fix: hand a read-through operation no fold spells one way out as an atom as well, once its operands are walked, so `Classes::of` asks its checker about two of one kind; count over `/std` what the extra questions cost before it lands. Check: the rows above in `ATOMS_HELD` of `tests::laws::written`, each checker asked alone, and an audit pass holding every generated law at a commuted spelling of a compound operand. Size: about a day.
- **The procedure that proves a bound tells two spellings of one call apart where conversion holds them one.** Under `f: (Nat) -> Nat, step: (k: Nat) -> Holds(f(k) <= f(k + 1)), n: Nat`, a proof of `Holds(f(0) <= f(n))` by induction on `n` holds, in its successor arm over `p`, the hypothesis `Holds(f(0) <= f(p))` and `step(p)`, and `True/proved()` refuses the arm's bound, reporting that it fails at `f(0) = 1, f(p + 1) = 0, f(p) = 1, f(p + 1) = 1`: one call, two atoms. The bound's argument is the successor the arm refined `n` to and `step(p)`'s is the sum as written, which reduction does not open inside a stuck call. The reader of facts hands the view each operand's reduct with solved metavariables substituted (`as_read`, `curios-elab/src/entailment/facts.rs`), which holds an operator's witness one term and nothing more, and `curios-core`'s `linear` module states the rest as the declining direction, an atom being one term up to universe instances. Conversion classes a pair's atoms by its own verdict ([A term is one where conversion says so](../../design/arithmetic/a-term-is-one-where-conversion-says-so.md)), so the two read one call differently, and a proof written by hand over the two spellings checks. Fix: the reader classes the leaves it hands the view by the elaborator's conversion, asked with nothing committed, as the conversion chain classes a pair's; count over `/std` what the questions cost before it lands. Check: the statement above as a row of `curios`'s `tests::bounds`. Size: about a day.
- **Uncertain: the procedure that proves a bound finds a proof that does not check.** `True/proved()` answers "a proof was found and did not check, which is the compiler's fault" at `bf21a9065` for three true statements. Two take no hypothesis, at `Nat` and at `Int` alike: `Holds(Nat/min(a + c, b + c) <= Nat/min(a, b) + c)` and `Holds(Nat/max(a, b) + c <= Nat/max(a + c, b + c))`. Each closes by hand, by a match on `a <= b` that names `min/of_le` and `min/of_ge` over each node in its arm and leaves the rest to `True/proved()`, and the neighbours are proved: the other direction of each, a literal in place of `c`, either node beside a variable, and `Nat/min(a, b) + c <= Nat/max(a + c, b + c)`. The third stands in a proof by recursion, `let bounds(n: Nat, @ok: Holds(0 < n)) -> Holds(Nat/pow(2, Nat/log2(n)) <= n)`: in the `false` arm of `match n < 2`, under `let half = Nat/div(n, 2, @True/qed())` and `let inner: Holds(Nat/pow(2, Nat/log2(half)) <= half) = bounds(half)`, the bound `Holds(2 * Nat/pow(2, Nat/log2(half)) <= n)` is reported as following from the facts in scope and then as the fault. It is proved with a parameter `g` in place of `Nat/log2` and the fact taken from a parameter in place of the call. Not known: the cause of either, or whether they share one. One lead for the first two: their nodes split on `a + c <= b + c` and on `a <= b`, one comparison to conversion, and `Reader::extrema` (`curios-elab/src/entailment/facts.rs`) joins two nodes in one split only where their guards are one term. A match written by hand on the one guard inside the other checks, with both guards' facts read in the arm where they contradict each other, so two splits over one comparison are not the whole of it. Investigate before any fix: have `check` (`curios-elab/src/entailment.rs`) say what the candidate is refused for. Check: the three statements as rows of `curios`'s `tests::bounds`. Size: unknown until the cause is.
- **Uncertain: the procedure that proves a bound cancels a factor above zero and not one below it.** Over `Int`, `True/proved()` proves `Holds(a <= b)` from `Holds(a * k <= b * k)` and `Holds(+0 < k)`, and refuses it from `Holds(b * k <= a * k)` and `Holds(k < +0)` at `bf21a9065`, naming both facts as read and no assignment that satisfies them. It proves the other direction under either sign, `Holds(b * k <= a * k)` from `Holds(a <= b)` and `Holds(k <= +0)`. The refused bound is the fact summed with the product of `k + 1 <= +0` and the negated bound `b + 1 <= a`, a pair of the kind `products` (`curios-elab/src/entailment/facts.rs`) forms for the factor above zero. Not known: whether the pair is formed and the search misses its sum, or it is left out. A lemma at a factor below zero is proved today by handing the case above zero the factor negated. Check: the two statements as rows of `curios`'s `tests::bounds`. Size: unknown until the cause is.
