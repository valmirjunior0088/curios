# The standard library's indispensable tier

## Status

Researched and probed, not started. This file records a survey of what nine standard libraries agree on, the distance between that and `/std` — read from source and probed through `wonder` on 2026-09-01, every claim marked *probed* elaborated through it — the items that close it in two phases and the order they land within each, the test that pins each item, and the decisions taken while planning them. A second survey, of the same libraries' filesystem and subprocess surfaces, is the second phase together with the two terminal rows the terminal specification found it needed: together they are the host half the command-line and terminal specifications presuppose, and every host row this campaign adds is stated here and nowhere else. The modules keep their own contracts in `curios-prelude-archive/std/` and nothing here restates one. Nothing is started.

## Why it exists

`/std` grew by consumer: every module exists because a codec, a benchmark or a proof needed it. That made it deep where a consumer pushed — a certified `BigNat` algebra, a crit-bit `Map` with an injectivity law, a poll-driven `Async` — and thin where none did. Nothing in it sorts. A string cannot be compared. `List` has ten functions. `Vec` cannot be indexed. There is no `Set`. A proof cannot recurse along a measure.

A survey of Lean 4 (`Init`, `Std`, Batteries), Agda (`agda-stdlib` 2.x), Idris 2 (`base`), Rocq (`Stdlib`), Haskell (`base`), OCaml (`Stdlib` 5.x), Rust (`core`, `alloc`, `std`), Gleam and Zig was taken to separate what every one of them ships from what only some do. The tier every library agrees on is the one specified here: a capability is in it when each of the nine has it and a program of ordinary shape meets its absence early. Two further tiers — what most libraries ship, and what a consumer must first ask for — came out of the same survey and are deliberately not in this file. The survey is the yardstick for the program-facing surface, where an item is cheap and the two sibling specifications already consume it; the proof-facing items are held to the tree's own rule that a proof is written when a consumer asks, because that is where the cost and the risk sit, and two of them were found to have blockers the first draft of this file did not state.

The same thinness holds where a program meets the machine. The host implements open, read, write and close on files, sockets and TLS, the clocks, randomness, arguments, one environment variable at a time, and exit — so a program can read a file whose path it knows and replace it whole, and cannot list a directory, remove or rename anything, ask whether a path exists, learn its working directory, run another program, take its terminal out of canonical mode, or learn how wide that terminal is. The command-line specification ([command-line-interface-spec.md](command-line-interface-spec.md)) and the terminal one ([terminal-ui-spec.md](terminal-ui-spec.md)) describe programs that act on a machine they cannot inspect. A second survey took the host surface of the same nine — Lean's `IO.FS` and `IO.Process`, Agda's `System.Directory.Primitive` and `System.Process.Primitive`, Idris's `System`, `System.Directory` and `System.File`, Haskell's `directory` and `process`, OCaml's `Sys` and `Unix`, Rust's `std::fs` and `std::process`, Zig's `std.fs` and `std.process` — and WASI preview 1 beside them as the floor a WebAssembly host offers. Two of the nine forced the counting rule to bend, and the bend is stated rather than hidden: Rocq's `Stdlib` has no host interaction at all, and its de facto `coq-simple-io` binds `Sys.command`, `getenv` and `argv` and nothing of the filesystem; Gleam's standard library has none either, and `simplifile` and `shellout` are what every Gleam program uses. So for the host, a capability is in the tier when every library that has host interaction has it, Gleam's de facto packages counted and Rocq excused.

## What is certain

Read from source and probed through `wonder`. The library facts first, then the host facts.

- **Ordering stops at the numerics.** `Cmp` and `Ord` have witnesses on `Nat`, `Int`, `Flt`, `Char`, `Byte`, `BigNat` and `BigInt`; the positional tuple shapes up to eight have `Ord`, `Eql` and `Show` and no `Cmp`; and none of `Str`, `Bytes`, `Bool`, `List`, `Option` or `Result` has either. `"a" < "b"` is refused with `no witness of Cmp(Str) found` (probed). `Key(Str)` lets a string be a map key and not be compared. `Vec` and `Map` have no `Eql` or `Show`. `Ord` carries `use Eql(A)` and `cmp` and no law: nothing relates `cmp(a, b)` to `cmp(b, a)`, and nothing could be demanded of every witness, since `Int` has no inductive order to prove a law from and `Flt` cannot satisfy one.
- **`/std/List` holds `get`, `len`, `slice` and `map` from `/sys` — appending is the spread literal — and `try_get`, `drop`, `flatten`, `fold`, `find` and `balanced`.** Nothing sorts, filters, reverses, zips, takes, partitions, searches by index or traverses under a monad. Two idioms walk a list. `fold` is an index loop over `get` under the `Nat/Lt` invariant it proves once, which is the shape that threads an accumulator as a parameter. `find` is written the same way today, and the command-line specification's first item rewrites it as a case split on `[head, ..tail]` with a tail call, which needs no invariant, is total by structure, and is accepted in a type position, where the index loop is not. Every predicate function below is two lines of that second shape.
- **Every fold walks left to right with `(elem, acc)`.** `List`, `Bits`, `Bytes`, `Str` and `Map` agree, eight `/std` callers rely on the order, and `List.crs` states none of it. The right fold is the language's: the `; ih` binding on a `[head, ..tail]` arm is the structural recursion, on every carrier, and the optimizer works on it.
- **`List/balanced` is partial, and so is everything written over it.** Its halving on `(lo + hi) / 2` is not a decrease the arithmetic rung grades, so a proposition computed through it is refused as reaching a definition not known to terminate (probed). `Str/repeat` and `Str/join` are written over it and inherit the verdict. A `sort` that a proposition mentions cannot split its input that way.
- **`/std/Str` holds `concat`, `slice`, `flatten`, `eql`, `eql_ascii_ci`, `repeat`, `fold`, `len`, `is_empty`, `find_index`, `find`, `try_get`, `get`, `join` and `trim`.** It composes and walks; it does not decompose. Its bytes are UTF-8 under a validity proof, `slice` counts scalars and transports the proof across the window through `Str/utf8`'s `take_valid` and `drop_valid`, and UTF-8 bytewise order is scalar-value order, so a code-point comparison needs no decoding.
- **`/std/Option` and `/std/Result` have `bind`, `map`, `unwrap_or` and their predicates; `/std/Vec` has `len`, `append`, `map`, `first` and `rest`.** `Vec` cannot be indexed, folded, zipped or built from a list. Indexing a `Str` or `Bytes` takes `(i: Nat, @ok: Nat/Lt(i, len))` with the bound discharged by reduction, which is the shape a `Vec` index takes. An `Option/get` under a decided `IsSome(o)` — `True` at `some`, `False` at `none` by a match — discharges on `some(3)` and refuses an opaque option by naming the binder it could not fill (probed).
- **Symbolic `Nat` arithmetic is largely definitional, and division is the exception in both directions.** `n + 0 = n`, `0 + n = n`, `(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)`, `a + b = b + a`, `a * b = b * a`, `a * (b + c) = a * b + a * c`, `n * 0 = 0`, `n - n = 0`, `(n + m) - m = n`, `n * 2 = n + n`, `n / 1 = n`, `n < n = false`, `n < n + 1 = true` and `Nat/Le(n, n + m)` all close by `Eq/refl()` or `True/qed()`. `n < m = not(m <= n)` does not. The reducer decides `n % 256 < 256` from the remainder's bound and folds a zero dividend, a unit divisor and self-division, and that is the whole of what it knows about a quotient: neither `Nat/Le(n / 256, n)`, nor `Nat/Lt(n / 256, n)` under `Nat/Lt(0, n)`, nor the recombination `256 * (n / 256) + n % 256 = n` in any spelling closes (probed), and an intrinsic quotient has no constructors, so none of them can be proved by induction either. Totality is the other direction: its arithmetic rung counts a call on `n / k` for a literal `k` of two or more, under a guard that rules zero out, as a decrease, so a proposition that recurses on `n / 256` is accepted and closes on a literal (probed). The lemma corpus Rocq's `Arith` and Agda's `Data.Nat.Properties` carry is therefore mostly unnecessary; what is missing is an induction principle stronger than the successor's, and `pow`, `gcd`, `lcm`, `log2`, `sqrt` and `Int/sign`, which every peer has and `/std` does not. `Int/of_nat` is not missing: it is `Nat/to_int`.
- **Strong induction by a bound elaborates.** `strong(P, step, n)` written by ordinary induction on a bound `k` under `Nat/Le(n, k)`, with the step's hypothesis at `Nat/Lt(m, n)` and the descent through `Nat/Lt/le_succ_of_lt` and `Nat/Le/trans`, elaborates at a `Type`-valued motive, and that one function serves a `Prop` use through cumulativity (probed).
- **An insertion sort with a decided `Sorted` closes by reduction, and its theorem needs a law.** `Sorted` as a match over adjacent pairs closes `Sorted(sort([3, 1, 2]))` with `True/qed()` and refuses `Sorted([1, 3, 2])` as `False` (probed). The general theorem does not follow: whichever way the adjacent pair is oriented, one branch of `insert` places the new element on the side its comparison did not look at, so proving that branch sorted needs `cmp(a, b) = flip(cmp(b, a))`, which `Ord` does not carry and, by the first bullet, cannot.
- **There is no `Set`, and `Map`'s keys are `Bytes` through `Key`.** `Map.crs` records that `Key(Nat)` was attempted and abandoned because its base-256 encoding recursed on `x / 256` and "intrinsic `Nat` eliminates only by unary successor, so no induction principle reaches that recursion". The recursion is no longer the obstacle: the arithmetic rung above accepts it. The equation is. Injectivity reaches the point where the remainders agree and the quotients agree, and concluding that the numbers agree is exactly the recombination the reducer does not perform. `Map` has `get`, `has`, `insert`, `remove`, `fold`, `entries`, `keys`, `values` and `of`, and no update, map, filter or union. Its trie is canonical, so structural equality of two maps is equality of their entries.
- **Well-founded recursion can be declared, and the analysis now reads the descent a proof needs.** An accessibility predicate declared as `induct Accessible(@A: Type, R: (A, A) -> Prop): (A) -> Prop | intro(@x: A, below: (y: A, r: R(y, x)) -> Accessible(R, y)): (x) end` is accepted, passes positivity, and a fixpoint over it elaborates at a `Type`-valued result. At a `Prop`-valued result the same fixpoint was refused — `a proof in '/strong' is a proof position but reaches '/strong', which is not known to terminate` — because the recursive call goes through `below(m, lt)`, a function-typed constructor payload, which size-change termination did not count as a decrease. It does now: an application of a constructor payload reads as the payload, and `documentation/soundness/whole-module-passes/record_totality-t.md` records the rule and its probes. Programs never needed the predicate — general recursion is unrestricted where erasure keeps the result — and proofs, which do, can use it; the library half is item seven.
- **The host ABI has one filesystem row, no process row and no terminal row.** `curios-abi`'s table (`curios-abi/src/host/ops.rs`) holds `file/open` over a `Mode` of read, write-truncating or append, the handle rows `read`, `write`, `poll` and `close`, the socket, DNS and TLS rows, `clock/wall`, `clock/mono`, `rand/bytes`, `proc/args` and `proc/env`; `/std/File` wraps the first into `open`, `with`, `read`, `write`, `close` and `read_all`, and `/std/proc` holds `args`, `env` and `exit`. A row is written five times — the table, `/sys`'s mirror, which the prelude derives from the table by subject with nothing beside it to update, `OsHost` with `MockHost` beside it, the runtime's hand-written codec binding in `engine.rs`, and the browser harness, where `open` answers `permission_denied` and `args` is unsupported. The codec lifts and lowers tuples up to arity three, and lifts no list of byte strings: a row with more results than three, or a bytes list among its parameters, extends `lift.rs` and `lower.rs` first. A `Handle` is an fd token, so a pipe from a child would be read, written, polled and closed by rows that already exist. The named status codes are `ok`, `eof`, `not_found`, `permission_denied`, `exists`, `refused`, `tls` and `would_block`, with an errno passthrough lane below them that the browser cannot fill, and `/std/Handle/Error` has no `Show`. `curios-runtime` enables rustix's `event` feature alone.
- **Every library that has a host surface agrees on the same short list.** Remove a file; rename a file or directory; create a directory; remove an empty one; list one by name; ask whether a path exists and whether it is a file or a directory; read the working directory; write a whole file in one call; run a program with an argument list, wait for it, and read its exit code with its output captured or inherited. WASI preview 1 has every one of these except the working directory, which it replaces with preopens, and process creation, which it has none of — the browser harness's answer too. Beyond the list agreement stops: `chdir` is missing from `simplifile`; seek from Idris; exclusive create from Lean, Haskell and Idris; recursive create and remove from Idris and OCaml; symbolic links from Idris; a temporary directory from Zig and Idris; a shell-string runner from Lean, Rust and Zig; and killing a child from Idris, Agda and Gleam.
- **`set_nonblocking` is recorded as a no-op on every resource that is not a socket.** `OsHost::with_socket` answers `Ok` for the standard streams, for a file, for a config token and for an in-flight lookup without touching the descriptor, which is what lets `Async/read` serve them, and it means their reads block the whole scheduler. A pipe to a child filed as one of those kinds would block the same way, and a program draining a child's two pipes would deadlock on the first read of whichever pipe the child is not writing.
- **Nothing sets a terminal mode or reads a window size, and stdin is served through a buffer `poll` cannot see.** Raw mode and the terminal's dimensions are unreachable from Curios by any spelling. `curios-runtime/src/os_host.rs` builds a `PollFd` for `Handle::Stdin`, so a fiber can wait for stdin to become readable, and a read on it is `stdin().lock().read(&mut buffer)`, Rust's shared buffered reader: a request smaller than what arrived leaves the remainder in a buffer `poll` cannot see, so a loop that waits on fd 0 and then reads a small count can stall with input already inside the process. A write to stdin already goes through `rustix::io::write` on the raw descriptor, so the read has a shape to copy. The native host restores no state at a trap today, and where `OsHost` is dropped relative to `instantiate`'s trap path is unread — the termios record of item eight would be the first host state with an exit obligation. The release targets are Linux and macOS (`.github/workflows/release.yml` builds two), so there is no Windows console to serve and termios is the whole of raw mode. The browser harness holds stdin at EOF (`curios-js/src/harness.js`), so a terminal program cannot run there whatever the rows answer.

## Phase one: the library

Ordered by consumer: what the command-line and terminal specifications reach for first lands first, and the proof-facing items last, where a blocker can be paid for or cut without holding up the rest. Each item depends on the ones before it and on nothing after, and each names the test file that pins it under `curios/src/tests/`.

### 1. `Str` decomposition

`split`, `split_once`, `starts_with`, `ends_with`, `contains`, `replace`, `lines`, `to_list`, `of_char`, `trim_start`, `trim_end`, `pad_start`, `pad_end`. Every result is a `Str` carried under the validity proof `slice` already threads; nothing re-validates. `lines` splits on `\n` and drops a preceding `\r`. Pinned in `strings/str_tests.rs`. The command-line specification's tokenizer helpers are deleted in favour of this item when it lands.

### 2. `Option`, `Result` and `Vec`

`Option`: `or`, `or_else`, `filter`, `flatten`, `to_result`, `unwrap_or_else`, and `get(o, @ok: IsSome(o))` with `IsSome` reducing to `True` or `False` by a match — the shape `Nat/Lt` has, probed above. `Result`: `to_option`, `unwrap_or_else`. `Vec`: `get(v, i, @ok: Nat/Lt(i, n))`, whose `cons` arm cancels the shared successor and whose `nil` arm eliminates the `False` the bound reduced to; `to_list`, `of_list`, `replicate`, `zip`, `fold`. `of_list` returns the dependent pair `{n: Nat, Vec(T, n)}`, since the length is not known statically; it is not an `Option`. `Eql` and `Show` on `Vec`. Pinned in `aggregates.rs`. The terminal specification's images are `Vec` twice and consume `get`, `zip` and `of_list`.

### 3. Ordering witnesses

`Cmp` and `Ord` on `Str`, `Bytes`, `Bool`, `List`, `Option` and `Result`; `Eql` and `Show` on `Map`. `Cmp(Bytes)` is a bytewise walk, and `Cmp(Str)` is that walk over the string's bytes, justified by the one line above: UTF-8 bytewise order is scalar-value order, so `sort` on strings means code-point order and decodes nothing. `List` compares lexicographically, `Option` with `none` below `some`, `Result` with `failure` below `success`, `Bool` with `false` below `true`, each stated once in its module. `Ord` gains no law. Pinned in `concepts/resolution_tests.rs` beside the witnesses already there.

### 4. `List`

The functions every peer has: `reverse`, `filter`, `filter_map`, `any`, `all`, `zip`, `unzip`, `take`, `take_while`, `drop_while`, `partition`, `contains`, `index_of`, `find_index`, `last`, `range`, `replicate`, `concat_map`, `intersperse`. Each predicate and search is a case split on `[head, ..tail]` with a tail call, the shape the command-line specification gives `find`, so each is total by structure and usable in a type; the accumulator-threading functions stay over `fold`, and each module says which shape a function has. Two that structure the rest:

- **`traverse` and `each`** over any `Monad`: `traverse(@M, use Monad(M), l, f: (T) -> M(B)) -> M(List(B))`, and `each` discarding the results. Plain functions, one per container, not a concept.
- **`sort` over `Ord` and `sort_by` over a comparator**, written as the `; ih` right fold inserting each element before the elements equal to it, which is what makes it stable and is the recursion a later proof follows without the program changing. `sort_by` is the definition and `sort` is `sort_by` at `Ord/cmp`. Both are pinned by properties in `property.rs`: a sorted output under `is_sorted`, and the same element counts as the input.

`fold` keeps its name and its `(elem, acc)` order, and `List.crs` gains the line stating its direction. No `fold_right` is added: the `; ih` arm is the right fold, and a library twin would spend a stack frame per element on what the syntax already optimizes. No `fold_until`: the first draft of this file introduced one, with a `Step` inductive, to prove the index loop's invariant once for every predicate; the structural shape has no invariant to prove, and no peer ships the function.

### 5. Numerics and strong induction

`Nat`: `pow`, `gcd`, `lcm`, `log2`, `sqrt`, `is_even`. `Int`: `min`, `max`, `sign`. `gcd`, `log2` and `sqrt` are general recursion, classified partial and retained, and a type may not mention them; `pow` is structural on its exponent. And `Nat/Lt/strong`, course-of-values induction — `strong(P: (Nat) -> Type, step: (n, ih: (m, Nat/Lt(m, n)) -> P(m)) -> P(n), n) -> P(n)` — proved by ordinary induction on a bound `k` under `Nat/Le(n, k)`, which needs no new judgment and which the probe above elaborated. One function at a `Type`-valued motive serves proofs too. It is named under `Nat/Lt` because the hypothesis it hands the step is `Lt(m, n)`; the bound it descends on is the mechanism, not the principle. Pinned in `numeric/bound_tests.rs`.

### 6. `Set` and `Map`

`Set(K)` is a struct over `Map(K)` whose value is the key itself: `insert(s, k)` is `Map/insert(m, k, k)`, so `to_list` and `fold` hand back the typed elements through `Map/values` with no decoder and no new law, where a `Set` over `Map({})` would hand back the `Bytes` the trie is keyed on. It mirrors `Map`'s surface with the value elided: `empty`, `len`, `has`, `insert`, `remove`, `fold`, `to_list`, `of`. `Map` gains `update`, `map`, `filter`, `union` and `get_or`, and `Set` gains `union` with it; set algebra exists exactly where `Map` has the counterpart, so there is no `intersect` or `difference` until a `Map` consumer wants one. Pinned in `map.rs`.

`Key(Nat)` is not in this item. Its encoder recurses on `n / 256` and is total by the arithmetic rung; its injectivity needs `256 * (n / 256) + n % 256 = n` for a symbolic `n`, which is neither definitional nor provable by induction, as the facts above record. That recombination is one reducer law with its soundness argument, on the perimeter, and it is the compiler item numeric keys wait on; until it lands a numeric key enters through `Bytes/of_nat` as `programs/README.md` describes, and the `Ord`-keyed tree every peer's `Set` is built on remains the alternative should the law prove unwelcome.

### 7. `/std/WellFounded`

The module's namesake is `WellFounded(R)`: every element is accessible under `R`, the constructive definition Rocq, Agda, Idris and Lean all use, and the one that is the induction principle. Beneath it, `WellFounded/Accessible(R, x)` as the inductive predicate above, `WellFounded/recurse` as the fixpoint, and `WellFounded/lt` proving `<` on `Nat` well-founded. The names are chosen over `Acc` because `/std` names its propositions as adjectives about the thing they qualify — `Finite`, `Valid`, `Canonical` — and `WellFounded` is the claim a user makes about their own relation.

The item's second half is in the compiler and has landed: totality counts a call as decreasing when its argument is a constructor payload bound by the pattern on the scrutinee, applied to anything. That is the rule under which `recurse` is usable in a proof position; it lives in `curios-analysis`, so both drivers and the kernel's re-check run it. What remains is the library half above; item five's `Nat/Lt/strong` serves the proofs that do not want a relation. Pinned in `soundness/totality_tests.rs`, where the accepting probe already lives.

## Phase two: the host

The host half depends on nothing in phase one and nothing in phase one depends on it. It is the second phase rather than interleaved because a row costs five implementations where a library function costs one, and its items are ordered by consumer too: the terminal rows the terminal specification cannot start without, then the filesystem, then subprocesses. Each row is a `Status`-bearing row over the slot vocabulary the table already has, mirrored into `/sys` by the prelude, and implemented by `OsHost`, `MockHost`, the codec binding and the browser harness, which answers every row of this phase with `permission_denied` exactly as `open` does today.

### Prerequisites in the runtime

Four changes in `curios-runtime` that no row can do without, landed before the first row and pinned in `host/stream_tests.rs`.

- **Stdin is read on the raw descriptor.** `read` on `Handle::Stdin` goes through `rustix::io::read` as the write already does, so nothing sits between `poll` and the bytes.
- **A pipe is a resource the host can make non-blocking.** `OsResource` gains a `Pipe` kind holding an `OwnedFd`, `set_nonblocking` applies `O_NONBLOCK` to it through `fcntl` rather than recording nothing, and `read`, `write`, `poll` and `close` serve it as they serve a file. Item ten's streams are filed as this kind.
- **The codec carries the new shapes.** `lift.rs` gains a lift for a list of byte strings, and both `lift.rs` and `lower.rs` gain tuple arities up to seven, the widest row below. `trait_param_of!` gains the `ListBytes` arm the parameter side lacks.
- **rustix's `termios`, `pipe` and `process` features are enabled**, which is where raw mode, the pipe and the child's descriptors come from.

### 8. Terminal

Two rows, found necessary by [the terminal specification](terminal-ui-spec.md) and stated here because every host row of this campaign is:

```
raw  as tty/raw  [h: Handle, on: Bool] [status: Status];
size as tty/size [h: Handle] [status: Status, cols: Nat, rows: Nat];
```

`raw(h, true)` records the descriptor's termios on first use and applies the raw settings — no canonical mode, no echo, no signal keys, no output post-processing, `VMIN` 1, `VTIME` 0; `raw(h, false)` restores the record. The native host also restores the record when it is dropped, so a trap or an `exit` leaves the terminal usable, and reading where `OsHost` is dropped relative to `instantiate`'s trap path is the first step of the item; a status of `other(ENOTTY)` is how a program learns it has no terminal. `size` is `TIOCGWINSZ`. Both are `Status`-bearing rows over the existing slot vocabulary, so the change is the table, `/sys`'s mirror, `OsHost`, `MockHost`, the codec binding and the JavaScript harness, and nothing more. Pinned in `host/stream_tests.rs` through `MockHost`, which records the mode it was asked for and answers a scripted size.

This item has no library half of its own. The rows are reached as `/sys/tty/raw` and `/sys/tty/size`, and what wraps them — the session bracket, the decoder, the renderer and the loop — is the terminal specification's to shape, written over `/sys/tty` directly.

Rejected: learning the size by writing `CSI 18 t` and parsing the reply from stdin, which needs no row but leaks the reply to the inner shell under tmux and ssh and puts a parser in the path of every keystroke. Rejected: a resize signal row, which would be the first signal in the ABI and carries the race that in-band resize reports were introduced to remove; a library polls `size` on a tick instead, and the interval is the terminal specification's to choose.

### 9. Filesystem

Seven rows over `Bytes` paths as `file/open` is:

```
stat        as file/stat   [path: Bytes] [status: Status, kind: Nat, size_hi: Nat, size_lo: Nat, mtime_hi: Nat, mtime_lo: Nat, mtime_nanos: Nat];
remove_file as file/remove [path: Bytes] [status: Status];
rename      as file/rename [from: Bytes, to: Bytes] [status: Status];
list        as dir/list    [path: Bytes] [status: Status, names: ListBytes];
create_dir  as dir/create  [path: Bytes] [status: Status];
remove_dir  as dir/remove  [path: Bytes] [status: Status];
cwd         as proc/cwd    [] [status: Status, path: Bytes];
```

`kind` is a tag for file, directory, symbolic link or other, and its codes are a `kind` module in `curios-abi`'s `codes.rs`, mirrored into `/sys/kind` by the prelude and into the harness's configuration by `curios-js`'s `abi.rs`, exactly as `mode` is. The size and the modification time are split base-10⁹ as `clock/wall` splits its seconds, so the i31 envelope is never asked to hold a file size. Three named codes join the status table — `not_empty`, `is_directory` and `not_directory` — because a program removing a directory has to tell them apart portably, and the browser has no errno to pass through; every other failure stays in the passthrough lane. `/std/Handle/Error` gains the three constructors, so every exhaustive match on it gains three arms, `host/file_tests.rs` included, and gains a `Show` witness rendering each constructor by name and `other` with its errno, because a program that fails has to say why and every peer's error displays.

The library half is `/std/fs`, lowercase as `proc`, `time` and `tcp` are, since it names a host service rather than a type: `stat` returning a `Metadata` struct whose `Kind` is the tag above, whose `size` is the pair `{hi: Nat, lo: Nat}` the row hands over with `size_nat` reading it as an `Option(Nat)` that is `none` past the envelope, and whose `mtime` is a `time/Instant`; `exists`, `is_file` and `is_dir` as one-liners over it, reading `not_found` as `false` and every other failure as the failure it is; `list` returning `List(Str)`; `create_dir`, `remove_dir`, `remove_file`, `rename` and `cwd`; and `create_dir_all` and `remove_all`, written over the rows with no row of their own, since a majority ships them and the host pays nothing for them. `/std/File` gains `write_all`, the twin `read_all` has lacked. Four pure functions over `Str` sit in `fs` beside the rest, because a listing returns names and opening one needs the join: `join`, `parent`, `name`, `extension`. The release targets are Linux and macOS, so the separator is `/` and the functions say so. Pinned in a new `host/fs_tests.rs`, with `MockHost`'s file map gaining directories.

### 10. Subprocess

Three rows:

```
spawn as proc/spawn [program: Bytes, args: ListBytes, cwd: Bytes, env: ListBytes, stdin: Nat, stdout: Nat, stderr: Nat] [status: Status, child: Handle, stdin: Handle, stdout: Handle, stderr: Handle];
wait  as proc/wait  [child: Handle] [status: Status, code: Nat, signal: Nat];
kill  as proc/kill  [child: Handle] [status: Status];
```

Each standard stream takes a tag of inherit, pipe or null — the shape Lean's `Stdio`, Haskell's `StdStream`, Rust's `Stdio` and Zig's `StdIo` share, with its codes in a `stdio_mode` module beside `kind` — and a stream that is not piped comes back as the empty handle a failed `open` already returns. A piped stream is filed as the `Pipe` resource the prerequisites add, so `read`, `write`, `poll`, `close`, `Async/nonblocking` and `Async/wait` work on it, and `Async/nonblocking` does what it says. An empty `cwd` inherits the parent's; `env` is a list of `NAME=VALUE` entries laid over the inherited environment, which is `execve`'s own shape. The child handle is pollable: the native host reaps on a thread and signals a pipe whose read end is the handle, the pattern `dns/lookup` already uses, so the scheduler waits on a child with the `wait` it has and the deadlock detector sees a fiber blocked on a handle. `wait` after the handle is readable returns at once with the exit code, or the signal that ended the child.

The library half lives in `/std/proc`, which already holds `args`, `env` and `exit`: a `Command` struct of program, arguments, an optional working directory, environment overrides and the three stream settings, with `Command/new(program, args)` as the all-inherit default and the rest written as struct updates; `spawn(cmd) -> Async(Result(Child, Error))`, the child acquired with `kill` as its finalizer so a cancelled task kills what it started, and `Child/wait` releasing it; `Child/stdin`, `Child/stdout` and `Child/stderr` as the piped handles; `Exit` as `exited(Nat)` or `signaled(Nat)`; `run(cmd) -> Async(Result({exit: Exit, stdout: Bytes, stderr: Bytes}, Error))`, capturing both outputs by draining each pipe in a task of its own under `Async/spawn` and joining both, since draining one to its end while the other fills is the deadlock every `process` library documents, and `select` would cancel whichever drain finished second; and `status(cmd) -> Async(Result(Exit, Error))`, inheriting everything. Pinned in `host/proc_tests.rs`, with `MockHost` gaining a scripted child: a program name mapped to the bytes it writes on each stream and the exit it ends with.

## Decisions taken

- The survey states the program-facing surface; the consumer rule governs the proof-facing items. The certified sort and `Key(Nat)` are held to it, and both leave this tier for the reasons their items record.
- Sort lands as a program, stable by inserting before equals, written as the right fold so a later proof follows it, and pinned by properties. The certified sort is a later item with a consumer, and it lands with both halves — `Sorted`, decided over adjacent pairs, and a permutation, which has no decided form and is an inductive relation — under an antisymmetry hypothesis on the comparator, since `Sorted` alone is satisfied by an empty output and `Ord` cannot carry the law.
- `Set(K)` stores the key as its value, so its elements come back typed. An `Ord`-keyed tree waits for the reducer law or for a consumer with non-`Bytes` keys, whichever comes first.
- `Key(Nat)` waits on the recombination law in the reducer, a compiler item; item five's `Nat/Lt/strong` is not on its path, since the arithmetic rung already accepts its recursion.
- Predicate and search functions on `List` are structural case splits, not loops over an invariant. No `fold_until`, no `Step`.
- `traverse` is a function per container. No `Functor`, `Applicative`, `Traversable` or `Monoid` concept: `map` is per type, `/sys/List/map` is an intrinsic, and a concept costs resolution on every call for no consumer.
- `fold` keeps its name and order; no `fold_right`.
- `Vec/get` and `Option/get` take a decided-proposition bound, as `Str/get` does; `Vec/of_list` returns a dependent pair.
- `Nat/Lt/strong` is one function at a `Type`-valued motive, and it lands before `WellFounded`; the totality rule is this campaign's, not a blocker deferred elsewhere.
- Each item names the test that pins it, as the command-line specification does.
- The runtime prerequisites land first and on their own: the raw stdin read, the non-blocking pipe, the codec shapes and the rustix features are what every row of phase two stands on.
- A capability every library reaches by one of two mechanisms is in the tier by one of them. Every library runs a child in another directory — half through `chdir`, half through a spawn argument — and the argument is the one that survives fibers, so there is no `chdir` row. The same holds for a child's environment: half set the process's, half pass a table to the spawn, and the spawn takes it.
- A listing returns names, not entries. Five of the seven return names and leave the kind to `stat`; the system call per entry is the accepted cost, and a `kind` beside each name is the row a consumer asks for.
- A name that is not UTF-8 fails the listing. The row carries `Bytes`, so a consumer that wants raw names is served without a new row.
- `stat` follows symbolic links, and reports the `symlink` kind only where the target is missing. A `stat` that does not follow is a second row with no consumer.
- A file size is the pair of limbs the row hands over, read as an `Option(Nat)` inside the envelope. `Nat` cannot hold it at runtime and `BigNat` is more than a size needs.
- A path is a `Str`, as `File/open` already takes. No `Path` type: the four functions in `fs` are what a listing needs, and a type would owe a witness roster before its first consumer.
- Cancellation kills the child. `Async` releases what a task acquired on both exits, and a child that outlives the task that spawned it is the one thing that rule cannot mean.
- `run` drains the two pipes under `spawn` and `join`, never `select`, which cancels the loser.
- The terminal's size is a row, not an escape query, and resize is polled, not a signal: the tmux and ssh reason for the first, and the race the second would reintroduce, are stated under item eight.
- The browser harness answers every row of phase two with `permission_denied`, as it answers `open`, rather than gaining a virtual filesystem or a terminal: WASI has no process creation either, and the harness's job is to run the playground.
