# Soundness: findings

- **Three readers abort on a bound variable no scope in the term binds, where the rule that admits one says none can.** [`curios-cert`'s README](../../../curios-cert/README.md) states, of the position boundary, that "`infer`'s own `Var` rule refuses an index as the boundary does, so no reader of one can abort ahead of the verdict". `infer`'s rule does refuse one (`Error::NotClosed`, `f8eda8f64`) and `step_var` stands stuck on one rather than asserting — so reduction hands such a term back, and three readers then read it as the variable it is not, each through `Var::unwrap`, which expects a free occurrence and panics. Reduction's own eta probe is the first: `step_func` opens a lambda's telescope and reads each argument's identity to see whether the lambda only forwards (`curios-cert/src/kernel/whnf.rs`), so a lambda whose body applies something to an index reaching past its binders aborts inside `whnf` — the one mechanism the README gives the job of taking every shape a caller can hand it. The sort lookup is the second: `Sort::classify`'s neutral arm reads a stuck spine's type off `synth_neutral`, whose `Var` arm is `kernel.local_type(var.unwrap())` (`curios-cert/src/kernel/sort.rs`). Conversion's two-variable case is the third, `left.unwrap() == right.unwrap()` (`curios-cert/src/kernel/convert.rs`), reached once `reduce_forced` has left both sides stuck. An abort is no refusal, which is [the soundness board](../../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md)'s fourth boundary. Three more sites read an index the same way and are not shown: `curios_analysis::pinned_by_targets` (`curios-analysis/src/invert.rs`), unguarded, where a constructor's index target that is an index reaches it; and `curios_core::eta_head` with the machine's own variable lookup, each reached from the machine only behind `accelerable`'s closedness clause, which is what that clause is for. `curios_analysis::solve_indices` guards its reader with `var.as_bound().is_none()`, which is the shape the others want. Seen at `7b63491ff` by three tests, one per reader, each aborting in `Var::unwrap` rather than returning a verdict, which land with the fix: `whnf::rules_tests::an_eta_probes_argument_may_be_a_bound_index` holds `whnf` of `Term::func([(x, nat_type())], Term::apply(Term::var(Var::bound(3)), [Term::var(Var::bound(2))]))` to the lambda itself; `sort::classification_tests::a_bound_index_is_refused_rather_than_aborted_on` holds `Sort::of` of `Term::var(Var::bound(0))` to an error; and `convert::tests::structural::two_bound_indices_are_refused_rather_than_aborted_on` holds `convert` of `Var::bound(0)` against `Var::bound(1)` at `Term::type_ground()` to an error. No route from a module is known, and the entry rests on the rule rather than on a route: `open_positions_of` refuses every position before any rule runs, but it files that verdict and the walk goes on, so what is not known is whether `accept`'s reductions or `check_group`'s assume-before-typing reaches one of the three before `infer`'s `Var` rule meets the index. Fix: each of the three answers through `Var::as_free` as `infer` and `step_var` do — the eta probe declining to contract, since there is no identity to compare and the lambda is then its own normal form; `synth_neutral` answering `None`, as it does for every shape it cannot type; and conversion's two-variable case comparing `as_free` against `as_free`, so two indices are apart unless both sides are free. The structural reading is `Var::unwrap` deleted, so the type carries the obligation and every reader is made to answer. Check: those three tests, plus `cargo xtask workspace lint`. Size: one commit for the three answers, two if `Var::unwrap` goes.
- **Uncertain: a typing made while the position and call recorders are suppressed is remembered, and the walk that types the same term is answered from it, so neither the local gate nor the positions inside the term are decided again.** `Kernel::read` raises `Positions::reading`, under which `record_call` records no call and `close_group` skips its partiality bump (`curios-cert/src/kernel/calls.rs`). `Reader::infer` is `infer`, so it still files the term's type in `Memos::types` with `closed` false — false for a group that does not descend, because the bump was skipped — and the entry stands until `restore_budget`. A later `infer` of the same term hits it, hands back the type, recalls no partiality and never re-enters `check_group`: the group's verdict stays the suppressed walk's `Total`, `Error::NotDescending` is never raised, and the positions inside the term, which the suppressed typing recorded none of, are recorded by nothing — where `Kernel::tried` discards the typings for exactly this reason. It breaks the invariant `curios-cert/src/kernel/spend.rs` states, that memos change only resource verdicts and never semantic ones, and the verdict it moves is the one `check_group`'s local gate exists for: "a member that erasure deletes must descend, or assuming it at its declared type certifies `rec f : False = f`". Seen at `7b63491ff` on `rec f(n : Nat) -> Type = f(n)`, whose member yields a sort and must therefore descend: after a `Kernel::read` has typed it, the same kernel admits it and reports `Total`, where a kernel with the memos off refuses it and reports `Partial`. **Not known:** whether anything types a term under suppression before the walk types it. `Positions::classifying`, the other suppression, is raised only around `erased_half`, which reaches `declared_into_prop`, `reduce_forced` and `Sort::of` and never `infer`; `Kernel::read` is `curios-erase`'s and runs after `certify_definitions` has returned its verdicts, and `certify_definitions_over` builds a fresh `Kernel`. So no route is known today, and what is wrong is that the gate rests on that order of two passes rather than on the memo. Fix: file no typing while `Positions::suppressed` holds, as none is filed for a term naming a member of a group under check — the one case `Memos` already refuses, and for this reason, that the answer would stand for positions and calls the hit does not record. Check: `cargo test --package curios-cert -- calls::tests`, with the case above asserting `Err(Error::NotDescending { .. })` and `Partial` under both readings, plus `cargo xtask workspace lint`. Size: one commit.
- **A registry entry's own erased positions are drained with the next judged item, so its obligation is reported under that item's name — or under the entrypoint's where its declaration is the module's last.** `verdicts_within` drains an item's positions with `kernel.take_checked()` and runs `check_positions` over them, and only then calls `accept`, which types the entries the item's formers carry. Those typings record positions of their own — a constructor's payload field type is a type position, recorded by `infer` — and nothing drains them until the next judged item's drain, which checks them against that item's totals and pushes any refusal under that item's name. `curios-pipeline` renders it as "the kernel refused `<name>`", so the reader is sent to a declaration that carries nothing of the fault; where the declaring item is the module's last, the positions are drained by the post-loop block and the verdict carries no name at all, which `Verdict`'s own doc reserves for the entrypoint expression and `xboard`'s report renders "at the closing term" — for a unit that may have no entrypoint. Measured against [a diagnostic spells what its reader can write](../../design/tools/a-diagnostic-spells-what-its-reader-can-write.md), and against the rule that what is decided of a declaration reads another only through what it published. Not a soundness hole and not uncertain: the obligation is checked, one item later, against a totals set that has only grown, so the direction is weakly stricter; what is wrong is which declaration the verdict names. Seen at `7b63491ff` over `loops : Nat` partial through a group that does not descend, a family whose constructor payload type reaches it, and a following definition — the verdict names the following definition, and with it removed, names nothing. Fix: drain and check the positions where the item's judgment ends, after `accept` rather than before it, so each item's `check_positions` reads the positions its own judgment recorded, its entries' among them. The move is sound in the direction it changes: the item's own classification already ran before `accept` does, and an entry's positions can only reach names declared before it. Check: `cargo test --package curios-cert -- totality_tests registry_tests`, with a case asserting the verdict's name is the declaring item's for both the followed and the last-item shapes, plus `cargo xtask workspace lint`. Size: one commit.
- **A refused walk drops the classification its positions could not decide, and where the refusal is withdrawn the declaration carrying them is admitted.** `Kernel::record_checked` classifies a position's type and files the answer in `Memos::halves` whatever the outcome was, where `Positions::settle` keeps the first failure for the drain to surface and answers `None`. `Kernel::tried` then takes the positions back through `Positions::take_back`, which drops a failure that arose inside the walk — on every refused walk, not only on one that is withdrawn — while the one memo it discards clears the typings and nothing of the halves. So a declaration whose check could not classify one of its positions is refused only if some other refusal survives, and where the refusal was an arm's and `unless_never_taken` withdraws it, nothing is left: `curios-cert/src/kernel/positions.rs` says such a failure is kept and surfaced with the drain, because a recording site returns nothing and cannot report it, and that a quiet one would leave positions silently unconstrained — which is what the walk then does. Seen at `7b63491ff` by two fixtures, each in three readings — the body as it stands, the body in the `true` arm of a split that reduces to `false` so the arm is withdrawn, and the body in the `true` arm of a split that reduces to `true` so the arm is taken. A `rec` group whose members `check_group` assumes at their stated types before its signature loop types any of them, so one member stands at the empty tuple *value* that no rule has typed: refused `NotAFunction` and `Unclassified` as it stands, refused `NotAFunction` where the arm is taken, and **admitted** where the arm is withdrawn. And a definition the walk refused and defined as stated anyway, `alias : Type 0 = ()`, used as a type, where `infer_type` reads the declared sort and passes while `Sort::of` reduces the body and refuses: the `Unclassified` disappears where the arm is withdrawn, the `alias` refusal alone surviving. **No `False` follows, and that is shown rather than argued.** The failure is about a type `infer_type` passes and `Sort::of` refuses, and within a module the walk judges whole there are exactly two places such a type can stand, each leaving a verdict of its own: a `rec` member's assumption, which the signature loop then types, refusing the group in the same item; and a name whose declared type and body disagree, which `check_definition` holds together, so such a name exists only where the walk refused it and `define_as_stated` put it in scope anyway. A constructor payload field type, which `infer_type` never visits inside the item's own check, is no third place — its domains are typed by `check_signature`. What is left outside this entry is a unit in scope whose declaration's type and body disagree, which is [the board](../../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md)'s second boundary. Fix: file no erased half where the classification failed, so a later position asks again and the failure is recorded where it stands; and `Positions::take_back` keeping a failure it did not find, which is the half that makes the admission above impossible. Check: `cargo test --package curios-cert -- totality_tests identity_tests`, with a case per fixture asserting `Error::Unclassified` present in every reading, plus `cargo xtask workspace lint`. Size: one commit.
- **A baseline's classification is read with no coverage question, where its two sibling readers ask one.** `curios-cert/src/baseline.rs`'s `part_digest` states the rule: the classification is read only where the record covers its unit, as `Globals::partial` reads one, since a record naming fewer definitions than the unit holds was not made by a walk over it — "here as there". Two of the three readers ask it: `Globals::mount` computes `covers` and `Globals::partial` requires it, and `part_digest` computes it and gates on it. `Environment::passed_partial` (`curios-cert/src/kernel/globals.rs`) does not — `Baseline` carries no such flag and nothing asks — and the read is fail-*open*: a missing entry answers `None`, which reads as total, and the three readers are OR-ed, so a declaration the walk does not visit reads total where no walk classified it. That is the identification obligations (T) and (V) exist to prevent, and the shape `recheck::totality_tests`' mutation check pins for the mounted twin. Not shown to reach `False`: the invariant holds by construction rather than by the check. An unvisited place holds the baseline's item by pointer equality, so the baseline declares the name; the baseline's own record covered its module, every declared name being classified on the whole path and only names the module no longer holds being dropped; and a unit carrying a refusal is never filed. So reaching the fail-open read needs a record no walk filed, which [the board](../../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md)'s second boundary puts outside the claim. What is wrong is that one fact is spelled in three places with one copy disagreeing, and that the disagreeing copy is the one that fails open. `Environment::passed_vectors` reads the baseline's vectors with no coverage question either, but both fall back fail-closed, so only the classification is exposed. Fix: carry `covered` on `Baseline` as a mounted unit carries it, computed once where a `Baseline` is built, and require it in `passed_partial` as `Globals::partial` requires its own. Check: `cargo test --package curios-cert`, plus a test beside the mutation-checked twin putting a non-covering record on a baseline rather than on a mount. Size: one commit.
- **Uncertain: a model small enough to run locally can be the candidate oracle of a hunt.** [A probe asks the board's question of a program on no ticket](../../../xboard/README.md) puts a candidate to the checkers exactly as a witness and exits 0 where it is admitted, so a hunt needs of its oracle programs, not judgment: every ticket on the board was found by a frontier model under an agent, and nothing shown says the question needs one. Seen by the paper's review of 2026-10-24, as an observation on the discipline, not a measurement. Not known: whether a model small enough for one RTX 4070 Super, run on its own, produces an admitted candidate against a commit known to admit one (a fixing commit's parent, its ticket's witnesses withheld), how long it takes to the first, whether different models reach the same flaw or different ones, and so whether a hunt scales out to many small oracles rather than a few large ones. Settled by: a run per model, sequential on that machine, against that commit under a time cap, each candidate put to `cargo xboard probe`, the time to the first admission and the flaw it shows recorded; an admission within the cap restates this as a finding on the hunt's harness, and none within the cap drops it. Size: unknown.
- **Uncertain: the size order reads a proposition's own case as structure, which irrelevance leaves no room for.** [Totality of the erased program](../../design/soundness/totality-of-the-erased-program.md) reads what reduction took apart, and an arm's case is read for a `Prop`-sorted scrutinee as for any other (`refined_by_arm`, `curios-analysis/src/totality/grade.rs`), so a recursion descends on an inductive proposition's constructors. A match on a proposition fires with the proof unreduced ([`Prop` is strict, proof-irrelevant, and definitionally K](../../design/theory/prop-is-strict-proof-irrelevant-and-definitionally-k.md)), so the arm's payloads are no part of the value it was handed, and where `Prop` is a subsingleton a strict decrease between two proofs is a decrease in a one-element set. `curios-analysis/src/totality.rs`'s module doc defends the reading by finiteness — every payload is sized under its family, so none is applied at the family to hand a value of it back — which argues the payload tree is finite and not that the order respects the equality irrelevance imposes. Seen by `wonder diagnostics -` at `aaaf861d2`, which accepts an accessibility recursion with its predicate declared `Prop` and another with it at `Type`, against a control refusing a proof recursion with no descent. Not known: whether the reading reaches `False`, narrowed at `7b63491ff` to a clash concluded at a position whose type distinguishes its inhabitants, where inhabitation is refused by the same conversion. Coverage is not one hurdle among several but one half of a closed tension: a witness needs an inhabited proposition whose every arm recurses, so every non-recursive constructor's arm must be ruled out by a clash at the scrutinee's indices — but the recursive constructor's payload must be at a type convertible to the scrutinee's own or the recursive call does not type, so the non-recursive constructor inhabits the scrutinee's type too and its target is therefore convertible to those indices, which is what the clash denies. The one asymmetry that could answer them apart is a clash between two of a proposition's constructors, which conversion denies and inversion would grant, and `unify_node` (`curios-analysis/src/invert.rs`) removes it: at a `Variant`/`Variant` position whose family's declared result sort is the literal `Prop` it answers with no clash and no equations, so the tag test is licensed only at a relevant family. Shown by three probes at `7b63491ff` — the reading accepted on a proposition's own case, and the witness it needs refused `MatrixUncovered` both with the recursion and without it. The reading had a guard once and lost it to a sibling: `ebf65a7c0` skipped a solved binder that `bears_proof` held of, for `a_proof_index_refined_by_irrelevance_is_graded_as_the_constructor_it_was_unified_with`, and `48db7bbd0` then took every inversion solution out of the order for `a_proofs_constructor_inside_an_arms_solution_is_graded_as_structure`, so the guard went with the carrier it guarded and the scrutinee's own refinement never had one. What a witness must get past is coverage: a recursion graded by that refinement needs an inhabited proposition whose every arm recurses, so every non-recursive constructor's arm must be ruled out by its index targets — `check_induct_arms` demands exactly that of an absent arm, since undecided is not absent — which is the route both fixed tickets took. Agda restricted it and reverted it (`agda/agda` PR #6639, commit `cca1e0d`), Lean keeps it at the cost of a definitional equality Carneiro shows undecidable and non-transitive, and Felicissimo, Leray, Pujet, Tabareau, Tanter and Winterhalter (LICS 2026) answer the undecidability by unfolding an accessibility predicate propositionally rather than definitionally — a price Curios does not pay, since irrelevance decides a `Prop` comparison before either side reduces and a proposition holding a payload its indices do not pin eliminates into no data. Settled by: a witness put to `cargo xboard report`, filed open where the kernel admits it; where none is found the reading is the totality decision's to state, naming what the alternative costs — `/std` descends on a bound and declares no accessibility predicate ([`curios-prelude`'s README](../../../curios-prelude/README.md)), and a predicate declared at `Type` keeps the recursion while erasure retains its tree. Size: unknown.
