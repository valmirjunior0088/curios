# Soundness: findings

- **A unit reordered over its baseline is admitted where a walk over every declaration refuses it.** The walk over a baseline (`curios-cert/src/recheck.rs`'s `Changed::of`, `kernel/globals.rs`'s `Unvisited::passed`) visits the items whose terms are not the baseline's and what reads them, and reads every other declaration where the baseline holds it once the walk has passed its place; nothing holds an unvisited declaration's own reads to the module's order, so a module storing a declaration ahead of one it reads — which Core text writes in any order — is admitted over a baseline that held the two the other way, while a walk over every declaration refuses it with `Undeclared`. At `6b7b91bf7`, a history stepping `/lib` from `let /lib/Q : Prop = True; let /lib/t : /lib/Q = qed;` to the same two items with `t` first builds, where the second text with no baseline is refused `Undeclared` in `/lib/t`, against [Cached verdicts](../../design/soundness/cached-verdicts.md)' "Whole and baseline compiles are required to agree" and its reading of an unvisited declaration as a walk that adopted it in its turn would: adopting `t` in its turn asks its read of `Q`, which is not settled at `t`'s place. No `False` follows that was found: the reordered declarations are the baseline's, so what they read is acyclic, and every edit to what they read visits them; what is stored is a unit out of the order every later walk and the link take items in (`Definitions::ordered`). Fix: in `Changed::of`, visit each held declaration whose filed reads name a declaration of the module stored at or after it, so `adopts` asks it in its turn. Check: a `curios-verdict` history test beside `believed_tests` stepping the two texts and expecting `Undeclared` in `/lib/t`. Size: one commit.
- **The kernel loses what a lambda's binder stands for once an arm splits the binder, and refuses the descent it carries.** `curios-cert/src/kernel/infer.rs`'s `infer_telescope` enters each binder of a lambda applied on the spot as standing for its argument (`Kernel::refine_size`), and an arm scrutinizing that binder enters its case for the same binder (`Kernel::assume_arm`, `refined_by_arm`), where `SizeContext::open` (`curios-analysis/src/totality/grade.rs`) inserts the case over the argument, so `x ↦ n` becomes `x ↦ k + 1` and `k` is no longer below the parameter `n`; a binder standing for a payload loses the same way, as `pub let twice(n: Nat) -> True = match n | 0 => True/qed() | k + 1 => ((x: Nat) => match x | 0 => True/qed() | j + 1 => twice(j) end)(k) end;` shows, refused by the kernel alone. At `6b7b91bf7`, `pub let below(n: Nat) -> True = ((x: Nat) => match x | 0 => True/qed() | k + 1 => below(k) end)(n);` is accepted by the elaborator and refused by the kernel (`NotDescending`, "a recursive proof or type … does not descend"), where the same split written over `n` descends, against [`curios-cert`'s README](../../../curios-cert/README.md#a-groups-calls-are-the-ones-the-kernel-types), whose size context holds both each arm's own case and each binder of a lambda applied on the spot standing for its argument. It refuses more, so no `False` follows. Fix: where an arm's scrutinee binder stands for a bare binder in the size context, the arm's case refines that binder too, in the same bracket, the two being one value wherever the lambda's body runs. Check: a `curios-cert` test beside `kernel::calls::tests::a_descent_through_an_applied_lambda_is_read_through_its_binder` splitting the lambda's binder, and a `curios` program test admitting `below`. Size: one commit.
- **A family whose only edit is the variance vector it carries is admitted over its baseline, where a walk over every declaration refuses it.** `curios-cert/src/kernel/globals.rs`'s `induct_alike` and `struct_alike` leave the carried `variances` out, as "elaboration's record, which no judgment here reads", but `recheck::accept` reads it, refusing an irrelevance an entry carries past the vector the walk computes (`Error::VarianceDenied`), since a later unit's elaborator compares by what an entry carries ([A nominal type's levels compare by variance](../../design/soundness/a-nominal-types-levels-compare-by-variance.md)); over a baseline `Changed::of` visits an item only where `Baseline::holds` is false, so a family whose terms are the baseline's and whose carried vector now claims an irrelevance is never asked, and the unit is stored carrying it beside the baseline's vector on record. At `c80cc2ee7`, a history stepping `/u` from `induct /u/Box.{u}: Type (u + 1) | box(T: Type u)` carrying `[Invariant]` to the same entry carrying `[Irrelevant]` builds, where the second text with no baseline is refused `VarianceDenied` in `/u/Box`, and a record `/u/R` holding a `Type u` field stepped the same way builds through `struct_alike` where it alone is refused alike, against [Cached verdicts](../../design/soundness/cached-verdicts.md)' "Whole and baseline compiles are required to agree". No `False` follows: the kernel compares two instances only by the vector the walk filed, so a coercion a later unit's elaborator admits on the lie is refused by the kernel. Fix: read `variances` in `induct_alike` and `struct_alike`, comparing it with the baseline's, and correct the comment's "which no judgment here reads". Check: `curios-verdict` history tests beside `believed_tests` stepping the family's two texts and the record's, each expecting `VarianceDenied`. Size: one commit.
- **A verdict the conversion memo filed from no pair answers inside that pair's own unfolding, so the kernel's acceptance follows the order its goals were met in, and switching the memos off refuses what it admits.** `compare` (`curios-cert/src/kernel/convert.rs`) asks `Kernel::convert_hit` before the spine, group and unfolding rules and files a verdict wherever `Pairs::apart` stood still across it, and `Pairs` holds only the pairs being unfolded on the current path, so a verdict records nothing of the pairs its own derivation unfolded: a `true` reached from no pair by unfolding a pair once is answered for the same goal met inside that pair's unfolding, where [Conversion recurrence](../../design/soundness/conversion-recurrence.md) leaves the pair apart and the kernel with the memos off answers `false`. At `c80cc2ee7`, over a local `g(x, y, z) = match z == 0 | true => 0 | false => g(y, 0, z - 1) end`, a Core text module asking `second : Holds(g(p, a, m) == g(q, b, m))` alone has it refused `Mismatch`, its inner pair `g(a, 0, m - 1)` against `g(b, 0, m - 1)` met inside `g`'s unfolding against itself, while asked after `first`, which compares that inner pair under an arm from no pair, `second` is admitted. The sentences that fail are the decision's "A remembered verdict answers wherever its goal is met, whatever is being unfolded there, since it rested on none of it", its **Rejected** reason for a closure over accepted pairs, that the result would depend on the order goals were met in, which the memo's does, and `curios-cert`'s README, under which switching the memos off moves an exhaustion point and the identities minted after a hit and nothing else. No `False` follows: what is admitted is true by a second unfolding, and the elaborator, whose remembered problems carry the unfoldings they stand inside, refuses `second` either way, so only a module built by hand meets the extra acceptance. Fix, one of two, each setting what an equal graph costs inside an unfolding: file with each verdict the pairs its derivation unfolded and answer it only where none of them is being unfolded on the asking path, which keeps `two_towers_built_apart_are_compared_once_per_pair_of_nodes` (recommended); or file and answer verdicts only where no pair is being unfolded. Check: a `curios-cert` `kernel::convert::tests::recursion` test asking the inner pair from no pair and then inside the pair's own unfolding, with the memos on and off, expecting `true` and then `false` in both, with `cargo xtask workspace lint` and `curios`'s `tests::runaways` with `runaway_measurements`, since a rule by which conversion ends a comparison changes. Size: one commit.
- **A group member whose binder is typed at a sibling is refused, since its body restates the binder with the sibling standing for nothing where its signature reads the sibling folded.** `curios-cert/src/kernel/module.rs`'s `check_group` checks each body, opened at the members' locals, which stand for nothing, against its type at the folded selection, so a lambda in a body whose annotation names a sibling — the elaborator writes it from the signature — compares the local's `x(0)` with the group's projection applied, and is refused `Mismatch`, though the body reads nothing of the sibling's value; [`curios-cert`'s README](../../../curios-cert/README.md#a-let-is-bound-for-typing-and-read-by-value) holds bodies over members that stand for nothing so that no body reads a value of its own group, and an identity reads none. At `910ab8f34`, `let outer(n: Nat) -> Nat = let T(m: Nat) -> Type = match m | 0 => Nat | k + 1 => T(k) end and f(x: T(0)) -> T(0) = x; f(n) + 1;` is refused by the elaborator ("type mismatch, inferred: T(0), expected: T(0)", the two printed alike), the member written `f: (x: T(0)) -> T(0) = (x) => x` is admitted by the elaborator and refused by the kernel, and Core text holding the same lambda is refused `Mismatch` in `/T`, where the lambda stating its binder at `#NatType` stands. It refuses more, so no `False` follows. Fix, one of three, each setting what a body reads of a sibling, in both checkers: within a group's bodies, convert a member's local with that group's folded selection of the same member, terminally, so a spelling echoed from the signature meets the signature and no local unfolds (recommended: the local denotes exactly that selection wherever the group is read, the tail binds it so, and a body still computes nothing of its group); or check each body at its type opened at the locals, which refuses `val: T(2) = taint`; or a member computing in the bodies checked after it, which makes acceptance follow the order members are written in. Check: a `curios-cert` Core text test admitting the lambda, a `curios` `tests::recursion` program running both forms, and `a_member_stands_for_nothing_in_its_groups_bodies` and the `annotated` half of `a_group_signature_reads_its_members_bodies` still refusing. Size: one commit.
- **A value or an arm of a case its family does not declare is refused as the family undeclared.** Where a case's signature is looked up against a family the environment holds — `curios-cert/src/kernel/infer.rs`'s `Subterm::Variant` rule and four sites of `curios-cert/src/kernel/infer/eliminate.rs` — a tag the family has no case for is refused by `Error::Undeclared` of the family, whose doc says the declaration itself is missing: `#{node = Variant} /sys/Bool/False/mk()` reads "no declaration registered for `/sys/Bool/False`" at `0d7d13c32`, naming a family that is declared and not the case that is not, against [A diagnostic spells what its reader can write](../../design/tools/a-diagnostic-spells-what-its-reader-can-write.md), and a board witness expecting `Undeclared` cannot tell the two apart. The family lookups in `curios-cert/src/kernel/at.rs` name rightly. Fix: refuse a tag a family does not declare with an error naming the family and the tag, named in the exhaustive match `xboard` reads. Check: a `curios-cert` Core text test putting that text and expecting the new error with the tag. Size: one commit.
- **Uncertain: a model small enough to run locally can be the candidate oracle of a hunt.** [A probe asks the board's question of a program on no ticket](../../../xboard/README.md) puts a candidate to the checkers exactly as a witness and exits 0 where it is admitted, so a hunt needs of its oracle programs, not judgment: every ticket on the board was found by a frontier model under an agent, and nothing shown says the question needs one. Seen by the paper's review of 2026-10-24, as an observation on the discipline, not a measurement. Not known: whether a model small enough for one RTX 4070 Super, run on its own, produces an admitted candidate against a commit known to admit one (a fixing commit's parent, its ticket's witnesses withheld), how long it takes to the first, whether different models reach the same flaw or different ones, and so whether a hunt scales out to many small oracles rather than a few large ones. Settled by: a run per model, sequential on that machine, against that commit under a time cap, each candidate put to `cargo xboard probe`, the time to the first admission and the flaw it shows recorded; an admission within the cap restates this as a finding on the hunt's harness, and none within the cap drops it. Size: unknown.
