# Soundness: findings

- **The η-rule contracts a function across a mark.** [Plicity is part of function identity](../../design/theory/plicity-is-part-of-function-identity.md) makes a binder's mark part of a function type's identity, so a term keeps its type's marks under reduction. `step_func` (`curios-cert/src/kernel/whnf.rs`) contracts `(x) => f(x)` to `f` where the arguments are the binders in order and `f` mentions none, and never reads a mark: `(@n: Nat) => ((x: Nat) => x)(n)`, at `(@n: Nat) -> Nat`, reduces to `(x: Nat) => x`, at `(x: Nat) -> Nat`. The elaborator's reducer and the closed machine (`curios-core/src/machine.rs`) share the rule. At a position handed no type, conversion then holds two β-equal terms of one type apart — `(@n) => n` against `(@n) => ((x) => x)(n)` is refused by `same_marks` once the second has contracted — and equates two terms of two types, `(n) => n` with `(@n) => ((x) => x)(n)`, which no typed question asks and `ground` does. A source program reaches the refusal at a typed position: `let stated: Eq()((@n: Nat) => n, (@n: Nat) => ((x: Nat) => x)(n)) = Eq/refl();` is refused by the elaborator's type mismatch, the expected index printed contracted to `(x) => x`, so a true equation between two lambdas of one type cannot be stated; the kernel is not asked, and the same equation under the explicit mark is admitted. Fix: a function contracts only where each argument's mark is its binder's, in the three reducers. Check: `plicity_distinguishes_two_function_literals`, put to both checkers under one name beside `plicity_distinguishes_two_function_types`, its control two literals under one mark whose bodies differ by a β-step and its refusal two apart in a mark; `eta_contracts_a_function_that_only_forwards` (`curios-cert/src/kernel/whnf/rules_tests.rs`) gains the near miss, a forwarding across a mark that stays as written; `cargo xtask workspace lint`, `cargo xtask workspace test`. Size: one commit.
- **A function literal's proof mark is held where the literal is inferred and not where it is checked.** [The closed machine binds a proof unreduced](../../design/compilation/the-closed-machine-binds-a-proof-unreduced.md) has the kernel refuse a mark its own reading of the binder's type does not bear. `hold_proof` (`curios-cert/src/obligation.rs`) is called where `infer_telescope` opens an inferred literal's binders, where a `let` binds and where a declaration's or a group's telescope is walked. `check_lambda` (`curios-cert/src/kernel/infer.rs`), the checked half of the λ rule, opens a literal's binders against its expected function type and never asks. That half is the route a definition's leading lambda takes, so a literal whose binder states a proof at `Nat` is admitted there and its argument is bound unreduced by the closed machine. No source program reaches it, since the elaborator states marks from the binder's type; a module built by hand does. Today the consequence is a cost and no verdict, since erasure reads a sort; under the form [A proof is known by its mark](300-a-proof-is-known-by-its-mark.md) reserves, erasure reading the mark, it would be a miscompilation. Fix: `check_lambda`'s entry step holds the literal's own mark at its domain, as `infer_telescope`'s does, with `Lockstep` handing over the left scope's `binds_proof`. Check: `a_checked_binder_marked_at_a_type_that_is_no_proposition_is_refused` beside `a_binder_marked_at_a_type_that_is_no_proposition_is_refused` in `curios-cert/src/kernel/infer/proof_binder_tests.rs`, putting `stating(Term::func([(n, nat_type())], Term::free_var(&n)), &[true])` to `check` against `Term::func_type([(binder(1, "m"), nat_type())], nat_type())` and expecting `Err(Error::NoProof(nat_type()))`; `cargo xtask workspace lint`. Size: one commit.

- **Uncertain: a model small enough to run locally can be the candidate oracle of a hunt.** [A probe asks the board's question of a program on no ticket](../../../xboard/README.md) puts a candidate to the checkers exactly as a witness and exits 0 where it is admitted, so a hunt needs of its oracle programs, not judgment: every ticket on the board was found by a frontier model under an agent, and nothing shown says the question needs one. Seen by the paper's review of 2026-10-24, as an observation on the discipline, not a measurement. Not known: whether a model small enough for one RTX 4070 Super, run on its own, produces an admitted candidate against a commit known to admit one (a fixing commit's parent, its ticket's witnesses withheld), how long it takes to the first, whether different models reach the same flaw or different ones, and so whether a hunt scales out to many small oracles rather than a few large ones. Settled by: a run per model, sequential on that machine, against that commit under a time cap, each candidate put to `cargo xboard probe`, the time to the first admission and the flaw it shows recorded; an admission within the cap restates this as a finding on the hunt's harness, and none within the cap drops it. Size: unknown.
- **The ground of "what is already in scope is believed" is a trust in who writes a record, and nothing states it.** [The soundness board](../../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md) says the environment's entries are believed on the ground that "the environment's entries were judged by the walk that built them", which is [Cached verdicts](../../design/soundness/cached-verdicts.md)' premise. The archived prelude's ground is checked: `curios-prelude`'s build script walks every item again and fails the build on a refusal. A unit's stored record is not: `Globals::mount` stands a scope entry at its type with its body, unjudged, and `Globals::partial` reads the mounted totality as filed. The store's slot is keyed by the compiler's digest and the verified read-set, which bind a record to this compiler over these inputs and attest nothing about a walk having concluded it. `curios-cert`'s `recheck::adoption_tests::a_forged_total_verdict_mounted_in_scope_admits_a_looping_proof` mounts a `Certification` filing `loop : Absurd = loop` as `Total` and the kernel admits `loop` at the empty proposition `Absurd` with no verdict, where the same record filing it `Partial` refuses it `NotTotal`. No program reaches it, and the board's witnesses mount only the real prelude, so it is no ticket; a forged store slot, or a compiler that filed a record without walking, is what would. Cached verdicts' **Where it is weakest** names invalidation and moved paths and not this, so the claim is narrower than a reader of either decision is told. Fix (sets): state the ground in both decisions as what it is — a record is believed as the compiler that wrote it is, the store is trusted local state, and bytes no walk wrote are outside the claim — since no check inside a record can tell a walk's verdict from a forger's without a secret or a second walk, and a second walk is the cost the store exists to save. Check: the docs build; the test above keeps the boundary's behaviour stated where a change to it is seen. Size: one commit.
