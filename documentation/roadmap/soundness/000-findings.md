# Soundness: findings

- **Reduction asserts on a block whose tail binds a count its siblings do not state, where its own rule says it goes stuck.** [`curios-cert`'s README](../../../curios-cert/README.md) states that reduction is total on arbitrary terms — "`whnf` never asserts on a shape a caller could hand it" — and gives that mechanism a job the other two cannot do, closing a class rather than instances of it. It does not hold for a block whose tail and siblings disagree: `unfold_rec` opens a `rec` tail over one term per member and `step_let` opens a `let`'s over one binder per binding, each through `Scope::open`'s `assert!` (`curios-cert/src/kernel/whnf.rs`, and the closed machine's twin of both in `curios-core/src/machine.rs`), so such a block aborts reduction instead of standing stuck. No route to one is known, and the entry rests on the rule rather than on a route: `check_group` and `under_bindings` read both counts as of this commit, so a module carrying such a block is refused before any rule reduces it, and the one reader that reduces a registry entry before typing it — `positivity_vectors`, run from `accept` ahead of `check_induct_decl` — reads a group's members and not its tail, shown at `1abdb06ae` by a constructor payload whose negative occurrence it found through such a block, reporting `NotPositive` beside the boundary's `Arity` and aborting on neither. An assert reachable only because another pass refuses first rests on the order of two passes, which is what the README's first mechanism exists not to rest on. Fix: both reducers answer that the block does not fire where the counts disagree — the `rec` block its own value at either demand, the `let` the same — with a case in each, since the kernel's reduction and the closed machine's are two statements of one rule and a fix to one is not a fix to the other. Check: `cargo test --package curios-cert -- whnf`, with a case per block form in `kernel::whnf::rules_tests` and its twin in `machine::tests`, plus `cargo xtask workspace lint`. Size: one commit.
- **A loose bound variable in a module a walk is handed aborts it: no boundary reads `reach`, and `Var::unwrap` is behind every rule that meets one.** `curios_core::Var::bound` is public, so a module built by hand can carry an occurrence no scope binds; `infer`'s `Var` rule reads it first (`kernel.local_type(var.unwrap())`, `curios-cert/src/kernel/infer.rs`) and `Var::unwrap`'s `expect("a free occurrence")` (`curios-core/src/scope.rs`) kills the walk, where [The soundness board](../../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md) states that the Rust implementation fails closed — "A panic, an abort or a stack overflow is acceptable only where it refuses" — and an abort inside `certify_module` is no verdict. Reduction reaches it through the layer both checkers share rather than through the kernel's own `step_var`: `accelerable` is `!has_local_free() && !has_metavar()` and reads nothing of `reach`, so the closed machine's `Var` arm (`curios-core/src/machine.rs`) takes such a term. `sort`'s `synth_neutral`, `convert`'s two-variable case, `step_var`'s let-bound assert and both statements of the η probe (`curios-cert/src/kernel/whnf.rs`, `curios-core/src/reduce.rs`) are the rest of the sites, with `curios-erase`'s eraser and `curios-analysis`'s inversion beside them. Every boundary is blind for one reason: `free_locals_of`, `Entrypoint::free_locals`, `validate_stored_identities` and `Zonked::project`'s `unfinished` all ask `Mentions`, which is built from `var.as_free()` and answers nothing for a bound occurrence — so the board's own `Proof::Module` path admits it and a ticket carrying one would kill `cargo xboard report`. The only `reach` read in the trusted base is `Memos::judged`, which declines to remember such a term and names the case. Distinct from a tail arity, which `check_group` and `under_bindings` now read: that is a count a boundary could read, and this is an index with no binder, which no arity check sees. Seen at `b1e398b5e` by three `fixture_verdicts` tests over a hand-built module — a body that is a loose bound variable, one loose past a lambda's binder, and the entry's body — each aborting in `Var::unwrap` rather than returning a verdict, and by a fourth over `whnf` aborting in the closed machine, the two sites discriminated by a distinct panic at each. Fix: refuse a carrier whose `reach()` is non-zero beside the free local `free_locals_of` reports, over the same positions and the entry's, by an `Error` variant of its own, since `Error::Unbound` carries a `Free`; the same clause in `validate_stored_identities` with a `Positional` variant and in `unfinished`; and name a closed position in the boundary rule of [`curios-cert`'s README](../../../curios-cert/README.md) beside the free local it states. `Scope::reach` is the body's less the arity, so the clause refuses no legitimately open scope body. Check: `cargo test --package curios-cert -- identity_tests`, with a test per position, plus `cargo xtask workspace lint`. Size: one commit.
- **Uncertain: a model small enough to run locally can be the candidate oracle of a hunt.** [A probe asks the board's question of a program on no ticket](../../../xboard/README.md) puts a candidate to the checkers exactly as a witness and exits 0 where it is admitted, so a hunt needs of its oracle programs, not judgment: every ticket on the board was found by a frontier model under an agent, and nothing shown says the question needs one. Seen by the paper's review of 2026-10-24, as an observation on the discipline, not a measurement. Not known: whether a model small enough for one RTX 4070 Super, run on its own, produces an admitted candidate against a commit known to admit one (a fixing commit's parent, its ticket's witnesses withheld), how long it takes to the first, whether different models reach the same flaw or different ones, and so whether a hunt scales out to many small oracles rather than a few large ones. Settled by: a run per model, sequential on that machine, against that commit under a time cap, each candidate put to `cargo xboard probe`, the time to the first admission and the flaw it shows recorded; an admission within the cap restates this as a finding on the hunt's harness, and none within the cap drops it. Size: unknown.
- **Uncertain: the size order reads a proposition's own case as structure, which irrelevance leaves no room for.** [Totality of the erased program](../../design/soundness/totality-of-the-erased-program.md) reads what reduction took apart, and an arm's case is read for a `Prop`-sorted scrutinee as for any other (`refined_by_arm`, `curios-analysis/src/totality/grade.rs`), so a recursion descends on an inductive proposition's constructors. A match on a proposition fires with the proof unreduced ([`Prop` is strict, proof-irrelevant, and definitionally K](../../design/theory/prop-is-strict-proof-irrelevant-and-definitionally-k.md)), so the arm's payloads are no part of the value it was handed, and where `Prop` is a subsingleton a strict decrease between two proofs is a decrease in a one-element set. `curios-analysis/src/totality.rs`'s module doc defends the reading by finiteness — every payload is sized under its family, so none is applied at the family to hand a value of it back — which argues the payload tree is finite and not that the order respects the equality irrelevance imposes. Seen by `wonder diagnostics -` at `aaaf861d2`, which accepts an accessibility recursion with its predicate declared `Prop` and another with it at `Type`, against a control refusing a proof recursion with no descent. Not known: whether the reading reaches `False`. The reading had a guard once and lost it to a sibling: `ebf65a7c0` skipped a solved binder that `bears_proof` held of, for `a_proof_index_refined_by_irrelevance_is_graded_as_the_constructor_it_was_unified_with`, and `48db7bbd0` then took every inversion solution out of the order for `a_proofs_constructor_inside_an_arms_solution_is_graded_as_structure`, so the guard went with the carrier it guarded and the scrutinee's own refinement never had one. What a witness must get past is coverage: a recursion graded by that refinement needs an inhabited proposition whose every arm recurses, so every non-recursive constructor's arm must be ruled out by its index targets — `check_induct_arms` demands exactly that of an absent arm, since undecided is not absent — which is the route both fixed tickets took. Agda restricted it and reverted it (`agda/agda` PR #6639, commit `cca1e0d`), Lean keeps it at the cost of a definitional equality Carneiro shows undecidable and non-transitive, and Felicissimo, Leray, Pujet, Tabareau, Tanter and Winterhalter (LICS 2026) answer the undecidability by unfolding an accessibility predicate propositionally rather than definitionally — a price Curios does not pay, since irrelevance decides a `Prop` comparison before either side reduces and a proposition holding a payload its indices do not pin eliminates into no data. Settled by: a witness put to `cargo xboard report`, filed open where the kernel admits it; where none is found the reading is the totality decision's to state, naming what the alternative costs — `/std` descends on a bound and declares no accessibility predicate ([`curios-prelude`'s README](../../../curios-prelude/README.md)), and a predicate declared at `Type` keeps the recursion while erasure retains its tree. Size: unknown.
