# Soundness: findings

- **A `rec` item is believed to be its baseline's under a universe context no walk assumed, which admits Hurkens' paradox.** `Baseline::holds` (`curios-cert/src/baseline.rs`) decides which of an earlier walk's verdicts a walk adopts, and its own doc states the test as "the same names bound to the same terms under the same universe context" — but its `Item::Rec` arm compares `this.group`, the member count, and each member's name and proof mark, and not `this.universe_context`, where its `Item::Let` arm compares `this.universe_context` beside the terms. A `RecItem` holds that context because it is the *declaration's*, positionally the parameters its members' levels mean, and `globals::held` publishes `universes: &rec.universe_context` of the unit in hand to every reader of a member — so an adopted group's readers read the filed context and there is no truthful second copy of it. That is what tells this from the one check an adopted item is already known to miss: `a_carried_variance_the_walk_denies_is_refused_where_the_item_is_judged_and_not_where_it_is_adopted` argues a carried variance lie harmless because "the vector the kernel files for the entry is the baseline's, which the walk computed over the same telescopes", and no such sentence can be written here. Seen at `7b63491ff` by two tests in `recheck::adoption_tests`: `a_groups_universe_context_dropped_is_judged_again_as_a_whole_compile_judges_it` fails, the walk over the baseline filing no verdict where a whole walk over the same module files `Mismatch` and `NotTotal`, while the `let` twin `a_schemes_constraint_strengthened_reaches_the_declaration_that_instantiated_it` passes; and a second composing three real walks admits the paradox. A baseline whose one item is the `carrier` of [a group met as a bare projection](../../../xboard/src/formation/a_group_met_as_a_bare_projection_is_checked_under_the_enclosing_declarations_constraints_and_read_under_the_readers.rs), a one-member `rec` here where the ticket has a `let`, is accepted under `u + 1 <= w, v <= w, v + 1 <= x, w + 1 <= x`; the same item with the same group and an empty constraint list is then offered over it, adopted unjudged and filed; and a reader of that unit writes `carrier@{0, 1, 0, 2}`, which the real declaration forbids, projects the four products now quantifying over `Type 0` and standing at `Type 0`, and closes with Hurkens' paradox boxed through a record at `Type 0`. The kernel returns no verdict. The control in the same test, the same reader over the unit as the baseline's walk judged it, is refused `Error::UniverseInstance`, so the admission is the filed context's and nothing else's. Inside the claim [the board](../../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md) draws, and not a forged record: no bytes are written by hand, each of the three walks is a real `certify_module`, and `Baseline::holds` is the kernel's own check of what a caller offered — `curios-cert/src/baseline.rs` states that "no caller says which declarations are kept, so the trusted base rests on no closure it did not compute", so a pairing it accepts is a closure the kernel computed wrongly rather than a claim it was entitled to believe. Not a ticket, for the board's shape and not for its merit: `Proof` (`xboard/src/support/ticket.rs`) has two forms, a source program and a module put to the kernel with the prelude in scope, and this witness needs a module judged *over a baseline* and then a reader judged in the scope that walk filed — so it cannot be put by `put_module`, and its `False` is a locally declared empty proposition rather than `/std/Bool/False`. Widening the board is [its README](../../../xboard/README.md)'s decision to make and sets. Not known: whether a source edit can produce the pair, identical group terms under different assumed constraints, the constraints being the elaborator's solver's output from the declaration's own elaboration; the route stands on the kernel's own check being incomplete rather than on the pipeline offering it. Extent: the `Item::Rec` arm alone. The `Item::Let` arm compares the context, `induct_alike` and `struct_alike` compare a registry entry's, and the signature read digest (`Stood`, `read_digest`) covers "its type, its universe context, its registry entries and the certifier's verdict on it". Fix: `this.universe_context == that.universe_context` in the `Item::Rec` arm of `Baseline::holds`, which brings the arm to its own doc and to what the `Item::Let` arm does. Then the first test passes and the second's adoption step refuses, so the second is a demonstration rather than a test to land. Shown to shut the route at `7b63491ff`: with the conjunct added, the whole `curios-cert` suite passes with the premise test among it, and the walk over the baseline judges the item under the empty context and refuses it `Mismatch` and `NotTotal`, so the unit is never filed and the reader never gets its scope. The diff is corrective and additive — one conjunct, nothing deleted or loosened. Check: `cargo test --package curios-cert -- recheck::adoption_tests`, with the premise test landed, plus `cargo xtask workspace lint` and `cargo xtask workspace test`, since what the kernel adopts reaches every stage after it, and `cargo test --package curios-pipeline` for the `incremental_*` differentials. Landing the tests crosses `.claude/rules/rust.md`'s 25-test split, `adoption_tests.rs` holding 23 today. Size: one commit.
- **Reduction aborts on a `rec` group whose member scopes bind a count the group does not hold, where its own rule says it goes stuck — and the count is read before any typing rule reaches it.** [`curios-cert`'s README](../../../curios-cert/README.md) states that reduction is total on arbitrary terms, naming among the shapes it stands stuck on "a `rec` or `let` block whose tail binds other than one binder per member or per binding". The clause names the *tail*, and `Rec::opens` (`curios-core/src/term/shape.rs`) reads the tail alone: a group whose **member** scopes bind other than one binder per member satisfies it, and every reducer asks `Rec::as_proj` before it, so the guard is never reached. A member selection reduces by stepping to the member's body, `RecGroup::member_body`, which opens that member's scope over one term per member through `Scope::open`'s `assert!` — the kernel's `force`, `unfold_spelling` and `unfold_rec_apply` (`curios-cert/src/kernel/whnf.rs`), the closed machine's `eval` and its `Frame::Head` at the forced demand (`curios-core/src/machine.rs`), and the elaborator's twin of both (`curios-elab/src/reduce.rs`); `member_type` carries the same count into `synth_neutral` (`curios-cert/src/kernel/sort.rs`), the size-order walk (`curios-analysis/src/totality.rs`) and `RecItem::opened` (`curios-core/src/module.rs`). An abort is no refusal, which is [the soundness board](../../design/soundness/the-soundness-board.md)'s fourth boundary: "A panic, an abort or a stack overflow is acceptable only where it refuses". Unlike the tail's count, this one is read before the boundary that checks it: `positivity_vectors` runs from `accept` ahead of `check_induct_decl` and forces every constructor payload type, so a payload whose type is such a member selection aborts the whole-module walk with no verdict filed. Seen at `7b63491ff` by three tests, each aborting in `Scope::open` rather than returning a verdict — one in `kernel::whnf::rules_tests` for the kernel's own reduction, with the recursive strategy and the closed machine each reached, its differential twin in `machine::tests`, and one in `recheck::recursion_tests` over a hand-built module whose only item declares `induct Bad | c(x : <a member selection out of such a group>)`, whose positions are all closed so the boundary of `f8eda8f64` passes it. A top-level `rec` item is the sharper route, and there the refusal is not lost but never produced: such an item is read through `RecItem::definitions`, whose `opened` (`curios-core/src/module.rs`) opens every member's `type_` and `body` over one term per *definition*, and `item_positions` calls it from both boundary loops of `verdicts_within` — so the walk dies in the opened view before `check_group` is entered, and the `Counted::GroupBinders` clause that has stood since before this run is unreachable for a `rec` item. It is the one place where the count already had a boundary and the boundary cannot run. Shown at `7b63491ff` by a `fixture_verdicts` over a module whose one item is such a `rec`, narrowed by a probe whose whole body is `item.definitions()`, which aborts standing alone with no walk around it. Read in the same function and not run: `opened` zips the definitions with the group's members and opens at the definitions' count, so a `definitions.len() != group.length()` disagreement — what `check_rec_group` refuses by `Counted::GroupMembers` — opens each visited scope at the wrong count in the same line. No route from a surface program is known: `curios-elab`'s `RecItem::try_new` closes every member scope at the member count, and what reaches this is a module built by hand or a restored archive, through `RecGroup::new` and `RecItem`'s `group` field. Fix, and the choice sets: either hold the count where the representation can carry it — `RecGroup::new` admitting only a members vector whose every scope binds one binder per member, with the same count read at the store seam (`validate_stored_identities`) for the archive route, after which no malformed group reaches a reader and the eight above lose the question at once — or, narrowly, a `RecGroup::opens` beside `Rec::opens` that each reducer asks first, with a stuck answer in each of the three. The first makes the forbidden form unrepresentable and leaves reduction untouched, and it is the only one of the two that reaches `RecItem::opened`, which no guard in a reducer covers; the second states the relation in as many places as there are readers. Check: `cargo test --package curios-cert -- whnf::rules_tests recheck::recursion_tests` and `cargo test --package curios-core -- machine::tests`, with a case per reducer and one for the walk, plus `cargo xtask workspace lint` and `cargo xtask workspace test`. Size: two commits for the representation, the constructor then its callers; one for the narrow guard.
- **Uncertain: a model small enough to run locally can be the candidate oracle of a hunt.** [A probe asks the board's question of a program on no ticket](../../../xboard/README.md) puts a candidate to the checkers exactly as a witness and exits 0 where it is admitted, so a hunt needs of its oracle programs, not judgment: every ticket on the board was found by a frontier model under an agent, and nothing shown says the question needs one. Seen by the paper's review of 2026-10-24, as an observation on the discipline, not a measurement. Not known: whether a model small enough for one RTX 4070 Super, run on its own, produces an admitted candidate against a commit known to admit one (a fixing commit's parent, its ticket's witnesses withheld), how long it takes to the first, whether different models reach the same flaw or different ones, and so whether a hunt scales out to many small oracles rather than a few large ones. Settled by: a run per model, sequential on that machine, against that commit under a time cap, each candidate put to `cargo xboard probe`, the time to the first admission and the flaw it shows recorded; an admission within the cap restates this as a finding on the hunt's harness, and none within the cap drops it. Size: unknown.
- **Uncertain: the size order reads a proposition's own case as structure, which irrelevance leaves no room for.** [Totality of the erased program](../../design/soundness/totality-of-the-erased-program.md) reads what reduction took apart, and an arm's case is read for a `Prop`-sorted scrutinee as for any other (`refined_by_arm`, `curios-analysis/src/totality/grade.rs`), so a recursion descends on an inductive proposition's constructors. A match on a proposition fires with the proof unreduced ([`Prop` is strict, proof-irrelevant, and definitionally K](../../design/theory/prop-is-strict-proof-irrelevant-and-definitionally-k.md)), so the arm's payloads are no part of the value it was handed, and where `Prop` is a subsingleton a strict decrease between two proofs is a decrease in a one-element set. `curios-analysis/src/totality.rs`'s module doc defends the reading by finiteness — every payload is sized under its family, so none is applied at the family to hand a value of it back — which argues the payload tree is finite and not that the order respects the equality irrelevance imposes. Seen by `wonder diagnostics -` at `aaaf861d2`, which accepts an accessibility recursion with its predicate declared `Prop` and another with it at `Type`, against a control refusing a proof recursion with no descent. Not known: whether the reading reaches `False`. The reading had a guard once and lost it to a sibling: `ebf65a7c0` skipped a solved binder that `bears_proof` held of, for `a_proof_index_refined_by_irrelevance_is_graded_as_the_constructor_it_was_unified_with`, and `48db7bbd0` then took every inversion solution out of the order for `a_proofs_constructor_inside_an_arms_solution_is_graded_as_structure`, so the guard went with the carrier it guarded and the scrutinee's own refinement never had one. What a witness must get past is coverage: a recursion graded by that refinement needs an inhabited proposition whose every arm recurses, so every non-recursive constructor's arm must be ruled out by its index targets — `check_induct_arms` demands exactly that of an absent arm, since undecided is not absent — which is the route both fixed tickets took. Agda restricted it and reverted it (`agda/agda` PR #6639, commit `cca1e0d`), Lean keeps it at the cost of a definitional equality Carneiro shows undecidable and non-transitive, and Felicissimo, Leray, Pujet, Tabareau, Tanter and Winterhalter (LICS 2026) answer the undecidability by unfolding an accessibility predicate propositionally rather than definitionally — a price Curios does not pay, since irrelevance decides a `Prop` comparison before either side reduces and a proposition holding a payload its indices do not pin eliminates into no data. Settled by: a witness put to `cargo xboard report`, filed open where the kernel admits it; where none is found the reading is the totality decision's to state, naming what the alternative costs — `/std` descends on a bound and declares no accessibility predicate ([`curios-prelude`'s README](../../../curios-prelude/README.md)), and a predicate declared at `Type` keeps the recursion while erasure retains its tree. Size: unknown.
