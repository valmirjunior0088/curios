# Soundness: findings

- **A function literal's proof mark is held where the literal is inferred and not where it is checked.** [The closed machine binds a proof unreduced](../../design/compilation/the-closed-machine-binds-a-proof-unreduced.md) has the kernel refuse a mark its own reading of the binder's type does not bear. `hold_proof` (`curios-cert/src/obligation.rs`) is called where `infer_telescope` opens an inferred literal's binders, where a `let` binds and where a declaration's or a group's telescope is walked. `check_lambda` (`curios-cert/src/kernel/infer.rs`), the checked half of the λ rule, opens a literal's binders against its expected function type and never asks. That half is the route a definition's leading lambda takes, so a literal whose binder states a proof at `Nat` is admitted there and its argument is bound unreduced by the closed machine. No source program reaches it, since the elaborator states marks from the binder's type; a module built by hand does. Today the consequence is a cost and no verdict, since erasure reads a sort; under the form [A proof is known by its mark](300-a-proof-is-known-by-its-mark.md) reserves, erasure reading the mark, it would be a miscompilation. Fix: `check_lambda`'s entry step holds the literal's own mark at its domain, as `infer_telescope`'s does, with `Lockstep` handing over the left scope's `binds_proof`. Check: `a_checked_binder_marked_at_a_type_that_is_no_proposition_is_refused` beside `a_binder_marked_at_a_type_that_is_no_proposition_is_refused` in `curios-cert/src/kernel/infer/proof_binder_tests.rs`, putting `stating(Term::func([(n, nat_type())], Term::free_var(&n)), &[true])` to `check` against `Term::func_type([(binder(1, "m"), nat_type())], nat_type())` and expecting `Err(Error::NoProof(nat_type()))`; `cargo xtask workspace lint`. Size: one commit.

- **Uncertain: a model small enough to run locally can be the candidate oracle of a hunt.** [A probe asks the board's question of a program on no ticket](../../../xboard/README.md) puts a candidate to the checkers exactly as a witness and exits 0 where it is admitted, so a hunt needs of its oracle programs, not judgment: every ticket on the board was found by a frontier model under an agent, and nothing shown says the question needs one. Seen by the paper's review of 2026-10-24, as an observation on the discipline, not a measurement. Not known: whether a model small enough for one RTX 4070 Super, run on its own, produces an admitted candidate against a commit known to admit one (a fixing commit's parent, its ticket's witnesses withheld), how long it takes to the first, whether different models reach the same flaw or different ones, and so whether a hunt scales out to many small oracles rather than a few large ones. Settled by: a run per model, sequential on that machine, against that commit under a time cap, each candidate put to `cargo xboard probe`, the time to the first admission and the flaw it shows recorded; an admission within the cap restates this as a finding on the hunt's harness, and none within the cap drops it. Size: unknown.
