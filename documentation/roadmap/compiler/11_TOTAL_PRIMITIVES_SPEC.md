# Retiring `/sys`'s partial primitives, and the obligations that replace them

This document specifies removing partiality from the intrinsic roster: every `/sys` operation becomes total by carrying its precondition in its type. The enabling artifact is a trivial-inhabitation rule in the elaborator, without which a proof-carrying primitive is unusable at a literal argument.

Four of six milestones have landed, and with M4 the roster holds no partial operation. M1 built the slot mechanism and put `get` behind its bound, M2 made that bound discharge itself wherever it is already decided, M3 and M4 spent that mechanism on `slice` and on division. What remains is the two that change no mechanism: M5 restates what the `/sys` gate is for, M6 asserts the premise it never held up. The `Problem` section below describes the state before any of it — `/std/Map`'s bypasses in particular were closed independently while this was being written.

The first draft admitted a second answer — a canonical total extension, where one is determined by the structure — and assigned `slice` to it. That was refuted before any code landed; the Design section records the refutation, because the criterion that produced the wrong answer is also what caught it and is worth keeping. `Nat/sub`'s existing clamp remains the one operation the roster totalizes that way, and this document does not disturb it.

It also settles what the `/sys` provenance gate is for, which is the question the work started from, and asserts the one premise about `/sys`'s surface that the gate never held up.

## Problem

`/sys/Bytes/get` has two safe faces in `/std/Bytes` — `at`, carrying `Nat/Lt(i, len(b))`, and `get`, returning `Option(Byte)` — and `/std/Map` bypassed both, calling the raw intrinsic three times behind `choose` and `match` guards that establish the bound semantically and refine nothing (`std/Map.crs:45`, `:57-58`). The guard is correct; the language has a way to say so; the code says it in a comment instead.

The provenance gate did not catch this and structurally cannot: `RootKind::Internal` grants whole-root trust to whole roots, so `/std/Map` and `/std/Bytes` are indistinguishable to it. The point is made conclusively by `/std/List/find` (`std/List.crs:32`), which performs the same bypass from *inside* `/std/List` — the one module any conceivable reach rule would authorize. The defect is what the code does, not where it sits.

Underneath both is a deeper fact. `Bytes/slice` out of range is not a stuck term and not a junk value: it is `Err(ReduceError::BinSliceOutOfRange)`, a reduction that *fails*. Definitional equality is therefore defined only where the bounds happen to be good, and the condition appears in no type. `SOUNDNESS.md`'s *Intrinsic fold laws and the free-monoid peel* row concedes exactly this — window fusion is stated at arbitrary bounds with no ordering side condition and holds "for a reason outside the law… an argument made while reading, not a fixture."

No mature implementation does this. Coq, Lean, Agda, Isabelle and F* all totalize, differing only in how; none ships a partial primitive that participates in conversion. `Nat/sub` shows Curios already agrees — it clamps (`curios-core/src/reduce/intrinsic.rs:657-669`), the truncated-subtraction convention — for one operation and not the other nine, with no recorded reason for the split.

## Constraints, verified

- **The roster is ten operations in six `ReduceError` variants** (`curios-core/src/reduce.rs:28-64`): `Bytes/get` and `Bits/get`; `List/get`; `Bytes/slice` and `Bits/slice`; `List/slice`; `Nat/div`, `Nat/rem`, `Int/div`, `Int/rem`; `Int/to_nat`. Only the two `get` families are guarded in `/std`; the other six are raw `pub use` re-exports, and `div`/`rem` are additionally reachable through the `/` and `%` operators, so every division a user writes is a partial operation.
- **The reducer and the emitter currently agree exactly on `slice`.** `PackedBin::slice` requires `start <= end && end <= len` (`curios-base/src/packed.rs:126-131`); the emitted `bytes/slice` traps on `s > e || e > len(r)` (`curios-cont/src/into_wasm/rope_emitter.rs:767-783`). The empty-window and whole-window fast paths agree too. There is no divergence today, which is precisely why a half-migration would introduce one.
- **Window fusion is unguarded, and that is what makes `slice`'s partiality load-bearing.** `curios-core/src/spine.rs:138-152` fuses on a matching seam alone, with no check that either window's bounds are ordered; `:137` drops a window only when `lo == hi` syntactically. The Window atom's own contract — a length-`hi - lo` chunk — assumes `lo <= hi` and nothing enforces it. See the Design section for why this refutes clamping.
- **Slice bounds are decided in four places, not three.** Besides `PackedBin::slice`, the core folds and the emitter, `curios-ersd/src/semantics.rs:503-511` decides them again in the constant folder, answering `TrapKind::SliceOutOfBounds`. Any change to slice's domain must move all four together — and changing `PackedBin::slice`'s return type is what makes that mechanical, since every participant becomes a compile error until it is addressed.
- **`curios-ersd` already models the arithmetic four as trapping** (`semantics.rs:235`), which blocks speculation and hoisting.
- **`Int` overflow is not a type-level concern.** Folds are unbounded in core (`reduce.rs:53`) and the i31 check lives in `into_wasm` (`code_emitter.rs:136-166`), matching the stated layering: unbounded in core, width-32 in Ersd and Cont, width-31/30 only at emission. So `NonZero` is the whole type-level precondition for `Int/div`.
- **`Nat/Lt` is decided and `Nat/Lte` is not.** `Lt(a, b)` reduces through the machine comparison and `Lt/try` decides it in one line (`std/Nat/Lt.crs:5-8`); `Lte` is an inductive family with constructors `z`/`s` and no decision procedure (`std/Nat/Lte.crs:4-7`). Any design spelling a precondition in `Lte` inherits a proof-construction problem that the `Lt` spelling does not have.
- **A concept field may depend on an earlier one.** `SYNTAX.md`'s *Concept declarations* states the field list is a dependent telescope whose later fields may refer to earlier named ones, with the reference becoming a projection of the resolved witness in the generated wrapper; the `Idem` example is exactly that shape at `Prop`. `check_is_sort` (`curios-elab/src/typing.rs:71-84`) admits any field whose type is sort-classified, and `(T) -> Prop` is. **No existing concept or struct in the prelude has a `Type`- or `Prop`-valued field**, so this is permitted by construction rather than exercised, and the universe level a concept holding one must sit at is open.
- **An implicit proof parameter does not discharge itself, and cannot be made to by witness resolution.** Metavariables are solved by unification alone, reaching `zonk` unsolved and reporting `cannot_infer` (`curios-elab/src/zonk.rs:24`, `:910`). Proof irrelevance equates inhabitants but manufactures none, and is deliberately sequenced after the metavar dispatch (`convert.rs:1987`). `HeadKey` keys only on type heads (`concept.rs:51-125`), so a value-indexed `NonZero(b)` is unkeyable and cannot be a concept. This is the one constraint that came back negative, and M2 exists because of it.
- **The machinery M3 needs already exists.** `ParkedWork` (`context/solutions.rs:34-49`) carries goals that cannot be solved yet, retried at `elaborate/module.rs:923` and finalised at `:1006`; `ProofSyntax.true_qed` names the term to emit and is already emitted from Rust at `into_core/lowerer.rs:187`.
- **A `/std` facade re-export is reachable from ordinary consumers.** The gate checks the resolved module *prefix* and never re-guards a re-exported binding, so `/std/List`'s `pub use /sys/List/{len, slice, map}` is callable from an entry program — `curios/src/tests/aggregates.rs:133` does exactly that.
- **The wire-code mirror is generated, not authored.** `/sys/status`, `/sys/poll` and `/sys/mode` are built from `curios_abi`'s constants at prelude-build time (`curios-text/src/prelude.rs:680-717`). Host and guest stay in sync because both derive from one source; moving those names into authored `/std` source would replace a derived constant with a typed-in number.

## Design

**The criterion: does the operation have a canonical total *extension*?** Not merely a canonical inhabitant of its result type — that test is too coarse, since `0` inhabits `Nat` and would wrongly license `x/0 = 0`. The question is whether one total function agrees with the partial one on its domain and is determined by the structure rather than chosen. Where the answer is yes, totalize and the obligation disappears. Where it is no, the precondition belongs in the type, because an arbitrary convention justified only in prose is the artifact this work exists to remove.

**`slice` has no canonical extension either — clamping was specified here and is refuted.** The first draft of this document argued that "the elements from index 5 to index 2" is genuinely empty, that clamping was therefore forced as `Nat/sub`'s already is, and that window fusion would become *unconditionally* true. The last claim is false, and it is false in the admitting direction.

The spine fuses adjacent windows with no ordering check at all (`curios-core/src/spine.rs:138-152`): `Window{b, lo₁, hi₁}` followed by `Window{b, lo₂, hi₂}` collapses to `Window{b, lo₁, hi₂}` whenever `hi₁ == lo₂`. Under today's partial semantics that is safe for exactly the reason `SOUNDNESS.md` gives — an out-of-order slice is a reduction error, so neither side of the equation ever holds a value and the disagreement cannot be observed. Clamping hands those terms values, and then `slice(b, 5, 2) ++ slice(b, 2, 8)` is six bytes while the fused `slice(b, 5, 8)` is three. The corpus already carries the tool to exhibit it: `curios/src/tests/aggregates.rs:99-101` proves `split` **universally quantified over its bounds**, so `split(b, 5, 2, 8)` is a proof of a false equation between two concretely different values, and congruence with substitution carries that to `False` by the route this file records elsewhere. Reducing out-of-order slices to empty eagerly does not save it, because `split`'s bounds are variables.

**What the error was, and what survives it.** The criterion is sound; it was applied to the wrong structure. "Empty" reads as the canonical answer from the *sequence*, but the structure the laws are actually stated over is the free-monoid spine, where a window is a length-`hi - lo` chunk — meaningful only when `lo <= hi`. The ordering is not incidental to slicing; it is built into the representation. So `slice` fails this document's own test and lands beside `get` rather than beside `Nat/sub`, and the criterion earns its keep by being what caught the mistake.

`slice` therefore takes a precondition (`s <= e <= len`) in the decided style. The Window invariant becomes a consequence of typing, the fusion rule stays unguarded and sound, and the laws in `aggregates.rs` gain the side conditions they always semantically had.

**`get` does not extend; its result is an element.** For `List(T)` at a generic `T` there is no inhabitant to return, so the precondition or the `Option` is forced — which is what `/std` already provides. The work here is not designing a face but stopping the raw intrinsic from having one that bypasses it.

**Division does not extend either, and `x/0 = 0` is rejected.** Lean and Isabelle adopt it, and their reasoning inverts here on both halves. Junk is free at runtime in a proof assistant; in Curios it must be *emitted*, since Wasm's `i32.div_u` traps, so every division pays a compare-and-select to define a case correct programs never reach — while a proof parameter erases to nothing. And the authoring burden that made the precondition style unworkable for Lean is largely absent, because a decided proposition reduces away: `NonZero(256)` becomes `True` and discharges definitionally, leaving evidence required only where the divisor is a variable, which is exactly where a programmer should have established it.

**The domain belongs to the concept, so `/` stays one operator.** `Div(A)` gains an `Ok(A) -> Prop` field that `div` depends on; `Nat` and `Int` set it to `NonZero`, and `Flt` sets it to `True` and pays nothing, IEEE division being genuinely total. That last case is the check that the shape is right rather than fitted — the concept can express "this instance has no precondition" without a special case.

**Trivial inhabitation is not proof search.** A metavariable whose type reduces to an inductive with exactly one constructor taking no arguments is solved with that constructor. The answer is unique, so there is no choice, no backtracking and no incompleteness — it is the introduction-side dual of the singleton condition the elaborator already applies to elimination (`elaborate/match_.rs:490`) and of proof irrelevance, which already holds that every inhabitant is interchangeable. The rule fires exactly when the obligation is definitionally discharged and refuses exactly when it is not: `n / 256` fills silently, `n / m` demands evidence. `Lte(3, 5)` is excluded because `Lte/s` takes an argument, and `False` because it has no constructor. The kernel is untouched — it rechecks an ordinary application.

**The evidence route is narrower than this document assumed, and the gap is a `rec` application.** Every claim above about a symbolic operand rests on the programmer being able to *supply* what the evaluator cannot reach — decided where it decides, proved by induction and passed as `@` where it does not, which is exactly what `/std/Str/utf8`'s `drop_width_within` does. Measured after M3 landed: a decided proposition's scrutinee does not unfold a program-local `rec` application, though conversion unfolds the same term without difficulty. `Eq(Bytes/len(go(0, acc)), Bytes/len(acc))` checks; `Nat/Le(Bytes/len(acc), Bytes/len(go(0, acc)))` does not, with `go(0, acc)` left standing in the goal. The discriminator is `rec` alone — a non-recursive function, and a non-recursive function containing a `match`, both discharge.

The consequence is not that a bound is expensive but that it is *unstatable*: the base case of any lemma about a recursive function is unprovable in decided form, so the inductive route is shut for precisely the operands that need it most. `drop_width_within` is the same shape and works, so this is not universal; the unverified difference is a program-local `rec` elaborating to an inline `Rec` term with slots against a prelude definition's global body. Recorded rather than fixed, because it belongs to whichever milestone takes reduction on and not to this one — but the sentence above about evidence "required only where the divisor is a variable" should be read against it.

**The `/sys` gate survives on a different justification.** After the above, nothing behind it is a soundness hazard: the partial ops are gone, the total ones never were, and reaching a host row past its `/std` facade yields worse code rather than wrong code. The gate never protected the one premise that does depend on `/sys`'s surface — that `/sys/Io` exports no eliminator — since that is a property of what is *exported*, which the gate does not constrain. What remains is API stability: `/sys` is generated from `curios-abi`'s foreign store and moves when the ABI moves. That is a real reason to keep it, and it should be recorded as the reason, replacing the assumption that it is a safety mechanism.

## Milestones

Numbered in execution order. `slice` was M1 in the first draft and is now M3, because the precondition route makes it depend on both the slot mechanism and trivial inhabitation, where clamping had depended on neither.

- **M1 — decided-proposition slots, and `get`'s obligation. _Landed._** `Lt` is declared in `syn.crs` itself rather than given a file, a `ProofSyntax.lt` slot names it, and `sys_module` takes `&SYNTAX`. The checked accessor is `/sys/{Bytes,Bits,List}/at(b, i, ok : Lt(i, len(b)))` and `/std` re-exports it, so the wrapper that used to hold the obligation is gone rather than forwarding. `/std`'s `Option`-returning `get` is built on `at`, and `List/find` scrutinises `len(l)` directly rather than a `let`-bound alias. **This milestone's real product is the slot mechanism**, which M3 and M4 both reuse. *Acceptance, met: no raw accessor call site remains in the prelude; the prelude build's slot check passes; whole corpus green.*
  - **The bound is spelled `len(b)`, not the `BinLen` intrinsic — _changed from the plan_, and the correction generalises.** Building it as `Lt(i, BinLen(grain, b))` was the obvious reading and it does not work: a scrutinee refinement is keyed on *the term written*, a caller can only write `i < len(b)`, and definitional equality between the two spellings is not what the lookup consults. So `sys_module` resolves one name where it otherwise only plants nodes. **Any precondition this document adds later inherits the rule**: state it the way source can state it, or the obligation is unpayable.
  - **`Lt` cannot be written with `<`, and the reason is an ordering edge nothing can see — _discovered here_.** `into_core` leaves an infix unresolved and the `Cmp` projection is inserted during elaboration, so the topological sort has no `Lt → Cmp(Nat)` edge. It ordered correctly by source-order accident until `/sys/at` depended on `Lt` and pulled it ahead of the witness. Scrutinising `/sys/Nat/lt` removes the dependency, and the refinement still fires — the reducer normalises both spellings to the same `NatLt` head. **The hazard outlives this milestone**: any `/syn` declaration using an operator carries the same invisible edge, and only source-order luck orders it.
  - **Two call-site facts the plan had wrong.** `/std/Map`'s two bypasses were already closed independently, in `8fe92f39`, by refining on `g < Bytes/len(k)` so the proposition reduces to `True` — the idiom this milestone then followed rather than the `Lt/try` the plan named. And `Async`'s poll loop was a fourth site of a different class: its index is in range because the host answers one result per handle, which is a promise rather than a fact about the program, so it reads through the `Option` face and a short list parks the job instead of trapping. That is the two-faced accessor working as designed — where the bound is provable you take `at`, where it crosses a trust boundary you take `get`.
- **M2 — trivial inhabitation. _Landed._** `trivially_inhabited` in `curios-elab/src/elaborate/metavar.rs`, consulted by `insert_auto_argument` before it mints an implicit hole: an obligation already decided in the goal's favour is filled on the spot, everything else mints and reports as before. `/sys`'s accessors take their bound implicitly, and the five hand-written witnesses in the prelude are gone. *Acceptance, met: `Map`, `List/find` and the three `Option` faces index with no witness written; the four sites holding a real proof supply it as `@proof`; whole corpus green and the kernel accepts the result with no new rule.*
  - **It must fire where the obligation is born, not at a later sweep — _changed twice, and the second time by a failure_.** The first draft parked a `ParkedWork` variant; planning replaced that with a sweep over unsolved implicit-origin metavariables at the per-item retry, on the grounds that the origin is already stamped and the `MetaEntry` already freezes its telescope. The sweep does not work, and the reason is worth keeping: **a scrutinee refinement lives only inside its arm.** A caller who guards with `i < len(b)` establishes the bound *there*; by the time any item-boundary pass reduces `Lt(i, len(b))`, the refinement is out of scope, the proposition is stuck, and the discharge fails against a caller who did everything right. The frozen telescope carries assumptions, not case equations. So the check sits at `insert_auto_argument`, where the deciding facts are still in scope — and the sweep was removed rather than kept as a second chance, since it can only ever catch goals that never needed the refinement.
  - **Recognised narrowly, by name rather than by shape — _changed from the plan_.** The predicate was specified as "an inductive with exactly one constructor taking no arguments"; it ships as "reduces to `/syn`'s trivially true proposition", which needs one registry slot (`ProofSyntax.true_type`) instead of a registry lookup and telescope arithmetic separating declaration parameters from payload. It covers every *decided* proposition, which is the whole family `/sys` draws preconditions from. A user's own single-nullary-constructor proposition is not discharged; widening is mechanical and isolated if a consumer ever wants it.
  - **Implicit arguments fill positionally, which taxes `List/at`.** `Bytes/at` and `Bits/at` have one implicit, so a real proof reads `at(b, i, @below)`. `List/at` leads with `@T`, so the same call must spell the element type: `at(@T, l, i, @below)`. Four sites pay this; every discharged site pays nothing, which is the trade the milestone is for.
- **M3 — `slice` takes its precondition. _Landed._** `s <= e <= len` in the decided style, as two implicit bounds on the `/sys` primitive, so the `Bits`/`Bytes`/`List` re-exports stop handing out an unguarded op. *Acceptance, met: `split`, `empty` and `full` in `curios/src/tests/aggregates.rs` restate with their real side conditions and still check by `refl`, and both seam-mismatch controls still reject; the fusion rule in `spine.rs` is left unguarded because typing now protects it; whole corpus green.* **Nothing in the reducer, the emitter or `curios-ersd` changed** — the earlier plan to move all four bound-deciding sites belonged to clamping, which was refuted; a precondition leaves the partial fold exactly where it was and makes the ill-bounded call unwritable instead.
  - **The library grew the crossings it was missing, and one was already hand-written.** `/std/Nat/Le` is now a module — `refl`, `of_lte`, `to_lte`, `succ_r`, `trans`, `try` — beside `Lt/{lte_of_lt, le_of_lt, lte_succ_of_lt, le_succ_of_lt, le_one_of_positive}`. A decided proposition discharges but cannot be recursed over; an inductive one recurses but never discharges; every one of these is the passage between, and none was writable before summand cancellation. `lte_succ_of_lt` already existed *inside `std/Str/utf8.crs`*, written where it was first needed — the corpus asking for a `Nat` ordering library and having nowhere to put one.
  - **The `Parse` contract was changed and then changed back — _the largest wrong turn in this document_.** Bounding `run`'s position cascaded into ~900 lines of `Toml` scanner work, and **no `Toml` file slices `Bytes` at all**; it also contradicted the module's own design, since `Parse/any_byte` already accepts an unbounded position and validates with `Lt/try`, failing into the error channel. The rule that survives: **where a position is computed locally, carry its bound; where it arrives from outside, test it.** `take_while` carries both bounds through its fold and tests only the incoming position; `Fmt/flush`, `http/rest` and `http/take_n` test, each falling into an answer they already had for unusable input.
  - **A comparison could not decide `Le(n, n)`, and the fix was owed elsewhere.** `compare_nat`'s equality was syntactic, so two occurrences of a polymorphic name — independently instantiated, identical in every respect a report shows — failed to cancel and left the bound stuck. Both it and `Nat::cancel_common` now compare under `project_erased_universes`, which `convert.rs` already licenses for values (*"identical erased spellings are definitionally equal"*, naming *"independently fresh instances"*) and which the refinement key already probes under. Reduction had simply never been told what conversion already asserted.
- **M4 — division, `rem`, and `Int/to_nat`. _Landed._** The obligation lands on the `/sys` primitives *and* on the concept: `Div`/`Rem` gain an `Ok(A) -> Prop` field their method depends on, `Flt` supplies `True` and pays nothing. **Both layers, not just the concept** — `/std/Nat` re-exports `div`/`rem` raw, so a concept-only obligation would leave `/std/Nat/div(a, 0)` reachable, which is this document's own opening bug one level down. `Int/to_nat` takes `NonNeg(x)` by the same rule rather than gaining an `Option` face, so the roster keeps one answer instead of two. `curios-ersd`'s `semantics.rs` then relaxes `NatDiv`, `NatRem` and `IntRem` off `LocalBehavior::trap()`. *Acceptance, met: `17 / 0` and `+17 / +0` are refused at elaboration where they used to trap at runtime; `17 / 5`, `17 % 5`, `+17 / +5` and `+17.0 / +5.0` need no written witness, and a variable divisor under a `0 < b` guard needs none either while the unguarded one is refused; whole corpus green.*
    - **_Changed from the plan_ — `Nat` supplies no `NonZero`.** A natural is nonzero exactly when `0 < n`, which `ProofSyntax.lt` already states, so a second proposition would have been a second spelling of one fact and neither would reduce into the other. `/syn/Int` declares `NonZero` and `NonNeg`; `/syn/Nat` declares only the ordering. This is the same economy the `Ok` field buys at the concept level, one layer down.
    - **_Changed from the plan_ — the universe question answers itself.** A `Prop`-valued field forces no level bump: `Div`/`Rem` stay where they were. This was the one open item that could have forced a different field shape, and it did not.
    - **_Changed from the plan_ — the positional hazard was real, but not where it was predicted.** Inserting `Ok` before `div` did shift the index, and the prelude build's label check caught it as designed. The failure that cost the milestone was elsewhere and neither hazard named it: `elaborate_infix` rebuilt its dispatch with `Term::apply`, which marks *every* argument explicit. For a two-argument method that was indistinguishable from correct; against `rem`'s `(a, b, @ok)` it claims three explicit arguments for two explicit slots, and the arity check rejects it the moment anything **re-elaborates** the node — which first elaboration never does and erasure's re-derivation always does. The fix is `Term::apply_marked` carrying each slot's declared plicity. This is `elaborate_func_check`'s documented idempotence requirement, which the lambda side states and the application side did not: **a rebuilt term must re-check consuming every slot directly and inserting nothing.** The bug was latent under a corpus where no dispatched method had a hidden slot, so it was reachable only by this milestone and would have been reachable by any concept method that ever grew one.
    - `denoise.rs`'s `operator_for` needed no change: it folds a projection back to its operator by concept and field, and neither moved.
    - **Three corpus sites moved, and one of them turned out to be mis-filed.** `throw.rs`'s `checked_div` guarded on `b == 0` and divided in the refuted arm — the `Map/bit` shape exactly, and fixed the same way. `numeric.rs`'s differential probe narrows a runtime-tainted `Int`, so nothing settles its sign statically and it takes a guarded helper; both arms fold identically at the literal, so the fold-versus-runtime comparison still measures the conversion rather than the guard. The third is the interesting one: `overflowing_computations_trap_at_the_backend_boundary` carried `Int/to_nat(i - 1)` and `Nat/div(5 + n, n)` among its overflow probes, and **neither is an overflow.** That list is about a *valid* computation whose value leaves the i31 envelope — a range fact the backend enforces at materialization — where out of domain is a different failure with a different, and now earlier, answer. They move to their own test asserting refusal, so the coverage is kept rather than dropped, and the split the milestone draws between range and domain is finally drawn in the corpus too.
    - **`curios-ersd`'s own classification tests reached for `NatDiv` as their example of a trapping operation**, which it has stopped being. Both now use `IntDiv`, which still traps and states why — signed overflow is the range half, untouched by a divisor precondition — and the three that shed the classification are asserted to have shed it, so the reclassification is pinned in both directions rather than merely not contradicted. They surfaced late only because `cargo test` stops at the first failing binary: the run before this one died in `curios --lib` and never reached them.
    - **One residue, named rather than absorbed: `Int/abs` decides its bound twice and answers `0` on a branch no `Int` reaches.** `to_nat` now demands `NonNeg`, and in the negated branch nothing derives `NonNeg(0 - n)` from `n`'s failure to be non-negative — `Int` carries no ordering family to recurse over and no reduction relates the two, so the branch re-decides and needs an answer for a `none()` that cannot occur. A junk value justified in prose is the artifact this document exists to remove, and it is here on a technicality: it is *unreachable* junk rather than junk on a reachable path, which is weaker than the standard set above and is not the standard. **The successor is named: an `Int` normal form with an offset and a symbolic tail, under which `0 - n` normalises to `-n` and `abs` proves instead of deciding.** Until then this is the one place in the roster where the obligation is discharged by re-deciding rather than by evidence. It is also strictly better than what it replaced, which produced *reachable* garbage at `Int`'s minimum where this produces `0`.
- **M5 — the gate, re-scoped.** `RootKind::Internal`'s justification is restated as ABI stability at `curios-base/src/mount.rs:52`, which owns the decision and currently describes the mechanism without saying why. No mechanism changes.
- **M6 — the `/sys/Io` roster, asserted.** `SOUNDNESS.md`'s *A type is a pure term* rests on there being no operation from `Io(T)` to `T`, and states that the premise is "one library file wide": `io_ops` returns `pure` and `bind` under a comment saying so, and an eliminator added beside them reopens three demonstrated routes — one of which compiled, passed the compile-path recheck and trapped at runtime — with no test failing. **The surface is two Rust tables, not the whole library.** A foreign row cannot introduce an eliminator, because `host_fn` wraps every store row's result in `Io` at one site and `WireType` is a closed enum with no case that could name an `Io` in a domain (`curios-abi/src/host/store.rs:33-40`); and no `.crs` can define one without already having one. So what remains is `/sys/Io`'s exported roster and the intrinsic roster. *Acceptance: adding an `Io(T) -> T` entry to either table fails a test rather than compiling.* **This narrows the premise rather than discharging it, and the row stays argued** — a check over two tables establishes that those two tables are clean, not the general property. Sequenced last because it is independent of M1–M5 and shares only their subject.

## Non-goals

- **A finer reach rule.** Per-module audiences (`/sys/Bytes` reachable only from `/std/Bytes`) were the first design considered and are rejected: they would have caught `Map` and missed `List/find`, their audience table would be derived from the corpus they are meant to police, and the two largest entries — `/sys/Handle` and `/sys/status` — would need five and six exceptions each. Once M1–M4 land, reach carries no correctness weight and can stay blunt.
- **Exposing `status`/`poll`/`mode` past the gate.** All eight consumers are `/std` internals already inside it. The one real user-facing case — a user's own `foreign` declaration returning a raw `status : Nat` — is better served by `std/Handle.crs:24`'s public `error_of`, which hands back the `Error` taxonomy instead of the wire.
- **`Flt/to_nat` and `Flt/to_int`.** Both trap at runtime (`semantics.rs:235`) but are invisible at the type level, by [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md)'s *`Flt` is opaque at the type level*. Nothing here reaches them, and closing them means reopening that decision.
- **The `i31` range as a type-level concern.** It stays at emission, per the layering above.
- **Discharging *A type is a pure term* in general.** M6 asserts the two tables an eliminator could enter through; the general property — that no operation takes an `Io(A)` to a non-effect result — is left open, because its naive form is already false in the corpus. `std/Async.crs:68`'s `lift(@A : Type, action : Io(A)) -> Async(A)` consumes an `Io` without returning one, legitimately: `Async` is itself a description, so nothing escaped. Stating the rule properly needs a notion of *what counts as an effect carrier* that admits `Async` and any future `Lift` edge while still refusing a runner for the host carrier — and Curios does have runners, `DESIGN.md` naming `block_on` and `State/run` as the sanctioned downward direction. That is a design question rather than a fixture, and it is named as the successor rather than smuggled in.
